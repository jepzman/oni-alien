Is the Work Actually Happening?

TL;DR

Once the plan is agreed, the project manager's daily job is to keep the work moving: a steady rhythm, a clear view of what is finished (not what is busy), and fast removal of whatever blocks the people doing it. You don't do the work or tell experts how. You make sure everyone knows what is next, who is waiting for whom, and that nothing sits blocked for a week because nobody knew.

  • Family: running and steering
  • Lives in: one weekly meeting, one board, your calendar between them
  • Pairs with: the issue log, sprint goals, the decision log

1The situation Larder, 5 March 2026

A week after the plan went to the steering group, everyone is busy. Sanne asks three leads in the corridor what they finished this week. Eli: “working on the zones.” Ben: “nearly there.” Dee: “waiting for Eli.” In seven days the count of finished must-haves has gone from 33 to 34. Dee has been waiting four days. Eli didn't know.

  • Effort is visible; finished work isn't.
  • People wait for each other without saying so.
  • A plan on a page doesn't move work by itself.
meanwhile, in Ana’s notebook
  • Decision Log (Ana, vol. 2): from 16 March Ana and Sanne review the decision log together every Monday: 6 open decisions had no owner.

2Real-life analogy the pass in a restaurant kitchen

During service the head chef stands at the pass, where every plate stops before it leaves. The chef isn't cooking. The chef sees what is ready, what is late, and which station is waiting for another, and calls it out: “two minutes on the fish, hold the sauce.” Without the pass, each cook is busy and the tables wait.

3How it solves it a rhythm, three questions, a board that counts “done”

Sanne sets a weekly rhythm. Monday: open decisions, with Ana. Thursday 09:30, thirty minutes, the seven leads, three questions each: what did you finish? what do you need from someone here in the next two weeks? what is blocking you? Friday: the status page. In between hangs a board of the 71 must-haves in four columns. Her own job between meetings is the blocked column: six cards on 5 March. Four are cleared by Tuesday with one conversation each. The next week the count moves from 34 to 41.

  1. Set a rhythm and keep it

    Same day, same time, short.

  2. Ask what was finished

    Against “done means”, not effort.

  3. Ask what each person needs from another

    Out loud, with a date.

  4. Ask what is blocked

    And write it where all can see.

  5. Clear blockers between meetings

    That is your work.

  6. Leave the how to the people doing it

    Step in only at the joins.

Vocabulary for the exam

Execution
Carrying out the work in the plan.
Deliverable
A finished, checkable piece of the result.
Blocker
Something that stops a piece of work from moving.
Cadence
The regular rhythm of meetings and reports.
Work in progress
Started, not finished. More is not better.
Stand-up
A short, regular meeting to co-ordinate.

4Light, standard, heavy how much of this do you need?

Light

one small team

  • A daily or weekly check-in.
  • A simple board.
  • Blockers said out loud.

Standard

Rotterdam: seven leads

  • Monday, Thursday, Friday rhythm.
  • A must-have board with a blocked column.
  • The project manager clears blockers.

Heavy

many teams and vendors

  • Work authorisation and formal progress reporting.
  • Integrated schedules updated weekly.
  • Dedicated co-ordinators per workstream.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Status theatre

    Each lead reports to the project manager; nobody talks to each other. Fix → ask what they need from someone in the room.

  • Counting effort

    “80 % done” for three weeks. Fix → done or not done.

  • Doing the work yourself

    The project manager configures zones. Fix → clear the blocker; leave the how.

  • A meeting that grows

    Problem-solving for all in front of all. Fix → 30 minutes; solve in pairs afterwards.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • From the day the plan is agreed.
  • When people are busy but little finishes.
  • When teams wait for each other.
  • On any project with more than one team.

Skip it when…

  • A single team with its own working rhythm: join it, don't add another.

8Self-check quiz

What are Thursday's three questions?

What did you finish? What do you need from someone here? What is blocking you?

Whose job is the blocked column?

The project manager's, between meetings.

Why count “done” and not “in progress”?

Effort doesn't open a store; finished must-haves do.

Ben says the app work is “90 % done” for the third Thursday running. What does Sanne ask?

Which must-haves are done by their “done means”, and what stands between the rest and done. Then whether something is blocking.

Two leads start solving a problem in the Thursday meeting. What does Sanne do?

Ask them to take it together straight after, and note who will report back. The meeting stays at thirty minutes.

try it

This week, ask each person on your project three things: what did you finish, what do you need from someone else, what is blocking you? Write the blockers on one list. Clear two before Friday.

Stand at the pass. Count the plates that leave.

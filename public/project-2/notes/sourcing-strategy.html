a.k.a. “sourcing strategy”: make or buy, and contract types

Make It, Buy It, or Hire It?

TL;DR

For every big piece of a project, decide: make it with your own people, buy it, or lease it. If you buy, the contract type decides who carries the risk. Fixed price: the supplier does, and charges for it. Time and materials: you do. Cost plus: you do, with the supplier's fee on top. Put each risk with the party who can control it, and decide early: suppliers have lead times too.

  • Lives in: a sourcing table in the plan; the contracts themselves with legal

1The situation Larder, 24 February 2026

The builder's offer has two prices. Fixed price: €148k, handover on 3 April written into the contract. By the hour: about €131k, no promised date. Marco, looking at a reserve of €6k, likes the second number. Sanne looks at who pays if the electrician is late. With the cheaper offer, Larder does: in money and in days it doesn't have.

  • The lowest price isn't the lowest cost.
  • Nobody has decided what Larder does itself and what it buys.
  • Contracts are being signed one by one, with no overall view.

2Real-life analogy getting to the airport

You can drive yourself: cheap, and every problem is yours. You can take a taxi on the meter: flexible, but you pay for the traffic jam. Or you can book a fixed-fare transfer: more money, and the jam is the driver's problem. Which is right depends on what missing the flight would cost you.

3How it solves it one table: make, buy or lease, and which contract

Sanne makes one sourcing table with Joost, Marco and Lena. For each item: make, buy or lease, the reason, and the kind of contract. The fit-out is bought at a fixed price: the scope is clear (drawings exist) and the date matters more than €17k. Lena adds two clauses: €1,000 off per late day, up to ten days, and 5 % held back until the snags are fixed. The cold room is supply-and-install from one supplier, so one party owns “it works”. E-bikes are leased by operations. Training and the city set-up are made in-house: that is knowledge Larder needs for four more cities.

  1. List the big pieces of the project

    Anything over a few thousand euros or a few weeks.

  2. For each: make, buy or lease?

    Keep what you'll need again; buy what others do better.

  3. If buying: how clear is the scope?

    Clear → fixed price. Unclear → time and materials, with a cap.

  4. Put each risk with whoever can control it

    And expect to pay for that.

  5. Check lead times against the plan

    Order dates, not just delivery dates.

  6. Have legal and finance read it once, as a whole

    Not contract by contract.

Vocabulary for the exam

Make-or-buy
The decision to do something yourself or pay someone else.
Fixed price
One agreed price for an agreed result; the seller carries the cost risk.
Time and materials
Paying for hours and materials used; the buyer carries the risk.
Cost plus
Paying the seller's costs plus a fee; used when scope is very uncertain.
Retention
Part of the price held back until defects are fixed.
Lead time
The time between ordering and receiving.

4Light, standard, heavy how much of this do you need?

Light

a small purchase or two

  • Ask: who pays if it's late?
  • One line of reasoning.
  • Standard terms.

Standard

Rotterdam: two main suppliers

  • A sourcing table.
  • Contract type chosen per item.
  • Late-delivery and retention clauses.

Heavy

large or public procurement

  • A procurement plan and tender process.
  • Evaluation criteria and scoring.
  • Contract managers and formal claims handling.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Cheapest offer wins

    The risk comes back with interest. Fix → ask who pays if it's late.

  • Fixed price for unclear scope

    Every change is an extra. Fix → fix the scope first, or cap time and materials.

  • Buying what you need to learn

    The knowledge leaves with the supplier. Fix → make what you'll repeat.

  • Ordering by delivery date

    Forgetting the lead time. Fix → plan the order date.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Any project that spends money outside.
  • Before the first contract is signed.
  • When a supplier offers two pricing models.
  • When lead times are long.

Skip it when…

  • Everything is done by your own team with what it already has.

8Self-check quiz

Who carries the cost risk in a fixed-price contract?

The seller.

When does time and materials fit?

When the scope is unclear or will change. Add a cap.

Why make the training in-house?

Larder needs that skill for four more cities.

In March Joost wants to add a second cold-room door. The builder quotes €6k as an extra. Why is that expensive, and whose decision is it?

Fixed-price contracts price changes high. It is over €5,000, so the sponsor decides, through change control.

What does the 5 % retention do?

It is paid only when the snags are fixed, so the builder comes back.

try it

List the three biggest things your project buys. For each, write who pays, in money and in days, if it arrives late. Is that the party who can control the delay?

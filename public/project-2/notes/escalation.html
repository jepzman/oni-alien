When Do I Take It Upstairs?

TL;DR

To escalate is to pass a decision up because it is above your authority, or because you can't resolve it at your level. It is part of the job, not a failure: the thresholds exist so the right person decides. Do it early, while options are still open, and do it well: the situation, what it means, two or three options, your recommendation, and when you need an answer.

1The situation Larder, 23 March 2026

Monday morning the builder phones. The electrical inspection has been re-booked; handover will be 8 April, not 3 April. Five days. Sanne's first instinct is to fix it herself and bring Joost a solved problem on Friday. Five days is past the three-day line she herself agreed. And the one fix that works, weekend crews, has to be booked by Tuesday.

  • Escalating feels like admitting failure.
  • Waiting for a solution burns the days that options need.
  • The decision involves €5k that isn't hers to spend.

2Real-life analogy the night nurse

On a hospital ward at night, a patient's temperature passes a set number. The nurse phones the doctor now, not at the morning round, and not after trying three more things. And the nurse doesn't just say “come quickly”. The call has a shape: what is happening, what I think it means, what I'd recommend. The doctor decides in a minute, because the thinking has been done.

3How it solves it early, on one screen: situation, impact, options, recommendation, deadline

Sanne takes ninety minutes to do three things: confirm the builder's date in writing, ask Tomás what seven stocking days would mean, and get a price for weekend work. Then one message to Joost: situation (8 April, five days late, the cause), impact (stocking shrinks from ten working days to seven; Gate 2 at risk), three options with cost and risk, her recommendation (B: two weekends of work, €5k, handover back on 3 April; the reserve covers it and falls to €1k) and a deadline (Tuesday noon, to book the crews). Joost phones Marco and decides B at 16:00. It goes in the issue log as I-09 and the change log as C-02. The fit-out is handed over on 3 April.

  1. Know your thresholds before you need them

    Days, money, scope.

  2. When one is crossed, escalate the same day

    Not after you've tried to fix it.

  3. Check the facts quickly

    An hour or two, not a week.

  4. Bring options, not just the problem

    Two or three, with cost and risk.

  5. Recommend one

    It's your project; say what you'd do.

  6. Say when you need the answer, then record the decision

    In the issue and change logs.

Vocabulary for the exam

Escalation
Raising a decision or problem to someone with more authority.
Threshold
A limit that, when crossed, requires escalation.
Tolerance
How far from the plan the project manager may go before escalating.
Options paper
A short comparison of choices with a recommendation.
Decision deadline
The latest moment a decision still helps.
Management by exception
Leaders are involved only when limits are crossed.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Escalating late

    With no options left. Fix → the same day the threshold is crossed.

  • Escalating only the problem

    “The builder is late, what shall I do?” Fix → options and a recommendation.

  • Escalating everything

    The sponsor stops reading. Fix → only what crosses a threshold.

  • No deadline

    The decision arrives after the crews are gone. Fix → say when you need it.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • A threshold is crossed.
  • A decision isn't yours to make.
  • Two leads can't agree and it affects a milestone.
  • An issue hasn't moved in a week.

Skip it when…

  • It is inside your authority: decide, and report it in the status.

8Self-check quiz

When do you escalate?

When a threshold is crossed or the decision isn't yours, the same day.

What five things does a good escalation contain?

Situation, impact, options, recommendation, deadline.

Is escalating a failure?

No. It's how the right person gets to decide in time.

The builder is two days late, with a plan to catch up. Escalate?

No: inside the three-day tolerance. Report it in the status and watch it.

Joost doesn't answer by Tuesday 11:00. What does Sanne do?

Phone him. If he can't be reached, go to the next level on the governance page (Femke) with the same message; the crews must be booked by noon.

try it

Write down your three thresholds: how many days, how much money and what kind of scope change can you decide alone? If you can't, ask your sponsor today. Then draft, for the most likely problem on your project, the message you'd send.

remember!!

Phone when the number is passed, with what you'd recommend.

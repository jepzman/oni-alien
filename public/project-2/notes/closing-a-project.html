Are We Done?

TL;DR

A project is finished when the work is done. It is closed when someone with authority has agreed that it is: results accepted and handed over, contracts and money settled, the file archived, lessons turned into changes, benefits passed to their owners, and the team thanked and released. Closing is short and easy to skip, and skipping it leaves open invoices, orphaned promises and a team that never heard “well done”. The same checklist, smaller, closes every phase.

  • Family: closing
  • Lives in: a closing report on one page and a checklist
  • Pairs with: the charter, handover, lessons learned

1The situation Larder, 20–22 May 2026

The store opened on time, three weeks ago. Everyone has moved on: Ben to approvals for Rotterdam, Eli to Dispatch, Joost to the summer. But Marco's team still has the Rotterdam budget code open, with two purchase orders on it and the builder's final invoice unpaid. Nobody has told the steering group it's over. And nobody has said thank you to forty people who opened a city in fifteen weeks.

  • Open orders and codes keep collecting costs.
  • Without a final report, nobody compares result with promise.
  • A project that fades out teaches the team that finishing doesn't matter.

2Real-life analogy leaving the campsite

A camping trip doesn't end when you reach the lake. Before driving home you put out the fire, pack the tents, pay the site owner, take the rubbish, and look back once to check nothing is left. It takes half an hour, and it's the difference between a good trip and a phone call from the site owner in a week. A fire left smouldering is still your fire.

3How it solves it compare with the charter, tick the checklist, get the decision

Sanne takes the charter out again and writes the closing report against it, line by line, on one page. Promised: open 27 April, €380k, 71 must-haves. Happened: opened 27 April, €379.6k, 71 of 71. Benefits: orders reached 590 a day in week four (target 500 by end of May: met); on time is 84 % against 90 % by the end of the summer (open; owner Joost); playbook v1 is written. Then the checklist: results accepted (Yara, 15 May), contracts closed, the budget code closed with Marco, the project file archived, lessons turned into 21 changes. On 22 May the steering group accepts the report. Femke says the word: closed. That evening Joost buys dinner for everyone, and Sanne reads out one thing each lead did that made the difference.

  1. Confirm the result is accepted and handed over

    With a signature.

  2. Settle contracts and money

    Invoices, orders, the budget code.

  3. Write the closing report against the charter

    Promised, happened, why different.

  4. Hand each benefit and open item to an owner

    By name, with dates.

  5. Archive the file; make sure lessons became changes

    Where the next project will find them.

  6. Get the decision to close, then thank and release the team

    Out loud.

Vocabulary for the exam

Project closure
The formal end of a project, agreed by the sponsor or steering group.
Final report
A summary of what was promised, what was delivered, and what remains.
Contract closure
Confirming all obligations under a contract are met and paid.
Financial closure
Closing budget codes and settling all costs.
Release
Formally freeing people and resources for other work.
Benefits review
A later check, by the benefit owner, of whether the benefits arrived.

tools: the charter, a one-page report, a six-line checklist and a dinner.

4Light, standard, heavy how much of this do you need?

Light

a small job

  • “Done, accepted, nothing open.”
  • A thank-you.
  • A line in the log.

Standard

Rotterdam

  • A one-page report against the charter.
  • A six-line checklist.
  • A decision by the steering group.

Heavy

large or contracted work

  • Formal acceptance certificates.
  • Contract and financial audits.
  • A post-implementation review months later.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Fading out

    Nobody says it's over. Fix → a decision to close.

  • Closing only the work

    Money and contracts stay open. Fix → settle them; close the code.

  • A report that only celebrates

    Nothing learned. Fix → include what went wrong.

  • No thank-you

    People remember. Fix → say it, specifically.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • At the end of every project.
  • At the end of every phase, in small.
  • When a project is stopped early (close it properly too).
  • When an old project “still has budget”.

Skip it when…

  • Never. A cancelled project needs closing even more.

8Self-check quiz

Finished or closed: “the store is open”?

Finished. Closed needs acceptance, settlement and a decision.

What is the closing report compared against?

The charter and the baseline.

One benefit is still open at closing. Is that a problem?

No, if it has an owner and dates to check it. Benefits often arrive after the project.

Femke cancels a project halfway. Does it need closing?

Yes: accept what exists, settle contracts and money, record why, release the team.

The on-time figure is 84 %, below the 90 % target. Should the report leave it out?

No. It reports it as open, with the owner (Joost) and the check dates.

try it

Think of a project of yours that ended in the last year. Run the six-line checklist on it now: accepted, contracts, money, file, lessons, team. Which line is still open?

remember!!

Put out the fire, pay the site, look back once. Then say thank you.

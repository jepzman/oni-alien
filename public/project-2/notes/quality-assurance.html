a.k.a. “quality assurance” (and how it differs from quality control)

Is Our Way of Working Working?

TL;DR

Quality control checks the result: is this shelf right, does this order work? Quality assurance checks the way of working: do our checks exist, are they being done, do they catch things? Control finds faults. Assurance finds out why faults get through, and improves the process. A project needs both; assurance is the one that gets forgotten, because when it is missing everything looks fine.

  • Lives in: a short review every few weeks; improvements in the plan

1The situation Larder, 12–20 March 2026

The nightly script that lists products without a price was Rotterdam's proudest quality measure: the lesson of Zuid, automated. On 12 March Eli finds it has been failing for four nights. A failed run produces no list, and no list looks exactly like “no problems”. The check existed. It wasn't working. Nobody was checking the check. When it runs again it lists 212 products.

  • A check that fails quietly gives false comfort.
  • Everyone looked at results; nobody looked at the process.
  • The same could be true of every other check.

2Real-life analogy the chef and the inspector

In a restaurant the chef tastes each dish before it leaves the kitchen. That catches a salty sauce. Once a month an inspector checks the kitchen: are the fridges cold, is the tasting really happening, are the thermometers accurate? The inspector never tastes a dish, and still prevents more bad dinners than the tasting does.

3How it solves it three questions about every check in the process

On 20 March (the date set in February to review the way of working) Sanne, Eli, Tomás and Ben go through the five checks built into Rotterdam and ask three questions of each: does it exist? is it being done? does it catch things? The script: existed, wasn't running; it now sends Eli an alert if it fails or finds nothing to check. The practice orders: 12 of 15 pickers have done them; three haven't. The weekly test order: running, and it has caught two real faults. Three improvements go into the plan with owners. The review takes forty minutes, and it is where the missing internet line comes up.

  1. List the checks your process relies on

    From “done means” and the plan.

  2. Ask: does each exist?

    Written down, with an owner.

  3. Ask: is it actually being done?

    Look at evidence, not intentions.

  4. Ask: does it catch things?

    A check that never finds anything deserves a look.

  5. Agree a few improvements

    Owner and date each.

  6. Repeat every few weeks

    And after any fault that got through.

Vocabulary for the exam

Quality assurance
Making sure the processes used will produce good results.
Quality control
Checking the results themselves against what was required.
Audit
A structured check that a process is being followed and is working.
Process improvement
Changing how work is done so faults are less likely.
Silent failure
A check that fails without telling anyone.
Escaped defect
A fault that got past the checks.

4Light, standard, heavy how much of this do you need?

Light

a small team

  • A retrospective question: “did any check let something through?”
  • One improvement at a time.
  • Every few weeks.

Standard

Rotterdam

  • A 40-minute review of each built-in check.
  • Three questions each.
  • Improvements with owners.

Heavy

regulated or safety-critical work

  • A quality management plan and standards.
  • Independent audits.
  • Measured defect rates and corrective-action tracking.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Only checking results

    The process decays unnoticed. Fix → review the checks themselves.

  • Checks that fail quietly

    No news looks like good news. Fix → alert on failure and on “nothing checked”.

  • Assurance as blame

    People hide gaps. Fix → ask about the process, not the person.

  • A long list of improvements

    None happen. Fix → three, with owners.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • A few weeks into execution.
  • After any fault that got through.
  • At each phase gate.
  • When all checks have been green for suspiciously long.

Skip it when…

  • Throwaway work with nothing built in to check.

8Self-check quiz

Assurance or control: scanning a label at the shelf?

Control: it checks a result.

Assurance or control: checking that every picker did the practice orders?

Assurance: it checks that the process is being followed.

What are the three questions?

Does it exist? Is it being done? Does it catch things?

The weekly test order has passed six times in a row. Good news?

Probably, but ask whether it still tests what matters. A check that never finds anything deserves a look.

Why did the failed script look like “no problems”?

It produced no list. It now alerts when it fails or when it has nothing to check.

try it

Pick one check your project relies on. Find evidence, today, that it ran this week and what it found. If you can't, you've found your first quality assurance result.

remember!!

The chef tastes the dish. Someone must also check the kitchen.

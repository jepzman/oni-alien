Who Looks After It Now?

TL;DR

A project's result is worth something only once someone else is running it. The handover gives the people who will operate it everything they need: the thing itself, how it works, what is still open, and who now owns each benefit. Plan it from the start, do it face to face, set a short period of help with an end date, and get a signature. Until then the project isn't finished. It has only stopped.

1The situation Larder, 11–15 May 2026

Rotterdam has been open for two weeks. Yara, the store manager, phones Sanne six times a day. Where is the cold-room warranty? Who do I call when the router drops? Is that second door still coming? Yara isn't doing anything wrong. Everything about how the store came to be is in Sanne's head and Sanne's binder, and Sanne is due on her other projects and then Utrecht.

  • The people who run the result weren't given what they need.
  • Open items have no owner after the project.
  • There is no moment when the project stops being responsible.

2Real-life analogy selling a house

On the day a house is sold, a decent seller walks the buyer round: here is the fuse box, the boiler was serviced in March, these are the manuals and the spare keys. The gutter at the back still leaks a little; here is the roofer's number. Then the keys change hands. Skip the walk and the buyer phones for a year. Skip the leak and they phone a lawyer.

3How it solves it four things, a walk-round, an end date, a signature

Gate 5 on 11 May: the hypercare exit criteria are met. That week Sanne builds a handover pack of four parts. As built: drawings, warranties, supplier and landlord contacts. How it runs: the five daily numbers, the nightly checks, who to call for what. Still open: two issues, the deferred second cold-room door (now Joost's to decide), and the builder's 5 % retention, released when the last snag was fixed on 15 May. The benefits: both store benefits, with Joost as owner and the dates to check them. She walks Yara through it on site, and they agree an end: Sanne answers within a day until 29 May, then questions go to Joost. Yara signs. The phone calls fall to one a week.

  1. Name the receiver at the start

    And involve them before the end.

  2. Agree what “ready to hand over” means

    Exit criteria, as a gate.

  3. Pack four things

    As built, how it runs, still open, the benefits.

  4. Walk through it in person

    On site, with time for questions.

  5. Set a short support period with an end date

    Then questions go to the new owner.

  6. Get it signed

    Acceptance is a decision.

Vocabulary for the exam

Handover (transition)
Passing the project's result to those who will operate it.
Acceptance
The receiver's formal agreement that the result meets what was agreed.
As-built documentation
Records of what was actually delivered.
Warranty period
The time during which the supplier must fix defects.
Hypercare
A short period of extra support straight after go-live.
Operational readiness
The receiving team's ability to run the result.

4Light, standard, heavy how much of this do you need?

Light

a small internal change

  • Show the new owner how it works.
  • A short note of what's open.
  • “It's yours from Monday.”

Standard

Rotterdam

  • A four-part handover pack.
  • A walk-round on site.
  • Two weeks of support with an end date; a signature.

Heavy

large systems, contracts

  • Operational readiness reviews.
  • Formal acceptance tests and certificates.
  • Service-level agreements and a warranty process.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Throwing it over the wall

    A link to a folder. Fix → walk through it, in person.

  • Hiding what's open

    It comes back as a complaint. Fix → list every open item with an owner.

  • Support with no end

    The project manager becomes the helpdesk. Fix → an end date, agreed.

  • Receiver meets it at the end

    They weren't ready. Fix → name them at the start; Yara joined on 2 March.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Every project that leaves something running.
  • At the end of each phase that hands to another team.
  • When the receiver keeps phoning.
  • When a supplier hands over to you (Gate 1 was one).

Skip it when…

  • The result is used once and thrown away.

8Self-check quiz

What four things are handed over?

As built, how it runs, what's still open, and the benefits with their owner.

Why set an end date for support?

So questions move to the new owner and the project manager can be released.

Who owns the deferred cold-room door now?

Joost. It was handed over as an open item.

On 3 June Yara phones Sanne about a broken fridge shelf. What does Sanne do?

Help her find the warranty contact in the pack, and remind her kindly that since 29 May these go to Joost.

When should the receiver first be involved?

From the start. Yara joined on 2 March and took part in Gates 1 and 2.

try it

Name the person who will run your project's result. Write the four headings (as built, how it runs, still open, benefits) and one line under each. What would they phone you about in month two? Put that in the pack.

remember!!

Walk them round, show them the leak, hand over the keys.

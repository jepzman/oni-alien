What's in the Way Right Now?

TL;DR

An issue is a problem that has already happened and is in the way now. (A risk is something that might happen.) Issues go in one log the day they are found: what it is, what it affects, one owner, a date, and its status. If the real fix can't arrive in time, the log also holds the workaround. The log is read every week, and an issue that isn't moving is escalated.

  • Family: running and steering
  • Lives in: one shared list, read every Thursday
  • Pairs with: the risk register, directing the work, escalation

1The situation Larder, 20–30 March 2026

At the review of the way of working on 20 March, Eli asks: “who is ordering the store's internet line?” Silence. The builder: not building work. Eli: not software. It was on nobody's plan; Sanne's own map of the whole launch had no line for store IT. The provider quotes six weeks. The store opens in five and a half. This isn't a risk any more. It has happened.

  • The problem sat between two workstreams.
  • For ten days it lived in chat, with no owner.
  • The line was ordered only on 30 March.

2Real-life analogy the ship's defect book

On a ship, anything that breaks goes in the defect book the same day: what, where, who found it, who will fix it, by when. The captain reads it every morning. A fault that isn't in the book doesn't get fixed. A fault that has been in the book for a week gets the captain's personal attention. And if a part can't arrive before the next port, the book says how the ship will manage without it.

3How it solves it log it the day it's found: owner, date, workaround

Sanne starts the issue log that afternoon, ten days later than she should have, and enters the line as I-07 with Eli as owner. The fix: the line is ordered on 30 March and will be live around 13 May, two weeks after opening. The workaround: a 4G router with a backup SIM (€600, within Sanne's authority), leaning on something Ana's team built months ago: the picker app works thirty minutes offline. It is tested in rehearsal week. The store opens on the router; I-07 is closed on 13 May. Then Sanne adds an eighth workstream line to her whole-launch map: store IT.

  1. Log it the day it's found

    One line; don't wait to understand it fully.

  2. Say what it affects

    Which must-have, which milestone.

  3. Give it one owner and a date

    A name, not a team.

  4. If the fix is late, agree a workaround

    And test it.

  5. Read the log every week

    Out loud, with the leads.

  6. Close it, and ask why it happened

    The answer may be a lesson.

Vocabulary for the exam

Issue
A current problem that is affecting the project.
Risk
An uncertain event that might affect the project.
Issue log
The list of issues with owner, date and status.
Workaround
A temporary way to manage while the real fix is on its way.
Root cause
The underlying reason the issue happened.
Ageing
How long an issue has been open.

4Light, standard, heavy how much of this do you need?

Light

a small team

  • A “blocked” column on the board.
  • An owner on each card.
  • Cleared within days.

Standard

Rotterdam

  • One log: issue, affects, owner, due, status.
  • Read every Thursday.
  • Workarounds written down and tested.

Heavy

large programmes

  • Severity levels and response times.
  • Root-cause analysis for serious issues.
  • Trend reporting to the board.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Issues live in chat

    Ten days pass. Fix → log it the day it's found.

  • Owned by “the team”

    Nobody. Fix → one name.

  • Waiting for the real fix

    And missing the date. Fix → agree and test a workaround.

  • Mixing risks and issues

    Might-happens hide the have-happeneds. Fix → two lists; a risk that happens moves across.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • From the first week of execution.
  • The moment something is found that blocks a must-have.
  • When a problem has been in chat for a day.
  • At every weekly meeting.

Skip it when…

  • Tiny problems fixed within the hour.

8Self-check quiz

Risk or issue: “the cold room might arrive late”?

A risk. It hasn't happened.

Risk or issue: “no internet line was ordered”?

An issue. It has happened.

What does every issue need?

What it affects, one owner, a date, a status, and a workaround if the fix is late.

On 22 April the 4G router drops out for ten minutes in rehearsal. What does Sanne do?

Log it as a new issue the same day, owner Eli, due before go / no-go: find out why and whether the backup SIM took over.

Why add “store IT” to the launch map after closing I-07?

The issue's cause was a gap between workstreams. Fixing the cause stops it happening in Utrecht.

try it

Look through this week's project chat. Find one problem that was discussed but has no owner or date. Put it on a list with both. That list is your issue log.

remember!!

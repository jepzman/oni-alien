May We Take the Next Step?

TL;DR

A phase gate is a planned point where someone with authority asks: is the last step truly finished, and are we ready for the next? The criteria are written before the gate, so the answer is a check, not a negotiation. There are three answers: go, go with conditions (named, dated, owned) or no-go. A gate closes one phase and starts the next, so it is initiating and closing in one meeting.

  • Family: starting
  • Lives in: the plan (the dates) and a one-page checklist per gate
  • Pairs with: the launch checklist, milestones, one-way doors

1The situation Larder, 2 March → 3 April 2026

Tomás tells Sanne the other half of the Zuid story. In November 2024 stocking began in a store where the cold room wasn't finished. Pickers worked around builders for two weeks; then every shelf was emptied so the floor could be sealed. Nobody decided that. It simply started, because the date said so. Rotterdam has five dates where the same thing could happen.

  • A milestone date says when; it doesn't say “ready”.
  • Under pressure, “nearly done” is treated as done.
  • Problems are carried forward and get more expensive.

  • Launch Checklist (Grace, vol. 3): Grace's launch checklist (from 30 March) is the product side of Gate 4: eight areas with owners, two reds on 24 April.

2Real-life analogy the canal lock

In a canal lock a boat moves up one level at a time. The next gate opens only when the gate behind is shut and the water is level. The lock keeper checks, then opens. Nobody argues with the keeper, because the rule was there before the boat arrived. Rushing the gate floods the lock: minutes saved, days lost.

Translating it to the job
In real lifeOn the project
Each water levelA phase: fit-out, stocking, rehearsal, opening, hypercare

3How it solves it criteria first, then three possible answers

On 2 March Sanne turns the five shared milestones into five gates and, with each workstream lead, writes four to eight criteria per gate. Each gate gets a decider. Then the first test, Gate 1 on 3 April: may stocking start? Sanne, Joost, Tomás and the builder walk the store with the list. Five of six criteria are met. There are 14 snags; two block stocking (a cold-room door seal, a missing fire-safety sign). Decision: go with conditions. Dry goods are stocked from Monday; chilled waits until both are fixed, by 7 April. The builder's handover is signed with the snag list attached.

  1. Pick the points where a mistake would be expensive to carry forward

    Usually the shared milestones.

  2. Write the criteria now, with the people who do the work

    Checkable, four to eight.

  3. Name who decides at each gate

    The level that owns the risk.

  4. At the gate, check the list on the spot

    Evidence, not opinions.

  5. Choose: go, go with conditions, no-go

    Conditions get an owner and a date.

  6. Record it, and start the next phase on purpose

    A line in the file.

Vocabulary for the exam

Phase
A stretch of the project that ends with something agreed.
Phase gate
A review at the end of a phase to decide whether to continue.
Exit criteria
What must be true to leave a phase.
Entry criteria
What must be true to start the next one.
Go / no-go
A gate before something hard to undo, such as an opening.
Snag list
Small defects found at handover, each with an owner and a date.

4Light, standard, heavy how much of this do you need?

Light

a small change

  • One question before release: “what must be true?”
  • The owner says go.
  • A line in chat.

Standard

Rotterdam

  • Five gates on the milestones.
  • Four to eight written criteria each.
  • Go, go with conditions, or no-go, recorded.

Heavy

large investments, safety, regulators

  • Formal stage gates with a board.
  • Independent review of evidence.
  • Funding released gate by gate.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Criteria written at the gate

    They bend to fit the day. Fix → write them weeks before.

  • Only “go” is possible

    A gate that can't say no is a ceremony. Fix → three answers, and a plan for no-go.

  • Conditions without owners

    “We'll sort it.” Fix → a name and a date for each.

  • A gate on everything

    Progress stops. Fix → only where carrying a mistake forward is expensive.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Before anything hard to undo.
  • Where one team hands over to another.
  • At the shared milestones of a hybrid.
  • Before releasing more money.

Skip it when…

  • Small, reversible steps: let them flow.

8Self-check quiz

When are gate criteria written?

Before the gate, with the people who do the work.

What are the three possible answers?

Go, go with conditions, no-go.

Which two focus areas meet at a gate?

Closing (the phase that ends) and initiating (the phase that starts).

At Gate 2 the stock is on the shelves but 180 products fail the nightly price check. Go?

Not a plain go. Either go with a condition (fixed and re-checked before G4, owner Eli) or no-go for the rehearsal, depending on whether the rehearsal can run without them.

Why not a gate after every task?

Gates cost time. Use them where a mistake carried forward is expensive.

try it

Find the next point in your project where “nearly done” could slip through. Write five criteria for it today, and the name of the person who will say go, go with conditions, or no-go.

remember!!

The gate opens when the water is level, not when the clock says so.

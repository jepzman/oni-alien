Telling a Business Client

TL;DR

When an incident hits a business client, they need a short report they can pass to their own managers: what they experienced, why (one honest sentence, no codes), what you did on the day, and what changes, with dates. Write it for their reader, not your engineers. Let the account owner send it, and keep the same format every time so trust builds with each report.

1The situation Larder, Tuesday 14 July 2026

During the Korenwolf pilot, the weekly pantry restock for the Amsterdam office is due between 07:00 and 09:00. On Tuesday 14 July it arrives at 09:35. Hanna asks Mo what happened. Mo forwards Eli's internal note: “MDM update at 06:00 bricked 9 HHs at Zuid, KE pending, PRB-005.” Hanna replies: “I don't know what any of this means. Will it happen again?” And she has to explain it to her own board.

  • Internal notes are written for engineers.
  • A business client needs something they can forward.
  • The pilot ends in July; the contract decision comes in September.

2Real-life analogy the airline's letter after a cancelled flight

A good airline letter after a cancelled flight has four parts: what you went through (“your flight on Tuesday was cancelled and you arrived six hours late”), why, in one honest sentence, what we did for you, and what we're changing. It has no internal codes and doesn't hide behind “technical issues”. You might still be annoyed, but you know they understood your day.

3How it solves it four parts, their words, dated changes, sent by the account owner

Eli and Mo write the report together on 16 July: Eli writes the middle, Mo the beginning and end. It's one page in four parts:

  1. What you experienced: “Your restock arrived at 09:35 instead of by 09:00: 35 minutes late.”
  2. Why: “An automatic app update on our store devices at 06:00 failed on 9 of 31 handhelds, so picking started late.” No codes, and no blaming the vendor.
  3. What we did on the day: “Our driver called your reception at 08:40. We should have called you earlier.”
  4. What changes, with dates: app updates on store devices move to 23:30, outside store hours, from 20 July; your order is picked first at 06:00; and if a delivery will be late, Mo calls you before 08:00.

Mo adds a one-line apology and sends it. Hanna replies: “Thank you, that's clear.” The template goes into ops/client-reports/. Every incident that affects a business client gets one within two working days, sent by the account manager. In September Ruben asks for it in the contract.

  1. Start with what they experienced

    Their time, their staff, their day.

  2. Explain why in one honest sentence

    No codes, no vague “technical issues”.

  3. Say what you did on the day

    Including what you should have done better.

  4. List changes with dates

    Things they can check.

  5. Let the account owner send it

    The relationship is theirs.

  6. Use the same format every time

    Trust builds with consistency.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Forwarding the internal note

    Codes, blame and abbreviations. Fix → write for their manager.

  • Hiding behind “technical issues”

    Reads as evasive. Fix → one honest sentence of why.

  • Promises without dates

    “We'll improve” can't be checked. Fix → dated, checkable changes.

  • Engineering sends it

    The relationship is skipped. Fix → the account manager sends it.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • An incident affects a business client.
  • A client asks “will it happen again?”
  • During a pilot or before a renewal.
  • When the contract asks for reports.

Skip it when…

  • Incidents that didn't touch the client: say nothing unless asked (and then be honest).

8Self-check quiz

What are the four parts?

What you experienced, why, what we did on the day, what changes (with dates).

Who sends it, and why?

The account manager (Mo): the relationship is theirs, and the client knows them.

Why one honest sentence of why?

It shows you understood the cause without burying the client in detail or codes.

Why dated changes?

The client can check them; “we'll improve” can't be checked.

How fast does Larder send one?

Within two working days.

what would you do?A delivery to Korenwolf's Utrecht office is late because the van broke down, not because of IT. Does this format still apply?

Yes: the format is about the client's experience, not the cause. Mo sends it with Joost's operations team writing the middle instead of Eli.

try it

Take your last incident that touched a customer and write the four-part report as if to their manager: what they experienced, why in one sentence, what you did, what changes with dates. Would they understand it without asking a follow-up question?

Why Do Action Items Never Close?

TL;DR

A review is only as good as the actions that get done. Keep them few (three per review), each with one owner, a date, a size and a test for done. Look at all open actions in a short weekly slot, and allow actions to be dropped on purpose, with a reason, rather than quietly forgotten. Vague actions (“improve monitoring”) are wishes; rewrite them or drop them.

1The situation Larder, 5 October 2026

Bram, Larder's second platform engineer, starts on 5 October. On his first day he asks what's open from past incidents. Eli counts: nine reviews since June have produced 37 actions, and 11 are done. Twelve have no owner, nine have no date, and four just say “improve monitoring”. The oldest open action is from June, and nobody remembers why it mattered.

  • Each review felt productive; the follow-through wasn't.
  • Vague actions can't be started, so they never finish.
  • The next incident finds the same gaps.

2Real-life analogy the fridge list after a burst pipe

After a burst pipe, a household writes a list on the fridge: “be more careful with pipes”, “look into insurance”, “fix things”. Nothing happens. Version two says “Sam: lag the loft pipe, Saturday” and “Kim: call the insurer about cover, Monday”. Both get done, because each has a name, a day, and something you can check.

3How it solves it few, owned, dated, sized, testable, and looked at weekly

On 6 October Eli sets four rules:

  • At most three actions per review (as in the review format).
  • Each action has one owner, a date, a size (an hour, a day or a week) and a done test you can check: “alert fires in a test”, not “monitoring improved”.
  • Tuesdays, 15 minutes, Eli and Bram go through every open action: done, on track, late with a new date, or dropped on purpose with a reason written down.
  • An action that misses its date twice comes to tech leads, either to get time or to be dropped.

The first cleanup is the same day. Of 26 open actions, 9 are dropped honestly: overtaken by events, or not worth it. 17 are rewritten with owners, dates and done tests. By 30 November, 15 of the 17 are done.

  1. Cap actions per review

    Three, the ones that matter most.

  2. Give each one owner and a date

    A person, not a team.

  3. Size it

    An hour, a day, a week: if bigger, it's a project.

  4. Write a done test

    Something anyone can check.

  5. Review all open actions weekly

    15 minutes: done, on track, late, dropped.

  6. Drop on purpose, with a reason

    Better than a list nobody believes.

Vocabulary for the exam

A change agreed in a review to prevent or reduce a repeat.
Owner
One person who makes sure it's done (not necessarily alone).
Done test
A check that shows the action is finished.
Dropping
Deciding on purpose not to do an action, with a reason.
Follow-through rate
Actions done ÷ actions agreed.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Vague actions

    “Improve monitoring” can't be started. Fix → rewrite with a done test, or drop it.

  • Team as owner

    Everyone owns it, so nobody does. Fix → one person.

  • Too many per review

    Everything is important, so nothing is. Fix → three at most.

  • Never dropping

    The list grows until nobody trusts it. Fix → drop on purpose, with a reason.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Reviews happen but the same gaps return.
  • Nobody can say what's open from past incidents.
  • Action lists are long and old.
  • A new person asks “what's still open?”

Skip it when…

  • Never, if you hold reviews.

8Self-check quiz

What does every action need?

One owner, a date, a size and a done test.

Why cap actions at three per review?

Few actions get done; long lists get ignored.

What happens in the Tuesday slot?

Every open action is marked done, on track, late with a new date, or dropped with a reason.

Why allow dropping?

A list with honest drops is believed; a list of forgotten items isn't.

What happened to “improve monitoring”?

Dropped, and replaced by specific actions with done tests.

what would you do?An action has missed its date twice and the owner says “I just haven't had time”. What happens?

It goes to tech leads: either it gets time in someone's plan, or it's dropped on purpose with a reason. It doesn't get a third quiet new date.

try it

Collect every open action from your last five reviews or retrospectives. For each: owner, date, size, done test. Drop anything that can't get all four, and write why. How many are left?

remember!!

A review is as good as its finished actions. Three per review, one owner each, a date, and drop on purpose rather than forget.

The Vendor Is Down at Peak

TL;DR

Every vendor on a critical path is part of your service, including its support hours, its emergency line (or lack of one) and its status page. Find these out on a quiet day and write them down. More important, decide what you will do on your side when the vendor is down, because their fix is out of your hands. Watch the vendor yourself instead of trusting their status page.

1The situation Larder, Friday 21 August 2026

At 17:30 on a Friday, StockPilot's API stops answering. For the first 40 minutes its status page says “all systems operational”. Its support works 09:00–17:00 on weekdays, and Cho's email gets an automatic reply. Larder's app keeps showing stock from 17:30, customers order items that have run out, and they turn into substitutions and refunds. It lasts 2 hours 10 minutes, across the Friday peak, and there is nothing on Larder's side to do except wait.

  • Nobody knew StockPilot's support hours until that evening.
  • The vendor's status page was later than our own alert.
  • There was no plan for “the vendor is down”, only for “our sync is stuck”.

2Real-life analogy the bakery's flour mill

A bakery buys its flour from one mill. The baker keeps the mill's emergency number on the wall, knows the mill closes at five, keeps two days of flour in the back, and knows a second mill she could phone if she had to. She worked all this out on a quiet Tuesday, not during the Saturday rush.

3How it solves it a sheet per vendor, our own plan B, and our own eyes

In the week after, Eli writes ops/vendors.md. For every vendor on a critical or important path it lists what the vendor does for us, its support hours, whether there's an emergency line, its status page, the contract owner at Larder, and what we do when it's down.

  • StockPilot: business hours only, no emergency line. Premium support would cost more than it's worth, because the contract ends in June 2027 (ADR 0016's exit plan). So plan B is on Larder's side: if the sync is stale for more than 15 minutes, a switch marks low-stock items as unavailable, so customers can't order what may have run out. Ben builds it in September.
  • PSP A: a 24/7 emergency line, now written down, and the backup route.
  • Address lookup: email support only; plan B is to allow manual address entry.

Larder's own alert on the sync caught the outage 40 minutes before StockPilot's status page did. So the rule is: trust your own monitoring, and use the vendor's status page as confirmation.

  1. List vendors on critical paths

    From the dependency maps.

  2. Find support hours and emergency lines

    From the contract, on a quiet day.

  3. Name a contract owner per vendor

    Who can push the vendor, and who pays.

  4. Decide plan B on your side

    Degrade, switch, or tell customers.

  5. Watch the vendor yourself

    Your alert is usually earlier than their status page.

  6. Review vendors after their incidents

    Is the contract still right?

Vocabulary for the exam

Vendor (supplier)
A company whose service is part of yours.
Support hours
When the vendor will answer; often not when you need them.
Underpinning contract
The contract that makes a vendor's part of your service possible.
Graceful degradation
Doing less, safely, when a part is missing.
Status page
The vendor's own report of its incidents, often late.
Exit plan
How you would leave the vendor; it affects how much to invest in them.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Trusting their status page

    It's often the last to know. Fix → monitor the vendor yourself.

  • Plan B is “call the vendor”

    At 17:30 on a Friday nobody answers. Fix → decide what you do on your side.

  • Buying premium support by reflex

    Paying for a vendor you're leaving. Fix → match it to the contract's future.

  • No contract owner

    Nobody can push the vendor or check the terms. Fix → one owner per vendor.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • A vendor sits on a critical path.
  • A vendor's outage caught you out.
  • Before a peak (Christmas, King's Day).
  • When choosing or renewing a vendor.

Skip it when…

  • Vendors nobody would notice for a day.

8Self-check quiz

What should a vendor sheet contain?

What it does for us, support hours, emergency line, status page, contract owner, and what we do when it's down.

What was StockPilot's plan B?

A switch on Larder's side: if the sync is stale over 15 minutes, low-stock items become unavailable.

Why not buy StockPilot's premium support?

The contract ends in June 2027; plan B on Larder's side is cheaper and works out of hours.

Who found the outage first?

Larder's own alert, 40 minutes before StockPilot's status page.

What's graceful degradation?

Doing less, safely, when a part is missing, instead of failing or misleading customers.

what would you do?The PSP's status page says “operational” but your checkout errors at the payment step are at 8 %. What do you do?

Trust your own signal: declare SEV2, call the PSP's 24/7 line, switch to the backup route if errors continue, and tell support to suggest cards.

try it

Pick the vendor your most important service depends on most. Find its support hours, emergency line and status page in the contract. Then write what you would do on your side if it were down for two hours on your busiest evening.

remember!!

The vendor's hours are a fact; your plan B is a choice. Make it on a quiet day, and watch them with your own eyes.

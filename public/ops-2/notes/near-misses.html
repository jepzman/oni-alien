What About the Near Misses?

TL;DR

A near miss is an incident that almost happened: caught by luck, by a person, or by a safety net. It carries the same lesson as the incident would have, at no cost. Make it easy to log (two minutes, anyone), review near misses with the incidents, and turn the patterns into actions. Log the good saves too: they show which safety nets work.

  • Family: the hard ones
  • Lives in: #near-miss channel, reviewed monthly with the incidents
  • Pairs with: post-incident reviews, problem management, incident metrics

1The situation Larder, December 2026

Three things almost go wrong in December. On the 3rd the database's log disk reaches 91 %, and Bram clears it at 14:00. On the 10th a deploy is rolled back by its canary before customers notice. On the 21st Eli spots that the courier app's new API certificate, set up by Dee's team in November outside the automatic renewal, will expire in three days. Each one is handled quietly and told to nobody. The certificate would have stopped every courier on Christmas Eve.

  • Near misses look like non-events, so they're forgotten.
  • The same gap (a certificate outside the renewal) can be anywhere else too.
  • Good saves, like the canary, are never credited, so nobody knows the safety net works.

2Real-life analogy the factory's near-miss board

A factory keeps a near-miss board by the canteen. A worker who almost slipped writes a card: “wet floor by press 4, nearly fell”. The safety officer reads the cards weekly and fixes the leaking pipe before somebody breaks an arm. Safety people say that for every serious accident there are many near misses. Each one is a free lesson, if someone writes it down.

3How it solves it two minutes to log, reviewed with incidents, patterns become actions

Eli opens #near-miss on 1 December. Anyone can add a line in two minutes: what almost happened, how it was caught, what it would have done, and whether it looks like a pattern. Nobody is ever asked “why didn't you prevent it?”. The question is what caught it, and what would catch it next time.

December has nine entries. At the monthly review with the incidents, two become actions. The courier certificate goes onto automatic renewal, and every new certificate must register in the expiry check: that's the pattern, a new service set up outside the normal path. The log disk gets an 80 % ticket alert. The canary entry is kept as a good save, because it's evidence that the canary works and is worth keeping. Near misses also go on Femke's monthly page, as a line: “9 near misses, 2 actions”.

  1. Open an easy place to log

    A channel, two minutes, anyone.

  2. Ask four things only

    What, how caught, what it would have done, pattern?

  3. Never blame the person who logs it

    Or the log stays empty.

  4. Review with the incidents monthly

    Same people, same patterns.

  5. Turn patterns into actions

    With the same rules as review actions.

  6. Log the good saves too

    They show which safety nets work.

Vocabulary for the exam

Near miss
An event that could have caused harm but didn't.
Good save
A near miss caught by a safety net (a canary, an alert, a person).
Safety net
Something that catches a mistake before it reaches customers.
Safety-II
Learning from what goes right, not only from failures.
Latent condition
A hidden weakness waiting for the right moment (a cert outside renewal).
Leading indicator
A sign of risk before an incident (near misses, rising disk use).

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Blaming the logger

    “Why was the disk at 91 % at all?” Fix → ask what caught it and what would catch it next time.

  • A form, not a line

    Nobody logs anything. Fix → four questions in a chat line.

  • Logged, never reviewed

    A graveyard of near misses. Fix → review with the incidents monthly.

  • Only failures

    Safety nets that work are never credited. Fix → log good saves too.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Things almost went wrong and nobody heard.
  • Before a peak, when the same gap could hit.
  • Incidents are rare, so there's little to learn from.
  • You want leading indicators, not only lagging ones.

Skip it when…

  • Truly trivial slips with no possible customer harm.

8Self-check quiz

What is a near miss?

An event that could have caused harm but didn't: caught by luck, a person or a safety net.

What are the four questions?

What almost happened, how it was caught, what it would have done, and is there a pattern?

Why log good saves?

They show which safety nets work, and that they're worth keeping.

What pattern did the certificate near miss show?

New services set up outside the normal path miss the safety nets; every new cert must register in the expiry check.

Why must logging never lead to blame?

People stop logging, and the free lessons disappear.

what would you do?A colleague says “I nearly ran the migration on production instead of staging, but I noticed in time. Not worth writing down.” What do you say?

It is worth it: two minutes in #near-miss. What caught it (they noticed), what would have happened, and is there a pattern (are the two environments too easy to confuse?). That's a cheap fix before someone doesn't notice.

try it

Ask your team: “What almost went wrong last month?” Write down each answer with the four questions. Which one shows a gap that could be anywhere else too?

remember!!

A near miss is a free lesson. Make it two minutes to log, never blame the logger, and fix the pipe before someone falls.

Why Did It Really Happen?

TL;DR

An incident is fixed when the service works again; a problem is the cause behind one or more incidents, and it's fixed only when that cause is gone. Look for patterns across incidents (the same service, the same hour, the same workaround), open a problem, analyse the cause properly, keep a workaround until the fix lands, then check the incidents stop. It's how you stop restarting the same thing three times a week.

1The situation Larder, early September 2026

Since spring the stock sync has been restarted about three times a week. It's been in Eli's toil log since June. Between June and early September it shows up in 13 of 41 incidents and tickets, and every one was closed as “restarted, works again”. Every incident was closed; nobody ever opened the problem. The 21 August review asked “why does it get stuck at all?” and nobody could answer.

  • Each restart is quick, so the pattern hides.
  • Incident work rewards getting things running again, not getting to the cause.
  • With every new store the sync does more work and gets stuck more often.

2Real-life analogy the leaking roof

Every time it rains, the drip comes back, and someone puts a bucket under it and mops the floor: today's rain is handled. Fixing the roof is a different job. You go up on a dry day, find the cracked tile, and replace it. Until then you keep the bucket handy, and after it you check that the next rain stays outside.

3How it solves it find the pattern, find the cause, keep the workaround, check it's gone

Eli opens PRB-003 on 7 September and works on it with Ben.

  • Pattern: the failures cluster between 17:00 and 19:00, and right after the sync has been down for a few minutes.
  • Causal analysis: StockPilot allows 600 requests a minute. When a call failed, Larder Core retried at once, with every store's batches retrying together. The burst went over the limit, StockPilot answered “429 too many requests”, and Core retried again: a retry storm. A restart only cleared the queue until the next blip.
  • Workaround until the fix, written as known error KE-03 (next note): restart, but only after checking the vendor is up (RB-09).
  • Fix, shipped on 14 September: retries with exponential backoff and jitter, and changes fetched in one batch per store.
  • Check: no restarts at all in October. PRB-003 is closed on 2 November, and the toil log loses its sync column.

Eli now looks through the month's incidents for patterns on the first Monday of each month. Two more problems are opened that way before Christmas.

  1. Look for patterns monthly

    Same service, same hour, same workaround.

  2. Open a problem with an owner

    Separate from any one incident.

  3. Analyse the cause properly

    Timelines, logs, “why” more than once, test the theory.

  4. Write the workaround as a known error

    So incidents are handled well meanwhile.

  5. Fix the cause and check it

    Did the incidents stop?

  6. Close it with evidence

    Numbers before and after.

Vocabulary for the exam

Problem
The cause, or possible cause, of one or more incidents.
Incident vs problem
Incidents restore service; problems remove causes.
Workaround
A way to reduce the effect of a problem until it's fixed.
Causal analysis
Working out why something happened, usually several causes, not one.
Retry storm
Many clients retrying at once, making a struggling service worse.
Proactive problem management
Finding problems from patterns before they cause a big incident.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Closing the problem with the incident

    The workaround becomes the fix. Fix → a problem stays open until the cause is gone.

  • Stopping at the first “why”

    “The sync crashed” isn't a cause. Fix → ask again, and test the theory.

  • Automating the workaround

    A script restarts it hourly; the cause stays. Fix → fix the cause, keep the workaround only meanwhile.

  • No check afterwards

    It's “fixed”, and still happens. Fix → compare incidents before and after.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • The same workaround keeps closing incidents.
  • A review can't answer “why does this keep happening?”
  • Toil comes from a fault, not from the job.
  • Monthly, as a pattern hunt.

Skip it when…

  • A true one-off with a clear, already-fixed cause.

8Self-check quiz

What's the difference between an incident and a problem?

An incident is fixed when service works again; a problem is the cause behind incidents, fixed when the cause is gone.

What was PRB-003's cause?

A retry storm: Core retried failed calls at once, from all stores, pushing past StockPilot's 600/min limit.

What's a workaround for, if the problem is still open?

To handle incidents well while the fix is built; it's written as a known error.

How did Eli know it was fixed?

Restarts went from about three a week to zero in October.

What is proactive problem management?

Looking for patterns across incidents before a big one forces you to.

what would you do?A colleague suggests a cron job that restarts the stock sync every hour “so it never gets stuck”. What do you say?

That's automating the workaround and hiding the cause. Open (or reopen) the problem, find why it gets stuck, and fix that.

try it

List last quarter's incidents and group them by service and by how they were fixed. Which fix appears three times or more? Open a problem for it, with an owner, a workaround and a date to look at the cause.

remember!!

The bucket handles today's rain. Open the problem, find the cracked tile, and check the next rain stays out.

When Things Break

Volume 2 of The IT Operations Notebook: incident and problem management, from the ITIL 4 practices and the SRE habit of blameless learning. 15 notes in three collections: responding together, learning afterwards, and the hard cases. Each note is a question someone running IT must answer; incident notes open with the pager log, the alert as it arrived. Every note follows Eli at Larder from June to December 2026.

the story so far

June 2026. Larder now has a catalogue, a shared on-call rota, fourteen alerts that mean something and eight runbooks. Rotterdam is open and the stores report problems in one place. What Larder still doesn't have is a way to handle an incident together: who leads, who tells the stores, when it is big, and what happens afterwards. This volume follows Eli from June to December 2026: a Friday payment failure nobody could size, a vendor that only answers in office hours, the network rule change of 29 October, and the Christmas failover of 18 December.

Responding size it, lead it, tell people, and get the right person

  1. 01How Bad Is It?Severity levels defined by customer harm, agreed in advance, so nobody argues about size during the incident.p. 01
  2. 02Who Is in Charge?One incident lead, one fixer group, one person on communication and one scribe, named in the first five minutes.p. 02
  3. 03Who Needs to Know?Short, regular updates in fixed places for support, stores, leaders and customers, including a status page.p. 03
  4. 04Who's Next If Nobody Answers?Escalation policies: who is paged next, after how long, and how to pull in a specialist.p. 04
  5. 05The Vendor Is Down at PeakKnow each vendor's support hours, contacts and status page before you need them, and plan for their outage.p. 05
  6. 06Who Fixes What?Support tiers for the common, swarming for the new: when to hand a problem on and when to pull people in.p. 06

Learning review without blame, find the cause, and finish the actions

  1. 07What Can We Learn from It?A blameless review: the timeline, what made sense at the time, what helped, and a few actions.p. 07
  2. 08Why Did It Really Happen?Problem management: look for causes and patterns across incidents, and fix the thing that keeps breaking.p. 08
  3. 09We Know the Bug, Now What?Known errors: write down the workaround where the desk can find it, until the real fix lands.p. 09
  4. 10Why Do Action Items Never Close?Few, small, owned and dated actions, reviewed weekly until they're done or honestly dropped.p. 10
  5. 11Which Incident Numbers Mean Anything?Count customer harm and trends by severity; treat averages like MTTR with care.p. 11

The hard ones changes, clients, major incidents and the ones that nearly happened

  1. 12Was It a Change?Ask “what changed?” first, and make recent changes visible to whoever is on call.p. 12
  2. 13Telling a Business ClientAn incident report a business client can use: what they felt, why, what we did, what changes.p. 13
  3. 14When Is It a Major Incident?A separate, practised procedure for the rare incident that hurts the whole business.p. 14
  4. 15What About the Near Misses?Collect the things that almost broke, because they are free lessons.p. 15

★The nine operations ratings what a good operations practice is judged on

Every note scores the pattern from 1 (poor) to 5 (great) on the same nine things, comparing an app without the pattern to the same app with it.

User impact
Fewer customers, pickers and couriers hurt when something breaks.
Reliability
Things fail less, and fail smaller.
Recovery speed
Time from “it's broken” to “it works again”.
Change safety
Changes rarely cause incidents, and can be undone.
Visibility
Anyone on call can see what runs, what's wrong and what changed.
Toil reduction
Repetitive manual work goes down instead of up.
Cost awareness
Money and people's time are spent where they matter.
Sustainable on-call
The load is shared and bearable; nobody is the only one who knows.
Low overhead
Time and ceremony the practice takes. High = light.

first law

An incident is over when the service works again; it is finished when the thing that caused it can't do it again. Restore first, learn second, blame never.

What Does It Leave Behind?

TL;DR

A project ends; what it builds runs for years. Sustainability in project work means weighing a choice's long effects (on the climate, on resources, on the people around it, on running costs) while the choice is still cheap to change: on the drawing, in the specification, in the contract. Put a line for it on the tailoring sheet, price the options over the life of the result, not just the build, and let the sponsor decide with both numbers.

1The situation Larder, Monday 6 March 2028

Korenwolf's annual supplier questionnaire arrives. Ana answers for the platform: about 9 tonnes of CO₂-equivalent a year in the cloud, 0.6 g an order. The other questions are about the stores: refrigerants, energy, waste, materials. That same week, Ghent's cooling plant is being specified with a synthetic refrigerant that, if it leaks, warms the climate about 1,400 times as much as the same weight of CO₂. The cheapest moment to change that choice is this week; after the plant is ordered it's ten years.

  • Launches were judged on the build, not on what they leave running.
  • Nobody had priced options over the store's life.
  • A customer asked a question the project had no answer to.
meanwhile, in Ana’s & Eli’s notebook
  • Green Software (Ana, vol. 5): Ana's green software: about 9 t CO₂e a year for the platform, 0.9 → 0.6 g an order, answered for Korenwolf's supplier questionnaire.
  • Prove It to the Auditor (Eli, vol. 5): Eli's part of the same questionnaire: 186 questions on changes, restores, access, patching and incidents, answered from evidence in six working days.

2Real-life analogy choosing on the drawing

A family building a house can choose the heating, insulation and windows on the drawing for the price of an eraser. During the build each change costs money; after they've moved in, it costs a lot. The builder finishes in a year. The family lives with the choices for thirty.

3How it solves it decide on the drawing; price over the life; the sponsor decides

Between 6 and 13 March Sanne and Nadia go through Ghent's choices while they're still on paper. Cooling: a CO₂ system (a natural refrigerant) instead of the synthetic one costs €7k more; with heat recovery warming the store it saves about €1.8k a year in energy, so it pays back in about four years and removes the leak risk. Femke approves it from the programme's money. Shelving: reused from Zuid's refit, about €5k saved (Antwerp's opportunity O-01, again). E-bike batteries: returned to the lease supplier for recycling, written into the contract. Food: unsold food to a Ghent food bank from week one, as in every city since Rotterdam. Neighbours: met before the permit is lodged, Antwerp's lesson. On 13 March Korenwolf gets an answer with platform and store numbers. Playbook v4.2 adds one line to every tailoring sheet: what does this launch leave running, and what does it cost over ten years?

  1. Find the choices that last

    Plant, materials, contracts.

  2. Make them while still on paper

    The cheapest moment.

  3. Price options over the life

    Build cost and running cost.

  4. Include people as well as carbon

    Neighbours, jobs, food waste.

  5. Let the sponsor decide with both numbers

    And record why.

  6. Answer the questions customers ask

    From the project's records.

Vocabulary for the exam

Sustainability
Meeting today's needs without harming the ability to meet tomorrow's: environment, society, money.
Life-cycle cost
Build cost plus running, maintenance and disposal over the life of a result.
CO₂-equivalent (CO₂e)
Greenhouse effect expressed as the weight of CO₂ that would do the same.
Global warming potential
How much a gas warms the climate compared with CO₂.
Natural refrigerant
A cooling gas such as CO₂ or ammonia with little or no warming effect.
Supplier questionnaire
A customer's questions about a supplier's environmental and social practices.

4Light, standard, heavy how much of this do you need?

Light

small work

  • Ask what it leaves behind.
  • Pick the better option if it costs little.
  • Note why.

Standard

a city launch

  • A line on the tailoring sheet.
  • Options priced over ten years.
  • The sponsor decides with both numbers.

Heavy

large construction

  • Life-cycle assessments.
  • Certifications and reporting.
  • Sustainability targets in contracts.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Too late

    Changes cost ten times more. Fix → decide on the drawing.

  • Build cost only

    Cheap now, dear for years. Fix → price over the life.

  • Carbon only

    People forgotten. Fix → include neighbours, waste, jobs.

  • Claims without numbers

    Customers stop believing. Fix → answer from records.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • When specifying anything that lasts.
  • When signing long contracts.
  • When customers ask.
  • On every tailoring sheet.

Skip it when…

  • Never; on small work it's one question.

8Self-check quiz

Why decide in March?

The cooling plant was still on paper; after ordering, the choice lasts ten years.

What did the CO₂ system cost and save?

+€7k; about €1.8k a year less energy; payback about four years.

What's the v4.2 tailoring line?

What does this launch leave running, and what does it cost over ten years?

A builder offers a cheaper floor that lasts five years instead of fifteen. What do you price?

Both over fifteen years, including the replacement and disruption; let the sponsor decide with both numbers.

Is sustainability only about carbon?

No: people (neighbours, jobs), resources, waste and running costs too.

try it

Find one choice in your project that will outlast it. Price two options over its life, not just to build. Who decides?

remember!!

Choose the heating on the drawing. You live with it for thirty years.

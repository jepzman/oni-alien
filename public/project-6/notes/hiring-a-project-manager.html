a.k.a. acquiring a project manager: the role card and the work-sample interview

Who Do We Hire?

TL;DR

To hire a project manager, decide first what the job is here (a role card with the three things that matter most), then watch candidates do it: a work sample built from a real situation (a status and an issue log, names removed), done in a set time, talked through with a small panel including someone who isn't a project manager. Write the scoring sheet before the first candidate, and give everyone the same task. Interviews test talking about the work; a work sample tests the work.

1The situation Larder, November 2027

Marco has approved one of his four central hires for 2028 for a second project manager, to run launches while Sanne runs the programme. The last time Larder hired one, Sanne herself, it was two conversations and a good feeling. Thirty-four people apply. Every one of them can talk fluently about stakeholders, risk registers and agile. Which of them can run a launch, and tell Femke the truth on a bad Friday?

  • Conversations reward people who talk well about the work.
  • Nobody had said what the job needs most at Larder.
  • Each interviewer would judge on something different.

2Real-life analogy the cook's trial

A restaurant hiring a cook doesn't only ask about recipes. It asks the candidate to cook one dish in its kitchen, on a real evening, and watches: do they taste as they go, ask where things are, handle the order that comes back? Everyone can talk about a risotto. Cooking one under pressure is the job.

3How it solves it role card, work sample, a scoring sheet first, a mixed panel

On Monday 8 November 2027 Sanne writes a role card: run a city launch from the playbook, with three things that matter most: honest status, working with people outside Larder (Antwerp's lesson), and tailoring. Thirty-four applicants; nine screening calls of twenty minutes; four work samples in the week of 6 December. The sample: Antwerp's status and issue log of 15 July 2027 (the day the permit became an issue), names removed; 45 minutes to write Friday's status and the first three actions; 20 minutes talking it through with Sanne, Joost and Yara, the Rotterdam store manager, who isn't a project manager and sees things they don't. All three score on a sheet written before the first candidate. Nadia, six years in a retail chain's store-opening team, writes amber with the issue in line one, and her first action is a question: “who at Larder owns the permit response?” Offer on Friday 17 December; she starts on Monday 7 February 2028 with a first week planned: the playbook, a day at Eindhoven, an afternoon with Maarten.

  1. Write the role card

    The three things that matter most here.

  2. Write the scoring sheet before interviewing

    Same for everyone.

  3. Build a work sample from a real case

    Names removed.

  4. Time it, then talk it through

    What did they see first? What would they ask?

  5. Include a panellist from outside the role

    They see different things.

  6. Plan the first week before they arrive

    Onboarding is part of hiring.

Vocabulary for the exam

Role card
A short description of what the job is for and what matters most.
Work sample
A realistic task from the job, done during selection.
Structured interview
The same questions and scoring for every candidate.
Scoring sheet
Criteria and scales agreed before interviewing.
Onboarding
Bringing a new person into their role: people, tools, first work.
Hiring cap
A limit on new hires; Marco's: four central hires a year.

ROLE        run a city launch from the playbook, under the Cities 2028 programme
MATTERS     1 honest status · 2 people outside Larder · 3 tailoring to the work
FUNNEL      34 applied → 9 screening calls (20 min) → 4 work samples (6-10 Dec) → 1 offer

4Light, standard, heavy how much of this do you need?

Light

a contractor for a few weeks

  • Look at past work.
  • One conversation on a real problem.
  • References.

Standard

a permanent PM

  • Role card, screening, a work sample.
  • Scoring sheet first, mixed panel.
  • First week planned.

Heavy

senior or many hires

  • Assessment centres.
  • Several work samples.
  • Structured references and probation reviews.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Conversation only

    Rewards talkers. Fix → a work sample.

  • Criteria after meeting them

    Bias decides. Fix → scoring sheet first.

  • Only PMs on the panel

    One kind of view. Fix → add someone the PM will serve.

  • No first week

    A slow, lonely start. Fix → plan onboarding before day one.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Hiring a PM or any role with a doable core task.
  • When many candidates sound alike.
  • When fairness matters (always).
  • Before writing a job ad.

Skip it when…

  • Never skip the role card; a sample may be shorter for short contracts.

8Self-check quiz

What were the three things that matter most?

Honest status, people outside Larder, tailoring.

What was the work sample?

Antwerp's status and issue log of 15 July 2027, names removed: write the status and first three actions.

Why was Yara on the panel?

She isn't a PM; she sees how a candidate would work with a store.

A strong talker scores 2 on the sample. Joost wants to hire anyway. What do you say?

The sheet was agreed first; the sample is the job; discuss the gap against the criteria, not the impression.

What did Nadia's first action show?

She looked for the owner of the response, the lesson Antwerp learned late.

try it

For the next hire you help with, write the role card's three things and the scoring sheet before you see any CV. Turn one real past situation into a 45-minute task.

remember!!

Don't ask about the risotto. Ask them to cook one.

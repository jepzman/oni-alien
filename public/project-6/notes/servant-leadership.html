a.k.a. servant leadership in practice: coaching another project manager

Who Serves Whom?

TL;DR

Servant leadership means leading by serving the people doing the work: removing what blocks them, growing their ability, and giving them the credit. With a less experienced colleague, the hardest part is not doing their work for them. Match how much you lead to what they already know (situational leadership: direct, coach, support, delegate), ask questions instead of rewriting, unblock what they can't, and step back where they're able. Rescuing gets a better result today and a weaker colleague tomorrow.

1The situation Larder, Friday 25 February 2028

This is Sanne's mistake for this collection. Nadia's first Ghent status reaches her at 18:00. It's honest and complete, but long, and it puts the wrong thing first: the builder's schedule before the lease that isn't signed yet. At 22:00 Sanne rewrites it and sends her own version to Femke, with a kind note to Nadia. Nadia's next two statuses are thin. Why write it well, if Sanne will fix it anyway? The rewrite got Femke a better page that night, and took the page away from Nadia.

  • Helping by doing the work took the work away.
  • Nadia learned her version didn't count.
  • Sanne was coaching by habit, not by what Nadia needed.

2Real-life analogy teaching a teenager to cook

A parent teaching a teenager to cook watches the onions start to catch. Grabbing the pan saves tonight's dinner and produces a teenager who never cooks. Asking “what's happening to the onions?”, and turning the heat down only if asked, gets a slightly worse dinner tonight and a cook by the summer. The second parent isn't less helpful. They're helping the cook, not the dinner.

3How it solves it match the style, ask, unblock, credit, step back

Sanne apologises on the Monday and changes how she leads. Situational leadership: Nadia ran store openings for a retail chain for six years, so on builders and fit-outs Sanne delegates; on Larder's ways, Belgium and the steering group she coaches. A weekly 1:1, Tuesdays, thirty minutes, on Nadia's agenda. For statuses, three questions instead of a red pen: what's the one thing Femke must know? why that colour? what will you do by Friday, and what do you need from me? Unblocking what Nadia can't do herself yet: an introduction to Maarten, Lena's time booked for the Belgian contracts, Hilde for the permit. Credit in public: when Ghent's permit is granted on 12 May (no objections; deliveries from 07:00 by design), Sanne names Nadia in the steering note. Ghent opens on Monday 19 June. Sanne wrote none of its statuses after 25 February.

  1. Ask what they already know

    And lead accordingly.

  2. Delegate what they can do

    Coach what's new.

  3. Ask questions instead of rewriting

    Three good ones.

  4. Remove the blocks they can't

    Introductions, access, time.

  5. Give credit in public

    By name.

  6. Notice when you rescue

    And give the work back.

Vocabulary for the exam

Servant leadership
Leading by serving the team's needs: removing obstacles, growing people.
Situational leadership
Changing style with a person's readiness: directing, coaching, supporting, delegating.
Coaching
Helping someone find their own answer, mostly through questions.
Rescuing
Doing someone's work for them when they could learn to do it.
1:1
A regular private meeting between a leader and one person.
Delegation
Handing over a piece of work together with the authority to decide it.

4Light, standard, heavy how much of this do you need?

Light

a colleague asks for help once

  • Ask what they think first.
  • Help on the block.
  • Hand it back.

Standard

a new project manager

  • Situational style per area.
  • Weekly 1:1, three questions, unblocking.
  • Credit in public; no rewrites.

Heavy

a development programme

  • Formal mentoring and coaching.
  • Development plans and stretch assignments.
  • Feedback from several people.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Rescuing

    Better today, weaker tomorrow. Fix → ask, then give it back.

  • One style for everything

    Micromanaging experts, abandoning beginners. Fix → match the style to the area.

  • Credit kept

    People stop trying. Fix → name them in public.

  • Blocks left in place

    Coaching without help. Fix → remove what they can't.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • When someone new takes on work you know.
  • When you catch yourself redoing others' work.
  • When handing over.
  • Every week, in a 1:1.

Skip it when…

  • A real emergency: act, then debrief and hand back.

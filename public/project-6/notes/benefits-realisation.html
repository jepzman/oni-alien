Did We Get What We Paid For?

TL;DR

A project delivers outputs; it exists for benefits, which often arrive after it closes. Benefits realisation is checking, on dates agreed at the start, whether each benefit was reached, by its owner (usually not the project manager), and acting when it wasn't. Book the checks before the project closes, or there will be no meeting to hold them in. Compare with the forecast, explain the gap, and feed it into the next decision and the playbook.

1The situation Larder, Monday 17 January 2028

This is Sanne's mistake for this collection. Rotterdam's value chain of 16 January 2026 named three benefits and three checks: at +4 weeks, +12 weeks and the start of Utrecht. The first happened, inside the closing report of 22 May. The +12-week check, due in August 2026, never did. The project had closed; the steering group had stopped meeting; nobody had booked it. Preparing the 2028 programme, Femke asks a simple question: “Did Rotterdam do what we paid for?” Twenty months on, nobody can answer for the on-time benefit without digging.

  • Benefit checks had no meeting once the project closed.
  • The owners were named, but never reminded.
  • A missed benefit could have changed the next launch.

2Real-life analogy the heat pump

A family fits a heat pump to cut its energy bill by a third. The installer finishes on time and on budget. Whether the family got what it paid for is only known a year later, when the bills come in. Families who never compare the bills never find out that the settings were wrong, and recommend the installer to their neighbours anyway.

3How it solves it each benefit, its owner, the result, the reason, the rule

Sanne and the owners do the check on 17 January 2028, from the data. 500 orders a day by the end of May 2026 (Joost): met, 590 in week four. 90 % express on time by the end of the summer (Joost): missed. It was 86 % in September and reached 90 % only in November 2026, after the second courier wave and a fix to zone RTM-2's street numbers (the issue handed over at closing, I-21). A +12-week check in August would have pulled both forward by two months. The next city in eight weeks or less (Femke): met: Utrecht (15 February → 12 April 2027), The Hague and Eindhoven each took eight weeks; Antwerp, abroad, didn't count. Marco adds the money: payback forecast about December 2026, actual January 2027. The rule goes into playbook v4 the same week: benefit checks at +4 and +12 weeks, run by the benefit owner, booked on the steering calendar before the project closes; a missed target goes to the portfolio review with its reason. Antwerp's +12-week check (10 January 2028) and Eindhoven's (14 February) go on the calendar that week; Ghent's are booked before it opens.

  1. Name each benefit with a measure and a date

    At the start.

  2. Give each an owner

    Usually the person who runs the result.

  3. Book the checks before closing

    On a calendar that outlives the project.

  4. Check from data

    Against the target and the forecast.

  5. Explain every gap

    And what would have closed it sooner.

  6. Feed it forward

    Into the playbook and the portfolio.

Vocabulary for the exam

Output
What the project produces: a store, an app setting.
Outcome
The change the output causes: customers ordering.
Benefit
The measurable gain the organisation wanted: 500 orders a day.
Benefit owner
The person accountable for reaching a benefit after the project.
Benefits realisation
Checking and sustaining benefits until they're reached.
Dis-benefit
A negative effect the project causes, also to be tracked.

4Light, standard, heavy how much of this do you need?

Light

small work

  • Say what it's for.
  • Look once afterwards.
  • Tell the sponsor.

Standard

a city launch

  • Benefits with owners, measures and dates.
  • Checks at +4 and +12 weeks, booked before closing.
  • Gaps explained, fed into the playbook.

Heavy

large investments

  • A benefits register and realisation plan.
  • Independent post-implementation review.
  • Benefits tracked in the portfolio for years.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Benefits in the charter only

    Forgotten at closing. Fix → carry them past closing.

  • No meeting after the project

    Checks never happen. Fix → book them before closing.

  • PM as benefit owner

    The PM has moved on. Fix → the person who runs the result owns it.

  • A miss without a reason

    Nothing is learned. Fix → explain it, feed it forward.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • At the start: name benefits, owners, check dates.
  • Before closing: book the checks.
  • After closing: run them.
  • Before funding the next one.

Skip it when…

  • Work with no benefit beyond its output (then question the work).

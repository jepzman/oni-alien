Incident Response & Postmortems

TL;DR

Treat incidents as a practised routine: declare them early with a severity, assign clear roles (incident commander, operations, communications), mitigate first (roll back, fail over) and diagnose after. Then run a blameless postmortem: a timeline, the contributing factors in the system, and action items with owners, shared widely so everyone learns.

  • Family: operations culture
  • Roles: incident commander, ops, comms, scribe
  • Influences: aviation, fire services (ICS), Google SRE
  • Pairs with: SLOs, observability, disaster recovery

1The problem why do we even need this?

Without a routine, incidents become chaos: everyone debugging at once, nobody coordinating or updating customers. And if the review hunts for who to blame, people hide details and the same failure returns.

  • Two engineers make conflicting fixes at the same time.
  • Customers learn about the outage from social media.
  • The “root cause” is “human error”, and nothing changes.

2Real-life analogy the cockpit and the crash investigation

In a cockpit, the captain flies and the first officer handles the radio and checklists: clear roles, especially in an emergency. Afterwards, air-crash investigators ask how the system allowed the error, not who to punish, and publish lessons so every airline gets safer.

  • Captain = incident commander; radio = comms lead.
  • Checklists = runbooks.
  • The investigation = a blameless postmortem.

3How it solves it practised roles, then learning

“Human error” is where an investigation starts, not where it ends: what made the mistake easy, and the damage large?

  1. Define severities

    SEV-1 to SEV-4 with examples and who to page.

  2. Declare early

    Anyone can declare; downgrading is cheap, late declaring is expensive.

  3. Assign roles

    Commander (coordinates, decides), ops (hands on keyboard), comms (updates every 30 min), scribe.

  4. Mitigate before diagnosing

    Roll back, fail over, turn off the flag; find root causes afterwards.

  5. Write a blameless postmortem

    Timeline, impact, contributing factors, what went well, action items with owners and dates.

  6. Close the loop

    Track action items; share postmortems; practise with game days.

Vocabulary for the exam

Incident commander
Coordinates the response and makes decisions.
Severity (SEV)
How bad an incident is, which sets the response.
Mitigation
Stopping the impact, even before fixing the cause.
Blameless postmortem
Review focused on systems, not individuals.
Contributing factors
The conditions that together allowed the incident.
Game day
A planned exercise simulating an incident.

Commander: ana · Ops: ben, cho · Comms: dee · Status: resolved

## Impact
6% of checkouts failed (≈ 1,900 orders retried, 140 lost). Error budget: 38% used.

## Timeline (UTC)
09:02 deploy web@9c1e (new payment client)   09:07 burn-rate page
09:09 SEV-2 declared                          09:14 rolled back to web@4a7b
09:43 error rate normal, monitoring           09:50 resolved

## Contributing factors
- Payment client default timeout changed from 10 s to 2 s
- Canary step was skipped for “small” changes
- No alert on payment-provider latency

## Action items
- [ ] Pin client timeouts in config (ben, 11-19)
- [ ] Canary required for every prod deploy (cho, 11-26)
- [ ] Latency SLI for payment provider (dee, 12-03)

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • Faster mitigation with clear roles.
  • Customers kept informed.
  • Real learning: systemic fixes, not blame.
  • Less fear, so people share what really happened.

What we lose

  • Process overhead for small incidents.
  • Time for postmortems and follow-ups.
  • Action items pile up if not prioritised.
  • Blameless is hard to keep under pressure.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Commander also debugging

    Nobody coordinates; work overlaps. Fix → the commander coordinates; others fix.

  • Diagnosing before mitigating

    An hour of root-causing while users suffer. Fix → roll back or fail over first.

  • “Root cause: human error”

    Nothing changes; it happens again. Fix → ask what made the error possible and costly.

  • Action items never done

    The same postmortem every quarter. Fix → owners, dates and tracking; review them.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Every team that runs production services.
  • Customer-facing outages and security incidents.
  • Near-misses worth learning from.

Skip it when…

  • Use a lighter version for tiny blips; keep the habit, not the paperwork.

8Self-check quiz

What does the incident commander do?

Coordinates the response, assigns work and makes decisions, rather than debugging.

Why mitigate before finding the root cause?

Users are affected now; rollback or failover stops the impact quickly.

What makes a postmortem blameless?

It examines how the system allowed the failure, not who to punish, so people share details honestly.

What turns a postmortem into improvement?

Action items with owners and dates that are tracked to completion.

Cost Visibility (FinOps)

TL;DR

Make cloud cost visible, owned and part of design. Tag every resource with team, service and environment (and enforce it), show cost per team and per unit (€ per 1,000 orders), then optimise: rightsize, switch non-production off at night, commit to discounts for steady load, delete idle things. Budgets and anomaly alerts catch surprises in days, not at month end.

  • Family: cost management
  • Phases: inform → optimise → operate (FinOps Foundation)
  • Tools: cloud cost explorers, Kubecost/OpenCost, Infracost
  • Pairs with: IaC, autoscaling, preview environments

1The problem why do we even need this?

Cloud makes it easy to spend and hard to see. Without tags and owners, the bill is one big number that only finance looks at, and only after the month ends.

  • The bill doubles and nobody knows which service caused it.
  • Forgotten demo GPUs and preview environments run for months.
  • Engineers never see the cost of their design choices.

2Real-life analogy a meter per room

In a shared flat, one big electricity bill leads to arguments. Put a meter in each room and everyone sees their own usage: the person running a space heater all day turns it down, and the flat agrees a monthly budget.

  • Meters per room are tags and per-team dashboards.
  • Turning down the heater is optimisation.
  • The agreed budget is budgets and alerts.

3How it solves it inform, optimise, operate

FinOps is a culture as much as a tool: engineers see the cost of their choices, finance understands the trade-offs, and both decide together.

  1. Tag everything

    team, service, env, cost_center; enforced in IaC and with policies.

  2. Show cost to owners

    Per-team dashboards and weekly reports; Kubecost/OpenCost for clusters.

  3. Track unit cost

    € per 1,000 requests, per order, per tenant: does cost grow with value?

  4. Optimise the big items

    Rightsize, delete idle resources, schedule non-prod off, storage lifecycle rules.

  5. Commit where steady

    Savings plans / reservations for base load; spot for interruptible work.

  6. Alert and review

    Budgets, anomaly detection, and a cost estimate (Infracost) in infrastructure PRs.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • Know who spends what, and why.
  • Surprises caught in days, not at month end.
  • Real savings from rightsizing and scheduling.
  • Cost-aware design decisions.

What we lose

  • Tagging discipline and enforcement effort.
  • Commitments can lock you in.
  • Over-optimising can hurt reliability.
  • Shared costs are hard to split fairly.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Tags as a suggestion

    Half the resources untagged after a month. Fix → enforce tags in IaC and with policies.

  • Cutting redundancy to save money

    Single-AZ database; the next outage costs more. Fix → optimise waste first, not resilience.

  • Big commitments too early

    Three-year reservation for a service you're about to retire. Fix → commit only to steady, long-lived base load.

  • Only finance looks at cost

    Engineers never see it. Fix → per-team dashboards and PR estimates.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Any organisation with a meaningful cloud bill.
  • Multi-team platforms and Kubernetes clusters.
  • SaaS products that need healthy unit economics.

Skip it when…

  • Tiny fixed-price hosting where there's nothing to optimise.

Related patterns

  • Autoscaling
  • Preview environments
  • Infrastructure as code
  • Multitenancy (Vol. 3)

8Self-check quiz

What are the three FinOps phases?

Inform (visibility and allocation), optimise (reduce waste and rates), operate (continuous governance).

Why track unit cost?

To see whether spending grows in line with business value, not just whether the bill goes up.

What should you optimise first?

Waste: idle and oversized resources and non-production running 24/7, not redundancy.

How do tags help?

They let costs be allocated to teams, services and environments, so owners can see and act on them.

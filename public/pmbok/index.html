The PMBOK Map

The PMBOK Guide is the Project Management Institute's description of how projects are run. It is long, careful and hard to read from front to back. This page is the way in: the whole of the eighth edition on one sheet, each part in plain words, and a link to the note in Sanne's notebook that explains it with a real project. Use it to find a topic, or to see how far you've got.

1The shape of the guide 6 · 7 · 5 · 40

The eighth edition has four layers. Start at the top and each layer answers the next question:

  • 6 principles: how should I think? They hold on every project, whatever the method.
  • 7 performance domains: what must I look after? Seven areas of a project that all need attention, all the way through.
  • 5 focus areas: what kind of attention, right now? Starting, planning, doing, checking, finishing.
  • 40 processes: what do I actually do? Each one sits in one domain and one focus area. None is compulsory: you choose and size them for your project, which the guide calls tailoring.

2Six principles what good project management rests on

  1. Adopt a holistic view

    A project is one connected system inside a bigger one. Look at the whole before you fix a part.

    See the Whole vol. 1 · 05

  2. Focus on value

    The point is the benefit the project exists for, not the list of tasks or the documents.

    Focus on Value vol. 1 · 06

  3. Embed quality into processes and deliverables

    Build quality into how the work is done. Don't inspect for it at the end.

    Build Quality In vol. 1 · 07

  4. Be an accountable leader

    Own the outcome, tell the truth about status, and look after the people.

    Own the Outcome vol. 1 · 08

  5. Integrate sustainability within all project areas

    Weigh the long-term effect on people, planet and money in each decision.

    Think Past the Finish vol. 1 · 09

  6. Build an empowered culture

    Give the people doing the work the room, the facts and the trust to decide.

    Let the Team Decide vol. 1 · 10

4Five focus areas what kind of attention the work needs now

  1. Initiating

    Agree that the project (or the next phase) should exist, why, and who is behind it.

  2. Planning

    Work out what, by when, for how much, with whom, and what could stop us.

  3. Executing

    Do the work and lead the people doing it.

  4. Monitoring and controlling

    Compare what is happening with the plan, and decide what to change.

  5. Closing

    Hand over, settle up, and keep what was learned.

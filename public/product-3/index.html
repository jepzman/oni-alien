From Idea to Backlog

Volume 3 of The Product Owner's Notebook: how a product owner turns a good idea into working software, with the team. 15 notes in four collections: shape the work into thin slices, write it down so everyone means the same thing, work as one team without meeting marathons, and ship it safely. Every note follows Grace at Larder from February to June 2026, through the approvals launch and Rotterdam's opening, and ends with a try it exercise for your own product.

the story so far

February 2026. The business case for substitution approvals is approved, the “no swaps” flag ships in a fortnight, and Rotterdam opens on 27 April. Now an idea has to become software that pickers and customers actually use. This volume follows Grace from February to June 2026, with Noor, the new product designer, and the engineers: shaping the work, writing it down, working as one team, and shipping it safely, in Amsterdam and in a brand-new city.

?How every note is laid out same eight sections, every time

  1. The situation: a scene from the case study where it goes wrong, with a doodle.
  2. Real-life analogy: an everyday version, plus a “translating it to the job” table.
  3. How it solves it: diagrams, steps, vocabulary and a template card.
  4. Gains vs losses: the trade-off spread.
  5. Ratings: nine qualities, before → after.
  6. Common pitfalls: each with its fix.
  7. When to use it: and when to skip it.
  8. Self-check quiz: tap a question to see the answer.

★The nine product ratings what a good product practice is judged on

Every note scores the pattern from 1 (poor) to 5 (great) on the same nine things, comparing an app without the pattern to the same app with it.

Customer value
Customers would notice it, and miss it if it went.
Business impact
It shows up in a number the business already tracks.
Evidence
Decided on what we learned, not on opinion or rank.
Learning speed
How soon we know if we're wrong.
Focus
The team works on a few things at once, and knows which matter most.
Shared understanding
Everyone can explain what we're building, for whom and why.
Stakeholder trust
Leaders and partners trust the plan and the updates.
Flow
Value reaches customers in small, frequent steps.
Low overhead
Time and ceremony it takes. High = light.

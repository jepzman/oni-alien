Refinement that Isn't a Meeting Marathon

TL;DR

Refinement (getting stories ready to build) often becomes a two-hour meeting where nine people listen to discussions about stories most of them won't touch. Break it up: a pre-read early in the week, small-group sessions per story (the three amigos, 25 minutes, example mapping), and one short whole-team session only for open questions and rough sizing. Refine just enough, just in time: about the next sprint or two.

  • Origin: backlog grooming in Scrum (renamed refinement, 2013); three amigos (George Dinwiddie)
  • Lives in: a weekly rhythm in the team calendar
  • Pairs with: example mapping, definition of ready, sprint goals

1The situation Larder, 23 March 2026

Every Thursday, the whole team of nine sits in refinement for two hours. They discuss six stories; two end up ready. Half the room is on laptops; Dee hears twenty minutes about a support table he'll never touch. That's eighteen person-hours a week for two stories, and everyone dreads Thursdays.

  • Everyone attends everything, so most people are bored most of the time.
  • Discussion starts from zero because nobody read anything first.
  • Deep detail gets debated by people who don't need it.

2Real-life analogy the band rehearsal

A band preparing a concert doesn't make everyone sit through the drummer learning a new fill. Sections rehearse separately, briefly and focused; then the full band runs the songs together once, fixing what only shows up when everyone plays. Everyone rehearses more, and waits less.

3How it solves it pre-read, small groups, short together

From 23 March: on Monday Grace posts the next stories with her open questions; on Tuesday and Wednesday three people per story map examples in 25 minutes; on Thursday the whole team meets for 45 minutes, only for remaining questions and rough sizing. The team spends about 8 person-hours a week instead of 18, and six to eight stories are ready instead of two.

  1. Post a pre-read

    Monday: the next stories, their links and your open questions.

  2. Refine in small groups

    The three amigos per story, 25 minutes, example mapping.

  3. Meet briefly as a team

    45 minutes: remaining questions, rough sizes, dependencies.

  4. Refine just enough

    Only the next sprint or two; later items stay rough.

  5. Check against ready

    Use the definition of ready, not the clock.

  6. Review the rhythm

    Ask at the retro: too much, too little, wrong people?

# Refinement rhythm · ordering & apps team

Mon (async)    Grace posts the next 6–8 stories: link to story map + open questions.
               Everyone comments where they know something.
Tue–Wed        Three amigos per story, 25 min, example mapping.
               Who: Grace or Noor + one engineer who'll build it + Dee (testing).
Thu 10:00–10:45  Whole team: remaining red cards, rough sizes, dependencies.
               No new stories presented here.

Rule: refine only for the next two sprints.
Before (to 20 Mar): 2 h × 9 people = 18 person-hours → 2 stories ready.
After  (from 23 Mar): ≈ 8 person-hours → 6–8 stories ready.

tools: the backlog tool's comments for the pre-read; example-mapping templates (Miro, FigJam) or sticky notes; a shared calendar rhythm.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • Fewer person-hours: 18 → about 8.
  • More stories ready: two → six to eight.
  • The right people on each story.
  • Better questions, because people read first.
  • Less dread on Thursdays.

What we lose

  • More scheduling: several small sessions.
  • Not everyone sees everything.
  • Pre-reads get skipped sometimes.
  • Needs a PO who prepares each Monday.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Everyone, everything

    Nine people on every story. Fix → small groups per story; whole team briefly.

  • No pre-read

    The meeting starts by reading the stories aloud. Fix → post them Monday, with questions.

  • Refining months ahead

    Detail for stories that will change. Fix → only the next sprint or two.

  • Presenting in Thursday's session

    New stories shown for the first time. Fix → Thursday is for questions and sizing only.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Teams whose refinement meetings run long.
  • When most attendees have nothing to say about most stories.
  • Alongside example mapping.
  • Teams of six or more.

Skip it when…

  • Teams of three or four: one short session is fine.
  • Kanban teams pulling just in time: refine at the top of the queue.

8Self-check quiz

What are the three parts of Larder's rhythm?

A Monday pre-read, three-amigos sessions per story, a 45-minute whole-team session.

What's the whole-team session for?

Remaining questions, rough sizing and dependencies, not presenting stories.

How far ahead should you refine?

Just the next sprint or two.

try it

Count your refinement's person-hours last week and how many stories ended up ready. Next week, post a pre-read on Monday, run 25-minute sessions with three people per story, and cut the team meeting to 45 minutes. Compare the two numbers.

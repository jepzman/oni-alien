Definition of Ready & Done

TL;DR

Two short, team-agreed checklists stop two kinds of waste. A definition of ready says what a story needs before it enters a sprint (rules clear, design ready, affected people consulted), so it isn't rebuilt later. A definition of done says what finished means: not merged, but live, measured and supported. Keep both short, make them visible, and treat ready as a checklist, not a gate.

  • Origin: Scrum's Definition of Done (Schwaber & Sutherland); Definition of Ready from Jeff Sutherland's teams (2008)
  • Lives in: the team's wiki page and the board's column rules
  • Pairs with: example mapping, user stories, launch checklist

1The situation Larder, 4 March 2026

Slice 1 shipped, but not smoothly. Two stories entered the sprint before anyone checked the picker flow with Tomás; both were redone. Three stories were moved to “done” when the code was merged, but they weren't in production, sent no tracking events, and Sam's support team had never heard of them. Nobody had agreed what ready and done mean.

  • Stories start half-understood and get rebuilt.
  • “Done” means different things to different people.
  • Tracking and support are forgotten until customers ask.

2Real-life analogy the restaurant kitchen

Before service, a kitchen does its mise en place: everything chopped, measured, ready. Nobody starts a dish and then goes to find the shallots. After cooking, the plate goes to the pass, where the head chef checks it before it leaves. A dish that's cooked but not checked and served isn't done.

3How it solves it two short lists, agreed by the team

The team wrote both lists in 30 minutes, from slice 1's mistakes. Ready: example map with no reds, design for customer screens, Tomás or Sam asked if their work changes, tracking event named. Done: live behind a flag, tracking firing, a help article or support macro, tested on a store handheld, accepted by Grace. Slice 2 had no rebuilt stories and no “done” items that weren't live.

  1. Start from real mistakes

    What went wrong last sprint? Each item answers one.

  2. Write ready: four or five items

    What a story needs before starting, no more.

  3. Write done: live, measured, supported

    Production, tracking, support, the device it runs on, acceptance.

  4. Agree as a team

    The team owns the lists, not the PO alone.

  5. Make them visible

    On the board's columns; check them at planning and review.

  6. Review every few sprints

    Add what keeps going wrong; drop what never does.

Vocabulary for the exam

Definition of ready

Definition of done

The PO's check that the story does what was agreed.

A hard stop; ready should be a checklist, not a gate.

# Ordering & apps team · working agreements            review every 3 sprints

READY (before a story enters a sprint)
  [ ] Example map done, no red cards left (or each has an owner and date)
  [ ] Customer-facing screens: design ready (Noor)
  [ ] Store or support work changes? Tomás / Sam consulted
  [ ] Tracking event named (e.g. swap_answered)

DONE (before a story moves to Done)
  [ ] Live in production, behind a flag if needed
  [ ] Tracking events firing in the dashboard
  [ ] Help article or support macro written (Sam has seen it)
  [ ] Tested on a store handheld (picker stories) or both apps (customer stories)
  [ ] Accepted by Grace against the examples

Ready is a checklist, not a gate: a story can start with a named open question.

tools: a wiki page and the board's column rules (Jira, Linear and Trello support checklists per column); the example map as the main “ready” artefact.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • Fewer rebuilt stories: rules and people checked first.
  • Done means live, measured and supported.
  • Support isn't surprised.
  • Tracking exists from day one.
  • Team-owned standards, not imposed ones.

What we lose

  • Can become bureaucracy if lists grow.
  • Ready as a gate slows everything.
  • Done takes longer per story (honestly).
  • Needs review to stay relevant.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Ready as a gate

    Nothing starts until every box is ticked. Fix → a checklist; a named open question is fine.

  • Done = merged

    Code on main, nothing live. Fix → done means live, measured, supported.

  • Lists too long

    Twenty items nobody reads. Fix → four or five each, from real mistakes.

  • PO-imposed

    Grace writes the lists alone. Fix → the team agrees and owns them.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Teams where stories get rebuilt or bounce back.
  • When “done” is argued about.
  • Features that need support and tracking.
  • New teams forming working agreements.

Skip it when…

  • Experiments meant to be thrown away (lighter lists).
  • Incidents: fix first.

8Self-check quiz

What does a definition of ready prevent?

Stories starting half-understood and being rebuilt.

What does “done” mean in Larder's definition?

Live in production, tracking firing, support ready, tested on the right device, accepted.

Should ready be a gate?

No: a checklist; a named open question can be allowed.

Who owns the lists?

The team, not the product owner alone.

try it

List the last three stories that bounced back or that customers found broken. For each, write the check that would have caught it. Turn those into a four-item ready list and a five-item done list, and agree them with your team at the next retro.

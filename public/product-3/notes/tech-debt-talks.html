Talking Tech Debt with Engineers

TL;DR

Engineers talk about technical debt in technical terms; product owners hear a hobby competing with features. Fix the language, not the relationship. Ask engineers to translate each debt item into an effect you own (refund tickets, launch dates, on-time rate, team speed), agree a fixed share of capacity, order debt by product impact, and report each paid item with a product number. Then debt work is a bet you both understand.

1The situation Larder, 8 June 2026

At Q3 planning Ben asks for 20 % of every sprint for technical debt. He explains sync conflict resolution and idempotency keys. Grace hears something interesting that competes with approvals in Rotterdam, and says “after the launch”. Ben hears “never”, again. Neither is wrong; they're speaking two languages at the same table.

  • Debt described in technical terms sounds optional to product.
  • “After the launch” repeated becomes “never”, and trust erodes.
  • Debt work, when it happens, isn't connected to anything product can see.

2Real-life analogy the chef and the fridge

A head chef tells the owner: “the walk-in fridge needs servicing”. The owner hears a cost and says next month. The chef tries again: “if it fails on a Saturday we bin €3,000 of fish and close the kitchen”. The owner says: book it this week. Same fridge; a sentence in the owner's terms.

3How it solves it translate, agree a share, report

Grace asked Ben to try again, in her terms. Picker sync conflicts (12 a week at Noord) meant wrong swaps packed, which meant refund tickets, her outcome. Rotterdam's three hard-coded settings meant Utrecht would launch slower. The Dispatch loop kept Rotterdam at 84 % on time. They agreed 20 % of every sprint, ordered by product impact, with each paid item reported at the review with a number. A week later Ana's debt register adopted the same 20 %.

  1. Ask for the translation

    “What does this cost customers, launches or the team, per month?”

  2. Map debt to outcomes you own

    Refund tickets, on-time, launch dates, team speed.

  3. Agree a fixed share

    A percentage of every sprint (20 % here), not a fight per item.

  4. Order by product impact

    Within the share, the item with the biggest product effect goes first.

  5. Report with a product number

    “Sync conflicts: 12 → 1 a week; swap refunds from wrong packing: −40 %.”

  6. Revisit the share quarterly

    Up after a big launch; down when the register is healthy.

Vocabulary for the exam

Technical debt
Shortcuts that make change slower or riskier later.
Capacity allocation
A fixed share of team time for a kind of work.
Product impact
The effect on outcomes the PO owns.
Interest
What debt costs every month (see Ana's register).
Translation
Saying a technical issue in the listener's terms.
Trust
Believing “later” means later, not never.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • Debt gets paid, steadily.
  • Shared language between product and engineering.
  • Trust: “later” has a schedule.
  • Better order: product impact decides.
  • Visible results in product numbers.

What we lose

  • 20 % less feature work, visibly.
  • Translation is hard for some debt.
  • Some debt has no product number yet.
  • The share needs defending in busy quarters.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Technical pitch

    “We need to refactor the sync layer.” Fix → say what it costs customers or launches.

  • Negotiating every item

    A fight in every planning. Fix → agree a share; order within it.

  • “After the launch”, again

    Later becomes never. Fix → a fixed share from now, reviewed quarterly.

  • No result shown

    Debt work disappears into the sprint. Fix → report each item with a product number.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Every product team with a codebase older than a year.
  • When “tech debt” keeps losing planning debates.
  • After a launch that took shortcuts.
  • When engineers start to distrust “later”.

Skip it when…

  • Brand-new products with almost no debt (yet).
  • Incidents: fix first, then translate.

8Self-check quiz

What does Grace ask Ben for?

A translation of each debt item into effects she owns: refunds, launch dates, on-time rate.

Why a fixed share instead of per-item decisions?

It stops the fight in every planning and makes “later” a schedule.

What share did they agree?

20 % of every sprint, ordered by product impact.

How is the result shown?

Each paid item at the review, with a product number.

try it

Ask your tech lead for the three debt items that bother them most. For each, write together what it costs customers, launches or the team, with a number if you can. Propose a fixed share of capacity and one product number you'll report at the next review.

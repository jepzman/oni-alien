Dark Launches & Rollouts

TL;DR

Don't switch a big change on for everyone at once. Dark launch it first: live in production but invisible, or visible only on one side (here: pickers), so you can watch real load. Then roll it out by percentage behind a feature flag (10 %, 25 %, 50 %, 100 %), with guardrail metrics agreed in advance, a kill switch, and permission to hold when a guardrail trips.

  • Family: shipping
  • Origin: dark launches at Facebook (2008) and Flickr; progressive delivery (James Governor, 2018)

1The situation Larder, 6 April 2026

Slice 1 worked with Noord's staff. Now approvals goes to all of Amsterdam's express customers. The simplest plan: switch it on for everyone on Monday 13 April. But nobody knows what happens to picking time at the Friday peak with hundreds of swaps waiting for answers, and if it slows, every customer feels it at once, on the busiest evening of the week.

  • Big-bang releases test real load in front of every customer.
  • Without agreed guardrails, nobody knows when to stop.
  • Rolling back everything is slow and visible.

2Real-life analogy the soft opening

A new restaurant does a soft opening: the kitchen cooks the full menu for staff first, then a friends-and-family night, then half the tables, then full service. At each step the chef watches how long dishes take. If the kitchen falls behind at half the tables, they stay at half until it doesn't.

3How it solves it dark first, then a ramp with guardrails

From 6 April the picker side ran dark in every Amsterdam store: pickers chose substitutes as if customers were asked, and Larder measured picking time. On 13 April customers joined at 10 %, then 25 % and 50 %. On Friday 17 April, Zuid's picking time per swap order rose 18 seconds, over the 15-second guardrail. The ramp held at 50 % over the weekend; Dee fixed a handheld lag; 100 % on Monday 20 April.

  1. Put it behind a flag

    On/off per user, per store, per percentage; test the kill switch.

  2. Dark launch first

    Run it in production without showing it (or one side only) and measure.

  3. Agree guardrails in advance

    Two or three numbers that must not get worse, with thresholds.

  4. Ramp by percentage

    10 → 25 → 50 → 100 %, including at least one peak at 50 %.

  5. Hold or roll back on a trip

    A tripped guardrail stops the ramp; no debate needed.

  6. Remove the flag after

    Once at 100 % for a while, delete the old path.

Vocabulary for the exam

Dark launch
Running new code in production without users seeing it.
Progressive rollout
Increasing exposure step by step.
Feature flag
A switch that turns a feature on for chosen users.
Guardrail metric
A number that must not get worse during the rollout.
Kill switch
Turning the feature off for everyone, instantly.
Canary
A small group that sees the change first.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • Real load tested before customers depend on it.
  • Problems hit a few, not everyone.
  • Clear stop rules: guardrails decide.
  • Instant off with a kill switch.
  • Calmer launches for team and operations.

What we lose

  • Slower to reach everyone: a week, not a day.
  • Flag code to maintain and remove.
  • Two paths to test while ramping.
  • Mixed experiences for customers during the ramp.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Ramping without guardrails

    “Looks fine” at each step. Fix → agree numbers and thresholds before 10 %.

  • Skipping the peak

    100 % before any Friday at 50 %. Fix → include a real peak at a middle step.

  • Untested kill switch

    It doesn't work when needed. Fix → test it before the ramp starts.

  • Flags forever

    Old paths and flags pile up. Fix → remove the flag once stable.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Features that change operations or load.
  • Anything risky at peak times.
  • Big changes to a high-traffic flow.
  • When you need real data before full exposure.

Skip it when…

  • Tiny copy or style changes.
  • Legal changes that must apply to everyone at once.

8Self-check quiz

What's a dark launch?

Running new code in production without users seeing it, to measure real load.

What are guardrails for?

Agreed numbers that stop the ramp when they get worse.

What happened at 50 %?

Friday's peak pushed Zuid's picking time +18 s, over the 15 s guardrail, so the ramp held until a fix.

What should happen to the flag afterwards?

Remove it and the old path once the feature is stable.

try it

For your next risky feature, write the rollout: what runs dark first, the percentage steps, three guardrails with thresholds, and how the kill switch works. Test the kill switch before the first step.

remember!!

Soft opening first. Hold at half the tables if the kitchen falls behind.

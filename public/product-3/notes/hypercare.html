Hypercare after Launch

TL;DR

The first weeks after a launch are when most problems show up, through many channels at once. Run a short hypercare period: a daily 15-minute stand-up with product, delivery, operations, support and engineering; one board with the few numbers that matter and the top issues, each with an owner; fast fixes; and exit criteria agreed on day one, so hypercare ends on purpose and hands over to normal operations.

  • Family: shipping
  • Origin: IT service management practice (early-life support in ITIL); go-live support in enterprise launches
  • Lives in: a daily stand-up and one board, for two weeks
  • Pairs with: launch checklist, dark launches, post-launch review

1The situation Larder, 28 April 2026

Rotterdam opened on 27 April. By the second morning, problems were arriving through five channels: support tickets, the store manager's phone, couriers, a dashboard alert, a tweet. Three customers were told “we don't deliver to your street” in the middle of a zone. Nobody owned the whole picture; each problem was fixed by whoever heard about it first, or not at all.

  • Post-launch problems scatter across channels and teams.
  • Without one board, the same issue is reported three times or never.
  • Without exit criteria, “launch mode” either ends too soon or never.

2Real-life analogy intensive care, then the ward

After surgery a patient goes to intensive care (constant watching), then the ward (daily rounds), then home with a check-up. Each move happens when clear criteria are met: stable for a day, eating, walking. Nobody stays in intensive care for a month “to be safe”, and nobody goes home from the operating table.

3How it solves it daily, one board, exit criteria

From day two, Grace ran a 15-minute stand-up at 09:00 with Sanne, Joost, Sam, Ben and Eli, around one board. Three issues were found and fixed in the first week: three streets missing from a zone (postcode table), Saturday slots sold out by Thursday (capacity raised), and an iDEAL error for one bank (provider setting). The board also showed express on time at 84 %, which went to Ana. Exit criteria were met on 11 May; Rotterdam moved to normal on-call.

  1. Agree exit criteria on day one

    Measurable: tickets per 100 orders, incidents, key flows working.

  2. Daily, short, cross-team

    15 minutes: product, delivery, operations, support, engineering.

  3. One board

    Three or four numbers and the top issues, each with one owner.

  4. One way in

    All channels feed the board; no side fixes without a line.

  5. Fix fast, decide fast

    Small fixes the same day; bigger ones go to the backlog with a date.

  6. End on purpose

    When criteria are met, hand over to normal on-call; review in a month.

tools: one shared board (a doc, a Jira board, a Notion page), the launch dashboards, a channel all reports go to; a 15-minute recurring meeting.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • One picture of every post-launch problem.
  • Owners and fast fixes, days not weeks.
  • Calm for support and operations.
  • Ends on purpose, with criteria.
  • Bigger issues surface (on-time) for the right people.

What we lose

  • Six people, 15 minutes a day, for two weeks.
  • Can drag on without exit criteria.
  • Side channels creep back.
  • Fixes in a hurry can add debt.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • No exit criteria

    Hypercare for six weeks “to be safe”. Fix → measurable criteria on day one.

  • Side fixes

    Problems fixed in DMs, never on the board. Fix → every channel feeds one board.

  • Long stand-ups

    An hour every morning. Fix → 15 minutes: numbers, top issues, owners.

  • No handover

    Hypercare ends; nobody owns what's left. Fix → hand remaining issues to normal on-call with owners.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • New cities, stores or countries.
  • Big launches touching operations and support.
  • The first weeks of a migration.
  • Any launch with a fixed date and many channels.

Skip it when…

  • Small feature releases behind a flag.
  • Launches with few users and one team.

8Self-check quiz

When should exit criteria be agreed?

On day one of hypercare.

What's on the hypercare board?

A few key numbers and the top issues, each with one owner.

What did Rotterdam's hypercare find?

Missing streets in a zone, Saturday slots too low, an iDEAL error, and the 84 % on-time rate.

How does hypercare end?

When the exit criteria are met, with a handover to normal on-call.

try it

For your next launch, write three exit criteria with numbers before the day. Book a 15-minute daily stand-up for two weeks with product, operations, support and engineering, and make one board the only place issues are tracked.

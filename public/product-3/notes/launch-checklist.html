Launch Checklist

TL;DR

Code is the smallest part of a launch. Everything around it (configuration, test orders, support, communication, tracking, rollback, people on the day) fails quietly unless someone checks it. Keep a launch checklist grouped by area, with one owner and a status per item, and hold a go / no-go a few days before, early enough to fix the reds. Reuse it for every launch, and add whatever went wrong last time.

1The situation Larder, 30 March 2026

Four weeks before Rotterdam opens, Joost tells Grace about store Zuid's opening day in November 2024: 340 products had no price, support learnt about the opening from customers, and the first push message went to the wrong area. Nobody had a list; everybody assumed somebody else had checked. Rotterdam is a whole new city, with a fixed date and a signed lease.

  • Launch work outside the code has no home in the backlog.
  • Each person checks their own part and assumes the rest is fine.
  • Problems surface on launch day, in front of customers.

2Real-life analogy the pre-flight checklist

Pilots with thousands of hours still run the pre-flight checklist: one reads each item aloud, the other checks it and answers. Fuel, flaps, doors, instruments. Not because they don't know how to fly, but because memory fails under pressure, and a forgotten item is expensive at 10,000 metres.

3How it solves it areas, owners, a go / no-go

Grace built the checklist from Zuid's story, the pre-mortem and Ana's open decisions: eight areas, one owner each. Joost's team checked every Rotterdam price; Ben placed five test orders per zone with real payments; Sam wrote Dutch help pages. At the go / no-go on 24 April two items were red (Saturday slot capacity too low, help pages unfinished); both were fixed by the 26th. Rotterdam opened on time, with every price.

  1. Start from past failures

    Zuid's opening day, the pre-mortem, last launch's surprises.

  2. Group by area

    Product config, test orders, support, comms, tracking, rollback, people, store.

  3. One owner per item

    A name, not a team; they confirm it's done.

  4. Track status openly

    Green, amber, red, visible to everyone.

  5. Hold a go / no-go

    Two or three days before, so reds can be fixed.

  6. Keep it for next time

    Add what went wrong; reuse it for Utrecht.

Vocabulary for the exam

Launch day
When customers first see it: not the time to discover gaps.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • Nothing forgotten: prices, support, rollback.
  • Clear owners: no “I thought you checked”.
  • Reds found days early, not on launch day.
  • Calm launch days.
  • Reusable for every new city.

What we lose

  • Upkeep: statuses need chasing.
  • Can become box-ticking without real checks.
  • Long lists hide what matters most.
  • No-go is hard on a fixed date.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Code-only checklist

    “All stories done.” Fix → include config, support, comms, rollback, people.

  • Team owners

    “Support” owns help pages. Fix → one named person per item.

  • Go / no-go the day before

    No time to fix reds. Fix → two or three days before.

  • Starting from zero each time

    A new list for Utrecht. Fix → reuse and extend the last checklist.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Any launch customers will notice: new city, big feature, pricing change.
  • Launches with operations and support involved.
  • Fixed-date launches.
  • Repeated launches (cities, countries).

Skip it when…

  • Small changes behind a flag with no customer-visible effect.
  • Experiments with a handful of users.

8Self-check quiz

What does a launch checklist cover besides code?

Configuration, test orders, support, comms, tracking, rollback, people and the store.

Why one owner per item?

So nobody assumes somebody else checked it.

When should go / no-go happen?

Two or three days before, so reds can still be fixed.

What were the two reds for Rotterdam?

Saturday slot capacity and the Dutch help pages.

try it

For your next launch, list eight areas beyond the code. Under each, write three items and one owner's name. Hold a 30-minute go / no-go three days before launch and fix every red before the day.

remember!!

Read it aloud, item by item. Go / no-go with time to fix.

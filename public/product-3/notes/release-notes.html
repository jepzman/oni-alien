a.k.a. “the platform change announcement”

Release Notes & Customer Comms

TL;DR

A feature customers don't understand looks like a bug, or a scam. For every change they'll notice, tell them before they meet it: what changed, why it's good for them, and what to do. Use the channels they already see (email, an intro card at the moment of use, a “what's new” note), and prepare the staff who'll be asked (support macros, help articles, a briefing for operations). Measure it: tickets per 1,000 uses in the first week.

1The situation Larder, 8 June 2026

When approvals reached all of Amsterdam in April, customers met a new pop-up with a countdown and a price, unexplained. In the first week Sam's team got 140 tickets: what is this pop-up?, why are you charging me €0.40?, and one is this a scam? Support had no prepared answers. The feature worked; the explanation didn't exist. Now approvals is going to Rotterdam.

  • Changes customers notice without context feel like errors.
  • Support learns about features from customers.
  • The first impression of a good feature is confusion.

2Real-life analogy the platform change

A good station announces a platform change before people walk to the wrong platform: what (platform 7, five minutes later), why (works on the line), and what to do (follow the yellow signs). Staff on the platform know it too. Announced after the train has gone, it's just an apology.

3How it solves it before the change, customers and staff

For Rotterdam on 8 June, Grace and Noor prepared both sides. Customers got an email the day before, a one-screen intro card on their first swap (“we'll ask before we swap; the price difference is shown”), and a “what's new” note. Staff got Sam's macros, a help article, and a two-minute briefing for Rotterdam's pickers. First week: 1.4 tickets per 1,000 approvals, against 6.1 in Amsterdam's first week.

  1. List who'll notice

    Customers, pickers, couriers, support, business clients.

  2. Write what, why, what to do

    Three short sentences, in the customer's words.

  3. Use the moment of use

    An intro card the first time they meet it; not just an email.

  4. Prepare staff first

    Macros, help article, a briefing, before customers ask.

  5. Put it in the launch checklist

    Comms is an area with an owner.

  6. Measure tickets per 1,000 uses

    In the first week; compare with the last launch.

Vocabulary for the exam

Release note
A short description of what changed, for users.
Intro card
A one-time explanation shown at first use.
Support macro
A prepared answer for a common question.
Help article
A page explaining a feature in detail.
Change communication
Telling people about a change before they meet it.
Tickets per 1,000 uses
Support questions normalised by usage.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • Fewer confused customers and tickets.
  • A better first impression of good features.
  • Support ready before the first question.
  • Trust: no “is this a scam?”.
  • A number to improve: tickets per 1,000 uses.

What we lose

  • Writing and translating takes time.
  • Intro cards annoy if overused.
  • Emails get ignored; the moment of use matters more.
  • Easy to forget without an owner.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • No explanation

    A pop-up appears; customers guess. Fix → what, why, what to do, at the moment of use.

  • Email only

    Nobody reads it. Fix → an intro card the first time they meet it.

  • Staff last

    Support learns from customers. Fix → brief staff before customers see it.

  • Intro cards for everything

    Every small tweak gets a card. Fix → only changes customers will notice and wonder about.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Any change customers will notice.
  • New steps in a flow, new prices, new rules.
  • Launches in new cities or languages.
  • Changes that support will be asked about.

Skip it when…

  • Invisible improvements (speed, fixes).
  • Experiments to a small group: a lighter note.

8Self-check quiz

What three things should every change message say?

What changed, why it's good for them, and what to do.

Why an intro card, not just an email?

Customers meet the change at the moment of use; emails are often ignored.

Who should be prepared before customers?

Support, pickers and anyone customers will ask.

What measure did Grace use?

Support tickets per 1,000 approvals in the first week.

try it

For your next visible change, write three sentences (what, why, what to do) and test them on two people outside the team. Prepare support with macros a day before, add a one-time intro card, and count tickets per 1,000 uses in week one.

remember!!

Announce the platform change before people walk to the wrong one. Staff first.

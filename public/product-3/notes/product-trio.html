The Product Trio

TL;DR

Hand-offs (PO to designer to engineers) lose what each person learned. A product trio (the product owner, a designer and a tech lead) does discovery together: they interview customers together, every week, visit the people who use the product, and decide on solutions as one group. The PO brings value and viability, the designer usability, the tech lead feasibility. Good solutions live where all three overlap.

1The situation Larder, 9 February 2026

Until February, Grace interviewed customers alone, wrote a twelve-page spec, and handed it to Dee, who drew screens in the evenings between mobile work; Dee handed them to Ben's engineers. The engineers had never met a customer or a picker. Each hand-off lost something: why a feature mattered, what the customer actually said, what the pickers' gloves are like.

  • Knowledge stays with whoever did the research.
  • Feasibility arrives late, after the design is “done”.
  • Engineers build what's written, not what's needed.

2Real-life analogy the location scout

A film crew scouts locations with three people: the director (does it serve the story?), the cinematographer (can we shoot it well here?) and the producer (can we afford it, and get permits?). They stand in the same street and decide together, on the spot, instead of passing memos for a month.

3How it solves it three views, one weekly rhythm

When Noor joined as product designer on 9 February, Grace, Noor and Ben became a trio. Every Thursday at 16:00 they interview one customer together; every other week they visit Noord to watch Tomás's pickers. It paid off fast: during slice 1, Ben watched a picker stand waiting for an answer and suggested applying answers at packing, the fix that made slice 2 work.

  1. Name the trio

    Product owner, designer, tech lead: the same three people for an area.

  2. Book a weekly interview

    One customer, 30 minutes, all three present; take turns asking.

  3. Visit the real users

    Stores, warehouses, offices: watch the work, not a demo.

  4. Share one opportunity tree

    Update it together, every week.

  5. Decide solutions together

    Value, usability and feasibility in one conversation.

  6. Bring the team along

    Rotate an engineer into the interview now and then.

Vocabulary for the exam

Product trio
PO, designer and tech lead doing discovery together.

Finding out what to build: interviews, tests, research.
Delivery
Building and shipping it.
Viability
Does it work for the business: cost, law, operations?
Feasibility
Can we build and run it, with our people and systems?
Usability
Can people use it, easily and without errors?

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • No lossy hand-offs: all three heard the customer.
  • Feasibility early: Ben's packing idea came from a store visit.
  • Better solutions in the overlap.
  • Engineers see why, through the tech lead.
  • Faster decisions: no memos between three desks.

What we lose

  • Two to three hours a week from a tech lead and a designer.
  • Needs a designer (Larder hired one).
  • Can exclude the rest of the team if not rotated.
  • Disagreements need a rule.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • A trio in name only

    Grace still interviews alone; Noor and Ben read notes. Fix → all three in the interview, every week.

  • A closed club

    The rest of the team feels shut out. Fix → rotate engineers into interviews.

  • No disagreement rule

    Stalemates between the three. Fix → agree who decides what, and write down why.

  • Discovery only

    The trio stops at the idea. Fix → stay together through delivery and the launch.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Any product area with a PO, a designer and engineers.
  • When specs keep getting misread.
  • When engineers never meet users.
  • Continuous discovery alongside delivery.

Skip it when…

  • Very small teams where everyone already interviews.
  • Pure platform work with no end users.

8Self-check quiz

Who's in a product trio?

The product owner, a designer and a tech lead.

What does each bring?

PO: value and viability. Designer: usability. Tech lead: feasibility.

What's the weekly habit?

Interviewing one customer together.

What came from Ben's store visit?

Applying answers at packing, so pickers never wait.

try it

Book a 45-minute customer interview next week with your designer and tech lead in the room. Take turns asking. Afterwards, spend 15 minutes on what each of you heard that the others didn't, and put one change on the opportunity tree.

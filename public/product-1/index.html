Discovering the Problem

Volume 1 of The Product Owner's Notebook: how a product owner finds the right problem before the team builds a solution. 15 notes in four collections: listen to customers, frame the problem, decide what to measure, and test ideas while they're still cheap. Every note follows Grace at Larder, in the same months and meetings as Ana's notebook, and ends with a try it exercise for your own product.

the story so far

October 2025. Larder delivers groceries in Amsterdam and has promised its investors six cities by the end of 2027. Grace joins as product owner for ordering and the customer apps, a week after Ana joins as architect. Six percent of orders end in a refund ticket and nobody owns the problem. This volume follows Grace's first four months: finding out what customers really need before anyone builds anything.

Understanding customers listen before you decide anything

  1. 01Customer InterviewsAsk customers about what they did last time, not what they'd like, and listen for the pain.p. 01
  2. 02Jobs to Be DoneFind the progress customers are trying to make; the product is just something they hire for the job.p. 02
  3. 03Proto-PersonasQuick, evidence-based sketches of the main kinds of customer, kept honest and kept few.p. 03
  4. 04Customer Journey MapDraw every step a customer takes, what they feel at each one, and where it breaks.p. 04

Framing the problem pick the problem worth solving, and say it clearly

  1. 05Problem StatementsWrite the problem in one sentence, from the customer's side, before anyone proposes a fix.p. 05
  2. 06Opportunity Solution TreeLink one outcome to the customer needs behind it, then to ideas, then to tests.p. 06
  3. 07Assumption MappingList what must be true for an idea to work, and test the riskiest first.p. 07
  4. 08Outcomes over OutputsJudge work by the change it makes for customers, not by the features shipped.p. 08

Measuring what matters know what “better” means before you build

  1. 09North Star MetricOne number that captures the value customers get, so every team pulls the same way.p. 09
  2. 10Leading vs Lagging IndicatorsPair the slow result you care about with fast signals you can move this week.p. 10
  3. 11Baseline Before You BuildMeasure today properly first, or you'll never know if the change worked.p. 11

Testing ideas cheaply find out if you're wrong in days, not months

  1. 12Testable HypothesesWrite each idea as a bet: we believe, so we'll, and we'll know when.p. 12
  2. 13Fake Door TestShow the button before building the feature, and count who knocks.p. 13
  3. 14Concierge & Wizard-of-Oz MVPDeliver the value by hand first; build the software only once it's proven.p. 14
  4. 15Prototype Testing with UsersPut a clickable fake in front of five real users and watch where they stumble.p. 15

  1. The situation: a scene from the case study where it goes wrong, with a doodle.
  2. Real-life analogy: an everyday version, plus a “translating it to the job” table.
  3. How it solves it: diagrams, steps, vocabulary and a template card.
  4. Gains vs losses: the trade-off spread.
  5. Ratings: nine qualities, before → after.
  6. Common pitfalls: each with its fix.
  7. When to use it: and when to skip it.
  8. Self-check quiz: tap a question to see the answer.

★The nine product ratings what a good product practice is judged on

Every note scores the pattern from 1 (poor) to 5 (great) on the same nine things, comparing an app without the pattern to the same app with it.

Customer value
Customers would notice it, and miss it if it went.
Business impact
It shows up in a number the business already tracks.
Evidence
Decided on what we learned, not on opinion or rank.
Learning speed
How soon we know if we're wrong.
Focus
The team works on a few things at once, and knows which matter most.
Shared understanding
Everyone can explain what we're building, for whom and why.
Stakeholder trust
Leaders and partners trust the plan and the updates.
Flow
Value reaches customers in small, frequent steps.
Low overhead
Time and ceremony it takes. High = light.

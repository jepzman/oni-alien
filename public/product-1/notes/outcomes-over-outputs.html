Outcomes over Outputs

TL;DR

An output is what you ship: a feature, a screen. An outcome is a change in what customers do that you can measure: fewer refund tickets, more repeat orders. An impact is the business result that follows. Judge work, and write the roadmap, by outcomes. Features become bets under an outcome, kept if the number moves and dropped if it doesn't. It's also the kindest way to say no: “which outcome does this serve?”

1The situation Larder, last year's roadmap

Larder's 2025 roadmap was a list of fourteen features with dates. Eleven shipped on time, and the team celebrated. In the same year the refund rate stayed at 6 % and express on-time stayed at 88 %. Now Femke adds feature fifteen: ML recipe ideas, suggested by an investor. Nobody can say what number it's meant to move, only that it would be delivered on time.

  • Success measured as “shipped” rewards busyness, not results.
  • Feature lists make every request look equally worth doing.
  • Saying no to a senior idea is personal when there's no outcome to point to.
meanwhile, in Ana’s notebook
  • Business Drivers & Goals (Ana, vol. 1): Ana's four drivers are the impact level of Grace's chain; refund tickets are an outcome that moves drivers 3 and 4.

2Real-life analogy the gym card

A gym card with twelve stamps looks like progress. But the goal was to run 5 km in 30 minutes by June, and you can collect every stamp without getting any fitter. The stamps are outputs. The time on the stopwatch is the outcome. Only one of them is why you joined.

3How it solves it outputs serve outcomes serve impact

Pick a few outcomes the team can influence directly (customer behaviour), each linked to an impact the business already tracks. Put outcomes on the roadmap, features underneath as bets, and review the numbers after each release. Requests that don't serve an outcome go to a parking list, with the reason.

  1. Name the impact

    The business result you're after: Ana's driver 4 (trust) and driver 3 (cost per order).

  2. Find the behaviour that drives it

    What customers do differently: answer swap requests; fewer refund tickets.

  3. Make it measurable

    Baseline, target, date: 6 % → 2 % by end Q2 2026.

  4. Hang features under it as bets

    Each with what you expect it to move, and by how much.

  5. Review after every release

    Did the outcome move? Keep, change or drop the bet.

  6. Park what serves no outcome

    With the reason: “recipes: no outcome on this quarter's roadmap”.

Vocabulary for the exam

Output
What the team makes and ships.
Outcome
A measurable change in customer behaviour.
Impact
The business result: revenue, cost, retention, trust.
Bet
A feature you expect to move an outcome, and will check.
Feature factory
A team judged only by how much it ships.
Parking lot
Ideas kept for later, with the reason they wait.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • Results, not busyness: success is a number that matters.
  • A kind way to say no: “which outcome does this serve?”
  • Freedom to find better fixes than the first feature.
  • Honest reviews: bets that don't work get dropped.
  • Leadership speaks the same language as the team.

What we lose

  • Less certainty: no feature list with dates to promise.
  • Measurement work: you need baselines and tracking.
  • Slower signals: outcomes can take weeks to move.
  • Uncomfortable for stakeholders used to feature promises.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Outputs dressed as outcomes

    “Outcome: launch approve-in-app.” Fix → an outcome is a change in behaviour with a number.

  • Outcomes the team can't move

    “Grow revenue 30 %” for one team. Fix → pick behaviour the team can influence, linked to the impact.

  • Never reviewing bets

    Features ship; nobody checks the number. Fix → review the outcome after each release; drop bets that don't work.

  • Using it as a weapon

    “No outcome, no discussion.” Fix → park with a reason and a date to revisit.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Writing or reshaping a roadmap.
  • When the team ships a lot and nothing moves.
  • When senior requests pile up.
  • Setting team goals for a quarter.

Skip it when…

  • Contractual or legal deadlines: those are fixed outputs.
  • Tiny fixes and maintenance.
  • When nothing can be measured yet: build the measurement first.

8Self-check quiz

What's the difference between an output and an outcome?

An output is what you ship; an outcome is a measurable change in what customers do.

How does impact relate to outcome?

Outcomes (behaviour) drive impact (business results like trust or cost per order).

Why were the ML recipe ideas parked?

They served no outcome on the quarter's roadmap; they'll be revisited with retention data.

What happens to a bet that doesn't move its outcome?

It's changed or dropped after the review.

try it

Take your last three shipped features. For each, write the outcome it was supposed to move, and check whether it did. If you can't name the outcome, write down what you'd measure next time before building.

remember!!

Count the running time, not the gym stamps. Features are bets.

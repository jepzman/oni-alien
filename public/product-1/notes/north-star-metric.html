North Star Metric

TL;DR

Pick one metric that captures the value customers get from the product and that leads to the business's success. Break it into a few input metrics that teams can actually move, and give each input an owner. The north star doesn't replace other numbers; it settles arguments about which number wins, and stops teams from hitting their own targets while customers get a worse service.

1The situation Larder, four dashboards

Every team at Larder has its own number. Marketing celebrates sign-ups up 20 % after a promotion. Operations watches picks per hour, support counts tickets closed per day, product watches conversion. The promotion floods Friday with one-time customers: picks per hour drop, tickets rise, and fewer orders arrive complete and on time. Every team can show its target was met.

  • Local metrics pull teams in different directions.
  • Nobody owns the number that describes what customers actually get.
  • Arguments about priorities become arguments about whose metric matters.

2Real-life analogy the rowing eight

In a rowing eight, if each rower pulls at their own pace the boat zigzags, however strong they are. The cox calls one rhythm, and everyone pulls to it. Each rower still has a job (their stroke), but there's one shared measure of whether the boat is going well: speed, in a straight line.

3How it solves it one value number, a few inputs

A good north star measures value delivered (not revenue alone), can be counted every week, and leads to the business result. Grace, Priya and Joost choose perfect orders per week: delivered complete or with an accepted substitute, on time, and with no refund ticket afterwards.

  1. Start from the customer's value

    What does a great experience look like, in one countable event? (A perfect order.)

  2. Check it leads to the business result

    Does it predict drivers 2 and 4, and repeat orders? Check with data.

  3. Define it precisely

    Complete or accepted swap, within the promised window, no ticket within 7 days.

  4. Break it into inputs

    Three to five factors teams can move: volume, completeness, on time, no refund.

  5. Give each input an owner

    One team per input; shared review weekly.

  6. Guard against gaming

    Watch counter-metrics: cost per order, picker workload, complaints.

Vocabulary for the exam

North star metric
One number that captures the value customers get.
Input metric
A factor teams can move that drives the north star.
Counter-metric
A number watched to catch harm from chasing the north star.
Vanity metric
A number that looks good but doesn't guide decisions (downloads).

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • One direction for every team.
  • Customer value at the centre, not only revenue.
  • Settles priority arguments: which input moves the star most?
  • Owners for each input, so it isn't nobody's job.
  • Cross-team reviews that talk about the same thing.

What we lose

  • Takes debate to define precisely.
  • Can be gamed without counter-metrics.
  • One number hides detail: inputs and segments still matter.
  • Needs good data, joined across systems.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Revenue as the north star

    Revenue grows while customers get worse service. Fix → measure value delivered; revenue follows.

  • Vague definition

    “Happy orders.” Fix → define it precisely enough to count automatically.

  • No counter-metrics

    Perfect orders up because pickers work unpaid overtime. Fix → watch cost, workload and complaints alongside.

  • Inputs nobody owns

    Everyone looks at the star; nobody moves it. Fix → one owner per input, reviewed weekly.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Several teams pulling in different directions.
  • Setting the product strategy or quarterly goals.
  • When revenue metrics hide customer problems.
  • Explaining product priorities to leadership.

Skip it when…

  • A single small team with one obvious goal.
  • Before you understand customer value: interview first.
  • As the only number: keep inputs and counter-metrics.

8Self-check quiz

What makes a good north star?

It measures value customers get, can be counted often, and leads to business success.

What is Larder's north star?

Perfect orders per week: complete or accepted swap, on time, no refund ticket within 7 days.

Why add counter-metrics?

To catch harm from chasing the north star, such as higher cost or picker overwork.

What are input metrics for?

They break the north star into factors teams can move, each with an owner.

try it

Write one sentence describing a perfect experience with your product, then turn it into a countable event. Check last month's data: what share of customers got it? Name three inputs and who could own each.

remember!!

One rhythm for the boat. Each team pulls one input.

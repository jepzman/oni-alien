Leading vs Lagging Indicators

TL;DR

Lagging indicators tell you whether you succeeded (refund tickets, repeat orders, revenue), but they arrive late and are hard to move directly. Leading indicators move quickly, are under the team's control, and predict the lagging ones (customers asked about a swap, answers within two minutes). Track both: steer with the leading, judge with the lagging, and check with data that the leading one really predicts the lagging one.

1The situation Larder, waiting for the number

Grace's outcome is refund tickets from substitutions. But a ticket arrives one to seven days after the order, and the effect on repeat orders shows a month later. If she waits for those, weeks of work pass with no signal. Meanwhile marketing reports app downloads every morning: they move fast, but they don't predict a single perfect order.

  • Results arrive too late to steer by.
  • Fast numbers are tempting even when they predict nothing.
  • Teams can't tell this week whether their change is working.

2Real-life analogy getting fitter

When you're getting fitter, the scale and your race time are lagging: they move slowly and tell you, weeks later, whether it worked. Steps today and what you ate today are leading: you can see them and change them every day, and they predict where the scale is going. You steer with the first and judge with the second.

3How it solves it a chain from fast to slow

Work backwards from the lagging result: what customer behaviour comes just before it? What does the team do that causes that behaviour? Each step is a candidate leading indicator. Keep only the ones that past data shows actually predict the next step in the chain.

  1. Start from the lagging result

    The outcome or north star: refund tickets, repeat orders, perfect orders.

  2. Work backwards

    What happens just before? (A customer is asked and answers in time.)

  3. Pick leading candidates

    Fast, frequent, and something the team's work can change.

  4. Check they predict

    Past data or a test: do days with more “asked” have fewer tickets?

  5. Set targets for both

    Leading: weekly targets. Lagging: quarterly targets.

  6. Review on two rhythms

    Leading in the weekly team review; lagging in the monthly or quarterly review.

Vocabulary for the exam

Lagging indicator
Shows the result after the fact; slow to move.
Leading indicator
Moves early and predicts the lagging one.
Predictive validity
Evidence that the leading indicator really predicts the result.
Proxy metric
A measurable stand-in for something harder to measure.
Signal delay
Time between a change and its effect showing in the data.
Balanced scorecard
A management framework mixing leading and lagging measures.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • Feedback in days, not months.
  • Steerable: the team can move leading indicators directly.
  • Honest judgement: lagging results still decide success.
  • Vanity metrics exposed when they don't predict.
  • Calmer reviews: no panic over slow numbers that haven't caught up.

What we lose

  • Checking predictions takes data work.
  • Leading indicators can be gamed (ask customers about everything).
  • More numbers to track and explain.
  • Predictions can break when the product changes.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Leading but not predictive

    App downloads go up; nothing else does. Fix → check with data that it predicts the lagging result.

  • Gaming the leading number

    Pickers ask customers about every item to hit 90 %. Fix → pair it with a counter-metric: picking time, customer annoyance.

  • Only lagging

    The team waits a month to know anything. Fix → add at least one daily or weekly leading indicator.

  • Only leading

    Leading targets met; repeat orders flat. Fix → judge success on the lagging result.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Outcomes that arrive slowly (retention, revenue, refunds).
  • Weekly team reviews that need something to steer by.
  • Setting OKRs with key results the team can move.
  • Exposing vanity metrics.

Skip it when…

  • Results that are already fast and direct.
  • When you haven't got a baseline: measure first.
  • Tiny experiments with a single, immediate measure.

8Self-check quiz

What's the difference between a leading and a lagging indicator?

Lagging shows the result after the fact; leading moves early, is controllable, and predicts the result.

Name one of Grace's leading indicators.

% of out-of-stock items where the customer was asked; % who answer within 2 minutes.

Why were app downloads dropped?

Data showed they didn't predict repeat orders.

How do you check a leading indicator is useful?

Use past data or a test to show it predicts the lagging result.

try it

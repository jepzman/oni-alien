Customer Journey Map

TL;DR

Draw every step a customer takes to get their job done, across all your teams and channels, with what they do, think and feel at each step. Add a backstage row for what your staff and systems do at the same moment. The low points on the mood line, and what was happening backstage just before them, show where the experience really breaks, usually between teams, where nobody owns it.

  • Family: understanding customers
  • Origin: service design (Lynn Shostack's service blueprint, 1984); UX practice
  • Lives in: a long sheet on the wall, then a one-page map
  • Pairs with: customer interviews, event storming, opportunity solution tree

1The situation Larder, one screen per team

At Larder every team owns a screen: the shop team the basket, payments the checkout, the store team the picker app, support the refund tool. Every screen works, and every team's dashboard is green. But Grace's interviews keep ending at the same place: the front door, where customers find out about a swap. No screen covers that moment, so no team owns it.

  • Teams optimise their own step; customers experience the whole journey.
  • The worst moments happen in the gaps between teams and channels.
  • Without the backstage view, you fix the symptom (refunds), not the cause.

2Real-life analogy the theme park

A theme-park manager can check every ride and find them all working. Then they spend a day as a guest and discover what visitors actually remember: the 40-minute queue in the sun with no sign saying how long. No ride is broken. The experience is, in between.

3How it solves it steps, mood, backstage

Map one persona doing one job (the parent, an express order). Build it with people from each team that touches the journey, using real interview stories and data. The insight is usually where a low point in the mood line meets a backstage step nobody can act on.

  1. Pick one persona and one job

    “The tonight-dinner parent, an express order.” One map per journey.

  2. List the stages

    From trigger to end, in the customer's terms, with real times from data.

  3. Add doing, thinking, feeling

    From interview quotes, not guesses. Draw the mood line.

  4. Add the backstage

    What staff and systems do at each step: picker app, support tool, dispatch.

  5. Find the moments of truth

    Low points, and the backstage step just before them.

  6. Turn them into opportunities

    Each moment of truth becomes an opportunity on the tree, with an owner.

Vocabulary for the exam

Customer journey map
The steps, thoughts and feelings of a customer doing a job.
Touchpoint
A moment the customer interacts with you: app, courier, email.
Front stage / backstage
What the customer sees / what happens behind it.
Service blueprint
A journey map with detailed backstage and support layers.
Moment of truth
A point that makes or breaks the experience.
Pain point
A step where the customer struggles or feels bad.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • The whole experience on one page, across teams.
  • Gaps between teams become visible and get owners.
  • Causes, not symptoms: the backstage row shows why.
  • Shared empathy: teams see customers' feelings at each step.
  • Opportunities for the tree, straight from the low points.

What we lose

  • Workshop time with several teams.
  • One persona, one job: you'll need more maps.
  • Can be invented if not grounded in research.
  • Goes stale as the product changes.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Mapping the happy path

    Every step goes well on paper. Fix → map a real story, including when it goes wrong.

  • Only front stage

    The map shows screens, not what staff do behind them. Fix → always add the backstage row.

  • Feelings guessed

    Smiley faces from a workshop opinion. Fix → take thoughts and feelings from interview quotes.

  • A poster, not a tool

    Framed on the wall, never used. Fix → turn each low point into an opportunity with an owner.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Problems that cross several teams or channels.
  • When every team's dashboard is green but customers are unhappy.
  • Onboarding new team members to the customer experience.
  • Before redesigning a service end to end.

Skip it when…

  • A problem inside one screen: use a usability test.
  • When you haven't talked to customers yet.
  • Tiny products with one simple step.

8Self-check quiz

What does the backstage row add?

What staff and systems do at each step, which shows the cause behind a low point.

Where was the moment of truth in Larder's express journey?

At 17:31, when the picker finds the gap and can't reach the customer: 17 minutes before the door.

Why map one persona and one job?

Different personas and jobs have different journeys; mixing them blurs the low points.

What happens to the low points?

They become opportunities on the opportunity solution tree, with owners.

try it

Map one real customer story from your last interview as a journey: stages, times, what they felt, and a backstage row. Mark the lowest point. Which team owns the step just before it, and do they know?

remember!!

Every ride works; the queue is what they remember. Look backstage for the cause.

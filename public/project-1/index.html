The Lay of the Land

Volume 1 of The Project Manager's Notebook, a plain-words walk through the whole PMBOK Guide (eighth edition). 15 notes in three collections: what a project is, the six principles, and the ways of working. Each note is one question a project manager must answer; the official PMBOK term is on the tag under the title. Every note follows Sanne at Larder, in the same months as Ana's and Grace's notebooks. Key notes go further with a going deeper chapter, and every note ends with a try it for your own project.

the story so far

January 2026. Larder has promised its investors six cities by the end of 2027. The second city, Rotterdam, must open on 27 April 2026: the store lease is signed. Ana is three months into the architecture and Grace into the product. There is a date, a building and a lot of goodwill, but no plan and nobody whose job is the launch. Sanne joins on 12 January as delivery manager. This volume follows her first seven weeks: working out what kind of thing she has been handed, what good project management rests on, and how much process Rotterdam needs.

The six principles what good project management rests on, whatever the method

  1. 05See the WholeTreat the project as one connected system, so a fix in one place doesn't break another.p. 05
  2. 06Focus on ValueSteer by the benefit the project exists for, not by the list of tasks.p. 06
  3. 07Build Quality InPut quality into how the work is done, instead of inspecting for it at the end.p. 07
  4. 08Own the OutcomeLead by taking responsibility for the result, the truth of the status and the people.p. 08
  5. 09Think Past the FinishWeigh the long-term effect on people, planet and money in every project decision.p. 09
  6. 10Let the Team DecideGive the people doing the work the room, the facts and the trust to decide.p. 10

★The nine project ratings what a good project practice is judged on

Every note scores the pattern from 1 (poor) to 5 (great) on the same nine things, comparing an app without the pattern to the same app with it.

Value focus
Keeps the project aimed at the benefit, not at the paperwork.
Predictability
Forecasts get closer to what really happens.
Risk reduction
Trouble is found while it is still cheap.
Transparency
Anyone can see where the project really is.
Stakeholder confidence
Sponsors and partners believe the updates.
Team ownership
The people doing the work own the plan.
Adaptability
Change is absorbed without chaos.
Quality built in
Problems are prevented, not inspected out at the end.
Low overhead
Time and ceremony it takes. High = light.

first law

A project is finished when the benefit shows up, not when the plan is ticked off. Use as much process as the risk needs, and no more.

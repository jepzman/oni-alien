What Must I Keep an Eye On?

TL;DR

A performance domain is an area of a project that needs looking after from start to finish. The eighth edition names seven: Governance (who decides), Scope (what), Schedule (when), Finance (how much), Stakeholders (for and with whom), Resources (people and things), Risk (what might happen). They affect each other, and none can be ignored for long. Scan all seven, every week.

1The situation Larder, 13 February 2026

Sanne's notebook is full of dates. The fridge order went out today, on time. Then Marco asks: “what has Rotterdam spent so far?” She doesn't know. Nor can she say whether the neighbours have been told about the building work, or who decides if the launch scope changes. She has been watching one dial very carefully.

  • What feels urgent gets all the attention.
  • Problems grow quietly in the areas nobody is watching.
  • A fix in one area (speed up) breaks another (cost).

2Real-life analogy the pilot's scan

A pilot is taught to scan: heading, altitude, speed, fuel, engine, weather, other aircraft, and round again. Each dial alone says little. Together they say whether the flight is safe. Pilots who stare at one dial, even the most important, are the ones who fly into the hill.

3How it solves it seven tabs, one line each, every week

Sanne sets up a binder with seven tabs and writes one line under each for Rotterdam. It takes an afternoon to fill the gaps: Marco's team gives her the spend (€61k of €380k spent or committed). Three tabs come out amber, and only one of them is the one she'd been watching. Scope: nobody has agreed what customers get at launch, so she books a session for 3 March. Stakeholders: the neighbours and the landlord haven't heard from Larder. Risk: three big ones, written down for the first time. From now on the weekly status has seven lines.

  1. Governance

    Who decides, how do we start, steer, change and close?

  2. Scope

    What will we deliver, and what not?

  3. Schedule

    By when?

  4. Finance

    For how much, and is the money there when needed?

  5. Stakeholders

    Who is affected, and what do they need from us?

  6. Resources

    Which people and things, and how is the team led?

  7. Risk

    What could go wrong, or better than planned?

Vocabulary for the exam

Performance domain
A group of related activities that matter for delivering the project's results.
Governance
The way decisions are made, checked and escalated.
Scope
The work and results the project will deliver.
Stakeholder
Anyone who affects the project or is affected by it.
Resources
The people, equipment, materials and places a project needs.
Risk
An uncertain event that would matter if it happened, for better or worse.
Knowledge area
The sixth edition's grouping; ten of them.

tools: a binder with seven tabs, or seven lines in a document. The discipline is the weekly scan.

4Light, standard, heavy how much of this do you need?

Light

a small, short project

  • Seven questions in your head.
  • A line each when something changes.
  • Five minutes a week.

Standard

Rotterdam

  • Seven lines in the weekly status.
  • A colour and a next step for each.
  • The pulls between them noted.

Heavy

large or contracted work

  • A management plan per domain.
  • Measures and thresholds for each.
  • Domain leads reporting to the project manager.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Staring at one dial

    Usually the schedule. Fix → scan all seven.

  • Seven silos

    Each domain managed alone. Fix → note where one pulls another.

  • Seven heavy plans

    For a fifteen-week project. Fix → one line each; tailor.

  • Green by default

    Nobody looked. Fix → a line of evidence, or it's unknown.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Every week, on every project.
  • When you inherit a project: where are the blanks?
  • Building a status report.
  • Finding your way around the PMBOK Guide.

Skip it when…

  • Never; shrink it to seven questions.

8Self-check quiz

Name the seven domains.

Governance, scope, schedule, finance, stakeholders, resources, risk.

Which domains answer “what”, “when” and “how much”?

Scope, schedule and finance.

How many knowledge areas did the sixth edition have?

Ten.

The builder offers to finish a week early for €9,000 of overtime. Which tabs does Sanne look at?

Schedule (a week gained), finance (reserve is €6k: not enough), risk (does a week of float reduce the top risks?), governance (over €5,000: Joost decides).

A tab is green because nobody has checked it. What is its real status?

Unknown. Sanne writes “not checked” and checks it.

try it

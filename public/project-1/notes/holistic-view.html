principle 1 of 6 · a.k.a. “adopt a holistic view”

See the Whole

TL;DR

A project is not a list of separate tasks. It is one connected system, sitting inside a bigger one (the company, the city, the market). Change one part and others move. A project manager's first habit is to look at the whole before fixing a part: draw the pieces, draw the links, and ask of every change, “what else does this touch?”

  • Family: the six principles
  • Lives in: one map on the wall, and a question at every change
  • Pairs with: the project environment, dependencies, the system context

1The situation Larder, 23 January 2026

Tomás has an idea for Rotterdam: open from 07:00 to 23:00, not 08:00 to 22:00 like Amsterdam. Joost likes it. It looks like one setting. Sanne asks who else it touches, and the list grows: delivery slots in the app, courier contracts, picker shifts, a noise clause in the lease, and the adverts already at the printer.

  • Each team sees its own tasks, not the links between them.
  • A good local decision can cost another team two weeks.
  • Some things sit between teams, so nobody owns them.

2Real-life analogy a mobile over a child's bed

A mobile is shapes on strings, balanced on rods. Pull one shape and the whole thing tilts. Someone who repairs mobiles never looks at one shape alone: they hold it up, watch all of it, and then touch one piece. A project behaves the same way, only the strings are harder to see.

3How it solves it draw the whole, then ask “what else does this touch?”

Sanne draws the launch as seven workstreams around one date, with a line wherever one depends on another. Then she walks Tomás's idea around the ring. Slots: a day's work for Ben. Courier contracts: Lena must check. The lease: no deliveries before 07:30. Adverts: already say 08:00. Decision, the same afternoon: open 08:00 to 22:00 at launch, review after hypercare. The drawing also shows two things outside every workstream: the food-safety registration and charging for forty e-bikes. Both get an owner that day.

  1. List the workstreams

    Five to nine, not fifty.

  2. Draw a line for every dependency

    Who waits for whom?

  3. Look for orphans

    Work that belongs to no workstream.

  4. Draw what the project sits inside

    Company, customers, rules.

  5. For every change, walk it round the map

    “What else does this touch?”

  6. Keep the map where people can see it

    Update it when links change.

Vocabulary for the exam

Holistic view
Looking at the project as a whole and at how its parts affect each other.
System
Parts that work together to produce a result none could produce alone.
Systems thinking
Understanding causes and effects across the whole, including delays.
Dependency
One piece of work that needs another to start or finish.
Workstream
A group of related work, usually with one lead.
Integration
Making the parts of a project work as one.

tools: a whiteboard, sticky notes, and the question “what else does this touch?”.

4Light, standard, heavy how much of this do you need?

Light

one team, one deliverable

  • Ask “what else does this touch?”
  • A quick sketch.
  • No upkeep.

Standard

Rotterdam: seven workstreams

  • A one-page map with leads.
  • Every change walked round it.
  • Updated monthly.

Heavy

many teams, vendors, countries

  • A dependency register.
  • Interface agreements between teams.
  • An integration lead.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Optimising one part

    A team's best choice hurts the whole. Fix → walk the change round the map.

  • A map with fifty boxes

    Nobody reads it. Fix → five to nine workstreams.

  • Drawn once

    Links change. Fix → update it monthly.

  • Only the inside

    Forgetting the lease, the neighbours, the market. Fix → draw what the project sits in.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • More than one team is involved.
  • A “small” change is proposed.
  • Things fall between teams.
  • At the start of every project.

Skip it when…

  • Never. Scale it down to one question.

8Self-check quiz

What question does a holistic view add to every change?

“What else does this touch?”

What is an orphan?

Work that belongs to no workstream, so nobody owns it.

Priya wants to move the launch adverts a week earlier. What does Sanne do first?

Walk it round the map: adverts depend on the app showing Rotterdam and on the final hours and zones. Then decide.

How many workstreams on the map?

Five to nine. Seven for Rotterdam.

What did older editions call making the parts work as one?

Integration management.

try it

Draw your project as five to nine workstreams around its goal. Add a line for each dependency. Circle anything that belongs to nobody. Then take the last change someone proposed and walk it round the map.

remember!!

Hold up the whole mobile before you touch one piece.

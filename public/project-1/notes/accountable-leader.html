principle 4 of 6 · a.k.a. “be an accountable leader”

Own the Outcome

TL;DR

A project manager answers for the outcome, without doing all the work and without owning all the decisions. That means three habits: tell the truth about where the project is, early and plainly; make commitments and keep them, or say first when you can't; and look after the people and the organisation's trust. When it goes well, it was the team. When it goes badly, say “mine”.

1The situation Larder, 30 January 2026

The first Rotterdam status meeting, with Femke, Marco, Priya and Joost. Twelve and a half weeks to go. Before the meeting Joost says, kindly, “let's show green: the team is working flat out.” Sanne has no agreed plan, three open questions and a fridge order that must go out by mid-February. Green would be comfortable. It would not be true, and in April it would be her green.

  • Status is treated as a mood, not a measurement.
  • Bad news travels late, when choices are gone.
  • Nobody has yet said “this is mine”.

2Real-life analogy the ship's captain

A captain doesn't row, cook or load the cargo. But the captain answers for the ship. When the weather turns, the owner hears it from the captain, at once, with what they'll do about it. When the cook burns dinner, the captain doesn't say “the cook's fault” to the owner. And a captain who wears out the crew to make one port on time has no crew for the next.

3How it solves it an honest status, a commitment, a request

Sanne reports amber and gives three reasons. Then two lists. I commit to: a first plan by 20 February, the fridge order by 13 February, each open question with a named owner and a date. I need from you: a decision on opening hours by Monday, and thirty minutes a week with Joost. She signs it. Femke's only question: “when will we know if it turns red?” Marco, afterwards: “first status report here I've believed.”

  1. Report what is true

    With reasons, not adjectives.

  2. Say what you commit to

    Few things, with dates.

  3. Say what you need

    From named people.

  4. Put your name on it

    And keep the promise.

  5. If you'll miss a promise, say so first

    Before the date, not after.

  6. Share credit, keep blame

    And protect the team's time.

Vocabulary for the exam

Accountability
Answering for the result, whoever did the work.
Stewardship
Looking after what you've been trusted with: money, people, reputation.
Integrity
Doing what you said, and saying what is true.
Commitment
A promise with a date and a name.
Transparency
Letting people see the real state of things.
Servant leadership
Leading by removing what stops the team.

tools: one page a week, a short list of promises, and your own name at the bottom.

4Light, standard, heavy how much of this do you need?

Light

a small team that talks daily

  • Say the true status out loud.
  • One promise at a time.
  • Own misses the same day.

Standard

Rotterdam

  • A weekly one-page status.
  • Commitments and needs listed.
  • “Turns red if…” written down.

Heavy

public money, contracts, regulators

  • Formal reporting and audit trail.
  • A code of conduct.
  • Independent assurance.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Watermelon status

    Green outside, red inside. Fix → reasons, not colours, come first.

  • Blame

    “The builder let us down.” Fix → say “mine”, then fix it.

  • Hero mode

    The project manager does everything. Fix → answer for it; let the team do it.

  • Promises without dates

    “Soon.” Fix → a date and a name, or it isn't a promise.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Every status, every week.
  • When the news is bad.
  • When someone asks you to soften a report.
  • When you inherit a project.

Skip it when…

  • Never.

8Self-check quiz

Why not report green?

It wasn't true: no plan, three open questions, a long-lead order not placed.

What three things did the status contain besides the colour?

Why, what Sanne commits to, what she needs.

What is a watermelon status?

Green on the outside, red inside.

On 12 February Sanne sees the plan won't be ready on the 20th. What does she do?

Tell Joost that day, with the new date and the reason. A missed promise is announced before its date.

The builder is a week late. What does Sanne say to the steering group?

“We are a week late on the fit-out; here is what I'm doing.” Not “the builder's fault”.

What Is My Job Here?

TL;DR

The project manager leads the team that delivers the project: one plan, a true picture of where things stand, risks and dependencies seen early, and a clean handover. The sponsor owns the reason for the project: the money, the big decisions, and clearing what the project manager can't. Neither decides what the product is or how it is built. Write the split down in week one.

1The situation Larder, 19 January 2026

Larder has never had a project manager. In her second week Sanne hears four versions of her job: Joost wants a task-chaser, Femke wants a date for the investors, Ben wants someone to keep meetings away from his team, and Grace asks, carefully, whether Sanne will now decide what the app does in Rotterdam.

  • If she chases tasks only, nobody owns the plan.
  • If she decides scope, she steps on Grace and Ana.
  • Nobody has said who the sponsor is.

2Real-life analogy the film set

On a film set, the producer finds the money and wants a film that earns it back. The director decides what the film is. The first assistant director runs the schedule and the set: who is needed when, what today costs, what is slipping. They tell the producer the truth every evening. They don't rewrite the script, and nobody thinks less of them for it.

3How it solves it a role card, agreed with the sponsor

Sanne writes a role card and takes it to Joost. She is accountable for one plan, the true status, risks and dependencies and the handover. Joost agrees to be the sponsor: the business case, the money (with Marco), the big decisions, the blockers. A third column lists what is not hers, with names. Three triggers send a decision up to Joost. Grace reads it and relaxes; Ben asks for a copy.

  1. Ask each key person what they expect of you

    Write their words down.

  2. Name the sponsor

    One person, with the authority to decide and pay.

  3. List what you are accountable for

    Plan, status, risks, dependencies, handover.

  4. List what you are not, with names

    Product, design, operations.

  5. Agree what goes up to the sponsor

    A few clear triggers.

  6. Share the card

    With everyone who gave you an expectation.

Vocabulary for the exam

Project manager
The person who leads the project team to deliver the project's results.
Sponsor
The person who provides money and support, and answers for the project's success in the business.
Accountable
Answers for the result. One person.
Responsible
Does the work. Can be several people.
Escalate
Pass a decision up because it is above your authority.
Steering group
The sponsor and a few leaders who decide what the project manager can't.

4Light, standard, heavy how much of this do you need?

Light

a team that knows each other

  • A five-minute talk with the sponsor.
  • Three triggers agreed.
  • A note in chat.

Standard

Rotterdam

  • A one-page role card.
  • Signed off by the sponsor.
  • Shared with the team.

Heavy

many teams, vendors, a contract

  • Roles in the charter.
  • A responsibility matrix (RACI).
  • A steering group with terms of reference.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • The task-chaser

    The project manager only asks “is it done?”. Fix → own the plan and the risks, not the nagging.

  • The mini-CEO

    Deciding scope and design. Fix → write “not mine”, with names.

  • No sponsor

    Decisions wait for nobody. Fix → one named person.

  • A sponsor in name only

    Never available. Fix → agree a weekly 20 minutes.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Your first weeks on any project.
  • A company's first project manager.
  • When decisions bounce between people.
  • When the sponsor is unclear.

Skip it when…

  • The roles are already written in a charter that people follow.

8Self-check quiz

Who owns the business case?

The sponsor.

Who decides what the app does in Rotterdam?

Grace. Sanne makes the effect on the date visible.

Ben says a change will cost four extra days. What does Sanne do?

It is over the three-day trigger: she takes it to Joost with options, and doesn't decide it herself.

Accountable or responsible: which can only be one person?

Accountable.

Why write “not Sanne's”?

So others keep their decisions, and nothing falls between two people.

try it

Write your own role card: four things you answer for, three things you don't (with names), and three triggers that go to your sponsor. Then check: does your sponsor know they are the sponsor?

remember!!

Run the set. Don't rewrite the script.

Focus on Value

TL;DR

A project exists to produce a benefit. Tasks, documents and even the deadline are only means. So steer by value: say what the benefit is, test every piece of work against it, and keep asking through the project whether it is still worth doing. “Done” means the benefit is on its way, not that the list is ticked.

1The situation Larder, 26 January 2026

Femke stops Sanne in the corridor: “Are we on track for 27 April?” Sanne has Joost's spreadsheet: 38 of 140 rows done. Is 27 % good? Nobody knows whether those 38 were the ones that matter. And “on track” for what: a store with its doors open, or a city that orders?

  • Progress is counted in tasks, not in value.
  • Every row looks equally important.
  • The date has become the goal.

2Real-life analogy packing for a hike

Two people pack for a mountain hut. One works down a long list and can hardly lift the bag. The other asks of every item: “does this get us to the hut safely by dark?” Water, a map, a coat: yes. A third pair of shoes: no. The second hiker isn't lazy. They know what the walk is for.

3How it solves it one question, asked of every row

Sanne and Joost go through all 140 rows in ninety minutes with one question: what happens to the benefit if we drop this? 71 rows are needed to open safely on 27 April. 47 help the benefit and are put in order. 22 have no link to it and are parked: tote bags, an animation in the app, a second coffee machine. From now on her status report opens with the benefit, and progress is counted against the 71.

  1. Write the benefit where everyone sees it

    A number and a date.

  2. Test each piece of work against it

    “What if we drop this?”

  3. Sort: must have, helps, no link

    Park the last group.

  4. Count progress against what matters

    Not against the whole list.

  5. Start every status with the benefit

    Then the date.

  6. Ask “still worth it?” at every review

    And act on the answer.

Vocabulary for the exam

Value
The worth of what the project brings, compared with what it costs.
Benefit
A measurable gain for the business or its customers.
Business case
The reasoning for doing the project: benefit, cost, risk.
Scope
The work the project will do. Less is often worth more.
Gold plating
Adding extras nobody asked for.
Value-based progress
Measuring how much of the benefit is secured, not how many tasks are done.

4Light, standard, heavy how much of this do you need?

Light

a short piece of work

  • One sentence of benefit.
  • Ask the question when adding work.
  • No sorting session.

Standard

Rotterdam

  • The benefit on every plan and status.
  • Each row sorted once.
  • Progress against must-haves.

Heavy

a large investment

  • A business case kept up to date.
  • Value reviewed at each gate.
  • Benefits tracked after closing.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • The date becomes the goal

    Opening on time with nothing on the shelves. Fix → state the benefit before the date.

  • Everything is a must

    The question is answered “disaster” for every row. Fix → ask for the evidence.

  • Gold plating

    Extras creep back in. Fix → parked means parked until the benefit owner asks.

  • Value checked once

    The world changes. Fix → ask at every review.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Always.
  • When the list is longer than the time.
  • When progress is reported as a percentage of tasks.
  • Before cutting or adding scope.

Skip it when…

  • Never.

8Self-check quiz

What is the one question?

“What happens to the benefit if we drop this?”

Is 38 of 140 rows useful progress information?

No: it doesn't say whether the important rows are done.

What is gold plating?

Adding extras nobody asked for.

Marketing asks to bring the tote bags back, at no cost to the date. What does Sanne ask?

What they do for the benefit, and who owns that claim. If nobody can say, they stay parked.

The store can open on 27 April with half the shelves empty. On track?

On time, but not on value. Sanne reports it that way.

try it

Write your project's benefit on a card. Take the ten biggest items on your list and ask of each: what happens to the benefit if we drop this? Park one.

remember!!

Pack for the hut, not for the list.

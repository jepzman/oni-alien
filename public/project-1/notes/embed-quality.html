Build Quality In

TL;DR

Quality is not a check at the end. It is built into each step: a clear definition of “done”, a check made by the person doing the work, and a way to see problems while they are small. Inspection at the end still has a place, as a confirmation. The earlier a fault is found, the cheaper it is, so design the work so faults can't travel far.

  • Family: the six principles
  • Lives in: a “done means” line on every step
  • Pairs with: definition of done, the walking skeleton, the launch checklist

1The situation Larder, 28 January 2026

Sanne asks Tomás how Zuid's opening went in November 2024. He laughs, not happily. 340 products had no price. Support heard it from customers. A push message went to the wrong part of town. Everything had been checked: in one long inspection the evening before, when nobody had the hours to fix what it found.

  • Checks came at the end, after the faults had piled up.
  • “Done” meant “I've finished my part”, not “it works”.
  • The people who made a fault never saw it.

2Real-life analogy the baker

One baker tastes the cake when it comes out of the oven. If the salt and sugar were swapped, the cake goes in the bin. Another weighs the flour, checks the oven, tastes the batter. A mistake is caught at its own step, when fixing it costs a spoonful. Both bakers care about quality. Only one of them has built it in.

3How it solves it a “done means” and a check, inside every step

Sanne, Tomás, Eli and Ben agree four habits. Platform set-up: a script runs every night and lists any product without a price or VAT rate. Stocking: the label is scanned as the product goes on the shelf. People: every new picker does twenty practice orders at Noord before their first shift. End to end: one order a week runs through the test store RTM-0, which Ana's team has had working since 15 January. The rehearsal week in April stays, as a confirmation.

  1. Write “done means…” for each step

    Something you can check.

  2. Put the check inside the step

    Done by the person doing the work.

  3. Automate the checks you can

    A nightly script beats a monthly audit.

  4. Run the whole thing early and often

    Small, end to end.

  5. Count faults by where they were made

    Fix the step, not only the fault.

  6. Keep the final inspection

    As a confirmation.

Vocabulary for the exam

Quality
How well the result meets what was needed.
Quality assurance
Making sure the way of working will produce good results.
Quality control
Checking the results themselves.
Definition of done
What must be true before a piece of work counts as finished.
Prevention over inspection
Stopping faults from being made, not finding them later.
Cost of quality
What you spend preventing and checking, plus what faults cost when they escape.

4Light, standard, heavy how much of this do you need?

Light

a small change, low stakes

  • A “done means” sentence.
  • A second pair of eyes.
  • Fix as you go.

Standard

Rotterdam

  • A check inside each step.
  • A weekly end-to-end run.
  • A final rehearsal to confirm.

Heavy

safety, money or law at stake

  • A quality plan with standards.
  • Independent audits.
  • Measured fault rates and formal sign-off.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Quality as a phase

    “Testing” in the last week. Fix → a check in every step.

  • Checker ≠ maker

    The maker never learns. Fix → the maker checks first.

  • Vague “done”

    “Mostly set up.” Fix → a “done means” you can check.

  • Dropping the final check

    Over-trusting the steps. Fix → keep it, as a confirmation.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Always.
  • After a launch that went wrong.
  • When faults are found by customers.
  • When “done” starts arguments.

Skip it when…

  • Never. For throwaway experiments, keep only “done means”.

8Self-check quiz

What went wrong at Zuid?

One inspection at the end: 340 products had no price on opening day.

Assurance or control: “the nightly script exists and runs”?

Assurance: it is about the way of working. The list it produces is control.

Who checks first?

The person doing the work.

Three weeks before launch the nightly script lists 60 products without VAT rates. Good news or bad?

Good: found with three weeks to spare, at the step that made them. Fix them and ask why that step let them through.

Is the rehearsal week still needed?

Yes, as a confirmation, not as the first test.

try it

Pick one step in your project. Write “done means…” for it in one line that someone else could check. Who makes that check today, and when? Move it one step earlier.

remember!!

Taste the batter. Don't wait for the cake.

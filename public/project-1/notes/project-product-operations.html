Is This a Project?

TL;DR

A project is temporary work that creates something new: it has a start, an end, and a result that didn't exist before. A product lives on and keeps changing for as long as customers use it. Operations is the same work, repeated, that keeps the business running. They need different management. Before you plan anything, sort the pile.

1The situation Larder, 14 January 2026

Sanne joined Larder on Monday 12 January 2026 as delivery manager. By Wednesday eleven people have asked her to “project-manage” something: the Rotterdam launch, substitution approvals, the picker rota at Noord, the broken cold room at Zuid. If everything is a project, she can't run any of them well.

  • Everything gets a deadline, even work that never ends.
  • Nobody knows who owns the result once it is “done”.
  • The one real deadline, Rotterdam on 27 April, hides in the pile.

2Real-life analogy a restaurant

A restaurant owner does three kinds of work. Opening a second restaurant starts, ends, and produces something new. The menu is never finished: it changes with the seasons and the guests. Tonight's dinner service is the same work every evening, done well. Nobody would plan tonight's service with a building schedule, or the new building with a rota.

3How it solves it three questions, then three piles

Sanne asks three questions of each item. Does it end? Is the result new? Who owns it afterwards? Four items are projects: Rotterdam, the Zuid cold-room replacement, the new helpdesk tool and the summer courier hiring drive. Three are products, and they already have owners: Grace and Dee. Four are operations, which Joost and Tomás run every day. She keeps the four projects, and Rotterdam goes to the top: it is the only one with a signed lease.

  1. List everything you've been asked to manage

    One line each, no judging yet.

  2. Ask: does it end?

    A real end, not a wish.

  3. Ask: is the result new?

    Or is it the same result, again?

  4. Ask: who owns it afterwards?

    Every project hands over to someone.

  5. Sort into three piles

    Projects, products, operations.

  6. Give each pile back to its owner

    Keep the projects. Rank them.

Vocabulary for the exam

Project
Temporary work that creates a new product, service or result.
Temporary
It has a start and an end. It doesn't mean short.
Product
Something that gives value for as long as it lives, and keeps changing.
Operations
Ongoing, repeated work that keeps the business running.
Deliverable
A thing the project hands over: a store, an app release, a trained team.
Handover
The moment a project's result becomes someone's daily work.

4Light, standard, heavy how much of this do you need?

Light

a small team, a few requests

  • Ask the three questions out loud.
  • Say whose it is.
  • No document.

Standard

Rotterdam: one person, eleven requests

  • A one-page sorted list.
  • Agreed with your manager.
  • Reviewed when new work arrives.

Heavy

a company with many projects

  • Written rules for what counts as a project.
  • An intake form.
  • A portfolio board decides.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Everything is a project

    Work that never ends gets a fake deadline. Fix → ask “does it end?” first.

  • Nothing is a project

    A launch run as daily work, with nobody watching the date. Fix → one date, one owner, one plan.

  • Temporary means short

    A project can take years. Fix → temporary means it ends.

  • No owner afterwards

    The project “finishes” and the result drifts. Fix → name the receiver on day one.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • You're new and handed a pile.
  • A team says “we have too many projects”.
  • Nobody can say who owns a result.
  • A deadline is put on never-ending work.

Skip it when…

  • The work is clearly one project already.
  • A product team with a clear owner and backlog.

8Self-check quiz

What makes work a project?

It is temporary (it ends) and it creates something new.

Does temporary mean short?

No. It means there is an end.

Joost asks Sanne to “project-manage” the weekly stock counts. What does she do first?

Ask whether it ends. It doesn't: it's operations, and Tomás owns it. She can help improve it once, as a small project, but not run it.

What happens to Rotterdam on 27 April?

The project hands over; running the store becomes operations.

Who looks after substitution approvals?

Grace: it is a product, it lives on.

try it

List everything you are managing this month. For each line answer: does it end, is the result new, who owns it afterwards? Mark each P, Pr or O. How many real projects are left?

remember!!

A project ends and leaves something new behind. Sort the pile before you plan it.

Plan It All, or Learn As We Go?

TL;DR

There are two basic ways to run work. Predictive: plan it all, then do it; best when you know what you want and change is expensive. Adaptive (agile): do a little, look, adjust; best when you'll learn as you go and change is cheap. Most projects are hybrid: each part uses the way that fits it. The choice is not about fashion. It follows from how certain the work is and what a change costs.

1The situation Larder, 9 February 2026

Two conversations on one Monday. The builder won't start the Rotterdam fit-out until the drawings, the permit and the price are fixed: every later change costs money and days. Ben won't promise a list of app features for April: his team works in two-week sprints and shows what works at the end of each. Sanne's old job was all builder. Larder's habit is all Ben. The launch needs both, and one plan.

  • One method for everything fits half the work badly.
  • A fixed plan for uncertain work is fiction, like the 2025 Gantt chart.
  • “We're agile” doesn't order a cold room on time.

2Real-life analogy a package tour or a road trip

A package tour is booked months ahead: cheap, certain, and hard to change. A road trip has a direction; each day is decided on the way. Most holidays are a mix: book the flights, because they sell out and changes are expensive, and leave the days open, because you'll know more when you're there.

3How it solves it choose per workstream, tie together with milestones

Sanne stops asking “are we agile or waterfall?” and asks it per workstream. The store fit-out, people and stock are predictive: a known sequence, long lead times, contracts. The platform set-up and apps and marketing are adaptive: two-week sprints, a demo, the list re-ordered each time. Five shared milestones tie them together, so the adaptive teams know which dates are not theirs to move: fit-out complete 3 April, stock on shelves 17 April, app ready 20 April, go / no-go 24 April, opening 27 April.

  1. Split the project into workstreams

    Use your whole-project map.

  2. Ask how certain each one is

    Do we know what we need?

  3. Ask what a change costs

    Days and money.

  4. Choose predictive, adaptive or a mix for each

    Say why.

  5. Set shared milestones

    The few dates all workstreams meet.

  6. Review the choice when the work changes

    It isn't forever.

＋Going deeper choosing an approach, and making a hybrid hold together

The note says “choose per workstream”. This chapter shows how to choose without arguing about taste, what a hybrid needs in order not to fall apart, and the four classic life cycles you will meet in other books and exam questions.

1Five questions that choose for you

For each workstream, answer five questions. How clear is what we need? How expensive is a change later? Can it be delivered in small pieces? How often can the customer look and react? How strict are the rules and contracts?

Answers on the left (clear, expensive, no, rarely, strict) point to predictive. Answers on the right point to adaptive. A split result is a hybrid inside the workstream: plan the fixed parts, iterate the rest.

2Two workstreams, scored

The fit-out: the drawings are agreed; moving a cold room after it is built costs weeks; a store can't open half-fitted; the “customer” sees it once, at handover; permits and a contract apply. Five on the left.

The apps: nobody yet knows exactly how Saturday slots should behave in Rotterdam; a screen costs hours to change; each sprint can ship something; Joost and Tomás can react every fortnight; the rules are light. Five on the right. Sanne didn't have to persuade the builder to be agile or Ben to draw a Gantt chart.

3What a hybrid needs to hold together

A hybrid fails at the joins. Three things hold it together:

  • Shared milestones. A few dates where workstreams meet. Adaptive teams choose what is in each sprint, but “app ready on 20 April” is fixed, so they manage scope to hit it.
  • One status, two measures. Predictive work reports against its plan (is the fit-out on its dates?). Adaptive work reports what is working (which must-haves are demonstrated?). Sanne's weekly page shows both, side by side.
  • A rule for change at the boundary. A change inside a sprint is the team's business. A change that moves a shared milestone goes to Sanne, and over three days to Joost.

4The four life cycles, in one paragraph each

  • Predictive: one pass through requirements, design, build, test, deliver. One delivery at the end.
  • Iterative: the same thing built several times, better each time, from rough to finished. One delivery, well tested. Good when the idea needs refining (a prototype).
  • Incremental: the thing delivered in pieces, each usable. Several deliveries. Good when early pieces bring early value.
  • Adaptive (agile): iterative and incremental together, in short fixed cycles, with the customer looking at every one.

A phase is a stretch of the project that ends with something agreed (design approved, store handed over). Any of the four can be used inside a phase. Vol. 2 covers the gates between phases.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • One true method

    “We're agile”, so nobody orders the cold room. Fix → choose per workstream.

  • Hybrid as an excuse

    No plan and no demos. Fix → each workstream names its approach and rhythm.

  • No shared milestones

    Workstreams drift apart. Fix → a few fixed meeting points.

  • Choosing by taste

    The loudest person's favourite. Fix → the five questions.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • At the start, before the plan.
  • When teams argue about method.
  • When a project mixes building things and writing software.
  • When the work changes character.

Skip it when…

  • The organisation has one approach that clearly fits all the work.

8Self-check quiz

When does predictive fit?

When needs are clear and change is expensive.

When does adaptive fit?

When you'll learn as you go and change is cheap.

What ties a hybrid together?

Shared milestones, one status, and a rule for changes at the boundary.

Ben's team wants to move “app ready” from 20 to 23 April to finish a nicer slot picker. Who decides?

Not the team alone: it's a shared milestone. It goes to Sanne; she looks at what it does to go / no-go on the 24th, and the nicer picker most likely waits.

Iterative or incremental: “deliver slots first, tracking second”?

Incremental: usable pieces, one after another.

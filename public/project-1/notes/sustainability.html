principle 5 of 6 · a.k.a. “integrate sustainability within all project areas”

Think Past the Finish

TL;DR

A project is short. What it leaves behind lasts. Sustainability means weighing the long-term effect of project decisions on people (staff, neighbours, customers), the planet (energy, waste, transport) and money (running costs, not just the project budget). It is not a separate workstream or a report at the end. It is three questions asked inside ordinary decisions.

  • Family: the six principles
  • Lives in: three questions on every big decision
  • Pairs with: focus on value, total cost of ownership, the decision log

1The situation Larder, 2 February 2026

The fit-out quote arrives with a choice. Open fridges are €14,000 cheaper. Fridges with doors use about €6,000 a year less electricity. Sanne's budget only feels the first number, and it is tight. The store will pay the second number every year for ten years, long after the project has closed and Sanne has moved on to Utrecht.

  • A project is judged on its own budget and date.
  • Running costs, staff and neighbours aren't in the plan.
  • “We'll fix it after launch” rarely happens.

2Real-life analogy planting a tree

When a family plants a tree, the planting takes an afternoon. The tree stays for fifty years. So they think about where the shade will fall, whether the roots will reach the neighbour's wall, who will clear the leaves, and what the fruit is worth. A tree planted for this summer's photo is in the wrong place by the time it has grown.

3How it solves it three questions, inside the decision

Sanne adds three questions to any decision over €5,000 or with a life longer than the project: people? planet? money over five years? That Monday three decisions get them. Fridges: doors, €14k more, paid back in about two and a half years; Marco agrees to move the money. First deliveries: 07:30, not 07:00, for the street. Unsold food: a daily pick-up by a Rotterdam food bank, arranged before opening instead of “later”. Each choice is written in the decision log with what it costs the project now.

  1. Pick the decisions that outlive the project

    Buildings, equipment, contracts, data.

  2. Ask: who is affected, and how?

    Staff, neighbours, customers.

  3. Ask: what does it use and waste?

    Energy, materials, transport.

  4. Ask: what does it cost over five years?

    Not just to buy.

  5. Decide with the person who'll live with it

    The store manager, the finance lead.

  6. Write down the trade-off

    What it costs the project now, and why.

Vocabulary for the exam

Sustainability
Meeting today's needs without harming the ability to meet tomorrow's.
Triple bottom line
Judging results on people, planet and profit together.
Total cost of ownership
What something costs to buy, run, maintain and remove.
Payback period
How long until the savings cover the extra cost.
Social impact
The effect on staff, neighbours and communities.
Life-cycle thinking
Looking at a thing from making to disposal.

4Light, standard, heavy how much of this do you need?

Light

a small internal change

  • Ask “who lives with this afterwards?”
  • A sentence in the decision.
  • No sums.

Standard

Rotterdam

  • Three questions on decisions over €5k.
  • A five-year cost line.
  • Recorded in the decision log.

Heavy

construction, public works, big supply chains

  • An impact assessment.
  • Sustainability targets and reporting.
  • Supplier standards in contracts.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • A sustainability section at the end

    Written after everything is decided. Fix → ask inside each decision.

  • Only “planet”

    Forgetting staff and running costs. Fix → three questions.

  • The project's budget wins every time

    Cheap now, dear for ten years. Fix → show the five-year number to the sponsor.

  • Good intentions, no owner

    “We'll donate unsold food.” Fix → a name and a start date.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Buildings, equipment, long contracts.
  • Decisions that affect staff or neighbours.
  • When “cheapest” is the only argument.
  • Before closing: what are we leaving behind?

Skip it when…

  • Short-lived choices that end with the project.

8Self-check quiz

What are the three questions?

People, planet, and money over five years.

Why did doors win on the fridges?

€14k more to buy, about €6k a year less to run: paid back in about two and a half years.

Is sustainability a separate workstream?

No. It is asked inside ordinary decisions.

The reserve is now €6k and the builder offers cheaper lighting that uses twice the power. What does Sanne do?

Run the same three questions with the five-year cost, and take both numbers to Joost. The project budget alone doesn't decide.

Who should be in the room?

The person who will live with the result: here, the store manager and finance.

try it

Find one decision in your project that will outlive it. Ask the three questions. Write the five-year cost next to the purchase price. Does the choice still look right?

remember!!

The planting takes an afternoon. The tree stays fifty years.

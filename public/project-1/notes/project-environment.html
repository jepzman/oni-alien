What Was Here Before Me?

TL;DR

No project starts on empty ground. Around it are things inside the organisation (its structure, habits, tools, people, what earlier projects left behind) and things outside (laws, contracts, the market, suppliers). Some are limits, some are help. A plan that ignores them is a plan for a different company. Scan the surroundings first, on one page.

  • Family: foundations
  • Lives in: a four-box page, redone each quarter
  • Pairs with: constraints and assumptions, the stakeholder map, tailoring

1The situation Larder, 21 January 2026

Sanne's last job was fitting out warehouses, where a plan was a contract. Her first Rotterdam draft looks like one. Then she shows it around. Marco: no more than four new hires this year. Ana: StockPilot can't be replaced before June 2027. Tomás: has anyone registered the new store with the food-safety authority? And Ben shows her the 2025 Gantt chart: 14 features, 9 late, which is why his team winces at the word “plan”.

  • The plan assumed freedoms Larder doesn't have.
  • It ignored things that could help: Zuid's lessons, the test store.
  • It used a style of planning the team no longer trusts.

2Real-life analogy walking the garden

A gardener who moves house doesn't plant on the first day. She walks the garden: the soil, where the sun falls, the old trees that will stay whatever she wants, the rule about hedge height, and the shed with the last owner's tools. Half of what she finds are limits. The other half she'd have paid for.

3How it solves it four boxes on one page

Sanne spends two days asking, then fills four boxes. Inside, limits: what Larder won't change for her. Inside, helps: what already exists and can be used. Outside, must follow: laws and contracts. Outside, watch: suppliers, rivals, lead times. Each line gets a name: who knows most about it. The fridge line (eight weeks to deliver) moves straight to the top of her plan.

  1. Ask five people “what should I know that isn't written down?”

    Different departments.

  2. List limits inside the organisation

    Budget rules, hiring, tools, structure.

  3. List what you can reuse

    Lessons, templates, people who did it before.

  4. List rules and contracts outside

    Laws, permits, leases, suppliers' terms.

  5. List outside things to watch

    Market, vendors, lead times.

  6. Put a name on every line, and redo it each quarter

    Surroundings change.

Vocabulary for the exam

Project environment
Everything around the project that affects it, inside and outside the organisation.
Internal environment
The organisation's own structure, culture, rules, tools and people.
External environment
Laws, markets, suppliers, society: outside the organisation's control.
Constraint
A limit the project must work within.
Organisational knowledge
Lessons, templates and records from earlier work.
Governance
How decisions are made and checked in the organisation.

4Light, standard, heavy how much of this do you need?

Light

a familiar team, a familiar kind of work

  • Ask “what's changed since last time?”
  • A short list.
  • No page.

Standard

Rotterdam: a new person, a new city

  • Five conversations.
  • The four-box page.
  • Names and a review date.

Heavy

a new country, a regulated industry

  • Legal and compliance review.
  • A market study.
  • A formal constraints and assumptions log.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Planning like your last company

    The old habits don't fit. Fix → scan before you plan.

  • Only listing limits

    Missing the help. Fix → ask what can be reused.

  • A scan done once

    The world moves. Fix → redo it each quarter.

  • No names

    Nobody to ask when a line changes. Fix → one name per line.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • You're new to the organisation.
  • A new city, country or industry.
  • Before the first real plan.
  • When a plan keeps hitting surprises.

Skip it when…

  • The same team repeats the same kind of project, and nothing around it changed.

8Self-check quiz

Name one internal and one external influence on Rotterdam.

Internal: the hiring limit of four. External: the lease date.

What did older editions call these?

Enterprise environmental factors and organisational process assets.

Why list what helps?

To reuse it: lessons, the test store, a training plan.

Sanne learns that fridges take eight weeks to arrive. What does she do with that?

Puts it in the scan with an owner, and moves the order to the top of the plan: 27 April minus eight weeks is 2 March, and they still have to be fitted, so the order goes out by mid-February.

How often is the scan redone?

Each quarter, or when something big changes.

try it

Ask three colleagues: “what should I know about how things work here that isn't written down?” Sort the answers into the four boxes. Which line changes your plan this week?

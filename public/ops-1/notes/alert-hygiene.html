Which Alerts Deserve a Human?

TL;DR

An alert that wakes someone up must mean customers are hurt now (or will be soon), a human must act, and it can't wait until morning. Everything else becomes a ticket for daylight, or a graph. Too many pages teaches people to ignore the phone, and then the real one gets missed too. Review every alert rule against those questions, delete without guilt, and keep counting how many pages were useful.

  • Lives in: the alert rules in the repo, reviewed monthly

1The situation Larder, March 2026

In March 2026 Larder's 63 alert rules sent 410 pages. Eli went through them: 9 needed a human to do something. The rest were CPU spikes during the nightly backup, a disk that had sat at 81 % for a year, and the same stock-sync warning every 20 minutes. People had started muting the phone at night. Sanne's levelling found him half a day for an “alert fix”, and he takes it on 15 April.

  • Noise hides the real page.
  • New people on the rota can't tell which alerts matter.
  • Every page costs sleep, even the useless ones.

2Real-life analogy the smoke alarm by the toaster

A smoke alarm that goes off every time someone makes toast ends up with its battery out within a week. Then there's a real fire. The answer isn't to ignore it or to buy a louder one. You move it away from the toaster, so that it rings only for smoke that matters.

3How it solves it four questions per rule, and count what was useful

Eli takes each of the 63 rules through four questions:

  1. Does it mean customers are hurt now, or will be within the hour?
  2. Does it need a human, or could a script fix it?
  3. Is the action urgent, or can it wait until morning?
  4. Is there a runbook that says what to do?

Four yeses means it pages. If it fails on urgency, it becomes a ticket for the morning. Anything else goes to a dashboard or is deleted. The result is 6 rules that page (the five top-row signals plus the database running out of connections), 8 that make tickets, and 49 gone. The stock-sync warning becomes a ticket, and a problem to fix (vol. 2). In May there are 22 pages, 17 useful. Every month he writes down the share of useful pages, and any alert that pages twice without needing action goes to the next review.

  1. Export every alert rule and last month's pages

    Count pages per rule.

  2. Ask the four questions of each rule

    Hurt now? Human? Urgent? Runbook?

  3. Page only on four yeses

    Everything else is a ticket or a graph.

  4. Delete without guilt

    Rules can always come back; sleep can't.

  5. Count useful pages monthly

    Pages that needed action ÷ all pages.

  6. Review any alert that pages twice for nothing

    Fix it, demote it or delete it.

Vocabulary for the exam

Page
An alert that interrupts a person immediately, day or night.
Ticket
An alert that waits in a queue for working hours.
Actionable
Someone can and must do something about it.
Alert fatigue
So many alerts that people stop reacting to them.
Flapping
An alert that fires and clears again and again.
Symptom-based alerting
Alerting on what users feel, not on every possible cause.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Alerting on every cause

    CPU, memory and disk alerts fire for things users never feel. Fix → alert on symptoms; keep causes on dashboards.

  • Afraid to delete

    “What if we need it?” Fix → rules live in the repo; you can always bring one back.

  • Pages without runbooks

    The person woken up doesn't know what to do. Fix → no runbook, no page.

  • Raising thresholds to silence noise

    The alert stops firing even when it should. Fix → ask whether it should page at all.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • People mute their phones or ignore pages.
  • More than a few pages a week, most for nothing.
  • New people are joining on-call.
  • After every incident: did the right alert fire?

Skip it when…

  • You get one or two pages a month and each one matters: just keep counting.

8Self-check quiz

What are Eli's four questions for a paging alert?

Are customers hurt now or soon? Does it need a human? Is it urgent? Is there a runbook?

What happens to an alert that's real but not urgent?

It becomes a ticket for working hours instead of a page.

What is alert fatigue?

So many alerts, mostly noise, that people stop reacting, and miss the real one.

Why not just raise the threshold on a noisy alert?

It may stop firing when it should; the question is whether it should page at all.

How does Larder track alert quality?

The share of pages that needed action, every month (2 % in March, 77 % in May).

what would you do?A new engineer wants to add an alert for “memory above 85 %” that pages. What do you ask?

What do customers feel when memory is above 85 %, what would the person do, and can it wait until morning? If there's no user symptom and no clear action, it goes on a dashboard.

try it

Export last month's pages. For each alert rule count the pages and how many needed someone to act. Pick the three noisiest rules and decide: page, ticket or delete.

remember!!

A page is a promise: customers are hurt and you must act now. Every other alert should wait for daylight.

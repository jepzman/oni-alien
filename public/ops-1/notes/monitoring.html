How Do We Know It's Broken?

TL;DR

Monitoring answers two questions: is it working for users? and, if not, why? Watch the first one directly: orders per minute, errors, speed, and whether each store's devices are syncing. Alert on that. Then keep machine graphs (CPU, disk, connections) to explain what you see. Green machines with failing orders is the most common blind spot.

1The situation Larder, 9 April 2026

Larder's dashboards in April 2026 show twelve graphs: CPU, memory and disk for the three VMs and the database. On Thursday 9 April at 18:20 they are all green. At the same moment Sam's support team gets six chats in ten minutes: iDEAL payment fails. The graphs watch the machines; nothing watches the orders. Support found out first, and Eli found out from Sam.

  • Customers are the alarm.
  • Failures outside our machines (the PSP, a store's Wi-Fi) are invisible.
  • Rotterdam's store, on a 4G router, has no monitoring at all.

2Real-life analogy the bakery queue

A bakery owner doesn't stare at the oven thermometer to know if the morning is going well. She watches the queue and the shelves. A long queue and empty shelves means trouble, whatever the oven says. When that happens, the thermometer helps her find out why.

3How it solves it watch what users feel; keep the rest to explain it

Eli builds a top row of five signals for the critical services. He uses the four golden signals (traffic, errors, latency, saturation) and adds one of Larder's own:

  • Orders per 5 minutes per city, against the same 5 minutes last week. A sudden gap means something is wrong, wherever it is: in Core, at the PSP or in DNS.
  • Checkout error rate and checkout p95 (95 % of checkouts are faster than this).
  • Dispatch assignment delay: how long a packed order waits for a courier.
  • Store heartbeat: the last sync from each store's picker app. No sync for 5 minutes means a store is cut off.

Machine graphs move to a second row, to look at after an alert. On opening day, 27 April, at 12:10, the Rotterdam heartbeat alert fires: the 4G router has dropped for 6 minutes. The picker app keeps working offline (ADR 0004) and orders continue. This time Eli knows before Yara calls.

  1. Start from the catalogue's critical services

    One dashboard each.

  2. Pick the signals users feel

    Traffic, errors, latency, plus your own (orders, heartbeat).

  3. Compare with a normal baseline

    Same time last week beats a fixed threshold for traffic.

  4. Watch from outside too

    A check from outside the cloud catches DNS and certificates.

  5. Keep machine graphs for explaining

    Second row, not the alarm.

  6. Test that alerts reach someone

    Fire a fake one before you rely on it.

Vocabulary for the exam

Monitoring
Collecting signals about a system and watching them.
Event
Any change of state worth noticing; some events become alerts.
Golden signals
Latency, traffic, errors and saturation.
Symptom vs cause
What users feel (slow checkout) vs why (DB connections full).
Heartbeat
A regular “I'm alive” signal; silence is the alarm.
Synthetic check
A robot that uses the service from outside, like a customer.
ops/monitoring/checkout.ymltop-row signals · 14 April 2026
service: checkout          owner: Ben         tier: critical
signals:
  orders_per_5min:   by city · compare with same 5 min last week
                     alert if < 50 % for 10 min (17:00–20:00: < 70 % for 5 min)
  checkout_errors:   alert if > 2 % for 5 min
  checkout_p95:      alert if > 1,500 ms for 10 min   (target 800 ms at 5× peak)
  synthetic_order:   every 5 min from outside the cloud, test basket, no payment
explain (no alerts):
  core CPU · memory · disk · db connections · db CPU · queue length
stores:
  heartbeat:         picker app last sync per store; alert if > 5 min, 07:00–23:00

tools: whatever metrics stack you have (most clouds include one), an outside synthetic check, and the paging tool. The tool matters less than which signals you choose.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Watching only machines

    Green graphs, broken checkout. Fix → alert on what users feel.

  • Fixed thresholds for traffic

    Sunday morning looks like an outage. Fix → compare with the same time last week.

  • Monitoring from inside only

    If the region or DNS fails, the monitor fails with it. Fix → add a check from outside.

  • Never testing the alert

    The first real alert goes to an old phone number. Fix → fire a test alert monthly.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Customers or support find problems first.
  • You have dashboards but no user-facing signals.
  • A new site or service is going live.
  • Outside services sit on your critical path.

Skip it when…

  • A batch job nobody waits for: check it in the morning instead.

8Self-check quiz

What are the four golden signals?

Latency, traffic, errors and saturation.

Why compare orders with the same time last week?

Traffic changes with the hour and day; a fixed threshold would alarm every Sunday morning.

What is a heartbeat, and why does Larder use one per store?

A regular “I'm alive” signal. Silence from a store's picker app means the store is cut off, even though our servers are fine.

What are machine graphs for, if not alerts?

Explaining why a user-facing signal went bad.

Why add a check from outside the cloud?

If DNS, a certificate or the whole region fails, inside monitoring fails with it.

what would you do?Orders in Amsterdam drop by 60 % at 18:30 on a Friday, and every machine graph is green. Where do you look first?

Outside our machines: the PSP's status, DNS, the certificate, the address lookup service. Follow the checkout dependency map, and look at errors by step.

try it

Write down how you learned about your last three problems. If any came from a customer or a colleague, choose one signal that would have told you first, and set it up this week.

remember!!

Watch the queue and the shelves, not the oven. Alert on what users feel; use the machines to explain it.

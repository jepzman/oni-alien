Where Do People Report Problems?

TL;DR

A service desk is the one front door for “something's wrong” and “I need something”. It's a queue where every report is written down, gets an owner and an answer, plus one urgent number for things that stop the work. It replaces messages to one engineer's phone. Even a two-person team needs it: it's what makes the work visible, countable and shareable.

1The situation Larder, April 2026

In April 2026 Eli counts the messages from the stores to his own phone: 146 in four weeks, by WhatsApp, text and calls. Noord sent 61, Zuid 52 and Rotterdam 33 in its first week. They arrive at dinner, on Sundays and in meetings. Two messages from Zuid about a broken label printer were never answered; Tomás thought Eli was ignoring him.

  • Nothing is written down, so nothing can be counted or handed over.
  • The on-call rota doesn't help: the stores still message Eli.
  • Urgent and trivial arrive the same way.

2Real-life analogy the caretaker's logbook

Before the logbook, the tenants of a block of flats knocked on the caretaker's own front door, at all hours. Now there's a logbook at his office: write the problem down, and he works through it in order and ticks each line. For leaks and fires there's one emergency number. Same caretaker, same work, but nothing is forgotten, and he gets his evenings back.

3How it solves it one queue, one urgent number, and a poster

Sam's team already uses a helpdesk tool for customers. On 11 May Sam adds an “IT & stores” queue to it. Stores reach it by scanning a QR code on a poster by each store's packing bench, or by email. The form asks four things: store, what's wrong, since when, and how many people are stuck.

For anything that stops picking or dispatch (no handhelds, no network, no labels at the Friday peak) there's one on-call number. It forwards to whoever is primary that week. Eli and the primary work the queue in working hours, and the queue is part of the Monday handover. Eli's rule for himself is just as important: a store message to his own phone gets a polite reply with the QR code, even when he could fix it in a minute.

In June there are 128 tickets, a 12-minute median first response in store hours, and 9 messages to Eli's phone.

  1. Pick one place for reports

    Reuse a tool people already have if you can.

  2. Make it easy to reach

    A QR code where the problem happens.

  3. Ask only what you need

    Where, what, since when, how many people stuck.

  4. Add one urgent route

    A number that forwards to on-call, for “work has stopped”.

  5. Redirect kindly, every time

    Personal messages get the link, even for easy fixes.

  6. Count and review

    Tickets per store, first response, top causes.

Vocabulary for the exam

Service desk
The single point of contact between IT and the people it serves.
Ticket
One written report or request, with an owner and a status.
Queue
Where tickets wait to be picked up.
First response time
How long until a person answers the ticket.
Interrupt
Unplanned work that arrives while you're doing something else.
Shadow support
Help given off the record, through personal channels.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • “Just this once” on WhatsApp

    Every exception teaches the store to message you again. Fix → reply with the link, kindly, every time.

  • A long form

    People don't fill it in, and message you instead. Fix → four questions, from a phone.

  • No urgent route

    Stopped picking waits in a queue. Fix → one number that forwards to on-call.

  • A queue nobody owns

    Tickets wait for days. Fix → it's part of on-call and of the Monday handover.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Problems arrive in personal chats.
  • You can't say how many problems the stores had last month.
  • Before sharing on-call.
  • A new site is opening.

Skip it when…

  • One small team in one room: a shared channel may be enough, as long as it's written.

8Self-check quiz

What is a service desk for?

One front door where every problem and request is written down, owned and answered.

Why does Eli reply to personal messages with the QR code, even for easy fixes?

Each exception teaches the store to message him again; written tickets can be counted and shared.

What are the four questions on Larder's form?

Which store, what's wrong, since when, how many people are stuck.

What goes to the on-call number instead of the queue?

Anything that stops picking or dispatch.

What changed between April and June?

146 personal messages in four weeks → 128 tickets with a 12-minute first response, and 9 personal messages.

what would you do?Yara phones Eli directly on a Sunday because a label printer is down. Picking still works. What does Eli do?

Thanks her, asks her to log it with the QR code so it's tracked, and if it doesn't stop picking it waits for Monday's queue. If it does, the on-call number is the route, not Eli's phone.

try it

For one week, write down every request for help you get, by whatever channel. Where did they come from? Which ones stopped someone's work? Use that to design one queue and one urgent route.

remember!!

Help that arrives through a personal phone can't be counted, shared or handed over. Give people one front door.

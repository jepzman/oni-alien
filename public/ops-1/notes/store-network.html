Is It the Network?

TL;DR

A store's work depends on its network: Wi-Fi for the handhelds, an internet line, a backup and a secure link to the cloud. Treat it as a service. Keep one sheet per store (line, backup, access points, VPN), measure the Wi-Fi where people actually work before blaming it or the devices, and test the failover on purpose every month. A backup you've never tried is a hope, not a backup.

1The situation Larder, May 2026

Noord has logged 23 tickets since April saying “picker app offline”. The pickers say it happens near the freezers. The answer every time has been “restart the handheld”. Noord has two access points for 31 handhelds, both at the front of the store. Meanwhile Rotterdam has run on a 4G router since opening, its internet line goes live on 13 May, and nobody has ever tested what happens when that line fails.

  • Devices get blamed for network gaps, and the gaps stay.
  • The offline-first picker app hides short drops, and so does a long ticket history.
  • A backup that has never been tried may not work at the Friday peak.

2Real-life analogy the dead spot in the back bedroom

A family has no phone signal in the back bedroom and keeps restarting their phones. A friend walks the house with a signal meter, finds the dead spot behind a thick wall, and adds a small repeater. While she's there, she makes them check once that the landline still works when the power is out. Measure before you blame, and test the backup before you need it.

3How it solves it a sheet per store, a survey, and a monthly pulled plug

Eli writes one network sheet per store: the internet line (provider, contract, support number), the 4G backup, each access point and where it is, and the VPN to the cloud.

At Noord on Thursday 14 May he walks the floor with a Wi-Fi survey app. The freezer aisle and the cold-room door read −82 dBm, too weak for a handheld to hold on. Two more access points go in on 21 May (€900), and “offline” tickets at Noord drop from 23 in April to 2 in June. Zuid's survey finds no gaps.

At Rotterdam the line goes live on 13 May, and the 4G router stays as backup. On Tuesday 19 May at 15:00, with Yara watching, Eli pulls the line: the router switches to 4G in 40 seconds, the picker app doesn't notice, and the store heartbeat stays green. The test goes in the calendar for the first Tuesday of every month, at a quiet hour, with the seconds written down. Noord and Zuid get 4G backup routers in June.

  1. Write one network sheet per store

    Line, backup, access points, VPN, support numbers.

  2. Count the “offline” tickets by place

    Where in the store, not just which store.

  3. Measure before blaming

    Walk the floor with a survey app.

  4. Fix the coverage, not the device

    Access points where the work is.

  5. Give every store a backup link

    And alert when it's in use.

  6. Pull the plug on purpose, monthly

    Quiet hour, someone watching, seconds written down.

Vocabulary for the exam

Access point (AP)
The box that provides Wi-Fi to devices nearby.
dBm
Signal strength. Around −65 is good; below −80 is too weak to work reliably.
Wi-Fi survey
Measuring signal across a site, where people actually work.
Failover
Switching to the backup when the main link fails.
4G backup
A mobile-data router that takes over if the line fails.
VPN
An encrypted tunnel from the store to Larder's cloud network.

4The maturity ladder where are we, and what is the next rung?

1By hand

restart and hope

  • Tickets closed with “restarted”.
  • Backup never tested.

2Written down Larder now

  • Surveys, a 4G backup, monthly failover.
  • “Store on 4G” is visible.

3Scripted

managed network

  • Access points and routers managed centrally.
  • Coverage problems flagged by the devices.

tested automatically

  • Failover tested by script at a quiet hour.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Blaming the device

    Restarting handhelds hides a coverage gap for months. Fix → count tickets by place and measure.

  • Untested backup

    The 4G SIM expired months ago. Fix → pull the plug monthly and write down the seconds.

  • Testing at the peak

    A failover test becomes an incident. Fix → quiet hour, store lead watching.

  • Surveying the empty store

    Freezers full of stock block more signal. Fix → survey during a normal working day.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • “Offline” tickets cluster in one store or one place.
  • A store depends on one line.
  • Before a new store opens.
  • Nobody remembers the last failover test.

Skip it when…

  • The store's work doesn't depend on the network (it does at Larder).

8Self-check quiz

Why did Noord's “restart the handheld” fix keep failing?

The problem was Wi-Fi coverage near the freezers, not the devices.

Roughly what signal is too weak for a handheld?

Below about −80 dBm.

How did Eli test Rotterdam's backup?

He pulled the line at a quiet hour with Yara watching: the 4G took over in 40 seconds.

Why test the failover monthly?

Backups fail silently: expired SIMs, changed settings. A monthly test finds that before a peak does.

What should “store on 4G” trigger?

A ticket, so someone checks the line, not a page: the store is still working.

what would you do?Zuid reports “app offline” three times in one evening, all at the packing bench. Zuid's survey found no gaps in May. What do you do?

Check the store heartbeat and the router first (is the line down, are they on 4G?), then survey again at the bench: a new freezer or racking can block signal. Don't close it as “restarted”.

try it

Pick one site that depends on a network. Write its sheet: line, backup, access points, link to your systems. When was the backup last tested? Schedule a pulled-plug test at a quiet hour.

remember!!

Measure before you blame the devices, and pull the plug on purpose before the line pulls it for you.

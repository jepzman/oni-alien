a.k.a. “joiners, movers, leavers”

Forty New People on Monday

TL;DR

Every new person needs accounts and devices on day one; every leaver must lose them on their last day. Joiners, movers and leavers (JML) is the process that does both from one checklist driven by the person's role: a picker gets the picker set, a store lead the lead set. It saves hours on every hiring wave, and it closes the doors that people forget to lock when someone leaves.

1The situation Larder, 13 April 2026

On Monday 13 April forty Rotterdam pickers, couriers and leads start training. Each needs a handheld login, a courier or rota account, a door code, and email for the leads. Eli creates them by hand, at about 25 minutes a person: roughly 17 hours. On the way he finds that Jasper's admin account still works, eight months after he left, and that three couriers who left in March can still sign in to the courier app.

  • Starters wait, so training starts late.
  • Each account is set up a little differently.
  • Nobody tells IT when someone leaves, so access never ends.

2Real-life analogy the hotel key card

At a hotel desk a new guest gets one key card, programmed in two minutes from one screen for their room, the gym and the breakfast room. At checkout the card simply stops working. Nobody changes the locks, and nobody has to remember which doors that guest could open.

3How it solves it the role decides what you get; the last day decides when it stops

Eli agrees three things with Yara and Joost's operations lead on 14 April:

  • Roles drive access. Four role groups (picker, courier, store lead, office), each with a fixed list of accounts and devices. A new picker is added to the picker group and gets the whole set at once: under 6 minutes.
  • One trigger in, one trigger out. HR's hiring form, with role and start date, starts a joiner; the last working day, on the same form, ends every account on that day. Devices go back to the store manager and are ticked off in the asset register.
  • A monthly check. Active accounts are compared with the staff list. The first check, on 14 April, closes Jasper's admin account, the three couriers' accounts and 6 others.

The second Rotterdam wave (5 pickers and 12 couriers, later in the year) takes about 2 hours instead of 7.

  1. Define a few roles

    Picker, courier, store lead, office: the access each needs.

  2. Make each role a group

    Add a person to the group, not account by account.

  3. Agree one trigger in, one out

    HR's hiring form, and the last working day.

  4. Remove everything on the last day

    Same day, every account, then the device.

  5. Handle movers on purpose

    A new role swaps the group; old access goes.

  6. Check monthly against the staff list

    Anything active and not on the list is closed.

Vocabulary for the exam

Joiner
A new person who needs access and devices.
Mover
Someone changing role; they need new access, and to lose the old.
Leaver
Someone leaving; all access must end on their last day.
Role-based access
Access given by role (group), not person by person.
Least privilege
Each role gets only what it needs to do the job.
Orphaned account
An active account whose person has left.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • No leaver trigger

    IT never hears that someone left. Fix → the last day goes on the same HR form.

  • Access added on top for movers

    Over the years people collect every door. Fix → a new role swaps the group.

  • One-off exceptions

    “Just give her admin for a week” becomes permanent. Fix → exceptions get an end date.

  • Forgetting founders and contractors

    The riskiest accounts are the oldest. Fix → the monthly check covers everyone.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • A hiring wave is coming (a new store, a peak).
  • You've found an active account for someone who left.
  • Starters wait days for access.
  • Before a security questionnaire from a business client.

Skip it when…

  • Five people who all need the same three tools: a shared checklist is enough.

8Self-check quiz

What are the three parts of JML?

Joiners get access on day one, movers swap to their new role's access, leavers lose all access on their last day.

Why give access by role rather than by person?

It's faster, consistent, and easy to check: the role says what you should have.

What must happen on a leaver's last day?

Every account is removed that day and devices go back and are ticked off in the register.

What did the first monthly check find?

Nine active accounts for people who had left, including Jasper's admin account.

What's the trap with movers?

Adding the new access on top of the old, so people collect access over the years.

what would you do?A courier leaves on Friday. On Monday his e-bike lock code still works. Where did the process break?

The leaver trigger: either the last day wasn't on HR's form, or removal wasn't done that day. Fix the trigger, then check the other leavers from that week.

try it

Export the active accounts from your three most-used tools and compare them with your staff list. How many belong to people who have left? Then write the role groups that would have prevented it.

remember!!

Access starts with a role and ends on the last day. Anything else is a door left open.

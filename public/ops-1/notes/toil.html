Why Am I Always Busy?

TL;DR

Toil is operational work that is manual, repetitive, could be automated, and grows as the business grows, and that leaves nothing lasting behind. A little is normal; too much crowds out every improvement and burns people out. Measure it for a few weeks, cap it (SRE teams use 50 % of the week), and remove the biggest piece every month, for good.

1The situation Larder, 8–12 June 2026

Ten weeks after the 160 % week, Eli has a rota, a desk queue, runbooks and a catalogue, and he's still always busy. So for the week of 8–12 June he logs every hour. 16.5 of 40 are toil: patching three VMs by hand (5 h), restarting the stock sync three times (2 h), renewing certificates by hand (1 h), copying store figures into a spreadsheet for Joost (2 h), resets only he can do (3.5 h) and small manual fixes (3 h). 41 % of his week, and every item grows with every new store.

  • Toil feels productive, because something got done.
  • It crowds out the work that would remove it.
  • With six cities planned, it grows faster than any hiring plan.

2Real-life analogy the farmer's buckets

A farmer carries water from the well to her animals in buckets every morning: two hours a day, and more with every new animal. It isn't hard work, it just never ends, and it grows with the herd. One week she lays a pipe and a trough. That week is harder than any morning with the buckets. After it, the two hours are hers again, and the next ten animals cost nothing extra.

3How it solves it log it, cap it, remove the biggest piece monthly

Eli uses the SRE definition of toil (manual, repetitive, automatable, grows with scale, no lasting value) and sets up three habits from 15 June:

  • Log it weekly. Ten minutes every Friday: hours of toil by kind.
  • Cap it. Toil may take at most 50 % of the platform engineer's week, with a target of 30 % by December. Over the cap, improvement work comes first, and Eli says so at tech leads.
  • Remove the biggest piece every month, for good. In June it's the store figures: a dashboard Joost opens himself saves 2 hours a week. In July, certificates move to automatic renewal. Stock sync restarts aren't toil to automate: they're a problem to fix (vol. 2). VM patching waits for the platform work (vol. 3).

By the end of July Eli's toil is 33 %. The log has a second use: it's the evidence for the second platform engineer Femke approves in August.

  1. Log every hour for two weeks

    By kind of task, honestly.

  2. Mark what is toil

    Manual, repetitive, automatable, grows with scale, no lasting value.

  3. Set a cap and a target

    At most 50 %; aim lower.

  4. Remove the biggest piece each month

    For good, not with a quicker manual trick.

  5. Tell problems from toil

    Some toil is a symptom: fix the cause.

  6. Use the log as evidence

    For hiring, for priorities, for saying no.

Vocabulary for the exam

Toil
Manual, repetitive, automatable operational work that grows with scale and leaves nothing lasting.
Overhead
Necessary work that isn't toil: meetings, planning, reviews.
Toil budget
The share of time toil is allowed to take.
Engineering work
Work that leaves the system better, so tomorrow needs less effort.
Automation
Turning repeated steps into something that runs without a person.
Symptom
Toil caused by a fault: fix the fault, don't automate the restart.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Automating a symptom

    A script restarts the sync every hour, and the fault is never fixed. Fix → ask whether it's a problem first.

  • Logging from memory

    Toil looks smaller than it is. Fix → log every Friday, by kind.

  • No cap

    Toil quietly eats the whole week again. Fix → 50 % at most, said out loud.

  • Swapping toil for faster toil

    A quicker manual trick, still every week. Fix → remove it for good, or it isn't removed.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • You're busy but nothing improves.
  • Work grows with every new site or customer.
  • You need evidence for help or priorities.
  • One person does all the handwork.

Skip it when…

  • Work that's done once: that's a project, not toil.

8Self-check quiz

What makes work toil?

It's manual, repetitive, could be automated, grows with scale, and leaves nothing lasting.

What cap does SRE put on operational work?

About 50 % of the week; the rest goes to engineering work.

Why isn't the stock sync restart solved with a script?

It's a symptom of a fault; the fault should be found and fixed (problem management).

What did Eli remove first, and why?

Copying store figures: the biggest piece he could remove for good in a day, with a dashboard Joost uses himself.

What else is the toil log good for?

Evidence: for priorities, for saying no, and for hiring the second platform engineer.

what would you do?A new store doubles the weekly resets. Eli suggests a script that resets every device nightly. What do you ask?

Why do the devices need resetting at all? If there's a fault behind it, the nightly reset just hides it. Find the cause before automating.

try it

Log your work for one week in half-hour blocks. Mark what's toil by the five tests. What share is it? Pick the biggest piece and remove it for good this month.

remember!!

Toil isn't hard, it just never ends and grows with the business. Count it, cap it, and lay one pipe a month.

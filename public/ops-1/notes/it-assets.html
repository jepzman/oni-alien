What Do We Own, and Where Is It?

TL;DR

IT asset management means knowing what you own or pay for, where it is, who has it, what it costs and when it renews or runs out. Devices, licences, SaaS seats, domains and certificates all count. A single list with a renewal calendar stops the three classic surprises: lost devices, paying for people who left, and things that quietly expire.

  • Family: knowing what you run
  • Lives in: ops/assets.csv + a shared renewals calendar

1The situation Larder, 9 April 2026

Eli tries to count what Larder owns. There are 92 handhelds on paper (31 at Noord, 31 at Zuid, 30 on their way to Rotterdam), but 4 at Zuid can't be found. There are 46 SaaS subscriptions, and 9 seats still belong to people who left: about €340 a month. Then the bad one: the domain larder.nl renews on 23 April on Jasper's personal card, and that card has expired.

  • Nobody has one list; it's split across invoices, inboxes and the stores.
  • Renewals happen, or don't, by surprise.
  • Rotterdam adds 30 handhelds, a router and a dozen new seats in three weeks.

2Real-life analogy the school's equipment cupboard

A school's equipment cupboard has a sign-out sheet: each projector and laptop has a number, the name of whoever has it, and when it's due back. Once a term the caretaker counts the cupboard against the sheet. Nothing fancy, and nothing walks away unnoticed for long.

3How it solves it one file, four sections, a renewals calendar

Eli makes one file with four sections. Devices each get a number, a location or person, a status and a purchase date. Subscriptions get an owner, seats, price and renewal date. Names and certificates covers domains and TLS certificates, with renewal dates and the card they're paid with. Leases covers things like the Rotterdam 4G router. Every renewal goes into a shared calendar with a reminder 30 days before.

First results. The domain moves to the company card on 10 April. The 9 leaver seats are cancelled. Tomás finds 3 of Zuid's 4 missing handhelds in a drawer of broken ones, and the fourth is written off. Each store manager now counts devices with Eli once a quarter, using the asset numbers stuck on the back.

  1. Collect every invoice and card statement

    SaaS hides in expense claims.

  2. Number every device

    A sticker on the back, the same number in the file.

  3. Write who has it

    A store or a person, never “somewhere”.

  4. Add renewal and expiry dates

    Contracts, domains, certificates, warranties.

  5. Put renewals in a shared calendar

    Reminder 30 days before, two names on each.

  6. Count it every quarter

    With the store manager, against the file.

Vocabulary for the exam

IT asset
Anything with value you own or pay for: device, licence, subscription, domain.
Asset register
The list of assets with owner, location, status and dates.
Lifecycle
Request → buy → use → repair → retire → dispose.
Renewal
A date when something continues (and charges) or stops.
Shadow IT
Tools bought by teams on their own, outside any list.
Write-off
Removing a lost or broken asset from the register, on purpose.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Forgetting SaaS

    Tools bought on a card never reach any list. Fix → go through card statements, not just invoices.

  • Renewals with one name

    That person is on holiday on the day. Fix → two names on every renewal.

  • Personal cards for company things

    The domain lapses when the person leaves. Fix → move everything to company accounts.

  • A register nobody counts

    It drifts from reality within months. Fix → count with each store every quarter.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • You can't say how many devices you have.
  • Something expired by surprise.
  • Before a new site adds dozens of devices.
  • Someone important has left.

Skip it when…

  • A two-person company with five laptops: a note is enough.
  • You already run MDM and a SaaS management tool: check them, don't duplicate them.

8Self-check quiz

What counts as an IT asset?

Anything you own or pay for that affects IT: devices, licences, SaaS seats, domains, certificates, leases.

What three surprises does an asset register prevent?

Lost devices, paying for people who left, and things that quietly expire.

Why put two names on every renewal?

So it doesn't depend on one person being there on the day.

What was the most dangerous finding?

The domain renewing on an ex-founder's expired personal card, two weeks away.

How often should you count devices, and with whom?

Every quarter, with each store manager, against the register.

what would you do?A store manager says “we've got about thirty handhelds”. Your register says 33. What do you do?

Count them together, by asset number, and update the register. “About” is how the four at Zuid went missing.

try it

Go through the last three months of card statements and invoices and list every subscription. Add when each one renews and whose card pays. Which one would hurt most if it lapsed next week?

remember!!

If you can't say where it is and when it renews, you don't own it. It just hasn't failed yet.

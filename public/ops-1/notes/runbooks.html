What Do I Do at 3 a.m.?

TL;DR

A runbook tells the person who was paged what the alert means, what to check, what to do, who to call if that fails, and how to know it's fixed, in short numbered steps that can be copied and pasted. Write one for every paging alert, for someone tired, stressed and new to this system, and test it by having someone else follow it.

1The situation Larder, late April 2026

The rota started on 20 April, and in May Cho, Dee and Ana take their first weeks. Eli pictures the first night: Cho, a payments engineer, gets “db_connections > 90 %” at 02:40. The only instructions are in Eli's head, and Eli is asleep. Cho's choices are to wake Eli, which means the rota hasn't really shared anything, or to guess.

  • A shared rota needs shared knowledge.
  • Guessing on a production database at night is how small incidents get big.
  • Eli is still the escalation for everything.

2Real-life analogy the pilot's engine-fire checklist

An aeroplane has a quick-reference checklist for an engine fire: short numbered steps in big print, the first few done from memory. It's written for a stressed pilot at 3 a.m. over the ocean, not for an engineer at a desk. It's tested in the simulator, and every pilot has practised it.

3How it solves it one page per paging alert, six parts, then test it

Eli writes one runbook for each of the 6 paging alerts, plus two for known weak spots (the price script on VM 2 and the Rotterdam 4G router). That's 8 by Friday 8 May. Each page has the same six parts:

  1. What it means for customers, in one line.
  2. Check first: two or three quick looks with links.
  3. The fix: numbered steps with commands to copy.
  4. Still broken? Who to call, and after how long.
  5. How to tell it's fixed: which graph goes back to normal.
  6. Last tested: by whom and when.

Then the test. Each rota member follows two runbooks on a quiet afternoon without Eli in the room, and every step they stumble on gets rewritten. On Saturday 9 May at 02:40 Cho is paged for database connections, follows RB-06 alone and has it fixed in 11 minutes. Eli finds out at breakfast.

  1. One runbook per paging alert

    No runbook, no page.

  2. Use the same six parts every time

    People find their place quickly under stress.

  3. Write commands to copy, not prose

    Exact commands, with the environment named.

  4. Say when to give up and call

    “After 10 minutes, call the secondary.”

  5. Test it with someone else

    They follow it; you watch and say nothing.

  6. Update it after every use

    If it was wrong at 3 a.m., fix it by noon.

Vocabulary for the exam

Runbook
Step-by-step instructions for handling one alert or task.
Playbook
Another word for runbook; sometimes a wider set of plays.
Copy-paste step
A command written exactly so it can be pasted safely.
Escalation step
The point at which you stop and call someone.
Runbook test
Someone other than the author follows it while the author watches.
Automation candidate
A runbook step done so often it should become a script.

# RB-06 · Postgres connections above 90 %                  pages: yes · tier: critical
1 What it means   New requests will soon fail to get a DB connection: checkout errors next.
2 Check first     a) dashboard "core / db": which app holds most connections?
                  b) any deploy in the last hour?  (#deploys channel)
                  c) Dispatch's copies: more than 6 running?
3 The fix         a) if one Core VM holds > 150: restart its app (not the VM):
                     ssh core-2 'sudo systemctl restart larder-core'   # one VM at a time
                  b) if Dispatch scaled up: set copies back to 4 (ops/dispatch.md)
4 Still broken?   after 10 min → call the secondary; after 20 → Eli (+31 6 …)
5 Fixed when      connections < 70 % for 5 min and checkout errors < 1 %
6 Last tested     Cho, 5 May (step 3a took 2 tries: wording fixed)

4The maturity ladder where are we, and what is the next rung?

1By hand

in one person's head

  • Wake that person, or guess.
  • Every night depends on one person.

2Written down Larder now

a tested page per alert

  • Six parts, copy-paste steps.
  • Updated after every use.

3Scripted

scripted steps

  • The common fix is one command.
  • The runbook calls the script.

4Automatic

self-healing

  • The system runs the fix itself and tells you after.
  • People handle only the new.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Written for the author

    “Restart the usual thing.” Fix → write for someone new and tired.

  • Never tested

    The command has a typo you find at 3 a.m. Fix → someone else follows it first.

  • No point to stop

    People keep trying for an hour alone. Fix → say when to call, in minutes.

  • Stale after the first change

    The runbook describes last year's system. Fix → update it after every use and every change.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • You're sharing on-call.
  • An alert pages and only one person knows what to do.
  • After an incident where people guessed.
  • Before a launch that adds new failure modes.

Skip it when…

  • Alerts that shouldn't page at all: fix the alert instead.

8Self-check quiz

What are the six parts of Larder's runbooks?

What it means, check first, the fix, still broken? call…, how to tell it's fixed, last tested.

Who should a runbook be written for?

A tired, stressed person who has never seen this alert before.

How do you test a runbook?

Someone other than the author follows it, while the author watches and says nothing; fix every stumble.

Why give a time limit before calling for help?

So nobody struggles alone for an hour while customers wait.

When is a runbook step a candidate for a script?

When it's run often and is always the same.

what would you do?Dee follows RB-02 at night and step 3 doesn't work, but she fixes it another way. What happens next?

She updates RB-02 the next day with what actually worked, and notes it in the handover, so the next person doesn't hit the same wall.

try it

Pick the alert that last woke someone up. Write its runbook in the six parts. Then ask a colleague who has never handled it to follow it while you watch. What did you have to explain out loud?

remember!!

If the fix lives in one head, the rota is shared on paper only. Write it for someone tired and new, then test it.

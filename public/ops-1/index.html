the IT operations notebook · book 5 · vol. 1

Keeping the Lights On

Volume 1 of The IT Operations Notebook: the ground floor of IT operations, in plain words, following the ITIL 4 practices with the habits of site reliability engineering. 15 notes in four collections: knowing what you run, watching it, answering the stores, and getting it out of one person's head. Each note is a question someone running IT must answer, with an ITIL tag under the title, a maturity ladder from “by hand” to “automatic”, and a try it for your own systems. Every note follows Eli at Larder, in the same months as Ana's, Grace's and Sanne's notebooks.

the story so far

Late March 2026. Rotterdam opens on 27 April, on a 4G router, because the store's internet line won't arrive until May. In the week of 23 March, Sanne's plan showed Eli, Larder's only platform engineer, at 160 %: platform work, the Dispatch RFC, a week on call, the internet line and an alert fix. Ana took his on-call week. Eli realised the problem wasn't the week: everything about running Larder lives in his head. This volume follows him from 30 March to June 2026: finding out what Larder runs, who is woken up when it breaks, how the stores ask for help, and how to get it all written down.

What we run know what you run, what it hangs on, and who it's for

  1. 01What Is IT Operations For?Operations exists to keep the services customers pay for working; the value is in the outcome, not the servers.p. 01
  2. 02What Do We Run, and Who Owns It?A service catalogue: every service in plain words, with an owner, its users and how much it matters.p. 02
  3. 03What Is Connected to What?Map what each service depends on, so an incident starts with a picture, not an argument.p. 03
  4. 04What Do We Own, and Where Is It?One list of devices, accounts, licences and renewals, with who has each one.p. 04
  5. 05Forty New People on MondayJoiners, movers and leavers: accounts and devices from one checklist, on day one, and gone on the last day.p. 05

Watching it who is woken, by what, and what they do next

  1. 06Who Gets Woken Up?A shared on-call rota with a handover, a backup and rest afterwards, so no one person is the pager.p. 06
  2. 07How Do We Know It's Broken?Watch what customers feel first (orders, errors, speed), then the machines behind it.p. 07
  3. 08Which Alerts Deserve a Human?Page only for things that hurt customers now and need a person; everything else waits for daylight.p. 08
  4. 09What Do I Do at 3 a.m.?Short, tested steps for each alert, written for a tired person who has never seen it before.p. 09

Answering the stores one door for help, standard requests, networks and kit

  1. 10Where Do People Report Problems?One door for every store and office problem, instead of one engineer's phone.p. 10
  2. 11Can I Have a New Handheld?Standard requests with a form, an owner and a promised time, so they stop being favours.p. 11
  3. 12Is It the Network?Know each store's Wi-Fi, line and 4G backup, test the failover, and measure it before blaming it.p. 12
  4. 13How Does a New Store Get Its IT?A store IT kit: the line ordered in week one, devices enrolled centrally, and a checklist the next city reuses.p. 13

Out of one head write it down, and count the handwork

  1. 14Where Is That Written Down?Write down the why as well as the how, next to the thing it describes, and prune what's stale.p. 14
  2. 15Why Am I Always Busy?Measure the repetitive manual work, cap it, and remove the worst piece each month.p. 15

★The nine operations ratings what a good operations practice is judged on

Every note scores the pattern from 1 (poor) to 5 (great) on the same nine things, comparing an app without the pattern to the same app with it.

User impact
Fewer customers, pickers and couriers hurt when something breaks.
Reliability
Things fail less, and fail smaller.
Recovery speed
Time from “it's broken” to “it works again”.
Change safety
Changes rarely cause incidents, and can be undone.
Visibility
Anyone on call can see what runs, what's wrong and what changed.
Toil reduction
Repetitive manual work goes down instead of up.
Cost awareness
Money and people's time are spent where they matter.
Sustainable on-call
The load is shared and bearable; nobody is the only one who knows.
Low overhead
Time and ceremony the practice takes. High = light.

first law

If it only works because one person remembers how, it isn't running: it's being held up. Write it down, share the pager, then automate.

Availability Maths

TL;DR

When a request needs several parts in series, their availabilities multiply: five parts at 99.9 % give about 99.5 %. Parts in parallel (a spare that takes over) combine the other way: two at 99.9 % give about 99.9999 %, if the switch-over is automatic. Turn every target into minutes a month, compare with what you measure, and take parts off the critical path before promising more nines.

  • Origin: reliability engineering; SRE practice
  • Lives in: the availability sheet and every SLA you sign
  • Pairs with: failure mode analysis, DR tiers, SLOs and error budgets

1The situation Larder, 29 November 2027

Korenwolf's renewal draft asks for 99.95 % availability for business ordering. Mo wants to sign; Femke asks Ana whether Larder can promise it. Nobody knows what the ordering path achieves today, nor that 99.95 % allows only 22 minutes of downtime a month.

  • Nines sound similar; the minutes are very different.
  • Every part a request touches lowers the total.
  • Promises made without maths become penalties.

2Real-life analogy Christmas lights in series

Old Christmas lights were wired in series: one bulb fails, the whole string goes dark, so the longer the string, the darker December. Newer strings are wired in parallel: one bulb fails and the rest keep shining. Same bulbs, very different evenings.

Translating it to the job
In real lifeOn the project
Bulbs in seriesCDN, portal backend, Core, database, billing: all needed

3How it solves it multiply, convert to minutes, measure

Ana multiplied the business ordering chain: CDN 99.99, portal backend 99.95, Core 99.9, database 99.95, billing 99.9: about 99.69 %, over two hours a month. Taking billing off the path (orders accepted, invoices queued) lifts it to 99.79 %; twelve months of measurements say 99.81 %. Mo and Ana offered Ruben 99.8 % a month for ordering, measured, with service credits, and 99.9 % as a joint goal for 2028. Ruben accepted.

  1. Draw the request's path

    Every part a request must pass through.

  2. Multiply in series

    A × B × C …; the weakest part dominates.

  3. Combine spares in parallel

    1 − (1 − A)²; only if switch-over is automatic.

  4. Convert to minutes

    Per month: 99.9 % = 43 min; 99.95 % = 22 min.

  5. Compare with measurements

    Twelve months of real data, per path.

  6. Shorten the path, then promise

    Remove parts from the critical path before adding nines.

Vocabulary for the exam

Availability
The share of time a service works as expected.
Nines
Shorthand: three nines = 99.9 %.
In series
All parts are needed; availabilities multiply.
In parallel
Any one part is enough; failures must coincide.
SLA
A promise in a contract, usually with penalties or credits.
Critical path
The parts a request can't succeed without.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • Honest promises backed by numbers.
  • Minutes, not nines: everyone understands.
  • Shows where to invest: the weakest link.
  • Shorter paths beat more spares.
  • Better contracts: measured, with credits.

What we lose

  • Component figures are estimates.
  • Ignores correlated failures (one zone down).
  • Partial outages are hard to count.
  • Can disappoint salespeople.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Nines without minutes

    “Only 0.05 % more.” Fix → always convert to minutes a month.

  • Parallel without automation

    A manual switch counted as parallel. Fix → count switch-over time as downtime.

  • Vendor figures only

    The SLA page, not reality. Fix → measure the path yourself.

  • More spares first

    A second region before shortening the path. Fix → remove parts from the path first.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Before signing an SLA.
  • Comparing designs for a critical path.
  • When leaders ask for “more nines”.
  • Deciding where to add redundancy.

Skip it when…

  • Internal tools where a few hours down costs little.

8Self-check quiz

How do availabilities combine in series?

They multiply, so the total is lower than any one part.

How many minutes a month is 99.9 %?

About 43.

What lifted business ordering from 99.69 % to 99.79 %?

Taking billing off the ordering path, queueing invoice lines.

When does a spare in parallel really help?

When switch-over is automatic and quick.

try it

Draw the path of your most important request. Write each part's availability (measured if you can), multiply them, and convert to minutes a month. Then find one part you could take off the path.

Load Profiles

TL;DR

A load test is only as good as its load profile: the mix of requests (mostly browsing? mostly writes?) and the shape over time (a steady ramp, a sudden spike, a long soak). Build profiles from production traffic, add the known special events (campaign pushes, morning batches), test on production-sized infrastructure, and keep the profiles as code to re-run before every peak.

1The situation Larder, 1 November 2027

Last year's load test sent a flat 1,000 requests a second to checkout for thirty minutes. It passed. Real traffic looks nothing like it: mostly browsing, a Friday ramp, and a sharp spike when a campaign push goes to every customer at once. The test proved checkout works on a quiet, imaginary day.

  • A flat line misses spikes, ramps and queues building up.
  • Testing one endpoint misses contention between them.
  • Special events (pushes, batches) cause the real peaks.

2Real-life analogy testing a bridge

Engineers don't test a bridge by parking one heavy lorry in the middle. They model rush hour, a crowd crossing in step, wind gusts and a traffic jam that sits for hours, because each shape stresses the bridge differently. The parked lorry tells them almost nothing about Monday morning.

3How it solves it the real mix, the real shapes

Eli took a month of production logs: 78 % browsing and search, 12 % basket, 6 % checkout, 4 % picker, Dispatch and business API. Ana added four shapes: the Friday ramp, the push spike after a campaign to 300,000 customers, the business morning release at 06:30, and a four-hour soak. On 8 November the push spike exhausted Larder Core's workers at 2.6×. Fixes: a cached home screen and pushes in waves of 50,000. Re-run on 15 November: 5× Friday peak, checkout p95 640 ms.

  1. Take the mix from production

    Requests by type from a real peak period.

  2. Model the shapes

    Ramps, spikes, batches, soaks; from real events.

  3. Add the special events

    Campaign pushes, morning batches, sales.

  4. Test production-sized

    Same instance sizes, data volumes and limits.

  5. Watch the whole system

    Queues, pools, connections, not just response times.

  6. Keep profiles as code

    Re-run before every peak and after big changes.

Vocabulary for the exam

Load profile
The mix and shape of traffic in a test.
Traffic mix
The share of each kind of request.
Ramp
Load rising steadily over time.
Spike
A sudden jump in load.
Soak test
Steady load for hours, to find leaks and slow build-ups.
Thundering herd
Many clients acting at the same moment, e.g. after a push.

tools: k6, Gatling, Locust or JMeter; production logs or analytics for the mix; a production-sized staging environment; dashboards for pools and queues.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • Finds the real failure: the push spike.
  • Tests contention between request types.
  • Repeatable before every peak.
  • Confidence grounded in real shapes.
  • Fixes found weeks early.

What we lose

  • Production-sized staging costs money.
  • Profiles go stale as behaviour changes.
  • Takes days to build well.
  • Test data needs care (privacy).

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Flat lines

    Constant load for thirty minutes. Fix → ramps, spikes, soaks.

  • One endpoint

    Only checkout tested. Fix → the production mix.

  • Toy environment

    Small database, no limits. Fix → production-sized, with real quotas.

  • Response times only

    Pools and queues not watched. Fix → watch saturation everywhere.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Before peak seasons, launches and campaigns.
  • After big architecture changes.
  • When capacity plans need evidence.
  • Checking a fix for a past incident.

Skip it when…

  • Internal tools with tiny, steady traffic.

8Self-check quiz

What two things make up a load profile?

The mix of requests and the shape of traffic over time.

What did the push spike reveal?

Larder Core's workers ran out at 2.6× because the home screen was built per request.

Why test on production-sized infrastructure?

Limits, data volumes and pools behave differently at small size.

Why keep profiles as code?

To re-run them before every peak and after changes.

try it

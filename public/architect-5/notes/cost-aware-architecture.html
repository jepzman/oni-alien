Cost-Aware Architecture

TL;DR

Cost-aware architecture treats cost as a quality like speed or availability. Tag every resource with its capability and owner, show cost per unit of business (per order, per customer) for each part, and give teams that number. Then fix the big lines (logging, idle environments, oversizing, data transfer) and add a cost line to every design review. A bill nobody can read can't be designed down.

1The situation Larder, 21 February 2028

Cost per order was meant to reach €2.80 by the end of 2027; it ended at €3.05. Marco looks at the cloud bill: €78,000 a month and rising, as one number. Nobody can say which part costs what, or what an order costs in cloud, so every cost conversation ends with “let's be careful”.

  • One total can't be acted on.
  • Teams don't see the cost of their design choices.
  • Waste hides in logs, idle environments and oversizing.

2Real-life analogy the price of each dish

A restaurant that only knows its monthly food bill can only ask everyone to use less butter. One that knows the risotto costs €4 a plate and the steak €11 can redesign the menu, change suppliers, or price differently. Cost per dish turns a worry into decisions.

3How it solves it tag, unit cost, fix the big lines, review

Eli tagged every resource with its capability and team, and the platform dashboard now shows cost per 1,000 orders for each part. The big lines: logs €11k a month (mostly Dispatch's debug logging), six idle test environments €7k, uncompressed images €4k, an oversized replica €3k. Fixes: log sampling, test environments that switch off at night, compression, right-sizing. The bill fell to €58k; cloud cost per order from about 9.6 to 7.1 cents. Every design review now has a cost line.

  1. Tag everything

    Capability, team, environment; untagged = alert.

  2. Pick a business unit

    Cost per order, per customer, per city.

  3. Show it per part

    On the dashboard teams already use.

  4. Fix the big lines

    Logs, idle resources, oversizing, data transfer.

  5. Add a cost line to reviews

    Expected cost per order for each design.

  6. Set budget alerts

    Per team, so surprises are days old, not months.

Vocabulary for the exam

Unit cost
Cost per unit of business (order, customer).
Cost allocation tags
Labels that assign cloud costs to owners.
Right-sizing
Matching resource size to real use.
FinOps
Engineering, finance and product managing cloud cost together.
Showback
Showing teams the cost of what they run.

Data transferred out, often charged.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • A readable bill: cost per order, per part.
  • €20k a month saved in weeks.
  • Teams see their choices' cost.
  • Designs priced before they're built.
  • Marco and engineering speak one language.

What we lose

  • Tagging discipline needed.
  • Shared costs are hard to split.
  • Cost can dominate other qualities.
  • Ongoing attention.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • One big number

    The bill as a total. Fix → cost per order, per part.

  • Untagged resources

    30 % “unallocated”. Fix → alert on untagged.

  • Cutting blindly

    Reliability cut with cost. Fix → check against budgets and SLOs.

  • Once a year

    Annual cost panic. Fix → monthly review, budget alerts.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Growing cloud bills.
  • When cost per order is a business target.
  • Design reviews for new services.
  • After big launches or migrations.

Skip it when…

  • Tiny bills where the effort costs more than the savings.

try it

Tag your cloud resources by team or capability for one month. Divide each part's cost by your main business unit (orders, customers), and pick the biggest line to cut without hurting a quality you've promised.

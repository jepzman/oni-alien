Security Architecture Review

TL;DR

A security architecture review looks at a whole design from an attacker's side before it's widely used. Draw the system with its trust boundaries (where data or control passes between parties with different trust), and at each one ask the STRIDE questions and go through a short checklist: who can pass, how they prove it, what they can reach, what's logged, how secrets are kept. Rank the findings, fix the high ones on a date, and make the review a standard step for designs that cross a boundary.

  • Origin: Microsoft's STRIDE (1999), threat modelling and secure design reviews
  • Lives in: a review page per design, findings in the backlog with dates

1The situation Larder, 11 October 2027

The Business API, the business portal and single sign-on with clients were each built and reviewed by their own team, feature by feature. Three more office clients start next month. Nobody has looked at the whole design from an attacker's side, across the boundaries between Larder, its clients and its vendors.

  • Feature reviews miss problems between features.
  • Trust boundaries multiply as partners connect.
  • Fixes after many clients depend on a design cost far more.

2Real-life analogy the security survey

A security surveyor walks a building's doors between public and private: the street door, the loading bay, the office door behind reception. At each, they ask who can pass, how they prove it, what they could reach once through, and whether anyone would notice. Locks on individual cupboards come later.

3How it solves it each boundary, STRIDE and a checklist

Ana ran two half-days with Cho, Ben, Eli and an external reviewer. They marked four trust boundaries on the container diagram and did a STRIDE pass and a checklist at each. Eleven findings, three high: business API keys never expired (some had been shared by email); an office manager could see another office's orders by changing the number in the address; billing webhooks weren't signature-checked. The highs were fixed within two weeks; the rest went into the backlog with dates.

  1. Draw the design with boundaries

    Every place data or control crosses between parties.

  2. Ask STRIDE at each boundary

    Spoofing, tampering, repudiation, disclosure, denial, elevation.

  3. Run a short checklist

    Authentication, authorisation, data, secrets, logging, dependencies.

  4. Bring an outside eye

    Someone who didn't build it.

  5. Rank and date the findings

    High: fixed before more users; others in the backlog.

  6. Make it a standard step

    For every design that crosses a trust boundary.

Vocabulary for the exam

Trust boundary
Where data or control passes between parties with different trust.
STRIDE
Spoofing, Tampering, Repudiation, Information disclosure, Denial of service, Elevation of privilege.
IDOR
Insecure direct object reference: reaching others' data by changing an ID.
Attack surface
Every way in that an attacker could try.
Webhook signature
A code proving a webhook came from the real sender.
Finding
A weakness found in the review, with a severity.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • Whole-design problems found, not just feature bugs.
  • Before more clients depend on it.
  • Ranked, dated findings.
  • An outside eye sees what builders can't.
  • A repeatable step for future designs.

What we lose

  • Two half-days of senior people.
  • External reviewers cost money.
  • Findings can be uncomfortable.
  • Doesn't replace testing and code review.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Only the builders

    No outside eye. Fix → bring someone who didn't build it.

  • Feature checklists only

    Gaps between features missed. Fix → walk the boundaries.

  • Findings without dates

    “We'll fix it later.” Fix → owner and date for every finding.

  • Once, ever

    Reviewed at launch, never again. Fix → review each new boundary.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Before opening a system to partners or clients.
  • Designs that add a trust boundary.
  • After major architecture changes.
  • Before a client's security audit.

Skip it when…

  • Small changes inside one trusted boundary (code review is enough).

8Self-check quiz

What is a trust boundary?

Where data or control passes between parties with different trust.

What does STRIDE stand for?

Spoofing, tampering, repudiation, information disclosure, denial of service, elevation of privilege.

What were Larder's three high findings?

Non-expiring API keys, other offices' orders reachable by ID, unsigned webhooks.

Why bring an outside reviewer?

The builders' assumptions are exactly what they can't see.

try it

Draw your system's container diagram and mark every trust boundary. At each one, ask the six STRIDE questions and write down the weakest answer. Fix or date the worst one this month.

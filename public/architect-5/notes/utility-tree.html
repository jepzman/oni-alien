Quality Attribute Utility Tree

TL;DR

A quality attribute utility tree turns “everything matters” into a ranked list. Start with the qualities (performance, availability, security, cost…), refine each into concrete scenarios with numbers, and rate each scenario for business importance and technical difficulty (high, medium, low). The high-importance, high-difficulty scenarios drive the architecture; the rest are handled routinely, parked or dropped. Built with the people who own the priorities.

1The situation Larder, 20 March 2028

The board has approved eight cities and 60,000 orders a day by the end of 2028. In the planning meeting each leader names the quality that matters most: Femke resilience, Marco cost, Joost speed for pickers, Lena compliance, Priya app speed. Everything is top priority, so nothing is, and Ana can't plan a year of architecture work around five equal wishes.

  • Qualities in the abstract can't be ranked.
  • Leaders each see their own part.
  • Without a ranking, the loudest wish wins.

2Real-life analogy choosing a house

A family choosing a house lists what matters (space, schools, commute, garden, price), turns each into something concrete (three bedrooms, under thirty minutes to work), and marks each must-have or nice, easy or hard to find. The hard must-haves decide where they look; the easy ones take care of themselves.

3How it solves it qualities → scenarios → (importance, difficulty)

Ana ran a half-day workshop with Femke, Marco, Joost, Lena, Priya and Grace. Each quality became concrete scenarios, rated together. (H, H): checkout at 5× peak with 60,000 orders a day, p95 under 800 ms; a new city live in six weeks by configuration. (H, M): a new country's rules in a month; cloud cost under 6 cents an order. (M, H): back within an hour after a region outage, parked under ADR 0026's triggers. (L, M): real-time recommendations, dropped. The (H, H) scenarios became the 2028 architecture priorities, in that order.

  1. Invite the owners of the priorities

    Leaders and product, not just engineers.

  2. List the qualities

    Performance, availability, security, cost, modifiability…

  3. Refine into scenarios

    Concrete, with numbers: stimulus, environment, response.

  4. Rate importance and difficulty

    H, M, L each, together.

  5. Act on (H, H) first

    These drive the architecture work.

  6. Park or drop the rest

    With the reason written down.

Vocabulary for the exam

Utility tree
A tree from “utility” to qualities to rated scenarios.
Quality attribute
A property like performance, availability or cost.
Scenario
A concrete, testable statement of a quality.
ATAM
Architecture Tradeoff Analysis Method, where utility trees come from.
Business importance
How much the scenario matters to the business.
Technical difficulty
How hard it is to achieve with the architecture.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • A ranked list instead of five equal wishes.
  • Leaders rate together, so they own it.
  • Concrete scenarios become tests.
  • Parked and dropped items explained.
  • The year's plan follows directly.

What we lose

  • Half a day of leaders' time.
  • Ratings are judgments.
  • Needs good facilitation.
  • Must be redone when strategy changes.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Abstract qualities

    “Scalable”, “secure”. Fix → concrete scenarios with numbers.

  • Engineers only

    Leaders not in the room. Fix → the owners of the priorities rate.

  • Everything (H, H)

    No ranking after all. Fix → force a spread; compare pairs.

  • A poster, not a plan

    The tree never used again. Fix → turn (H, H) into the roadmap.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Yearly architecture planning.
  • Big new goals (cities, volume, markets).
  • When stakeholders disagree on priorities.
  • Evaluating a major design (ATAM-style).

Skip it when…

  • Small systems with one obvious quality.

try it

List your system's four most important qualities. Write two concrete scenarios with numbers for each, rate them (importance, difficulty) with your product lead, and circle the (H, H) ones as next quarter's architecture priorities.

DR Tiers

TL;DR

Disaster recovery tiers sort systems by how fast they must come back (RTO, recovery time objective) and how much data they may lose (RPO, recovery point objective). Each tier gets matching protection and cost: standby databases for the top, nightly backups for the bottom. Then you prove each tier with restore drills, because a recovery time nobody has tested is a guess.

1The situation Larder, 10 January 2028

Every runbook says “recovery: one hour”. On 10 January Eli runs Larder's first full restore drill: rebuilding the main database from backup takes 3 hours 40 minutes. Meanwhile the CRM, which could easily wait a day, is protected as expensively as checkout. The same promise for everything, kept by nothing.

  • Recovery times nobody has tested are wishes.
  • Equal protection overspends on some systems and underspends on others.
  • In a real disaster, people need to know what comes back first.

2Real-life analogy the hospital's emergency power

When the mains fail, a hospital's operating theatres switch to generators in seconds, the wards within minutes, and the café waits until the mains come back. Nobody pays for instant power in the café. And the generators are tested every month, under load, because an untested generator is a hope.

3How it solves it tiers with RTO and RPO, proved by drills

Ana and Eli sorted every system with Joost and Sam: Tier 1 (checkout, orders, payments, Dispatch) RTO 15 min, RPO ≤ 1 min, with a standby database in another zone and point-in-time recovery; Tier 2 (picker app, stock, business portal) 1 h / 15 min; Tier 3 (reporting, CRM, analytics) 1 day / 24 h; Tier 4 rebuilt from code. Backups are copied to a second region. The April drill restored the main database to a fresh standby in 22 minutes.

  1. List every system

    From the application portfolio.

  2. Ask the business

    How long can it be down? How much data can we lose?

  3. Group into tiers

    Three or four, each with RTO and RPO.

  4. Match protection to tier

    Standby, replicas, backups, rebuild.

  5. Drill each tier

    Restore for real, timed, on a calendar.

  6. Fix what the drill finds

    And write the real time in the runbook.

Vocabulary for the exam

RTO
Recovery time objective: how long until it works again.
RPO
Recovery point objective: how much data may be lost.
Standby
A copy ready to take over.
Point-in-time recovery
Restoring a database to any moment, from logs.
Restore drill
Actually restoring a system, timed.
Business continuity
Keeping the business running during a disaster.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • Clear order of recovery in a crisis.
  • Spending matched to importance.
  • Proved recovery times, not wishes.
  • Gaps found in drills, not disasters.
  • Contracts checked (WMS B's own DR).

What we lose

  • Drills take time and some risk.
  • Tier 1 is expensive.
  • Tiers drift as systems change.
  • Business owners must agree the numbers.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Untested numbers

    RTO copied from a template. Fix → drill and record the real time.

  • Everything Tier 1

    Every owner says “critical”. Fix → ask what an hour down really costs.

  • Forgetting vendors

    WMS B's recovery not in the plan. Fix → check vendors' DR in the contract.

  • Backups in the same place

    One region, one account. Fix → copy them somewhere else.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Any system the business depends on.
  • Before signing availability promises.
  • After moving to new infrastructure.
  • When backups have never been restored.

Skip it when…

  • Throwaway prototypes and test environments (Tier 4: rebuild).

8Self-check quiz

What do RTO and RPO mean?

How long until it works again, and how much data may be lost.

Why tiers instead of one target?

To match protection and cost to how much each system matters.

What did the first drill find?

A full database restore took 3 h 40, not one hour.

How was it fixed?

A standby with point-in-time recovery; the April drill took 22 minutes.

try it

List your five most important systems. Ask their business owners how long each can be down and how much data it can lose, group them into tiers, and book a timed restore drill for the top one this quarter.

remember!!

Power the theatres first, and test the generator.

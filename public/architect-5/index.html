Designing for Qualities

Volume 5 of The Solution Architect's Notebook: how architects design for the qualities that don't show up in a feature list. 15 notes in four collections: size and speed, availability, security and compliance, and cost and balance. Every note follows Ana at Larder from October 2027 to March 2028, through two city launches and a peak season, and ends with a try it exercise for your own project.

the story so far

October 2027. The warehouse move is done, Antwerp opens in October and Eindhoven in November, and Larder heads into its busiest season with five times the orders of two years ago. Femke wants to know it will hold, Marco wants the cloud bill down, Lena wants Belgian data handled properly, and Korenwolf asks for an emissions figure. This volume follows Ana from October 2027 to March 2028: sizing, availability, security, compliance and cost, done with numbers instead of hope.

Size & speed estimate, plan and budget before the peak arrives

  1. 01Back-of-the-Envelope EstimationRough numbers from a few known facts, in minutes, to see which part of a design matters.p. 01
  2. 02Capacity PlanningForecast demand, find the first thing to run out, and add capacity before it does.p. 02
  3. 03Load ProfilesTest with the real mix and shape of traffic: the ramp, the spike, the push, not a flat line.p. 03
  4. 04Performance BudgetsAgree limits on speed and size, check them in CI, and treat a breach like a failing test.p. 04

Availability do the maths, find the failures, plan the recovery

  1. 05Availability MathsMultiply the chain, add the spares, and turn nines into minutes a month.p. 05
  2. 06Failure Mode AnalysisList how each part can fail, what happens then, and fix the worst first.p. 06
  3. 07DR TiersSort systems into recovery tiers with RTO and RPO, and prove them with restore tests.p. 07
  4. 08When to Go Multi-RegionA second region is a big, ongoing cost: decide with numbers and agreed triggers.p. 08

Security & compliance review the design, know the data, build the rules in

  1. 09Security Architecture ReviewWalk the design's trust boundaries with a checklist and threats, before attackers do.p. 09
  2. 10Data Classification & ResidencyLabel data by sensitivity, know where every copy lives, and let the label set the rules.p. 10
  3. 11Compliance by DesignTurn legal rules into platform features and automatic evidence, not yearly scrambles.p. 11

Cost & balance what it really costs, and which qualities matter most

  1. 12Total Cost of OwnershipCount every cost over years, people included, before comparing options.p. 12
  2. 13Cost-Aware ArchitectureKnow the cost per order of each part, and design and tune with that number in view.p. 13
  3. 14Green SoftwareMeasure the platform's carbon, then use less, use it when it's cleaner, and use cleaner places.p. 14
  4. 15Quality Attribute Utility TreeRank the qualities with concrete scenarios, so trade-offs follow what matters most.p. 15

★The nine architect ratings what a good architecture practice is judged on

Every note scores the pattern from 1 (poor) to 5 (great) on the same nine things, comparing an app without the pattern to the same app with it.

Clarity
A newcomer can understand the system and the reasoning behind it.
Stakeholder alignment
Business, product and engineering share the same what and why.
Decision speed
How quickly the team can choose and get back to building.
Risk reduction
The big risks are surfaced and handled before they bite.
Adaptability
How cheaply the direction can change later.
Traceability
Months later, anyone can find why something was done.
Cost awareness
Money and effort are part of the decision, not a surprise.
Low overhead
Time and ceremony it takes. High = light.
Team autonomy
Teams can decide and act without waiting for the architect.

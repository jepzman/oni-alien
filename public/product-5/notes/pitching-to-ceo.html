Pitching to the CEO

TL;DR

A CEO has little time and many asks. Pitch with the answer first (what you want to do), then why it matters in their terms (money, customers, the strategy they've promised), the main risk and how you'll handle it, and a small ask: a test, a few weeks, a stop rule. Put it on one page, keep it to ten minutes, and leave the method for questions. A small yes now beats a big maybe later.

  • Family: managing up and across
  • Origin: the pyramid principle (Minto); lean experimentation
  • Lives in: a one-page pitch and a ten-minute slot

1The situation Larder, 30 November 2026

Grace wants to let customers add items for a few minutes after ordering: 8 % of express customers place a second order within 30 minutes, and each costs another courier trip. Her first draft for Femke is twelve slides that start with the research method. The answer is on slide 11, and Femke has ten minutes between board calls.

  • Leaders decide in minutes; decks are built for hours.
  • The method impresses the presenter, not the listener.
  • Big asks get “let's discuss later”.

2Real-life analogy the lift pitch

An investor steps into the lift on the ground floor and gets out on the sixth. In that time you say what you want, why it matters to them in their terms, and the small next step. The details come later, if the doors open on a yes.

3How it solves it answer, their terms, the risk, a small ask

Grace rewrote it as one page. Answer: let customers add items for ten minutes after ordering. Femke's terms: second orders cost about €4.50 each in courier time, about €22,000 a month, and baskets grow. Risk: picking may have started, so the window ends before release to the store (Ana's suggestion). Ask: four weeks of Dee's team and a test in Amsterdam in January, stopping if picking delays rise. Femke said yes to the test in the room: “show me in February.”

  1. Lead with the answer

    One sentence: what you want to do.

  2. Say why in their terms

    Money, customers, the promises they've made.

  3. Name the main risk

    And how you'll handle it.

  4. Make the ask small

    A test, a few weeks, a stop rule.

  5. Fit it on one page

    The method in the appendix.

  6. Leave with a decision

    Yes, no, or what would make it yes.

Vocabulary for the exam

Pitch
A short case for a decision.
Answer first
Starting with the conclusion, not the background.
Stop rule
A condition agreed in advance for ending a test.
Second order
A customer ordering again soon after, for forgotten items.
Courier trip cost
The cost of one extra delivery.
Ask
The specific decision you want.

# Pitch: add items after ordering · Grace → Femke · 30 Nov 2026 · 10 min

ANSWER    Let express customers add items for 10 minutes after they order.
WHY       8 % of express customers place a 2nd order within 30 min (≈ 4,900 a month)
          each costs a courier trip ≈ €4.50 → ≈ €22k a month · basket +€6 when added in-order
RISK      Picking may have started → window closes before release to store (Ana: no change
          to picking) · watch: picking delays, payment re-authorisations
ASK       4 weeks of Dee's team · test in Amsterdam, January, 50 % of customers
STOP IF   orders released to store later than today by > 1 min on average
DECISION  Yes to the test (Femke, 30 Nov) · results review in February

tools: one page; the numbers from the health page and support data; a stop rule on the test card.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • A decision in ten minutes.
  • Speaks the CEO's language.
  • Small asks get a yes.
  • Risk named up front builds trust.
  • Learning fast through a test.

What we lose

  • Hard to compress to a page.
  • Nuance waits for questions.
  • Small asks may need a second pitch.
  • Needs good numbers ready.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Building up to it

    Method, data, then the idea. Fix → the answer first.

  • Your terms

    Story points and NPS. Fix → money and customers.

  • A big ask

    “A team for a year.” Fix → a test with a stop rule.

  • Hiding the risk

    Found by the CEO's first question. Fix → name it first.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Asking leaders for money, people or a change of plan.
  • Short slots with busy executives.
  • Ideas that need a test before a commitment.
  • Any pitch that's grown past one page.

Skip it when…

  • Decisions inside your own authority: just decide and tell them.

8Self-check quiz

What comes first in a pitch?

The answer: what you want to do.

Why make the ask small?

A small, testable ask gets a yes; a big one gets “later”.

What did Grace ask Femke for?

Four weeks of Dee's team and a test in Amsterdam, with a stop rule.

How did she handle the main risk?

The edit window closes before the order is released to the store.

try it

Take an idea you want leadership to back. Write it on one page: the answer, why in their terms, the main risk, a small ask with a stop rule. Read it aloud in under three minutes.

remember!!

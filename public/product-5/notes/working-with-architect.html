Working with the Architect

TL;DR

A product owner and an architect answer different questions about the same product: why and for whom, and how, within which limits. Work as partners: meet regularly, bring your outcomes and bets early (before designs), ask for constraints and options with costs (before decisions), and record decisions on both sides (the PRD links the ADR; the ADR names the product outcome). Most clashes are just two good plans meeting too late.

  • Family: partners inside
  • Origin: product–engineering partnership; architecture decision records
  • Lives in: a weekly 30 minutes, and links between PRDs and ADRs

1The situation Larder, 12 January 2027

After the Christmas outage, Ana's new rule (ADR 0017) protects the critical windows: no changes on Fridays 17:00–20:00 or weekday mornings 07:00–09:00. Grace hears about it after it's decided, and her order-editing test, planned for Friday evenings, no longer fits. Ana didn't know about the test. Two good plans met each other too late.

  • Architecture decisions change what product can do, and when.
  • Product bets change what architecture must support.
  • Meeting only when there's a clash makes every meeting a clash.

2Real-life analogy the family and the architect

When a family builds a house, they bring how they live and what matters (a big kitchen, a quiet study); the architect brings what the ground, the budget and the building rules allow, with options and their costs. Neither designs the house alone, and both sign the plans.

3How it solves it the why early, the how before decisions

Grace and Ana now meet for thirty minutes every Tuesday. Grace brings next quarter's outcomes and bets before designs start; Ana brings constraints, options and costs before decisions are made. Decisions are recorded on both sides. For the order-editing test, Ana proposed closing the edit window before the order is released to the store, so picking never changes and the critical windows aren't touched. The test moved to weekday afternoons and ran in February.

  1. Meet regularly

    Weekly or fortnightly, not only when there's a clash.

  2. Bring the why early

    Outcomes and bets for next quarter.

  3. Ask for constraints and options

    With costs, before deciding.

  4. Share the roadmap and the ADR log

    Each reads the other's.

  5. Record on both sides

    PRD links ADR; ADR names the outcome.

  6. Disagree in private, decide together

    Then present one plan.

Vocabulary for the exam

Solution architect
Owns how the system is built to meet business needs.
ADR
Architecture decision record.
Constraint
A limit that must be respected.
Option
One possible way to do something, with its costs.
Critical window
Hours when no risky changes are allowed (ADR 0017).
Trade-off
Gaining one quality by giving up some of another.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • No late clashes between plans.
  • Cheaper options found early.
  • Decisions traceable both ways.
  • One plan presented to leadership.
  • Better designs from both views.

What we lose

  • Thirty minutes a week.
  • Plans shared before they're polished.
  • Disagreements surface earlier.
  • Two documents to keep linked.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Meeting only to fight

    Every meeting a clash. Fix → a regular slot.

  • Solutions, not problems

    “Ana, build an edit button.” Fix → bring the outcome; ask for options.

  • Decisions on one side

    An ADR without the why. Fix → record and link both sides.

  • Public disagreements

    Two plans in front of Femke. Fix → settle it together first.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Any product with an architect or tech lead.
  • Quarterly planning.
  • Bets that touch platform rules.
  • After a clash, to prevent the next one.

Skip it when…

  • Never; only the rhythm changes.

Working with Account Managers

TL;DR

Account managers bring the voice of your biggest clients, and with it the risk that one client designs the product. Work with them as partners: review requests together, regularly; for each, find the problem behind it and how many clients share it; solve shared problems for everyone (often as settings), solve others by process, and decline the rest with reasons the account manager can explain. No client-only code.

  • Origin: B2B product management; key account management
  • Lives in: a monthly request review and a decisions log Mo can share

1The situation Larder, 22 February 2027

In two months Mo has brought seventeen requests from Korenwolf: a custom invoice layout, an approval step for orders over €500, delivery photos, Korenwolf's logo in the portal, and more. Each arrives as urgent; Mo fears losing the renewal. Ben's team fears building a Korenwolf-only product that every future client will want copied.

  • Big clients' requests feel more urgent than they are.
  • Client-only features multiply upkeep.
  • Account managers need answers they can explain.

2Real-life analogy a new size, not one coat

An off-the-peg clothes shop listens when a regular asks for longer sleeves. If many customers want it, it adds a longer size for everyone. It doesn't sew one coat for one customer in its factory, because the factory would soon make nothing but exceptions.

3How it solves it the problem, how many, solve for all

Grace and Mo now hold a monthly request review, 45 minutes. For each request: what problem is behind it, how many business clients have it, and how to solve it for all. Of the seventeen: 5 built as settings for every business account (cost-centre splits, an approval threshold, delivery photos); 4 solved by process (operations or Mo); 8 declined with reasons Mo can explain (the custom invoice layout, the logo). Ruben accepted; by June two more office clients use the same settings.

  1. Review together, regularly

    Monthly with the account manager.

  2. Find the problem behind it

    Ask the client why, through the account manager.

  3. Count who shares it

    Other clients, other segments.

  4. Solve for all

    Settings and features for every account.

  5. Use process where it's enough

    Operations or the account manager.

  6. Decline with reasons

    Words the account manager can repeat.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • One product for every business client.
  • Mo has answers to give.
  • Real problems solved, not demands copied.
  • New clients benefit from the same settings.
  • Less upkeep than client forks.

What we lose

  • Some requests declined, and clients may push.
  • Settings add complexity.
  • A monthly meeting.
  • Account managers must carry hard answers.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Client-only code

    “Just for Korenwolf.” Fix → settings for every account, or no.

  • Taking requests literally

    Building the stated solution. Fix → ask for the problem.

  • No reasons

    “Product said no.” Fix → words Mo can repeat.

  • Bypassing the account manager

    Engineers called directly. Fix → one channel, the review.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • B2B products with key accounts.
  • When one client's requests dominate the backlog.
  • Before renewals.
  • Onboarding new business clients.

Skip it when…

  • Products without account managers or key clients.

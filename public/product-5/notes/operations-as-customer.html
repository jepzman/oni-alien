Operations as a Customer

TL;DR

The tools your operations staff use (pickers' handhelds, couriers' app, support's tools) are products with customers too, and they shape what end customers get. Treat them that way: give them their own outcomes, a fixed share of capacity, a roadmap reviewed with the operations lead, and a customer representative from the floor. Otherwise they get leftover time, and customer features fail in the kitchen.

  • Origin: service design; internal product management

1The situation Larder, 18 January 2027

The picker app gets whatever time is left after customer features. Joost's list of picker problems is two years old; sync conflicts at Noord still cause about twelve wrong swaps packed a week, undoing the approvals work at the last step. Joost puts it plainly: “ops is everyone's second customer and nobody's first.”

  • Internal tools get leftover time.
  • Operations problems reach customers anyway.
  • Without outcomes, internal work can't compete for priority.

2Real-life analogy the kitchen and the dining room

A restaurant owner who only buys for the dining room (new chairs, new menus) while the kitchen's oven runs cold on one side will get complaints about the food, however nice the chairs. The food that reaches the customer is made in the kitchen.

3How it solves it outcomes, capacity, roadmap, a rep

Grace and Joost gave the picker app its own outcomes: picking time per order and wrong items packed per 1,000 orders. It gets 20 % of Dee's team, a quarterly roadmap reviewed with Joost, and Tomás joins refinement every other week as the customer rep. First item: sync conflicts. By March, wrong swaps packed fell from 12 to 1 a week, and picking time per order from 6:40 to 6:05.

  1. Name the internal customers

    Pickers, couriers, support, finance.

  2. Give their tools outcomes

    Time per task, errors, satisfaction.

  3. Reserve capacity

    A fixed share, not leftovers.

  4. Run a roadmap with their lead

    Quarterly, like any product.

  5. Bring a rep from the floor

    In refinement and demos.

  6. Report their outcomes too

    In the update and the review.

Vocabulary for the exam

Internal customer
A colleague who uses a tool you own.
Operational tool
Software staff use to run the service.
Capacity allocation
A fixed share of team time for a kind of work.
Customer rep
Someone who speaks for the users in the team.
Picking time
How long it takes to pick an order.
Sync conflict
Two devices disagreeing about the same data.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • Operations problems fixed, not listed.
  • Customers benefit: fewer wrong items.
  • Trust with Joost and the stores.
  • Internal work competes on outcomes.
  • Faster stores: 35 seconds per order.

What we lose

  • Less capacity for customer features.
  • Another roadmap to run.
  • Store visits and reps take time.
  • Operations asks can grow.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Leftovers

    “When we have time.” Fix → a fixed share of capacity.

  • No outcomes

    A wish list from ops. Fix → time per task, errors.

  • Managers only

    Joost speaks for pickers. Fix → a rep from the floor.

  • Separate world

    Ops tools never in reviews. Fix → report their outcomes too.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Products with operations staff.
  • When customer problems start in operations.
  • Internal tools nobody owns.
  • Before scaling to new stores or cities.

Skip it when…

  • Products without operational users.

The Quarterly Product Review

TL;DR

A quarterly product review is where leaders see whether the product did what it set out to do. Send a short pre-read; in the meeting, cover results against goals (honestly), what you learned, the next quarter's bets and what they should move, and the decisions you need. No feature tours. Done well, it's the meeting where strategy and the roadmap get adjusted on evidence.

  • Family: communicating
  • Origin: business reviews (e.g. QBRs); OKR grading; product operations practice
  • Lives in: a quarterly calendar slot with a pre-read
  • Pairs with: OKRs, bets not promises, delivering bad news

1The situation Larder, 11 January 2027

The Q3 review in October was two hours and forty slides of features, screenshots and burndown charts. Femke left after an hour; Marco asked what any of it did for cost per order; next quarter's bets were never discussed. For Q4 Grace has harder news to bring, and a show-and-tell won't carry it.

  • Feature tours hide whether goals were met.
  • Leaders' time goes before the decisions come up.
  • No pre-read means the meeting is spent catching up.

2Real-life analogy the half-time talk

At half-time a coach doesn't replay every pass. In ten minutes: the score, what's working, what isn't, the plan for the second half, and what's needed from the bench. The same shape every match, so the players know where to listen.

Translating it to the job
In real lifeOn the product
The scoreResults against goals: 2.4 % vs 2.0 %
What's working, what isn'tWhat we learned this quarter
The second-half planNext quarter's bets and what they should move
What's needed from the benchDecisions needed today
Not replaying every passNo feature tour, no screenshots

3How it solves it pre-read, then results, lessons, bets, decisions

Grace sent a two-page pre-read three days before and kept the meeting to 45 minutes: results vs goals (swap refund tickets 2.4 % against a 2.0 % target, perfect orders 86.1 %, Korenwolf live on 4 January); what we learned (Rotterdam's slot orders drive most remaining refunds); next bets; decisions needed. No screenshots. Three decisions were taken in the room: the Q1 bets, the order-editing test, and the 2.0 % target moved to March.

  1. Send a pre-read

    Two pages, three days before.

  2. Start with results vs goals

    Including the misses.

  3. Share what you learned

    Two or three lessons that change plans.

  4. Propose next bets

    Each with the number it should move.

  5. List the decisions needed

    And take them in the room.

  6. Write down what was decided

    Same day, to everyone.

Vocabulary for the exam

Quarterly review
A regular review of results, lessons and plans with leaders.
Pre-read
A short document read before the meeting.
QBR
Quarterly business review.
Bet
A piece of work expected, not promised, to move an outcome.
Decision log
A record of what was decided and why.
Show-and-tell
A tour of features, without results.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • Decisions taken in the room.
  • Honest results, misses included.
  • Leaders' time respected.
  • Strategy adjusted on evidence.
  • One shape every quarter.

What we lose

  • The pre-read takes a day.
  • Misses are visible.
  • Leaders must read it first.
  • Less room for showing work.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Feature tours

    Forty slides of screens. Fix → results vs goals.

  • Hiding misses

    Only green numbers. Fix → show every goal, honestly.

  • No decisions

    A pleasant meeting. Fix → list the decisions needed.

  • No pre-read

    Half the time spent catching up. Fix → two pages, three days before.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Every quarter, with leadership.
  • Alongside OKR grading.
  • Before planning the next quarter.
  • When reviews have become show-and-tell.

Skip it when…

  • Teams with continuous, deep leadership involvement (rare).

8Self-check quiz

What are the four parts of the review?

Results vs goals, what we learned, next bets, decisions needed.

Why send a pre-read?

So the meeting is spent deciding, not catching up.

What decisions came out of the Q4 review?

The Q1 bets, the order-editing test, and moving the 2.0 % target to March.

What's left out?

Feature tours and screenshots.

try it

Draft a two-page pre-read for your next quarterly review: every goal against its result, two lessons, two next bets with numbers, and three decisions you need. Ask for 45 minutes instead of two hours.

remember!!

Give the half-time talk: the score, the plan, and what you need from the bench.

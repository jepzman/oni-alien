Working with Legal & Privacy

TL;DR

Legal and privacy colleagues become a gate at the end only when they're invited at the end. Bring them in at design time with a clear, one-page question: what data, whose, why, who sees it, how long it's kept. Have a short conversation, design privacy in (collect less, show less, keep less), and set up a regular office hour. Early questions cost a conversation; late ones cost weeks.

  • Family: partners inside
  • Origin: privacy by design (GDPR Art. 25); data protection impact assessments
  • Lives in: a one-page privacy question per feature, and a monthly office hour
  • Pairs with: one-page PRD, data classification, compliance by design

1The situation Larder, 9 November 2026

The live order timeline on the web shop will show the courier's first name, and Noor proposes their position on a map. Last time a privacy question came up, Lena saw the feature two days before launch and it slipped three weeks. Grace expects the same again, and is tempted not to ask.

  • Late legal reviews delay launches.
  • Not asking is worse: problems reach customers and couriers.
  • Vague questions get cautious answers.

2Real-life analogy the structural engineer

When you build a house you invite the structural engineer while you're sketching, not after the walls are up. A question on paper costs a conversation; the same question after building costs a wall.

3How it solves it a one-page question, early, and privacy designed in

Grace sent Lena one page at design time: what data, whose, why, who sees it, how long it's kept. They talked for thirty minutes. Agreed: the courier's first name only (couriers may choose a nickname); the map shown only for the last ten minutes, on approach; nothing kept after delivery; couriers told before launch. Done in a week; the timeline launched on time. Since then: a monthly privacy office hour.

  1. Ask at design time

    When designs are sketches, not code.

  2. Write a one-page question

    What data, whose, why, who sees it, how long.

  3. Talk, briefly

    Thirty minutes beats a long email thread.

  4. Design privacy in

    Collect less, show less, keep less.

  5. Tell the people affected

    Customers, couriers, staff.

  6. Hold a regular office hour

    Small questions answered early.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • No late surprises before launch.
  • Better designs: less data, shown less.
  • Trust with couriers.
  • Lena's time used well.
  • Launches on time.

What we lose

  • Writing the question takes an hour.
  • Some ideas get smaller.
  • Legal capacity is limited.
  • Not every answer is quick.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Asking at the end

    Two days before launch. Fix → at design time.

  • Vague questions

    “Is this GDPR-OK?” Fix → what, whose, why, who, how long.

  • Not asking

    Hoping nobody notices. Fix → ask early; it's cheaper.

  • Forgetting staff

    Couriers learn from customers. Fix → tell the people affected first.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Any feature using or showing personal data.
  • New markets, new data, new partners.
  • Contracts and terms changes.
  • Anything shown to people outside the company.

Skip it when…

  • Changes with no personal data and no legal effect.

Dependencies across Teams

TL;DR

When your outcome depends on other teams, make each dependency visible: what you need, from whom, needed by when, the date they agreed, and its status. Ask in person and early, explain the why, and track them together in a short weekly sync. Best of all, remove dependencies you can (build it yourself, change the design, cut the scope). Dependencies managed in scattered chat threads become surprises.

1The situation Larder, 16 November 2026

Korenwolf goes live on 4 January. Grace's work depends on three other teams: billing must create invoice lines per cost centre (Cho), Dispatch must plan van routes the evening before (Eli), operations must staff picking from 06:00 (Joost). Each has its own priorities, and each dependency lives in a different chat thread.

  • Other teams' priorities aren't yours.
  • “Should be fine” in a chat isn't a date.
  • Slips are found when they hit, not when they start.

2Real-life analogy the wedding planner's board

A wedding planner keeps a board: venue, caterer, band, flowers, each with what's needed, by when, and the date the supplier agreed. She calls each one weekly. And where she can, she removes a supplier: the cake comes from the caterer, not a separate baker.

3How it solves it visible, agreed, synced, removed

Grace listed each dependency on one board: what, who, needed by, agreed date, status. She asked each lead in person, with the why (Korenwolf's contract), and set a 15-minute weekly sync. One dependency she removed: the cost-centre field went into Ordering, built by her team, instead of waiting for billing's form. The van routes slipped a week; the board showed it three weeks early, and Eli's fix landed on 14 December.

  1. List every dependency

    What you need from whom, for which outcome.

  2. Ask in person, with the why

    Agree a date they own.

  3. Put them on one board

    Needed by, agreed, status.

  4. Sync weekly, briefly

    Fifteen minutes with the other leads.

  5. Remove what you can

    Build it, redesign, or cut the scope.

  6. Escalate early

    When an agreed date is at risk.

Vocabulary for the exam

Dependency
Something you need from another team to deliver.
Critical path
The chain of work that sets the end date.
Agreed date
A date the providing team committed to.
Programme board
A shared view of teams' dependencies and dates.
Decoupling
Removing a dependency by changing the design or ownership.
Escalation
Bringing a blocked dependency to a manager.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • Slips seen early.
  • Dates owned by the teams that deliver.
  • One place instead of five threads.
  • Fewer dependencies after removing some.
  • Better relationships through asking in person.

What we lose

  • A weekly meeting more.
  • Other teams' time.
  • Removing dependencies costs your team effort.
  • A board can become a blame list.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Assuming dates

    “Should be fine.” Fix → a date they agree to.

  • Asking by ticket

    A ticket in their backlog. Fix → ask in person, with the why.

  • Tracking without removing

    Ten dependencies forever. Fix → remove what you can.

  • Blame boards

    Red names in a slide. Fix → a shared plan, not a scorecard.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Any work that needs other teams.
  • Fixed dates and client go-lives.
  • Platform changes your feature relies on.
  • When work keeps getting blocked.

Skip it when…

  • Work fully inside your team.

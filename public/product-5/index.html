Stakeholders & Influence

Volume 5 of The Product Owner's Notebook: how a product owner works with the people around the product. 15 notes in four collections: communicate so people read it, manage up and across, partner with operations, legal and architecture, and handle the hard moments. Every note follows Grace at Larder from October 2026 to March 2027, in the same months as Ana's third notebook, and ends with a try it exercise for your own product.

the story so far

October 2026. Korenwolf has signed for three offices from January, the board expects swap refunds at 2 % by December, and Grace's work now depends on Dispatch, billing, operations and legal as much as on her own team. This volume follows her from October 2026 to March 2027: keeping people informed, managing up and across, treating operations, legal and the architect as partners, and handling fixed dates, bad news and decisions she disagrees with.

Communicating write and show so people actually take it in

  1. 01Product Update that Gets ReadA short weekly update: outcomes first, then shipped, learned, next and the asks.p. 01
  2. 02Demos that LandShow the customer's story with real data, to the people it changes, and ask for reactions.p. 02
  3. 03The One-Page PRDProblem, outcome, users, scope in and out, and open questions, on a single page.p. 03
  4. 04The Quarterly Product ReviewOnce a quarter, leaders see outcomes against goals, what was learned and the next bets.p. 04

Managing up and across your manager, the CEO, and the teams you depend on

  1. 05Managing UpNo surprises: a regular, short channel with decisions, risks and wins for your manager.p. 05
  2. 06Pitching to the CEOAnswer first, in the CEO's terms, with a small ask and a way to learn fast.p. 06
  3. 07Dependencies across TeamsSee them early, ask in person, agree dates both sides track, and remove them when you can.p. 07

Partners inside operations, legal and architecture as partners, not gates

  1. 08Operations as a CustomerTreat the pickers' and couriers' tools as a product with its own outcomes and roadmap.p. 08
  2. 09Working with Legal & PrivacyBring legal in early with a clear question, and design privacy in rather than bolting it on.p. 09
  3. 10Working with the ArchitectThe product owner brings the why and the outcome; the architect brings the constraints and options.p. 10
  4. 11Working with Account ManagersTurn a big client's requests into problems the whole product can solve, without a custom fork.p. 11

Hard moments scope, fixed dates, bad news and decisions you disagree with

  1. 12Negotiating ScopeTrade on interests, not lists: find what each side really needs and split the rest into later.p. 12
  2. 13When the Date Is FixedFix the date, flex the scope: cut lines, buffers and an early first release.p. 13
  3. 14Delivering Bad NewsEarly, in person, with the facts, the cause, the options and what you'll do.p. 14
  4. 15Disagree and CommitArgue your case once, clearly; once decided, commit fully, and agree how you'll know.p. 15

  8. Self-check quiz: tap a question to see the answer.

★The nine product ratings what a good product practice is judged on

Every note scores the pattern from 1 (poor) to 5 (great) on the same nine things, comparing an app without the pattern to the same app with it.

Customer value
Customers would notice it, and miss it if it went.
Business impact
It shows up in a number the business already tracks.
Evidence
Decided on what we learned, not on opinion or rank.
Learning speed
How soon we know if we're wrong.
Focus
The team works on a few things at once, and knows which matter most.
Shared understanding
Everyone can explain what we're building, for whom and why.
Stakeholder trust
Leaders and partners trust the plan and the updates.
Flow
Value reaches customers in small, frequent steps.
Low overhead
Time and ceremony it takes. High = light.

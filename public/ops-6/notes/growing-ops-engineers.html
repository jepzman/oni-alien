Growing Operations Engineers

TL;DR

Operations engineers grow when there's a path that describes their work (scope, incidents, automation, teaching), and when they're given real responsibility, step by step: leading a review, leading an incident, owning game days. Seniors' job includes teaching. Write the path with the people on it, have regular 1:1s with a growth goal each, and measure the lead by how well the team does without them.

1The situation Larder, August 2028

In August Bram, two years at Larder, asks Eli: “What's my next step?” There's no career path for operations, because the engineering ladder was written for product engineers shipping features. Lotte and Yusuf will ask the same soon. Eli realises the only person who has grown into the operations job at Larder is himself, by accident, at 160 %.

  • Without a path, good people leave to grow somewhere else.
  • Operations work (incidents, automation, teaching) is invisible on a feature ladder.
  • Responsibility stays with the lead, so nobody else grows into it.

2Real-life analogy training junior doctors

A hospital trains junior doctors with clear grades, each describing what you can do alone, and real responsibility, step by step, with a senior close by. Teaching is part of the senior's job, not a favour. The best seniors measure themselves by how well the juniors handle a night on their own.

3How it solves it a path by scope, incidents, automation and teaching; real responsibility

In September Eli writes an operations career path with Bram, Lotte and Yusuf, and agrees it with the engineering managers. It has three levels (SRE, senior SRE, staff SRE), each described in four areas:

  • Scope: a service → several services → the platform.
  • Incidents: handles → leads → leads major incidents and teaches.
  • Automation: scripts → whole jobs → strategy.
  • Teaching: learns → mentors → grows others.

Growth comes from real responsibility, not courses alone. Bram led Ordering's readiness review. Lotte led the first incident without Eli. Yusuf owns the game days from October. The incident lead rotates among all four. There are 1:1s every two weeks, each with one growth goal. Bram is promoted to senior SRE on 1 October. Eli's title becomes reliability lead, and he measures himself by how little he's needed at night.

  1. Write the path with the people on it

    Levels by what the work really is.

  2. Describe scope, incidents, automation, teaching

    Not tools.

  3. Hand over real responsibility

    Reviews, incidents, game days.

  4. Rotate leadership roles

    Incident lead, review facilitator.

  5. Hold regular 1:1s with a goal

    Every two weeks.

  6. Measure the lead by the team

    How well it does without them.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Borrowing the feature ladder

    Operations work invisible. Fix → describe scope, incidents, automation, teaching.

  • Keeping the interesting work

    Nobody else grows. Fix → hand it over, with support.

  • Courses instead of responsibility

    Knowledge without confidence. Fix → real work, step by step.

  • Writing the path alone

    It describes the lead's view. Fix → write it with the team.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Someone asks “what's next for me?”
  • Only one person can lead incidents.
  • A new team has formed.
  • You want to be able to leave without a gap.

Skip it when…

  • Never, if you want people to stay and grow.

Scope, incidents, automation, teaching.

How do people grow at Larder's reliability team?

By real responsibility: leading reviews, incidents, game days, plus a rotation and 1:1s.

Who did what?

Bram led the readiness review, Lotte the first incident without Eli, Yusuf owns game days.

When was Bram promoted?

1 October 2028, to senior SRE.

How does Eli measure himself now?

By how little he's needed at night.

what would you do?A SEV1 starts at 18:00. Eli is free and could lead, but it's Lotte's turn on the rotation. What happens?

Lotte leads; Eli offers to be on hand as an adviser, through her. Taking it back at the first big moment would undo the growth the rotation is for.

try it

Write down three things only you do in your team. For each, who could do it next, with your support? Hand one over this month, and agree a growth goal with that person.

remember!!

Grow people with real responsibility, step by step, and measure yourself by how little you're needed.

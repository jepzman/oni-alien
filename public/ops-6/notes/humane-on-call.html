On-Call People Can Live With

TL;DR

On-call is sustainable when it's measured (pages, night pages and hours, per rota), limited (enough people on each rota, a cap on night pages), and fairly paid and rested. When a rota goes over its limit, the fix is to change the system (the noisiest alerts get fixed before new features), not to ask people to be tougher. People leave over on-call long before they say so.

1The situation Larder, May 2028

In May, Eli measures on-call load across every rota for the first time. Dee's apps team: four people, one week in four, 9 night pages a month, and two people quietly saying they'd leave rather than do another year. Ordering: five people, 6 night pages a month. The platform rota of Eli and Bram: one week in two. Across Larder: 31 night pages a month, and nobody had counted them.

  • Small rotas mean frequent weeks and frequent nights.
  • Noisy alerts become a tax on sleep.
  • The cost shows up as resignations, not tickets.

2Real-life analogy the fire station's nights

A fire station keeps enough firefighters that each does a sensible number of night shifts. When night call-outs keep coming from the same faulty alarm in one building, the station gets the building's owner to fix the alarm. It doesn't ask its firefighters to be tougher.

3How it solves it measure, limit, pay and rest, and fix the system when it's over

From June, the same rules apply to every rota at Larder:

  • Measure monthly, per rota: pages per shift, night pages, hours worked out of hours.
  • Limits: at least six people per rota, or rotas merge; on average no more than two night pages per week-long shift.
  • Over the limit: the noisiest alerts go to a monthly pager review and are fixed before new features, much like the error budget policy.
  • Pay and rest: €300 a week as primary (Marco agrees, up from €200), time back for any night work, and the old rule: paged after midnight, start at noon.

Dee's apps rota merges with the business team's into one rota of eight. The platform rota becomes the reliability team's four-person rota when Yusuf joins in August. The pager review fixes eleven noisy alerts in two months. By September, night pages across Larder are down to 12 a month from 31, and the two people who'd planned to leave are still there.

  1. Measure load per rota

    Pages, nights, hours, monthly.

  2. Set limits

    People per rota; night pages per shift.

  3. Merge small rotas

    Six or more people each.

  4. Fix noisy alerts first when over the limit

    Before new features.

  5. Pay and rest fairly

    Money for the week, time back for nights.

  6. Ask people directly

    Load numbers miss how it feels.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Asking people to be tougher

    They leave instead. Fix → fix the system.

  • Tiny rotas

    One week in four, or two. Fix → six or more; merge if needed.

  • Pay without fixes

    Paid to be woken up for nothing. Fix → fix noisy alerts first.

  • Only numbers

    Load can be fine and still feel awful. Fix → ask people directly.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • On-call load is unmeasured.
  • Rotas are small.
  • People hint they'll leave.
  • Before giving any team a new pager.

Skip it when…

  • Teams with no out-of-hours duties.

8Self-check quiz

What's measured, per rota?

Pages per shift, night pages, hours worked out of hours.

What are Larder's limits?

At least six people per rota; on average no more than two night pages per shift.

What happens over the limit?

The noisiest alerts are fixed before new features, via the monthly pager review.

How did pay change?

€300 a week as primary, up from €200, plus time back for night work.

What was the result?

Night pages across Larder fell from 31 to 12 a month.

what would you do?A rota is over its night-page limit, but the team has a feature deadline in two weeks. What happens?

The rule says the noisiest alerts are fixed first; the team lead can ask Femke for an exception, in writing, like the error budget policy. Usually two or three alert fixes take a day and save the next month's nights.

try it

Count last month's pages for one rota: how many at night, per person? How many people share the rota? Find the single alert that woke people most, and fix it or demote it this week.

remember!!

On-call load is a design problem. Measure it, limit it, pay for it, and fix the alarm, not the people.

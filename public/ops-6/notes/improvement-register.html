Continual Improvement That Continues

TL;DR

Improvement ideas come from everywhere: reviews, problems, near misses, risks, surveys, maturity checks. Kept in separate lists, they compete, duplicate and stall. Keep one register for all of them, with a source, owner, benefit and size for each. Give it a fixed share of time, limit work in progress, and hold a short monthly choice with the people it serves. Improvement continues when it has a rhythm, not when someone feels enthusiastic.

  • Family: leading
  • Lives in: one register in the tracker; 20 % of the team's time; monthly 30 minutes
  • Pairs with: action items (vol. 2), the maturity check, automation strategy

1The situation Larder, July 2028

By July, Larder's improvement work lives in seven lists: review actions, problems, near-miss actions, risk actions, items from the IT pulse, the maturity check's focus areas, and 27 automation ideas. Each list has its own rhythm, or none. Some items appear on three lists, and others on none. The reliability team can't say what it's improving this month, or why that and not something else.

  • Improvement competes with itself.
  • Everything is started; little is finished.
  • When the team gets busy, improvement quietly stops.

2Real-life analogy the family's one board

A family replaces notes in five places with one board on the fridge: a column for “next”, a column for “doing” with room for only three cards, and “done”. Every Sunday they spend ten minutes choosing what moves into “doing”. Things finally get finished, because only three are open at a time.

3How it solves it one register, a share of time, a WIP limit, a monthly choice

In July Bram merges the seven lists into one register. Each item has its source (review, problem, near miss, risk, pulse, maturity, automation), an owner, a one-line benefit, a size and a score. Duplicates are merged: 64 items.

  • A fixed share of time: 20 % of the reliability team's week, every week, including busy weeks.
  • A work-in-progress limit: at most five items in progress at once.
  • A monthly choice: 30 minutes with Ben, Sam and Joost's operations lead, the people improvement is for. Items can be dropped on purpose, with a reason.

By October, 23 items are done and 11 have been dropped honestly, with 5 in progress. The action-item rules from 2026 still hold inside it: an owner, a date, a done test. Improvement now has a rhythm that doesn't depend on anyone's enthusiasm, including Eli's.

  1. Merge every improvement list into one

    Keep the source on each item.

  2. Give each an owner, benefit, size, score

    One line each.

  3. Reserve a fixed share of time

    Every week, busy or not.

  4. Limit work in progress

    Finish before starting.

  5. Choose monthly with the people it serves

    30 minutes.

  6. Drop items on purpose

    With a reason, so the list stays believable.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Many lists

    Duplicates and gaps. Fix → one register.

  • No reserved time

    Improvement stops when busy. Fix → a fixed share every week.

  • Starting everything

    Nothing finishes. Fix → a WIP limit.

  • Never dropping

    A list nobody believes. Fix → drop on purpose, with a reason.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Improvement ideas live in several places.
  • Things start but don't finish.
  • Improvement stops whenever the team is busy.
  • After a maturity check or a year of reviews.

Skip it when…

  • A tiny team that just talks about it weekly, and finishes things.

8Self-check quiz

What goes into the improvement register?

Everything that should get better, from reviews, problems, near misses, risks, the pulse, the maturity check and automation ideas.

How much time does it get?

20 % of the reliability team's time, every week.

What's the WIP limit?

At most five items in progress.

Who chooses, and how often?

The team with Ben, Sam and Joost's lead, monthly, in 30 minutes.

What happened by October?

23 done, 11 dropped with reasons, 5 in progress.

what would you do?A busy month: Christmas preparations start, and someone suggests pausing the 20 % until January. What do you say?

Keep it, perhaps on peak-related items: improvement that stops whenever it's busy never continues. If something must give, lower the WIP limit, not the time.

try it

Find every list of improvement ideas your team keeps. Merge them into one with a source on each. Reserve a share of next week for it, choose three items, and drop one on purpose.

remember!!

Improvement continues when it has one list, a share of time and a rhythm, not when someone feels enthusiastic.

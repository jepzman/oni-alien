Who Runs What?

TL;DR

There are two classic answers to “who runs a service?”: the team that builds it (they know it best, and feel its pain) or a specialist reliability team (deep operational skill, round-the-clock cover). Most organisations need both: product teams run their own services by default, and a reliability team runs the most critical ones, but only after a readiness review, with the product team as second line and a way to hand back. Write the terms down.

1The situation Larder, April 2028

Larder now has 30 engineers in five product teams, and on-call is uneven. Ben's ordering team carries the pager for checkout, the most critical service, with five people. Dee's team has four on a one-in-four rota for the apps. Eli and Bram carry the platform, and are everyone's escalation. Femke and Ana agree that Larder needs a reliability team by the autumn. Nobody has decided what it would actually run.

  • A reliability team that runs everything becomes the new bottleneck.
  • One that runs nothing is just another name for Eli and Bram.
  • Product teams worry about losing ownership, or being left alone with it.

2Real-life analogy wards and intensive care

In a hospital, each ward runs its own beds, day and night, because it knows its patients. But intensive care has a specialist team, because those patients need round-the-clock experts. Patients move into intensive care only after a check that they need it, and back out when they're stable. Both sides know the terms.

3How it solves it build it, run it by default; tier 1 to the reliability team, on terms

On Monday 10 April, Eli proposes a model to Femke, Ana and the tech leads, and they agree it:

  • By default, product teams build and run their own services: their own on-call and SLOs, with tools, training and help from the reliability team.
  • The reliability team (Eli as lead, Bram, and two SREs to hire) runs on-call for tier 1 services: Ordering from 1 October, then Dispatch in 2029. That happens only after a production readiness review.
  • The product team stays second line, fixes its own bugs, and the error budget policy still applies to it.
  • The reliability team also owns the platform, observability, the incident process and the continuity plans.
  • Either side can hand a service back if readiness drops or the error budget is spent twice in a quarter.

Ben's first question is whether his team will still be woken up. The answer: only as second line, and less often. Dee asks whether her team can join later, and the answer is yes, through the same review.

Vocabulary for the exam

You build it, you run it
The team that builds a service operates it, including on-call.
Reliability team (SRE team)
A team specialised in running and improving critical services.
Engagement model
The terms under which a reliability team takes on, helps with, or hands back a service.
Second line
The team called by the first responder for deeper knowledge.
Hand-back
Returning operational responsibility to the product team.
Platform team
A team providing shared tools and infrastructure to others.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • A reliability team for everything

    A new bottleneck; product teams stop caring. Fix → tier 1 only, after review.

  • No hand-back

    The reliability team is stuck with neglected services. Fix → write the conditions.

  • Product teams cut out

    They stop learning from production. Fix → second line, budgets apply.

  • Deciding without the teams

    Resistance from day one. Fix → agree it with every tech lead.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • On-call is uneven or held by heroes.
  • You're forming a reliability or platform team.
  • Product teams ask who should run their service.
  • Critical services need round-the-clock expertise.

Skip it when…

  • Small organisations where one team runs everything well.

8Self-check quiz

What's Larder's default?

Product teams build and run their own services.

What does the reliability team run?

Tier 1 services' on-call (Ordering from 1 October), after a readiness review, plus platform, observability, incident process and continuity.

What stays with the product team?

Second line, fixing its bugs, and the error budget policy.

When can a service be handed back?

If readiness drops or the budget is spent twice in a quarter, with 4 weeks' notice.

Why not have the reliability team run everything?

It becomes a bottleneck, and product teams stop learning from production.

what would you do?Grace's new recipes feature team asks the reliability team to run its service from day one. What do you say?

It's not tier 1, so by default the team runs it, with the reliability team's help (SLOs, alerts, runbooks). If it ever becomes tier 1, it can go through a readiness review.

try it

List your services by tier and who carries their pager today. Which are run by accident? Write a one-page model: who runs what by default, who runs the critical few, on what terms, and how to hand back.

remember!!

What to Automate Next

TL;DR

Automation ideas are endless; time isn't. Choose by numbers: hours of toil saved, how often the manual way goes wrong, and risk removed. Automate the whole job, so that in the normal case no person has to start, click or check it, because half-automation still needs someone at 5 a.m. Keep a toil budget: when toil goes over it, automation beats features. And make new things born automated.

1The situation Larder, June 2028

The reliability team's automation wish list in June has 27 ideas, from a fully scripted city set-up to a chat bot that answers “is it down?”. Everyone has a favourite. Half the scripts written so far automate part of a job: the city script sets up zones, but someone still clicks through monitoring and on-call; the restore check runs, but someone still starts the restore by hand. The team's toil is 28 % of its time, and Lotte and Yusuf are about to add a lot of new hours.

  • Favourites win over the jobs that cost the most.
  • Half-automation keeps a person in the loop at the worst moments.
  • New services keep arriving with handwork built in.
meanwhile, in Ana’s notebook
  • Golden Paths (Ana, vol. 6): Ana's golden path of 8 May 2028: `larder new-service`, from new service to first deploy in 1 day instead of 3 weeks.

2Real-life analogy the farmer's next machine

A farmer choosing her next machine counts which job takes the most hours across the year, and which goes wrong most dangerously, and buys for that first. She doesn't buy half a machine: a milking robot that still needs someone to carry the milk to the tank saves nothing at 5 a.m.

3How it solves it score by numbers, automate end to end, keep a toil budget

From June the team uses three rules:

  • Score every idea: hours of toil saved per month × how often the manual way goes wrong, plus risk removed. The top three go into the quarter.
  • Automate end to end: a job counts as automated only when, in the normal case, no person has to start, click or check it.
  • A toil budget: the team keeps toil under 30 % of its time. Above that, automation comes before feature requests from other teams.

Q3 2028's three: new-city set-up end to end (larder new-city creates zones, the WMS route, monitoring, on-call and the status page in 20 minutes, used for Haarlem in August); restore tests that start, check and report themselves; and access review lists generated from the identity provider. With Ana's golden path, every new service is born automated: an SLO file, alerts, a dashboard, a runbook skeleton and a catalogue entry. Team toil falls from 28 % in June to 19 % in October, with two new people on the team.

  1. Log toil as a team

    Hours per kind, monthly.

  2. Score ideas by numbers

    Hours × errors + risk.

  3. Take the top three per quarter

    Not the favourites.

  4. Automate the whole job

    No start, click or check in the normal case.

  5. Keep a toil budget

    Above it, automation first.

  6. Make new things born automated

    Through the golden path.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Automating favourites

    Fun scripts, little saved. Fix → score by numbers.

  • Half-automation

    Still needs someone at 5 a.m. Fix → the whole job, or it doesn't count.

  • Automating a broken process

    Faster mistakes. Fix → fix the process first.

  • No budget

    Toil creeps back as the business grows. Fix → a toil budget, checked monthly.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • The automation list is long and argued about.
  • Scripts still need someone to click.
  • Toil is rising.
  • New services keep arriving with handwork.

Skip it when…

  • One-off jobs: automation costs more than it saves.

What's the toil budget?

New services come with SLOs, alerts, dashboard, runbook skeleton and catalogue entry from the golden path.

what would you do?Yusuf wants to spend two weeks on the “is it down?” chat bot because stores ask him that often. It scored 19th. What do you say?

Look at why stores ask: the self-service page's “just us?” may need fixing (an hour). If the asking is real toil, log it and it'll score higher next quarter. Two weeks go to the top three.

try it

List five things you'd like to automate. For each, estimate hours saved per month and how often the manual way goes wrong. Which is first by numbers, not by preference? Does your current script do the whole job?

Leading Change in How Teams Work

TL;DR

New ways of working spread when they make people's own lives easier, not when a page says they should. Start with one willing team, help them in person, make the new way easier than the old (templates, tools), show a visible win in their numbers, and be patient with the rest. Resistance is often a fair question (“what does this cost us?”) waiting for a good answer.

1The situation Larder, June 2028

With “build it, run it” as the model, the reliability team wants every product team to set SLOs, error budgets and readiness checks for its own services. Eli sends a well-written page to all five teams. Dee's team answers “more process, less building”. Two teams don't answer at all. Ben's team already does it for Ordering. A page from the reliability team isn't changing anyone's habits.

  • People hear “more work” before they hear “less pain”.
  • A central team asking others to change sounds like a demand.
  • Silence isn't agreement.

2Real-life analogy the town's new recycling

A town introducing a new recycling scheme finds that a leaflet through every door changes little. What works: one street tries it first, with help; the new bins are easier than before; neighbours see the clean street and the lower bill; and the next streets ask to join. People adopt what makes their own life easier.

3How it solves it a willing pilot, help in person, an easier path, a visible win, patience

Eli changes approach in July:

  • A willing pilot: Cho's payments team, the most curious. Lotte sits with them for two days: they write SLOs together, set burn-rate alerts, and delete five noisy alerts. Their night pages fall from 4 to 1 a month.
  • Make it easier than before: the golden path now creates the SLO file, burn alerts and readiness checklist for any service, and the reliability team offers a two-hour session per team. Doing it is now less work than not doing it.
  • A visible win, told by the team: Cho, not Eli, shows the pager numbers at the engineering all-hands on Thursday 31 August.
  • Patience, and listening: Dee's “more process” was a fair worry about time. Her team's rota merge (this volume) answered it, and in September they ask to join. The data team follows in October.

By October, four of five teams have SLOs for their own services. Nobody was ordered to.

  1. Find a willing team

    Curious, not forced.

  2. Help in person

    Sit with them; do it together.

  3. Make the new way easier than the old

    Templates, tools, the golden path.

  4. Let them show their win

    Their numbers, their voice.

  5. Listen to resistance

    It's often a fair question about cost.

  6. Be patient

    Next teams ask when they see it working.

Vocabulary for the exam

Organisational change
Changing how people and teams work, not just systems.
Pilot
A first, small use of a change with a willing group.
Adoption
People choosing to use a new way of working.
Resistance
Pushback, often carrying a real concern.
Early adopters
The first people or teams willing to try something.
Path of least resistance
Making the desired way the easiest one.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Change by memo

    Compliance or silence. Fix → a pilot, in person.

  • Starting with the most resistant

    A fight you'll lose. Fix → the most willing first.

  • More work than before

    Nobody adopts it. Fix → make it easier than the old way.

  • Dismissing resistance

    You miss the real problem. Fix → listen: it's often about cost or time.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • You want other teams to change how they work.
  • A memo or page had no effect.
  • A central team is seen as imposing process.
  • A good practice works for one team and should spread.

Skip it when…

  • Changes required by law with a deadline: still help, but say it plainly.

8Self-check quiz

Why didn't the page work?

It asked for more work from a central team; it didn't make anyone's life easier.

Who was the pilot, and what changed for them?

Cho's payments team; night pages fell from 4 to 1 a month.

How was the new way made easier?

The golden path creates SLO files, alerts and checklists; two-hour sessions per team.

Why did Cho, not Eli, present at the all-hands?

A team's own win in its own voice persuades better than the central team's.

What did Dee's “more process” really mean?

A fair worry about time, answered by the rota merge.

what would you do?The business team still hasn't set SLOs in December. Femke suggests making it mandatory. What do you suggest?

Ask the team why (Haarlem took their autumn), offer the two-hour session at a better time, and show them payments' and apps' numbers. Mandate only if there's a real risk; usually patience costs less.

try it

Think of a practice you want other teams to adopt. Who's most willing? What would make it easier than their current way? What win would they see in their own numbers, and who should tell it?

remember!!

People adopt what makes their own life easier. One willing team, help in person, an easier path, and their win in their voice.

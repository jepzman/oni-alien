Did the Handover Work?

TL;DR

A handover isn't finished when the pager moves. Plan a period of early life support: the old team stays close as second line, there are short, frequent check-ins, and every page is reviewed. Write an exit test in advance (weeks without a major incident, incidents handled alone, runbooks complete, SLOs met), so “is it done?” has an answer. The best sign of success is an incident handled well without the people who used to be essential.

1The situation Larder, Monday 2 October 2028

The day after the handover, Femke asks Eli at the Monday meeting: “So, is it done?” The plan ended at “pager moves”. Nobody has said how anyone would know it worked: when Ben's team stops being on standby by default, when the extra checks end, or what success would even look like.

  • Without an end, the old team never really lets go.
  • Without checks, small gaps grow quietly.
  • Without a test, “done” is a feeling.

2Real-life analogy going home after an operation

A hospital doesn't just discharge a patient after an operation. A nurse calls daily for the first week, there's a check-up at two weeks, and a clear sign that recovery is complete: walking unaided, the wound healed. Then the follow-up ends, on purpose, not because everyone forgot.

3How it solves it a month of close care, and an exit test written first

Eli writes early life support for October, agreed with Ben:

  • Ben's team stays second line, and joins a 10-minute check-in every day in the first week, then twice a week.
  • Every page is reviewed the next morning, and its runbook is updated if anything was missing.
  • Exit test, written on 2 October: four weeks without a SEV1; the reliability team handles at least two incidents without calling Ben's team; every page in October has a runbook; SLOs met.

The first incident comes on Monday 9 October: checkout slows after a deploy at 17:31. Lotte leads and Yusuf rolls the deploy back using the way back in its deploy line. It's 18 minutes from page to normal. Eli is in a meeting and reads about it afterwards. It's the moment the whole book has been working towards. On 31 October the exit test is met, all four parts, and Ben's team goes back to being second line only when asked.

  1. Plan the weeks after the handover

    Not just the day.

  2. Keep the old team close, as second line

    With a clear role.

  3. Check in briefly and often, then less

    Daily, then twice a week.

  4. Review every page

    And fix the runbook the next morning.

  5. Write the exit test on day one

    Measurable, agreed by both teams.

  6. End it on purpose

    Say so, and celebrate the first incident handled alone.

Vocabulary for the exam

Early life support
Extra support and monitoring just after a service or responsibility is handed over.
Hypercare
Another name for a period of intensive support after a launch.
Exit test
Agreed conditions that end early life support.
Stabilisation
Getting to steady, predictable operation.
Check-in
A short, regular meeting during the support period.
Letting go
The old team stepping back on purpose.

4The maturity ladder where are we, and what is the next rung?

1By hand

handover = pager moves

  • No end, no test.
  • The old team never lets go.

2Written down Larder now

early life support with an exit test

  • Close care, then an end on purpose.
  • Every page reviewed.

3Scripted

the same for every transition

  • Launches, handovers and migrations share it.
  • Exit tests reused.

transitions are routine

  • Short support periods.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • No exit test

    Support never ends, or ends by neglect. Fix → write it on day one.

  • The old team as first line in disguise

    They're called for everything. Fix → second line, asked not assumed.

  • Skipping page reviews

    Small gaps grow. Fix → every page, next morning.

  • Not marking the end

    Nobody knows it's over. Fix → say so, and celebrate.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • After any handover of responsibility.
  • After a launch or migration.
  • When a new team takes on-call.
  • When leadership asks “is it done?”

Skip it when…

  • Tiny changes with no new people or responsibilities.

8Self-check quiz

What is early life support?

A period of extra support and checking just after a handover, ending with an exit test.

What was Larder's exit test?

Four weeks without a SEV1, two incidents handled alone, every page with a runbook, SLOs met.

What happened on 9 October?

Checkout slowed after a deploy; Lotte led, Yusuf rolled back; 18 minutes; Eli wasn't involved.

Why write the exit test on day one?

So “done” is measurable and agreed, not a feeling.

What's Ben's team's role after 31 October?

Second line, only when asked.

what would you do?In week three the reliability team calls Ben's team for every database question, even easy ones. The exit test says “two incidents without calling them”. What do you do?

Look at why: missing runbooks or confidence? Write the runbooks, pair on the next questions, and keep early life support going past 31 October if needed. Ending on time matters less than ending for the right reasons.

try it

Think of a recent handover or launch. When did the extra support end, and how did anyone know it had worked? Write an exit test you could have used.

remember!!

A handover ends when the exit test is met, not when the pager moves. The real sign: an incident handled well without you.

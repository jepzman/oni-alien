Jef’s Handbooks · Handbook 2

Identity and Access in the Cloud

From passwords to permission guardrails

Who it's for

Developers, operations people and new cloud engineers who copy IAM policies from the internet and hope. You can log in to a cloud console and run a CLI; by the end you can design least-privilege access for people and workloads on AWS, debug any “access denied”, and read the same setup on Azure or Google Cloud.

How it's built

Twenty-five chapters in six parts. Part I is provider-neutral: how proving who you are and deciding what you may do actually work. Part II takes AWS IAM apart, down to how a single request is allowed or denied. Parts III and IV give access to people and to software, without passwords or long-lived keys. Part V scales it across many accounts, and Part VI is about running it: auditing, detection and identity as code. Every chapter ends with a lab or a design exercise and questions to check yourself. IAM itself is free, so nearly every lab costs nothing.

The running example

The running example is Larder, the Amsterdam grocery-delivery company from the Practitioner’s Notebooks and Handbook 1. It begins with one AWS account its first CTO opened in 2024, shared IAM users and a deploy key with full admin. By the end, Larder has one identity per person, no long-lived keys, and guardrails across every account. You don’t need to have read the other books.

Start at Chapter 1, or read every written chapter on one page (also as a PDF). Chapters marked “coming” are planned; the topics they'll cover are listed.

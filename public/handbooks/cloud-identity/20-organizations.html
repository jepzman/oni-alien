Part V · Many accounts

AWS Organizations and guardrails

One account is a single blast radius. Many accounts, grouped and governed together, let you separate production from experiments, keep logs where nobody working in production can touch them, and draw lines that no administrator in any account can cross. This chapter explains how an AWS Organization is structured, how service control policies and resource control policies set those lines, and how to design a small organisation that can grow.

By the end you can

  • Explain the management account, organisational units and member accounts, and what each is for.
  • Design an account and OU structure for a growing company.
  • Write SCPs for the classic guardrails, and predict how they combine down the tree.
  • Remove root credentials from member accounts.
  • Delegate security services to a member account.

20.1The pieces of an organisation

An AWS Organization groups accounts under central management and billing. It has four parts:

  • The management account creates the organisation, pays for it, and can create, invite and remove member accounts and attach policies. SCPs and RCPs don't restrict it, so it should hold no workloads and as few people as possible (Chapter 7, Chapter 12).
  • Member accounts hold everything else, each still its own security boundary.
  • Organisational units (OUs) are folders of accounts, nestable up to five levels, to which policies attach. An account is in exactly one OU (or directly under the root).
  • Policies attached to the root, an OU or an account: SCPs and RCPs for permissions (20.3), and others for backups, tags and AI opt-outs.

20.2Designing accounts and OUs

Accounts are cheap, and each one is a fresh, empty boundary. Use them to separate things with different risks, owners or rules. OUs group accounts that should share the same guardrails, so design OUs around policy, not the org chart. A structure that works for most companies:

  • Security: an account for security services and the organisation's logs, which people in workload accounts can't change (Chapter 23); a backup account for copies that a compromised production account can't delete.
  • Infrastructure: shared platform pieces such as tooling, and later networking (Handbook 1, Chapter 24).
  • Workloads, split into Prod and NonProd OUs, so production gets stricter guardrails without new accounts inheriting the wrong ones.
  • Sandbox: individual accounts for experiments, with tight limits and budgets, far from production data.

Start small and add accounts when there's a reason: a separate team, a separate risk, a separate customer. Moving an account between OUs later is easy; untangling two workloads that share an account is not.

20.3Service control policies

A service control policy (SCP) sets the maximum permissions for every principal in the member accounts it applies to, including those accounts' root users (Chapter 10, 10.8). It never grants anything: a principal still needs IAM permissions. Two rules decide how SCPs combine down the tree:

  1. An explicit deny anywhere on the path from the root to the account wins.
  2. Every level must allow. An action must be allowed by some SCP attached at the root, and at each OU on the way down, and at the account. Each level is a filter.

By default every root, OU and account has the AWS managed SCP FullAWSAccess attached, which lets everything through. That gives two styles: deny lists, adding specific denies and keeping FullAWSAccess (simple, the usual choice), and allow lists, replacing FullAWSAccess at an OU with a list of allowed services (tight, good for sandboxes, more upkeep).

Key idea

IAM decides what someone may do; SCPs decide what anyone in that account could ever do. Put the lines nobody should cross in SCPs, where no account administrator can remove them.

20.4The classic guardrails

baseline SCPattached to the organisation root

{
  "Version": "2012-10-17",
  "Statement": [
    { "Sid": "StayInTheOrganisation", "Effect": "Deny",
      "Action": "organizations:LeaveOrganization", "Resource": "*" },
    { "Sid": "KeepTheAuditTrail", "Effect": "Deny",
      "Action": ["cloudtrail:StopLogging", "cloudtrail:DeleteTrail", "cloudtrail:UpdateTrail"],
      "Resource": "*",
      "Condition": { "ArnNotLike": { "aws:PrincipalArn": "arn:aws:iam::*:role/break-glass-admin" } } },
    { "Sid": "OnlyIMDSv2", "Effect": "Deny",
      "Action": "ec2:RunInstances", "Resource": "arn:aws:ec2:*:*:instance/*",
      "Condition": { "StringNotEquals": { "ec2:MetadataHttpTokens": "required" } } },
    { "Sid": "OnlyFrankfurt", "Effect": "Deny",
      "NotAction": ["iam:*", "organizations:*", "sts:*", "sso:*", "identitystore:*", "support:*",
                    "cloudfront:*", "route53:*", "budgets:*", "ce:*", "health:*", "account:*"],
      "Resource": "*",
      "Condition": { "StringNotEquals": { "aws:RequestedRegion": "eu-central-1" } } }
  ]
}
  • StayInTheOrganisation: an attacker who controls an account can't take it out from under your guardrails.
  • KeepTheAuditTrail: nobody but break-glass can stop or change the organisation's CloudTrail (Chapter 23).
  • OnlyIMDSv2: no instance can launch with the old metadata service (Chapter 15).
  • OnlyFrankfurt: everything except global services is denied outside eu-central-1, the legitimate use of NotAction in a deny promised in Chapter 9. Global services (IAM, Organizations, CloudFront, Route 53…) are served from us-east-1, so they're exempt; AWS publishes a fuller example list. Add a region the day you need one, deliberately.

Watch out: SCPs can break everything at once

An SCP attached to the root applies to every member account the moment you save it. Detaching FullAWSAccess from an OU with nothing else attached denies everything below it. Test new SCPs on a sandbox OU first, roll them out OU by OU, keep them in code with review (Chapter 24), and keep the break-glass role as an exception where it matters. SCPs don't affect service-linked roles, so AWS services keep working.

20.5Resource control policies

An SCP limits what principals in your accounts can do. A resource control policy (RCP) limits what can be done to resources in your accounts, by anyone, including principals from other organisations. They support a growing list of services, starting with S3, STS, KMS, SQS and Secrets Manager. RCPs are how you say “no bucket in this organisation can be read by an identity outside it, whatever a bucket policy says”, the foundation of the data perimeter in Chapter 22.

20.6Root credentials and delegated administration

With centralised root access (Chapter 7, 7.6) enabled in the organisation, administrators in the management account can delete member accounts' root passwords, access keys and MFA devices, and perform the few root-only tasks through short task-scoped sessions:

aws iam enable-organizations-root-credentials-management
aws iam enable-organizations-root-sessions
aws sts assume-root --target-principal 111122223333 \
  --task-policy-arn arn=arn:aws:iam::aws:policy/root-task/IAMDeleteRootUserCredentials

New accounts created in the organisation then start with no root credentials at all.

Delegated administration lets a member account run an organisation-wide security service (GuardDuty, Security Hub, IAM Access Analyzer, CloudTrail organisation trails' administration), so the people who operate those services don't need the management account. Larder's security account is the delegated administrator for all of them; only a handful of people ever sign in to the management account.

20.8Across providers

Table 20.1 · Organising many accounts at the big three

Permission ceilingsSCPs (principals), RCPs (resources)none for RBAC as such; deny assignments are system-createdIAM deny policies at organisation, folder or project

Landing-zone toolingControl TowerAzure landing zonesCloud Foundation Toolkit, Fabric

On Azure

Management groups hold subscriptions, and both RBAC role assignments and Azure Policy assignments inherit down them. Azure Policy is where most guardrails live: allowed locations, required tags, “no public IPs”, with effects such as deny, audit and modify. It governs resource configuration rather than who may call which API, which is the job SCPs do on AWS.

On Google Cloud

Folders group projects, IAM allow policies inherit down the hierarchy, and organisation policy constraints set configuration rules such as allowed resource locations or “no service account keys”. For permission ceilings, IAM deny policies attached to the organisation or a folder are the closest thing to SCPs.

20.9Lab: SCPs down a tree

Lab 20 · Larder's organisation in a hundred lines

You'll evaluate requests from different accounts against Larder's OU tree and SCPs, predict which level stops each one, then break the tree on purpose. Optionally, you'll test one SCP in a sandbox organisation. You need Python 3; for the optional part, the AWS CLI as an administrator of a sandbox organisation's management account. Cost: free.

scp_eval.pyPython 3.8+

# scp_eval.py · service control policies down an organisation tree: allowed at every level, denied by any
from fnmatch import fnmatchcase

def as_list(x):
    return x if isinstance(x, list) else [x]

FULL = {"Effect": "Allow", "Action": "*", "Resource": "*"}                       # FullAWSAccess
GLOBAL = ["iam:*", "organizations:*", "sts:*", "sso:*", "identitystore:*", "support:*",
          "cloudfront:*", "route53:*", "budgets:*", "ce:*", "health:*", "account:*"]
BASELINE = [
    {"Effect": "Deny", "Action": "organizations:LeaveOrganization", "Resource": "*"},
    {"Effect": "Deny", "Action": ["cloudtrail:StopLogging", "cloudtrail:DeleteTrail"], "Resource": "*",
     "Condition": {"ArnNotLike": {"aws:PrincipalArn": "arn:aws:iam::*:role/break-glass-admin"}}},
    {"Effect": "Deny", "Action": "ec2:RunInstances", "Resource": "*",
     "Condition": {"StringNotEquals": {"ec2:MetadataHttpTokens": "required"}}},
    {"Effect": "Deny", "NotAction": GLOBAL, "Resource": "*",
     "Condition": {"StringNotEquals": {"aws:RequestedRegion": "eu-central-1"}}},
]
PROTECT_ORDERS_DB = [
    {"Effect": "Deny", "Action": ["rds:ModifyDBInstance", "rds:DeleteDBInstance"],
     "Resource": "arn:aws:rds:*:*:db:orders-prod",
     "Condition": {"ArnNotLike": {"aws:PrincipalArn": [
         "arn:aws:iam::*:role/aws-reserved/sso.amazonaws.com/*AWSReservedSSO_DbChange_*",
         "arn:aws:iam::*:role/break-glass-admin"]}}},
    {"Effect": "Deny", "Action": ["rds:DeleteDBSnapshot", "rds:DeleteDBInstanceAutomatedBackup"], "Resource": "*"},
]
SANDBOX_ALLOW = [{"Effect": "Allow", "Action": ["ec2:*", "s3:*", "lambda:*", "logs:*", "iam:*"], "Resource": "*"}]

TREE = {   # node: (parent, SCPs attached)
    "Root":           (None,             [FULL] + BASELINE),
    "Security":       ("Root",           [FULL]),
    "Infrastructure": ("Root",           [FULL]),
    "Workloads":      ("Root",           [FULL]),
    "Prod":           ("Workloads",      [FULL] + PROTECT_ORDERS_DB),
    "NonProd":        ("Workloads",      [FULL]),
    "Sandbox":        ("Root",           SANDBOX_ALLOW),
    "prod":           ("Prod",           [FULL]),
    "dev-staging":    ("NonProd",        [FULL]),
    "sandbox-dee":    ("Sandbox",        [FULL]),
}
MANAGEMENT = "management"

def matches(st, req):
    acts = st.get("Action")
    if acts is not None and not any(fnmatchcase(req["action"], a) for a in as_list(acts)):
        return False
    if "NotAction" in st and any(fnmatchcase(req["action"], a) for a in as_list(st["NotAction"])):
        return False
    if not any(fnmatchcase(req["resource"], r) for r in as_list(st["Resource"])):
        return False
    for op, tests in st.get("Condition", {}).items():
        for key, want in tests.items():
            have = req.get(key, "")
            hit = any(fnmatchcase(have, w) for w in as_list(want))
            if op in ("StringNotEquals", "StringNotLike", "ArnNotLike") and hit:
                return False
    return True

def decide(account, req):
    if account == MANAGEMENT:
        return "ALLOW  (SCPs don't apply to the management account)"
    path, node = [], account
    while node:
        path.append(node)
        node = TREE[node][0]
    for level in reversed(path):                     # any deny, anywhere on the path, wins
        if any(st["Effect"] == "Deny" and matches(st, req) for st in TREE[level][1]):
            return f"DENY   explicit deny at {level}"
    for level in reversed(path):                     # and every level must allow
        if not any(st["Effect"] == "Allow" and matches(st, req) for st in TREE[level][1]):
            return f"DENY   nothing allows it at {level}"
    return "ALLOW  (now IAM decides, Chapter 10)"

SSO = "arn:aws:iam::111122223333:role/aws-reserved/sso.amazonaws.com/eu-central-1/AWSReservedSSO_{}_3f9a"
DB = "arn:aws:rds:eu-central-1:111122223333:db:orders-prod"
def r(action, resource="*", region="eu-central-1", who="arn:aws:iam::111122223333:role/some-role", **extra):
    return dict(action=action, resource=resource, **{"aws:RequestedRegion": region, "aws:PrincipalArn": who}, **extra)

cases = [
    ("prod",        "ProdOperator changes orders DB", r("rds:ModifyDBInstance", DB, who=SSO.format("ProdOperator"))),
    ("prod",        "DbChange changes orders DB",     r("rds:ModifyDBInstance", DB, who=SSO.format("DbChange"))),
    ("prod",        "a brand-new role, no boundary",  r("rds:ModifyDBInstance", DB, who="arn:aws:iam::111122223333:role/new-thing")),
    ("prod",        "PlatformAdmin stops CloudTrail", r("cloudtrail:StopLogging", who=SSO.format("PlatformAdmin"))),
    ("prod",        "break-glass stops CloudTrail",   r("cloudtrail:StopLogging", who="arn:aws:iam::111122223333:role/break-glass-admin")),
    ("dev-staging", "instance allowing IMDSv1",       r("ec2:RunInstances", **{"ec2:MetadataHttpTokens": "optional"})),
    ("dev-staging", "instance requiring IMDSv2",      r("ec2:RunInstances", **{"ec2:MetadataHttpTokens": "required"})),
    ("sandbox-dee", "instance in us-east-1",          r("ec2:RunInstances", region="us-east-1", **{"ec2:MetadataHttpTokens": "required"})),
    ("sandbox-dee", "a database in a sandbox",        r("rds:CreateDBInstance")),
    ("sandbox-dee", "a role (IAM is global)",         r("iam:CreateRole", region="us-east-1")),
    (MANAGEMENT,    "stop CloudTrail in management",  r("cloudtrail:StopLogging")),
]
for account, name, req in cases:
    print(f"{account:<12} {name:<32} {decide(account, req)}")

20.10Common mistakes

MistakeWhat happensInstead
Workloads in the management accountNo SCP protects them; compromise reaches the whole organisation.An empty management account; delegated administration.
OUs that mirror the org chartPolicies don't fit; reorganisations mean moving accounts.OUs by policy: security, infrastructure, prod, non-prod, sandbox.
Guardrails as identity policies onlyNew or forgotten identities bypass them.SCPs (and RCPs) where no account admin can remove them.
Untested SCPs attached at the rootEvery account breaks at once.Sandbox OU first, then OU by OU, from code with review.
Region denies without global-service exemptionsIAM, Organizations, CloudFront and friends stop working.Exempt global services with NotAction.
Member root credentials left in placeUnrestricted credentials nobody watches.Centralised root access; delete member root credentials.

✓Chapter summary

  • An organisation is a management account, member accounts and OUs; policies attach to the root, OUs and accounts.
  • Design OUs around policy: security, infrastructure, prod, non-prod, sandbox. Keep the management account empty.
  • SCPs cap what principals in member accounts can do: allowed at every level, denied by any. They never grant.
  • Classic guardrails: stay in the organisation, keep the audit trail, IMDSv2 only, approved regions, protect critical data.
  • RCPs cap what anyone can do to resources in your accounts, the basis of a data perimeter.
  • Remove member root credentials with centralised root access; run security services from a delegated account.

Key terms

AWS Organization
A group of accounts under central management, billing and policy.
Management account
The account that owns the organisation; not restricted by SCPs or RCPs.
Organisational unit (OU)
A container of accounts to which policies attach.
SCP
Service control policy: a ceiling on principals' permissions in member accounts.
RCP
Resource control policy: a ceiling on access to resources in member accounts.
FullAWSAccess
The default SCP that allows everything through a level.
Delegated administrator
A member account allowed to run an organisation-wide service.

Check your understanding

  1. An OU has only an allow-list SCP permitting S3 and EC2. A role in an account below it has AdministratorAccess. Can it create a DynamoDB table? recall

    Show answer

    No. Every level must allow the action, and that OU's SCPs don't allow DynamoDB; IAM never gets asked.

  2. Why is moving protect-orders-db from an identity policy to an SCP a real improvement? explain

    Show answer

    The identity policy only protected identities it was attached to; any new or forgotten role bypassed it, and account admins could detach it. The SCP applies to every principal in the account, including future ones and the root user, and can only be changed from the organisation's management.

  3. A region-restriction SCP denies everything outside eu-central-1. Engineers can no longer create IAM roles. Why, and what's the fix? apply

    Show answer

    IAM is a global service whose requests are made in us-east-1, so the deny catches them. Exempt global services with NotAction, as in 20.4.

  4. Someone proposes running the CI/CD tooling in the management account, “since it deploys to everything”. What do you say? explain

    Show answer

    No: SCPs don't restrict the management account, and a compromise of tooling there would control the whole organisation. Run it in a tooling account under Infrastructure, assuming narrowly scoped roles in workload accounts.

  5. Design the OUs and SCPs for a company adding a data-science team that needs GPUs in a second region. design

    Show answer

    A new OU (say Workloads/Data) whose accounts may also use the second region: the root's region guardrail moves down to each OU, or gains an exception condition for that OU's accounts. Keep IMDSv2, audit-trail and leave-organisation denies at the root; add a budget alarm and tag policy for GPU spend.

  6. Compare where you'd enforce “resources only in approved regions” on AWS and on Azure. compare

    Show answer

    AWS: an SCP denying requests outside the approved regions (exempting global services). Azure: an Azure Policy assignment with the “Allowed locations” definition at a management group, which denies creating resources elsewhere.

Appendix

D

Glossary

All 206 key terms from the book's chapters, in one place. Each entry links to the chapter that explains it properly.

# · A · B · C · D · E · F · G · H · I · J · L · M · N · O · P · Q · R · S · T · U · V · W · X · Z

#

5-tuple
Protocol, source IP, source port, destination IP, destination port. Ch. 3

A

Account vending
An automated process that creates accounts with the baseline applied. Ch. 24
Address plan
The written decision of which ranges are used for what. Ch. 10
Aggregation interval
The window over which a flow log record summarises a flow. Ch. 22
Alias record
A provider feature that answers with a resource's current addresses, usable at the apex. Ch. 5
Anycast
One address announced from many places; routing delivers users to the nearest. Ch. 17
Appliance mode
Keeps both directions of a flow on the same appliance. Ch. 11
ARP
Finds the MAC address for an IP address on the local link. Ch. 4
ASN
The number that identifies a network in BGP; 64512–65534 are private. Ch. 4
AS path prepending
Lengthening a route's AS path to make it less preferred. Ch. 13
Association
The TGW route table an attachment's traffic is looked up in. Ch. 11
Asymmetric routing
Request and reply taking different paths. Ch. 4
Attachment
One network's connection to a transit gateway. Ch. 11
Authoritative server
A server whose answers for a zone are the official ones. Ch. 5
Availability zone
One or more data centres with independent power, cooling and network, inside a region. Ch. 7
awsvpc mode
ECS networking where each task has its own interface and address. Ch. 20

B

Bandwidth
How much data a link can carry per second. Ch. 1
BFD
A fast liveness check that lets BGP fail over in under a second. Ch. 14
BGP
The protocol networks use to exchange routes. Ch. 4
Block size
How wide a range is in the octet where its prefix ends. Ch. 2

C

Cache hit ratio
The share of requests answered from cache. Ch. 17
Cache key
The parts of a request that decide which cached copy answers it. Ch. 17
CDN
A network of caches that serves content from the edge. Ch. 17
Centralised egress
All spokes reaching the internet through one checkpoint VPC. Ch. 18
Certificate
A signed binding of names to a public key, valid for a time. Ch. 6
Chain of trust
Leaf, intermediates and a root the client already trusts. Ch. 6
CIDR block
A range of addresses written as address/prefix, like 10.20.0.0/16. Ch. 8
CIDR notation
A range written as address/prefix length, like 10.20.0.0/16. Ch. 2
CNAME
A record that makes one name an alias of another. Ch. 5
CNI plugin
The component that gives pods addresses and connects them. Ch. 19
Colocation facility
A building where carriers, providers and customers interconnect. Ch. 14
Conditional forwarder
A DNS server setting that sends questions about one zone to specific servers. Ch. 15
Connected route
A route to a network the device is directly attached to. Ch. 4
Connection pooler
Shares a few database connections among many clients. Ch. 20
Connection tracking
How a stateful firewall remembers connections and allows their replies. Ch. 9
Cross-connect
A short fibre between two parties' equipment in the same building. Ch. 14
Cross-zone load balancing
Each load balancer node spreading traffic across targets in all zones. Ch. 16
Customer gateway
Your router or firewall at the site end, and AWS's record of it. Ch. 13

D

Database endpoint
The DNS name that points at the current primary or reader. Ch. 20
Data transfer out
Data leaving the provider to the internet; the most expensive path. Ch. 23
DDoS
An attack that floods a service from many sources to make it unavailable. Ch. 17
Dedicated connection
A physical private circuit into a cloud provider's network. Ch. 14
Default route
0.0.0.0/0 (or ::/0): the route of last resort. Ch. 4
Delegated subnet
An Azure subnet handed to one service for its resources. Ch. 20
Delegation
A parent zone pointing to a child zone's servers with NS records. Ch. 5
Deregistration delay
How long in-flight requests may finish after a target is removed. Ch. 16
Design review
A structured check of a design against requirements and known pitfalls. Ch. 25
Device posture
The state of a device (managed, encrypted, patched) used in access decisions. Ch. 21
Direct Connect gateway
A global object linking VIFs to gateways in many regions. Ch. 14
DNS firewall
Blocking lookups of chosen domains at the resolver. Ch. 15
Domain allow-list
The names workloads may reach; everything else is blocked. Ch. 18
Drift
Real infrastructure differing from its code. Ch. 24
Dual stack
Running IPv4 and IPv6 side by side on the same network. Ch. 2
Dynamic route
A route learned from neighbours by a routing protocol. Ch. 4

E

Edge location
A small site near users, running CDN, DNS and similar services. Ch. 7
Edge / point of presence
A provider site near users that runs CDN, WAF and DDoS services. Ch. 17
Egress
Traffic leaving your network. Ch. 18
Elastic IP
A static public IPv4 address you own and can move between resources. Ch. 8
Encapsulation
Each layer wrapping the data from the layer above in its own header. Ch. 1
Endpoint policy
A policy limiting the actions and resources reachable through an endpoint. Ch. 12
Endpoint service
Your service, behind an NLB, made available to consumers' endpoints. Ch. 12
Ephemeral port
A temporary high port the client's OS picks for one connection. Ch. 3
ESP
The IPsec protocol that encrypts and carries your packets. Ch. 13
Exfiltration
Data stolen by sending it out of the network. Ch. 18

F

Fail open
Sending traffic to all targets when every one is unhealthy. Ch. 16
Failure domain
A part of the system that can fail as a unit. Ch. 7
Forwarding rule
A rule sending one domain's queries to chosen DNS servers. Ch. 15
Forward proxy
A server that makes outbound requests on clients' behalf. Ch. 18
Forward secrecy
Past sessions stay safe even if the server's key is later stolen. Ch. 6

G

Game day
A planned exercise that causes failures to test systems and people. Ch. 25
Gateway endpoint
A free route straight to S3 or DynamoDB, bypassing NAT. Ch. 8
Gateway Load Balancer
Sends traffic transparently through a fleet of appliances. Ch. 18

H

Health check
A periodic probe that decides whether a target gets traffic. Ch. 16
Hosted connection
A share of a partner's Direct Connect capacity. Ch. 14
Host header
The site name a request is for; lets one address serve many sites. Ch. 6
Host part
The remaining bits, which vary inside the range. Ch. 2
HTTP
The request/response protocol of the web and most APIs. Ch. 6
Hybrid DNS
Resolving names across cloud and on-premises networks. Ch. 15

I

Identity-aware proxy
A proxy that checks user identity and device before forwarding to one app. Ch. 21
Idle timeout
How long a device keeps an unused connection before forgetting it. Ch. 3
IKE
The protocol that authenticates the gateways and agrees keys. Ch. 13
Inbound endpoint
Addresses in a VPC that accept DNS queries from outside and pass them to the VPC resolver. Ch. 15
Ingress / Gateway API
Kubernetes resources describing HTTP routing into the cluster. Ch. 19
Interface endpoint
Network interfaces in your subnets that reach a service through PrivateLink. Ch. 12
Internet gateway
The VPC's door to the internet; maps private IPs to public ones. Ch. 8
IP address
An internet-layer address that identifies an interface across networks. Ch. 1
IPAM
A tool that holds the plan as pools and allocates ranges from it. Ch. 10
IPsec
The protocol suite for authenticated, encrypted IP tunnels. Ch. 13

J

Jumbo frame
A packet larger than 1,500 bytes; up to 9,001 inside an AWS VPC. Ch. 7

L

Landing zone
The multi-account foundation that workloads are deployed into. Ch. 24
Latency
How long a trip takes; usually given as a round trip in milliseconds. Ch. 1
Layer
One level of the network's work, using only the layer below. Ch. 1
Least privilege
Allowing only the traffic each role actually needs. Ch. 9
Listener
A port and protocol a load balancer accepts traffic on. Ch. 16
Load balancer
A service that spreads client connections or requests over healthy targets. Ch. 16
Local route
The automatic route covering the whole VPC range. Ch. 8
Longest prefix match
The rule that the most specific matching route wins. Ch. 4

M

MAC address
A link-layer address, meaningful only on one local link. Ch. 1
MACsec
Link-layer encryption between two directly connected routers. Ch. 14
Module
Reusable infrastructure code with inputs and outputs. Ch. 24
MSS
The largest TCP payload per packet, agreed in the handshake. Ch. 13
MSS clamping
A router lowering the MSS in passing handshakes so packets fit. Ch. 13
MTU
The largest packet a link can carry without splitting it, typically 1,500 bytes. Ch. 1
MTU black hole
Large packets silently dropped because the “too big” message is blocked. Ch. 13
Mutual TLS
TLS in which the client also presents a certificate. Ch. 6

N

NAT gateway
Lets private servers start outbound connections; drops unsolicited inbound ones. Ch. 8
Network Access Analyzer
Finds paths that match a pattern across an account. Ch. 22
Network ACL
A stateless, ordered allow/deny list attached to a subnet. Ch. 9
Network allowance
An instance's limits on bandwidth, packets per second and tracked connections. Ch. 22
Network policy
Label-based rules allowing traffic between pods. Ch. 19
Network tier
Google Cloud's choice between Premium and Standard internet routing. Ch. 23
Next hop
The neighbouring router or interface a matching packet is sent to. Ch. 4
NSG / ASG
Azure's network security groups and application security groups. Ch. 9
NXDOMAIN
“No such name”, itself cached for a while. Ch. 5

O

Octet
One of the four 8-bit numbers in an IPv4 address. Ch. 2
Origin
The server or bucket the CDN fetches from on a miss. Ch. 17
Origin shield
A regional cache layer between edges and the origin. Ch. 17
Outbound endpoint
Addresses in a VPC through which the resolver forwards queries elsewhere. Ch. 15
Overlap
Two ranges sharing any addresses; blocks routing between them. Ch. 2
Overlay network
A virtual network carried inside another network's packets. Ch. 7

P

Packet
A small unit of data with a header (addresses and control fields) and a payload. Ch. 1
Perimeter security
Trusting everything inside a protected network boundary. Ch. 21
Pilot light
A minimal standby environment that can be scaled up to take over. Ch. 25
Pod
The smallest unit Kubernetes runs; one or more containers sharing an address. Ch. 19
Policy as code
Rules checked automatically against plans or resources. Ch. 24
Pool
A block in an IPAM tree, with rules for what can be allocated from it. Ch. 10
Port
A number that picks the program on a machine, like 443 for HTTPS. Ch. 1
Prefix delegation
Assigning /28 blocks instead of single addresses to node interfaces. Ch. 19
Prefix list
A named set of CIDR ranges usable in rules and routes. Ch. 9
Prefix / network part
The fixed leading bits shared by every address in a range. Ch. 2
Private address
An address from the RFC 1918 ranges, never routed on the internet. Ch. 2
Private API
An API Gateway API reachable only through an interface endpoint. Ch. 20
Private DNS (endpoint)
The service's public name resolving to the endpoint's private addresses. Ch. 12
Private hosted zone
A zone visible only inside the networks it's attached to. Ch. 5
PrivateLink
AWS's technology for private, one-way access to a service across VPCs. Ch. 12
Private services access
Google Cloud's peering link to the network where some managed services run. Ch. 20
Processing charge
A per-GB fee for data passing through a managed gateway. Ch. 23
Propagation
An attachment adding its routes to a TGW route table. Ch. 11
Proxy protocol v2
A header the NLB adds so the service can see the original connection's details. Ch. 12
Public subnet
A subnet whose default route points at an internet gateway. Ch. 8

Q

QUIC
A reliable, encrypted transport over UDP, used by HTTP/3. Ch. 3

R

Reachability Analyzer
An AWS tool that checks from configuration whether a path is open. Ch. 9
Record
A name, type, value and TTL in a zone. Ch. 5
Recursive resolver
The server that finds answers for clients and caches them. Ch. 5
Region
A separate geographic area with its own set of zones and services. Ch. 7
Regional data transfer
Data between zones in the same region. Ch. 23
Route propagation
Routes learned over BGP added automatically to a cloud route table. Ch. 4
Router / hop
A device that forwards packets between networks; each pass is one hop. Ch. 1
Route table
A device's list of destination ranges and next hops. Ch. 4
RST
A reset packet: “I don't know this connection”. Ch. 3
RTO
Recovery time objective: how quickly service must be restored. Ch. 25
Rule
A condition and action on an ALB listener, checked by priority. Ch. 16
Runbook
Written, tested steps for a specific operation, such as a failover. Ch. 25

S

SAN
Subject Alternative Name: the names a certificate covers. Ch. 6
Secondary CIDR
An extra range added to an existing VPC. Ch. 10
Security group
A stateful, allow-only firewall attached to network interfaces. Ch. 9
Security group reference
A rule whose source or destination is another security group. Ch. 9
Segmentation
Controlling which networks can reach which. Ch. 11
Service control policy
An AWS Organizations policy that limits what accounts may do. Ch. 24
Service (Kubernetes)
A stable virtual address and name for a set of pods. Ch. 19
Service mesh
Proxies beside each workload, managed by a control plane, handling service-to-service traffic. Ch. 21
Shared responsibility
The split of security and operations work between provider and customer. Ch. 7
Sidecar
A helper container running next to the application in the same pod. Ch. 21
Site-to-site VPN
An encrypted tunnel connecting two networks over the internet. Ch. 13
SNI
The server name sent in the ClientHello, used to pick a certificate. Ch. 6
SNI filtering
Allowing or blocking TLS connections by the server name in the handshake. Ch. 18
SPIFFE
A standard format for workload identities in certificates. Ch. 21
Split-horizon DNS
Different answers for the same name inside and outside a network. Ch. 5
State
Terraform's record of what it manages; keep it separate per owner. Ch. 24
Stateful firewall
Tracks connections and allows their return traffic. Ch. 3
Stateless firewall
Judges each packet alone, in each direction. Ch. 3
Static route
A route configured by hand or by code. Ch. 4
Subnet
A slice of a VPC's range; on AWS, tied to one availability zone. Ch. 8
Subnet group
The subnets a managed database may place instances in. Ch. 20
Subnet mask
The prefix written as an address of ones then zeros, like 255.255.255.0. Ch. 2
Summarisation
Covering many networks with one shorter-prefix route. Ch. 10

T

Target group
A set of targets with a port, protocol and health check. Ch. 16
TCP
Connection-based transport: reliable, ordered, paced. Ch. 3
Three-way handshake
SYN, SYN-ACK, ACK: how a TCP connection opens. Ch. 3
TLS
The protocol that encrypts and authenticates a connection. Ch. 6
TLS inspection
Decrypting and re-encrypting traffic to see inside it. Ch. 18
TLS termination
Decrypting TLS at a load balancer or proxy. Ch. 6
Traffic Mirroring
Copies an interface's packets to an analysis target. Ch. 22
Transit gateway
AWS's managed regional router for VPCs, VPNs and Direct Connect. Ch. 11
Transitive routing
Traffic passing through one network to reach another. Ch. 11
TTL
A counter in every IP packet, lowered at each hop, that stops endless loops. Ch. 1

U

UDP
Connectionless transport of independent datagrams. Ch. 3
Underlay
The provider's physical network that carries the overlay. Ch. 7
Usage type
The bill's label for a kind of charge, such as NatGateway-Bytes. Ch. 23

V

Virtual interface (VIF)
A VLAN with a BGP session over a Direct Connect connection. Ch. 14
VPC-attached function
A function whose traffic goes through interfaces in your subnets. Ch. 20
VPC flow logs
Records of traffic through network interfaces, with accept or reject. Ch. 22
VPC Lattice
AWS's managed service-to-service networking with IAM auth policies. Ch. 21
VPC-native networking
Pods with real VPC addresses, routed by the VPC. Ch. 19
VPC peering
A direct, non-transitive private connection between two VPCs. Ch. 11
VPC sharing
One central VPC whose subnets are shared with other accounts. Ch. 10
VPC / VNet
A private network in a cloud region with its own address range. Ch. 8

W

WAF
A firewall that inspects HTTP requests. Ch. 17
Warm pool
Spare addresses a node keeps ready for new pods. Ch. 19
Workload identity
A credential that proves which service a request comes from. Ch. 21

X

X-Forwarded-For
A header carrying the original client address through proxies. Ch. 6

Z

Zero trust
A model in which no network location is trusted; every request is verified. Ch. 21
Zone
A part of the DNS tree managed as one unit. Ch. 5
Zone apex
The bare domain itself, like larder.example. Ch. 5
Zone association
Linking a private zone to a VPC so its resolver answers for it. Ch. 15
Zone ID
AWS's stable identifier for a zone, the same in every account. Ch. 7

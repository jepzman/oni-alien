Jef’s Handbooks · Handbook 3

Linux for Cloud Engineers

The server under everything, explained

Who it's for

Developers and new operations people who can ssh into a server and cd around, but freeze when it misbehaves. You've used a terminal; by the end you can run, secure, automate and troubleshoot Linux servers in the cloud with confidence, and you know what a container is actually made of.

How it's built

Twenty-one chapters in six parts. Part I finds your way around: what a server is, the shell, the filesystem and permissions. Part II is about what runs: processes, signals, systemd, logs and packages. Part III covers the resources that run out (CPU, memory, disk, limits) and Part IV networking from the host, up to SSH done right. Part V automates and secures a server and opens up a container; Part VI is a troubleshooting method and a capstone. Every chapter has a lab you can run in any Linux terminal, a container or a free local VM; nothing needs a paid cloud account.

The running example

The running example is Stagedoor, a ticketing platform for small and mid-sized music venues, based in Lisbon. In January 2026 it runs on three rented servers that its CTO built by hand; nobody has written down how they work, and the root password is in the team chat. This book follows Rafaela, its first operations engineer, and Tiago, a junior developer who learns Linux alongside her, until the servers are understood, fixed and ready to move to the cloud. It's a new story: you don't need to have read the other books.

Start at Chapter 1, or read every written chapter on one page (also as a PDF). Chapters marked “coming” are planned; the topics they will cover are listed.

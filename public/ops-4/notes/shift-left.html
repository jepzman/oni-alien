What Can the Stores Fix Themselves?

TL;DR

Many tickets are problems the reporter could fix themselves in two minutes, given a safe button or a short guide. Moving those fixes closer to the person (“shift-left”) is faster for them and frees the desk for the new and hard. Give the people at the edge a “is it just us?” check, a few safe actions, very short guides, and one tap to a ticket that's already filled in when it doesn't work.

1The situation Larder, September 2027

With five stores, Larder gets about 55 store tickets a week. Sam's team fixes many from the known-error list, but about a third are things a store lead could fix in two minutes with the right button or guide: restart a frozen handheld, reload a label printer, check whether a problem is only this store, swap a battery. They still wait for the desk or on-call, sometimes for an hour at the evening peak.

  • Pickers stand idle while a two-minute fix waits in a queue.
  • The desk spends its time on things it has solved a hundred times.
  • Antwerp and Eindhoven are about to add more stores, and more tickets.

2Real-life analogy the self-checkout's help sign

A supermarket's self-checkout has a small sign: “Item not recognised? Press here and try again.” Most problems are solved by the customer in seconds. An assistant comes only for what the sign doesn't cover, and whenever the assistant answers the same question three times, the sign is changed.

3How it solves it “just us?”, safe buttons, short guides, one tap to a ticket

From 4 October every store lead has a self-service page on their phone, linked from the store poster's QR code:

  • Is it just us? The store's own status (line, Wi-Fi, heartbeat), the other stores, and Larder's status page.
  • Safe buttons, through device management: restart a handheld, push the latest picker app, lock a lost device. Nothing that can break anything for anyone else.
  • 40-second video guides in Dutch and French: reload the label printer, swap a handheld battery, check the Wi-Fi from the floor.
  • If it doesn't help, one tap opens a ticket with the device, the store and the steps already tried filled in.

Every action is logged and counts as a solved problem. A new guide is added whenever the desk sees the same fix three times. By December, 31 % of store problems are solved in the store, and tickets per store per week drop from 9.1 to 6.4, even with Antwerp and Eindhoven added.

  1. Sort tickets by “could they have fixed it?”

    With the right button or guide.

  2. Add an “is it just us?” check

    Most panic is about the wrong scope.

  3. Offer only safe actions

    Nothing that can affect anyone else.

  4. Make guides very short

    40 seconds, in their language, on a phone.

  5. One tap to a prefilled ticket

    When self-service doesn't work.

  6. Add a guide after three repeats

    And count self-solved problems.

Vocabulary for the exam

Shift-left
Moving the fix closer to the person with the problem.
Self-service
Users solving their own problems or requests with provided tools.
Safe action
An action a user can take without risk to others.
Deflection
A problem solved without a ticket.
Prefilled ticket
A ticket opened with context already captured.
First-contact resolution
Solved at the first attempt, wherever that is.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Unsafe buttons

    A store restarts something shared. Fix → only actions that affect this store.

  • Long guides

    Nobody reads a PDF during the peak. Fix → 40-second videos.

  • Dead end

    Self-service fails and there's no way forward. Fix → one tap to a prefilled ticket.

  • Guides that go stale

    Steps for last year's printer. Fix → owned by the desk, updated on repeats.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Many tickets are simple, repeated fixes.
  • People wait for help with things they could do.
  • The desk is swamped by repeats.
  • New sites are being added.

Skip it when…

  • Problems that need access or judgement the reporter shouldn't have.

8Self-check quiz

What does shift-left mean?

Moving fixes closer to the person with the problem.

What's on the store self-service page?

“Is it just us?”, safe buttons (restart, update, lock), 40-second guides, and one tap to a prefilled ticket.

What makes an action safe for self-service?

It can only affect this store's own devices, not anything shared.

When is a new guide added?

When the desk sees the same fix three times.

What changed by December?

31 % of store problems solved in store; tickets per store per week 9.1 → 6.4.

what would you do?A store lead asks for a button to restart the WMS B connection “because it fixes things”. Do you add it?

No: it's shared by every store. Find why it needs restarting (a problem), and give the store “is it just us?” so they can see it's being handled.

try it

Take last month's tickets and mark those the reporter could have fixed in two minutes with a safe button or a short guide. Build the guide for the most common one, as a 40-second video.

remember!!

Put the fix next to the problem: “just us?”, safe buttons, 40-second guides, and one tap to a ticket when it isn't enough.

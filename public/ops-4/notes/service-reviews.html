The Monthly Service Review

TL;DR

All the numbers about a service (SLOs, budget, incidents, changes, capacity, OLAs, vendors) belong on one page, generated, the same every month. Then hold a short meeting with the service's owner that ends in a few logged decisions. The report is for seeing; the review is for deciding. Where a client has an SLA, the same data produces their report too.

1The situation Larder, August 2027

By August Larder measures a lot: SLOs and error budgets for five services, an incident page, delivery numbers, a capacity watch, and soon OLAs and a contract report. Each lives somewhere different. Service owners like Ben and Joost see some of it, sometimes. Decisions about reliability happen in corridors, or don't happen at all.

  • Numbers without a decision are just reporting.
  • Owners can't own what they don't see together.
  • Each report is assembled by hand, slightly differently each time.

2Real-life analogy the annual check-up

At an annual check-up a GP looks at one sheet with the same measurements every year: blood pressure, weight, cholesterol, each compared with last year. Twenty minutes with the doctor end in one or two decisions: change the dose, come back in six months. The value isn't the numbers. It's the decisions.

3How it solves it one generated page per service, a short review, a few decisions

From September, on the first working day of each month, a script generates one page per service with the same sections every time: SLO status and error budget, incidents and orders affected, changes and change failures, capacity headroom, OLAs and vendor issues, open problems and actions.

On the first Thursday, each critical service gets a 30-minute review with its owner (Ben for ordering, Joost's lead for Dispatch, Cho for payments), Eli, and whoever is affected that month. Nobody presents slides. Everyone has read the page, and the meeting ends with at most three decisions, logged in the page itself.

For business ordering, the same data produces Korenwolf's monthly report in the portal, so the client and Larder see the same numbers. October's ordering review decides the portal pause (the budget was spent) and the second DNS provider. November's decides to retire two flaky API endpoints.

  1. Put every measure for a service on one page

    SLO, budget, incidents, changes, capacity, OLAs.

  2. Generate it, don't assemble it

    Same sections, every month.

  3. Hold a short review with the owner

    Read beforehand; no slides.

  4. End with at most three decisions

    Logged on the page.

  5. Reuse the data for client reports

    One truth, two audiences.

  6. Check last month's decisions first

    Did they happen?

Vocabulary for the exam

Service report
A regular summary of a service's performance against its targets.
Service review
A meeting with the owner and stakeholders to decide what to change.
Decision log
The record of decisions made, with owners and dates.
Single source of truth
One set of data used for every report.
Trend
How a number moves over months; often more useful than its level.
Read-ahead
Reading the page before the meeting, so the meeting can decide.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Reports without reviews

    Read by nobody. Fix → a short meeting that ends in decisions.

  • Slides

    The meeting is spent presenting. Fix → everyone reads the page first.

  • Different numbers for the client

    Two truths, endless disputes. Fix → one data source, two reports.

  • Too many decisions

    None get done. Fix → at most three, owned.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • You measure a lot and decide little.
  • Owners don't see their service as a whole.
  • Clients get reports built by hand.
  • After setting SLOs and OLAs.

Skip it when…

  • Services with no targets yet: set SLOs first.

8Self-check quiz

What's on each service's monthly page?

SLO and budget, incidents and orders affected, changes and failures, capacity, OLAs and vendors, open problems and actions.

How long is a service review, and who's there?

30 minutes, with the owner, Eli and whoever was affected.

How does a review end?

With at most three logged decisions.

Why generate the page rather than assemble it?

Same sections every month, no hand-work, and the same data for client reports.

What did October's ordering review decide?

Resume portal features after 7 days above SLO, a second DNS provider, and retiring a legacy API endpoint.

what would you do?A service review runs over because people debate October's numbers. What should change for November?

The page is sent out earlier and read beforehand; questions about the numbers go in comments before the meeting; the meeting itself is only for the decisions.

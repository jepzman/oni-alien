The Vendor Went Quiet

TL;DR

Some vendor failures make no noise: the webhooks just stop, a file doesn't arrive, nothing errors. Alerting on errors never catches them. Watch for absence: what should arrive on a rhythm, and hasn't. Back webhooks up with a poll. And manage each critical vendor as an ongoing relationship: a quarterly review with a scorecard covering incidents, response, honesty, contract changes and how ready an exit would be.

1The situation Larder, Tuesday 31 August 2027

At 08:10 the billing vendor stops sending payment webhooks. Nothing errors: Larder's systems simply receive nothing, so nothing alerts. In the portal Korenwolf's invoices start showing as unpaid. At 17:20 Ruben emails Mo: “Why does Larder say we haven't paid?” Nine hours of silence, found by the client, two months before the renewal talks.

  • Error alerts can't see a failure that makes no error.
  • The vendor's status page said nothing all day.
  • Nobody reviews vendors until something goes wrong.
meanwhile, in Ana’s notebook
  • SaaS & Vendor Integration (Ana, vol. 4): Ana's vendor integration checklist of 6 September: poll every 15 minutes, silence alerts, stay below rate limits, production contract checks, status-page alerts.

2Real-life analogy the school bus that doesn't come

The school bus always arrives at 15:40. When it's late, nobody phones. A parent notices because it's 15:55 and the bus isn't there: the alarm is the absence. A good parent also has the bus company's number, and a sense of how often the bus has been late this term, so they can complain with facts.

3How it solves it watch for absence, poll as backup, review vendors quarterly

After Ana's integration checklist (6 September), Eli adds the operations side:

  • Absence alerts for every vendor that sends something on a rhythm. Billing webhooks: none for 15 minutes in business hours makes a ticket, none for 45 pages. WMS B stock events: none for 5 minutes in opening hours. Settlement files: by 05:00. The PSP's daily report: by 06:00.
  • A poll every 15 minutes asks the vendor what changed, so a lost webhook is found even if the vendor thinks it sent it.
  • A quarterly vendor review, 30 minutes per critical vendor with its owner, and a scorecard: incidents, how fast they answered, whether their status page was honest, contract and price changes, and how ready an exit would be.

Eight vendors get scorecards. The billing vendor is amber in Q3, and at the review it agrees to an outbound health check that tells Larder when its own webhook queue backs up. Korenwolf is told what changed, in the four-part format, by Mo.

  1. List what each vendor should send, and how often

    Webhooks, files, reports.

  2. Alert on absence

    Nothing for N minutes in hours when something is expected.

  3. Back push with a poll

    Ask what changed, every 15 minutes.

  4. Keep a scorecard per critical vendor

    Incidents, response, honesty, contract, exit.

  5. Review quarterly with the owner

    And bring the scorecard to renewals.

  6. Tell affected clients what changed

    In their words.

Vocabulary for the exam

Silent failure
A failure that produces no error, only missing results.
Absence alert
An alert that fires when something expected doesn't arrive.
Heartbeat (vendor)
A regular signal a vendor sends to show it's working.
Polling
Asking a system for changes on a schedule, instead of waiting to be told.
Supplier scorecard
A regular rating of a vendor on agreed criteria.
Exit readiness
How easily you could move away from a vendor.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Alerting only on errors

    Silence looks like health. Fix → alert on absence.

  • Trusting push alone

    Lost webhooks are lost forever. Fix → poll as a backup.

  • Absence alerts at night

    Pages when nothing is expected. Fix → only in the hours something is expected.

  • Reviewing vendors only at renewal

    No facts, no leverage. Fix → quarterly scorecards.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • A vendor failure was found by a customer.
  • You depend on webhooks or files from vendors.
  • A vendor contract renews in the next year.
  • After setting OLAs that rely on vendors.

Skip it when…

  • Vendors you call only on demand: error alerts catch their failures.

8Self-check quiz

Why didn't anything alert on 31 August?

Nothing errored; the webhooks just stopped, and only errors were alerted on.

What is an absence alert?

An alert that fires when something expected doesn't arrive in time.

Why poll as well as receive webhooks?

A lost webhook is never retried; a poll finds what changed anyway.

What's on a vendor scorecard?

Incidents, response time, status page honesty, contract and price changes, exit readiness.

What did the billing vendor agree to after scoring amber?

An outbound health check on its webhook queue.

what would you do?The settlement file hasn't arrived by 05:00 on a Sunday. Does anyone get paged?

Not necessarily: if finance doesn't need it until Monday, a ticket is enough. The absence alert's urgency should match when the missing thing is needed.

try it

List every vendor that sends you something on a rhythm. For each, write how often it should arrive and what you'd do if it didn't. Add one absence alert for the one that matters most.

remember!!

The worst vendor failures make no noise. Alert on what should have arrived, poll as backup, and keep score quarterly.

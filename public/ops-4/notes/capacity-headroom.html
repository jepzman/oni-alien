Will Christmas Fit?

TL;DR

Capacity planning predicts what you'll need; capacity management watches whether you still have room, every week. Pick the few limits that actually stop you (connections, rate limits, links), show headroom at the busiest hour, alert as a ticket at 70 % and a page at 85 %, look at them weekly, and turn the plan for the next peak into dated changes done well before it. Never resize in a hurry two days before Christmas again.

1The situation Larder, October 2027

On 16 December 2026 the database primary was resized in a hurry, two days before the Christmas peak, because connections had run out at Friday's peak. The dashboard showed connections the whole time, but nobody looked at it until it hit the limit. For Christmas 2027, Ana's capacity plan of 18 October says database connections are at 310 of 400 at today's peak, and would run out at 1.3×. December is forecast at about 27,000 orders a day.

  • A plan predicts; somebody still has to watch.
  • Limits are spread across the database, vendors and stores.
  • Changes needed for the peak must land before the freeze on 13 December.

2Real-life analogy the ferry before the summer

A ferry company knows its limits: cars per crossing, crossings per day, staff per shift. From April it watches bookings against those limits every week, and adds crossings when a week passes 80 %. It never finds out on the first Saturday of July that the boat is full.

3How it solves it six limits, headroom at the peak hour, alerts, a weekly look, dated changes

From October Eli watches six limits, each shown as headroom at the busiest hour of the week (Friday 18:00–19:00), not as a daily average:

  • Database connections (310 of 400 before the pooler) and database CPU.
  • Dispatch copies (4 of 6 at peak).
  • The WMS B rate limit (20 requests a second; Larder queues at 15) and the payment provider's rate limit.
  • Each store's line.

Alerts: 70 % of a limit at the peak hour makes a ticket; 85 % pages. Every Monday, 15 minutes, Eli and Bram look at the trend. Ana's plan is carried out as dated normal changes: the pooler on 1 November, a browse replica on 8 November, the primary one size up on 15 November, all well before the freeze. The load test at 5× peak passes on 15 November. In December's peak hour, connections sit at 41 % of the new limit.

  1. Find the limits that actually stop you

    Connections, rate limits, links, copies.

  2. Show headroom at the busiest hour

    Not daily averages.

  3. Alert before the edge

    Ticket at 70 %, page at 85 %.

  4. Look weekly

    Trends, not just alerts.

  5. Turn the peak plan into dated changes

    Done well before the freeze.

  6. Test at the peak's size

    A load test, at a quiet hour.

Vocabulary for the exam

Capacity
The maximum a component or service can handle.
Headroom
How much room is left before a limit.
Saturation
How full a resource is; the golden signal for capacity.
Peak hour
The busiest hour of the week, where limits bite.
Connection pooler
Software that shares a few database connections among many clients.
Load test
Deliberately sending heavy traffic to see where limits are.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Daily averages

    Peaks hide in averages. Fix → headroom at the busiest hour.

  • Watching only your servers

    The vendor's rate limit stops you. Fix → include vendor and store limits.

  • A plan without changes

    The plan is right and nothing happens. Fix → dated changes, before the freeze.

  • Alerting only at the edge

    No time to act. Fix → ticket at 70 %, page at 85 %.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • A peak is coming.
  • A limit surprised you once.
  • Growth is steady (new cities).
  • Vendors have rate limits on your critical path.

Skip it when…

  • Fully elastic services with no hard limits: watch costs instead.

8Self-check quiz

Why show headroom at the busiest hour, not a daily average?

Limits bite at the peak; averages hide it.

What are Larder's alert levels?

70 % of a limit at the peak hour → ticket; 85 % → page.

Which limits are outside Larder's servers?

The WMS B and payment provider rate limits, and each store's line.

How was Ana's plan carried out?

As dated changes: pooler 1 Nov, browse replica 8 Nov, primary one size up 15 Nov, before the 13 Dec freeze.

What happened on 16 December 2026, and why?

The primary was resized in a hurry: nobody watched the connections' headroom until they hit the limit.

what would you do?On a Monday look in November, the WMS B rate is at 75 % of its limit at the peak hour and climbing. What do you do?

It's above the 70 % ticket line: talk to Ana and the WMS B vendor about a higher limit or batching, plan it as a change before the freeze, and watch it weekly until it's resolved.

try it

List the five limits that would stop your main service first. For each, find its value at last week's busiest hour. Which one is above 70 %? Put a dated change on the calendar for it.

remember!!

Watch the few limits that stop you, at the busiest hour, every week, and finish the peak's changes before the freeze.

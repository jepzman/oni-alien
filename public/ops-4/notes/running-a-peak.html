Running a Peak

TL;DR

The busiest weeks of the year deserve their own preparation, written down: a readiness review weeks before (capacity, failover, drills, vendors, spares, each green, amber or red with an owner), a freeze, extra on-call cover at the riskiest hours, and a short daily check-in with the people running the business. On the day, nothing new goes in. Ready is a checklist, not a feeling.

1The situation Larder, November 2027

Eli remembers December 2026 well: the database resized in a hurry on the 16th, the failover on the 18th, 38 minutes, about 900 orders, Femke calling three times. For December 2027 the forecast is about 27,000 orders a day across seven stores and two countries. The capacity changes are done and the freeze starts on 13 December. But when Femke asks “are we ready?”, the honest answer is “I think so”.

  • Readiness lives in several people's heads.
  • The riskiest hours have the same cover as any other week.
  • Operations, support and IT watch the peak separately.

2Real-life analogy the theatre's opening night

A week before opening night a theatre holds a full dress rehearsal, tells the understudies which nights they're on standby, checks the fire exits and briefs the box office. Every night there's a short call at the half hour. And on opening night, nothing new is added to the show. Ready is something you can tick off, not a feeling.

3How it solves it a readiness review, a freeze, extra cover, a daily check-in

On Monday 29 November Eli runs a readiness review: twelve checks, each with an owner and a green, amber or red. Among them:

  • Capacity changes done, and the 5× load test passed on 15 November.
  • Database failover tested on 7 December, before the freeze: 1:37.
  • A major-incident drill held on 30 November.
  • The automatic switch to the backup payment provider tested, and every critical vendor's holiday contact confirmed.
  • Store spares and spare batteries in every store.

Then the run itself. The freeze runs from 13 December to 3 January. From 17 to 24 December, Eli or Bram is on standby every evening behind the usual primary and secondary. There's a 15-minute check-in at 09:00 every day from 13 to 24 December, with Joost's operations lead, Sam, Mo and on-call.

On Friday 17 December at 18:12, PSP A slows down for 22 minutes. The circuit breaker from Ana's failure analysis moves payments to PSP B in 40 seconds, and no orders fail. The busiest day is Thursday 23 December, with about 29,400 orders. There's no SEV1 or SEV2 all month.

  1. Write what “ready” means

    A dozen checks, each with an owner.

  2. Review it weeks before

    Green, amber, red, with time to fix amber.

  3. Rehearse the worst case

    Load test, failover, a major-incident drill.

  4. Freeze, and add cover at the riskiest hours

    Standby behind the usual rota.

  5. Hold a short daily check-in

    IT, operations, support, client managers.

  6. Review afterwards

    What to keep for next year's checklist.

Vocabulary for the exam

Peak
A period of much higher demand than normal.
Readiness review
A structured check that a service is prepared for an event.
Standby
An extra person ready to join, beyond the normal rota.
Daily check-in
A short, fixed meeting during the peak to share status.
Circuit breaker
Stops calling a failing dependency and switches to a fallback.
Peak review
A look back after the peak to improve the next one.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Ready as a feeling

    “I think so.” Fix → a written checklist with owners.

  • Testing during the freeze

    The test itself becomes a change at the peak. Fix → test before the freeze.

  • IT watching alone

    Operations and support see problems first. Fix → a shared daily check-in.

  • No review afterwards

    Next year starts from scratch. Fix → keep what worked in the checklist.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • A known peak is coming: Christmas, King's Day, a campaign.
  • Last peak went badly.
  • Several teams need to watch the same weeks.
  • Leaders ask “are we ready?”

Skip it when…

  • Normal weeks: the usual rota and alerts are enough.

8Self-check quiz

What does Larder's readiness review contain?

About twelve checks with owners and status: capacity and load test, failover test, drill, PSP switch, vendors' holiday lines, store spares, freeze, standby, check-in.

Why test failover on 7 December?

Before the freeze starts on 13 December, so the test isn't a change at the peak.

What's the daily check-in?

15 minutes at 09:00, 13–24 December, with operations, support, client managers and on-call.

What happened on 17 December 2027?

PSP A slowed for 22 minutes; payments switched to PSP B in 40 seconds; no orders failed.

What extra cover was added?

Eli or Bram on standby every evening from 17 to 24 December.

what would you do?On 1 December the store spares check is amber: two stores have no spare batteries. What happens?

It gets an owner and a date before the freeze (here: the store leads, by 6 December), and goes green at the next check-in. Amber two weeks ahead is exactly what the review is for.

try it

Pick your next peak. Write twelve checks for “ready”, each with an owner. Review them four weeks before. Which one is amber today?

remember!!

Ready is a checklist, not a feeling: review it weeks before, freeze, add cover, and check in daily with the people running the business.

Dashboards People Actually Open

TL;DR

Dashboards multiply because making one is easy and deleting one feels risky. Most are never opened, and several disagree. Keep few dashboards, each answering one question for one audience: a “now” page for everyone, one per critical service for on-call (SLIs on top, causes below), one for leaders. Give each an owner and its question written at the top, and archive anything nobody opens.

1The situation Larder, October 2027

Eli counts the dashboards in October: 74. In the last 90 days only 9 were opened by more than one person. Three different “checkout” dashboards disagree about the error rate, because each counts differently. During a September incident, on-call opened the wrong one and spent ten minutes chasing a number that wasn't real.

  • Nobody knows which dashboard is the right one.
  • Old dashboards quietly show wrong numbers as systems change.
  • Leaders get screenshots of whichever one someone had open.

2Real-life analogy the car's dashboard

A car's dashboard shows speed, fuel and a few warning lights. It doesn't show the engine's 200 sensors; those are for the mechanic's laptop. Each screen answers one person's question while they do one job. Nobody wants the mechanic's view while driving.

3How it solves it few dashboards, one question each, owned, and archived when unused

In October Eli and Bram cut 74 dashboards to 11:

  • “Larder now”, on the office screen and on phones: orders per city against last week, SLO status per service, open incidents.
  • One per critical service, linked from every alert: SLIs on the top row, the rows that explain them below (connections, queues, vendors). The three checkout dashboards become one, with the SLI's definition.
  • A weekly SLO page for Femke, and the status page for stores and clients.

Every dashboard has an owner, and “the question it answers” written at the top. Anything unopened for 90 days is flagged and archived: kept, but out of the list. Only 2 are restored in three months. New dashboards are welcome, as long as they answer a question none of the 11 does.

  1. Count dashboards and their views

    Last 90 days, by distinct people.

  2. Write the question each one answers

    If you can't, it's a candidate to go.

  3. Keep one per audience and job

    Now, on-call per service, leaders.

  4. Put SLIs on top, causes below

    Symptoms first, like alerts.

  5. Give each an owner

    Who keeps it right as systems change.

  6. Archive the unopened

    Kept, out of sight, restorable.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Deleting outright

    Someone's quarterly view vanishes. Fix → archive, restorable.

  • A dashboard per person

    Sprawl returns. Fix → one per audience and job.

  • Different definitions

    Numbers disagree across screens. Fix → one definition per SLI, everywhere.

  • Causes on top

    On-call stares at CPU. Fix → SLIs first.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Nobody knows which dashboard is right.
  • Numbers disagree between screens.
  • On-call wastes time choosing where to look.
  • After setting SLIs.

Skip it when…

  • You have three dashboards and everyone uses them.

8Self-check quiz

What did Eli find in October?

74 dashboards, 9 used by more than one person in 90 days, and three checkout dashboards that disagreed.

What's at the top of every dashboard now?

The question it answers, and (for services) the SLIs.

What are the three main kinds of dashboard at Larder?

“Larder now” for everyone, one per critical service for on-call and owners, and a weekly SLO page for Femke.

What happens to a dashboard nobody opens for 90 days?

It's flagged and archived: kept, but out of the list.

Why one definition per SLI?

So numbers agree on every screen and in every report.

what would you do?Marketing asks for a new dashboard of orders by campaign. Should it exist?

If it answers a question none of the 11 does, yes: with an owner, the question at the top, and the same order definitions. If it duplicates “Larder now”, add a filter instead.

try it

Count your dashboards and how many people opened each in the last 90 days. For each one you keep, write the question it answers at the top. Archive the rest and see who notices.

remember!!

One dashboard, one question, one audience, one owner. Archive what nobody opens; SLIs on top.

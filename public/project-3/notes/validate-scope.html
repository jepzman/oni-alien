a.k.a. validating scope: formal acceptance of deliverables

Is This What We Asked For?

TL;DR

Validating scope means the people who asked for each deliverable formally accept it, against criteria agreed in advance. It isn't the same as quality control. Quality control is the team checking that the work is correct; validation is the customer checking that it's what they asked for. Do control first, then validation, then record the acceptance. Something can pass every internal check and still be the wrong thing.

  • Family: holding the line
  • Lives in: an acceptance log: deliverable, owner, criteria, date, conditions

1The situation Larder, 17–20 April 2026

Gate 2, Friday 17 April. The stocking team reports done: 2,500 products on the shelves, every label scanned against its product, 100 %. Tomás walks the aisles with a picker's handheld and stops at aisle 14. The labels carry Noord's aisle codes, from the template the label printer was set up with. The handheld sends pickers by Rotterdam's. Every label was correct, and two aisles would send pickers to the wrong place.

  • The team's check asked “is the label right for the product?”
  • Tomás's need was “can a picker find it?”
  • Only the person who will use it can check the second.
meanwhile, in Grace’s notebook
  • Definition of Ready & Done (Grace, vol. 3): Grace's definition of done (February) is the team's own check on each story; her acceptance at Gate 3 is a separate step.

2Real-life analogy the tailor and the client

Before handing over a suit, a good tailor checks the seams, the buttons and the measurements against the order. Then the client puts it on, walks across the room, sits down, and says whether it's what they asked for. Both checks are needed. Only the client can do the second, and a suit with perfect seams can still pull at the shoulders.

3How it solves it control first, then the owner accepts against criteria from February

Tomás rejects two aisles, not the gate. The stocking team reprints and relabels them over the weekend; Tomás walks them again on Monday 20 April and accepts. Sanne records it and uses it to fix the process for the other groups: every deliverable is accepted by the person named in the scope statement, against the criteria written in February, after the team's own check. Sanne arranges each acceptance; she never accepts anything herself. The acceptance log fills up: Joost accepts the store from the builder on 3 April (two conditions), Lena accepts payments and legal on 13 April, Grace accepts the app at Gate 3 on 20 April by placing real orders in each zone, Priya accepts the marketing on 24 April, and the hypercare exit criteria close the last group on 11 May.

  1. Write acceptance criteria early

    In the scope statement, from the requirements.

  2. Name who accepts each deliverable

    The person who asked for it, or will use it.

  3. Let the team check first

    Quality control: is it right?

  4. Then the owner tries it the way it will be used

    Validation: is it what we asked for?

  5. Accept, accept with conditions, or reject the part

    Not the whole, if only a part fails.

  6. Record it

    Who, when, against what, with which conditions.

Vocabulary for the exam

Validate scope
Formal acceptance of completed deliverables by the customer or sponsor.
Control quality
The team checking that work meets its requirements.
Acceptance criteria
Conditions agreed in advance that a deliverable must meet.
Accepted deliverable
One the owner has formally signed off.
Conditional acceptance
Accepted, with named fixes, owners and dates.
User acceptance test
The users trying the result the way they'll use it.

4Light, standard, heavy how much of this do you need?

Light

a small deliverable

  • Show it to the person who asked.
  • “Is this what you wanted?”
  • A message saying yes.

Standard

Rotterdam

  • Criteria written in February.
  • Team check, then the owner tries it for real.
  • An acceptance log with conditions.

Heavy

contracts

  • Formal acceptance tests and certificates.
  • Acceptance tied to payment.
  • Disputes handled under the contract.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • The team accepts its own work

    Correct, not right. Fix → the person who asked accepts.

  • Criteria written at the gate

    They bend to fit. Fix → write them in the scope statement.

  • Rejecting everything for one part

    Days lost. Fix → reject the part; accept the rest.

  • The project manager signs

    Nobody who will use it checked. Fix → arrange, never accept.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • At every gate.
  • Before a supplier is paid.
  • Before handing anything to operations.
  • Whenever the team says “done”.

Skip it when…

  • Work in progress inside a team: that's for their definition of done.

8Self-check quiz

Quality control or validation: the scan check of every label?

Quality control.

And Tomás walking the aisles with a handheld?

Validation.

Who accepts the app?

Grace, who owns its scope.

Why did Tomás reject two aisles, not the whole gate?

Only they failed; the rest met the criteria. Rejecting the part kept the schedule.

On 22 April Joost offers to sign the acceptance for marketing to save Priya time. What does Sanne say?

Priya asked for it and owns it; she accepts. Sanne will find twenty minutes in Priya's diary.

try it

Pick a deliverable your team calls done. Ask the person who asked for it to use it, the way they will really use it, against the criteria you agreed. Record what they say.

a.k.a. “defining scope”: the scope statement

What's In, and What's Out?

TL;DR

A scope statement says, on one page, what the project will deliver: the result in a few lines, the deliverables, how each will be accepted and by whom, what is deliberately out, the constraints, and the assumptions it rests on (each with someone who checks it). It turns requirements into a promise. Its most useful lines are the ones that say what won't be done.

  • Family: what, exactly?
  • Lives in: one page; its in / out lines go into the charter
  • Pairs with: requirements, the charter, the WBS

1The situation Larder, 16 February 2026

Three days before the charter, a short meeting about the waitlist goes wrong. Priya has been planning a Rotterdam landing page and a promo code: “it's a launch.” Joost has been planning for the project to end on opening day: hypercare is his store team's job. Each is sure. Neither had said it. Between them, the launch is either two features bigger or two weeks shorter than Sanne's plan.

  • Everyone carries a picture of the scope in their head.
  • The edges (what's out, when it ends) are where pictures differ.
  • Assumptions nobody checks become surprises.

2Real-life analogy the decorator's quote

A good decorator's quote lists the rooms, the colour and the number of coats, and how you'll check the work: “two coats, no drips, edges clean.” At the bottom is one more line: “not included: ceilings, window frames.” That last line costs nothing to write, and prevents most of the arguments decorators and clients have.

3How it solves it one page, six parts; settle the edges

Sanne writes the scope statement in six parts: the result (a dark store serving three delivery zones, live in the platform and apps), eight deliverable groups, acceptance for each, what is out, the constraints, and the assumptions, each with someone who checks it. The edges get settled in the room. Hypercare is in: two weeks, then a handover, because Joost's team can't absorb a new store's first days alone. The landing page is out: the existing waitlist page gets a city field, which meets the need (Priya's promo code stays open and comes back later). Sunday opening is written as an open question with an owner. Three days later the in and out lines go into the charter word for word.

  1. Describe the result in a few lines

    What exists when you're done.

  2. List the deliverables

    Groups, not tasks.

  3. Write how each is accepted, and by whom

    From the requirements' checks.

  4. Write what is out

    Especially what people might assume is in.

  5. List constraints and assumptions

    Each assumption gets a checker.

  6. Settle the edges with the people who disagree

    Then copy in / out into the charter.

Vocabulary for the exam

Scope statement
A description of the project's deliverables, acceptance, exclusions, constraints and assumptions.
Product scope
The features and qualities of the result.
Project scope
The work needed to deliver it, including training and hypercare.
Acceptance criteria
Conditions a deliverable must meet before it is accepted.
Exclusion
Something explicitly not part of the project.
Assumption
Something treated as true for planning, to be checked.

# ROTTERDAM · SCOPE STATEMENT

RESULT       a dark store in Rotterdam serving three delivery zones (RTM-1 to RTM-3),
             express and slots, live in the platform and the apps, from 27 April 2026
DELIVERABLES 1 store fitted out   2 staff hired and trained   3 store stocked
             4 city set up in the platform   5 Rotterdam in the apps
             6 payments and legal in place   7 launch marketing   8 two weeks of hypercare
ACCEPTANCE   1 Joost, at Gate 1 (snag list)   2 Tomás: 20 practice orders each
             3 Tomás, at Gate 2   4 Eli: nightly check = 0 errors   5 Grace, at Gate 3
             6 Lena: registrations on file   7 Priya   8 exit criteria, Gate 5
OUT          new app features for Rotterdam · Larder for Business in Rotterdam ·
             changes to Amsterdam · a launch event · a separate Rotterdam landing page
             (the waitlist page gets a city field)
OPEN         Sunday opening at launch → Joost, by 27 Feb
CONSTRAINTS  opens 27 April (lease) · €380k · max 4 new hires · no deliveries before 07:30
ASSUMPTIONS                                                         CHECKED BY
             StockPilot syncs a second city within its limits       Ana, by 1 Mar
             ~40 pickers and couriers can be hired by 30 March      Joost, weekly
             suppliers deliver to Rotterdam on Amsterdam's terms    Tomás, by 13 Mar

tools: one page, the requirements list, and the two people who disagree about the edges.

4Light, standard, heavy how much of this do you need?

Light

a small job

  • Three lines: what, how it's checked, what's out.
  • Agreed in a message.
  • Nothing more.

Standard

Rotterdam

  • One page, six parts.
  • Assumptions with checkers.
  • In / out copied into the charter.

Heavy

contracts, tenders

  • A detailed statement of work.
  • Acceptance criteria per deliverable in the contract.
  • Exclusions reviewed by legal.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • No “out”

    People assume the ceilings too. Fix → write what is excluded.

  • Assumptions with no owner

    Nobody checks; they turn into issues. Fix → a checker and a date each.

  • Tasks instead of deliverables

    A to-do list, not a result. Fix → name what exists at the end.

  • Written alone

    The disagreements surface in April. Fix → settle the edges with those who differ.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Before the charter or the contract.
  • When two people describe the project differently.
  • Before breaking the work down.
  • When suppliers quote against it.

Skip it when…

  • Tiny work whose edges nobody could disagree about.

8Self-check quiz

Name the six parts.

Result, deliverables, acceptance, out, constraints, assumptions.

Product scope or project scope: two weeks of hypercare?

Project scope: it's work the project does, not a feature of the store.

Why give each assumption a checker?

Unchecked assumptions become issues. StockPilot's limit is one Ana already found.

On 2 March Ana reports that StockPilot can sync a second city, but not a third. What does Sanne do with the assumption?

Mark it checked and true for Rotterdam, and pass the limit to the programme for Utrecht.

Priya asks again for a Rotterdam landing page on 5 March. What does Sanne point to?

The OUT line, and the need it was meant for (the waitlist page's city field). A real change would go through the triggers.

try it

Write your project's scope on one page in six parts. Spend most of the time on two boxes: what is out, and what you are assuming. Give each assumption a name and a date.

remember!!

The line that says “not included” prevents most of the arguments.

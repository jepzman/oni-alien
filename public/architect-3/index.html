Communicating Architecture

Volume 3 of The Solution Architect's Notebook: how architects make a system understandable to the people who build it, run it, pay for it and depend on it. 15 notes in four collections: diagrams with clear rules, documents people actually read, conversations with engineers and executives, and keeping it all current. Every note follows Ana at Larder from October 2026 to February 2027, and ends with a try it exercise for your own project.

the story so far

October 2026. Larder runs two cities, Korenwolf has signed for three offices, and four more cities are planned for 2027. The team has grown to 18 engineers, and the architecture now lives in too many diagrams, documents and heads. This volume follows Ana from October 2026 to February 2027: drawing the system so people can read it, writing documents people use, explaining choices to people who don't build software, and keeping all of it current.

★The nine architect ratings what a good architecture practice is judged on

Every note scores the pattern from 1 (poor) to 5 (great) on the same nine things, comparing an app without the pattern to the same app with it.

Clarity
A newcomer can understand the system and the reasoning behind it.
Stakeholder alignment
Business, product and engineering share the same what and why.
Decision speed
How quickly the team can choose and get back to building.
Risk reduction
The big risks are surfaced and handled before they bite.
Adaptability
How cheaply the direction can change later.
Traceability
Months later, anyone can find why something was done.
Cost awareness
Money and effort are part of the decision, not a surprise.
Low overhead
Time and ceremony it takes. High = light.
Team autonomy
Teams can decide and act without waiting for the architect.

first law

Everything in architecture is a trade-off. If you think you've found one that isn't, you haven't found the cost yet.

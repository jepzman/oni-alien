Inside MAVIS what's really there, who knows it, and what the night batch does

  1. 01What Is Actually in There?Map a legacy system from what it does (logs, schedules, data), not from what its documents say, and colour it by what's used.p. 01
  2. 02Who Knows How It Really Works?Capture the expert's knowledge without draining them: pair, record, write answers once, and plan their succession with them.p. 02
  3. 03How Do We Read Code Nobody Owns?Pin down what old code does with characterisation tests on real inputs before you trust any explanation of it.p. 03
  4. 04What Does the 02:10 Job Really Do?Trace a batch job step by step, by its inputs and outputs, and find the side effects everyone relies on and nobody wrote down.p. 04

The data and its seams the real data, getting it out, the billing boundary and the plan

  1. 05How Bad Is the Data, Really?Profile all the data, not a sample: completeness, validity, duplicates and consistency, with numbers per district and per field.p. 05
  2. 06How Do We Get Data Out of a Mainframe?Choose change capture by testing it: database logs, timestamps or snapshots, and the copybooks, encodings and packed numbers in between.p. 06
  3. 07What Does the Billing System Really Need?Treat another vendor's interface as a contract: test it, version it, and put a translation layer between their model and yours.p. 07
  4. 08How Do We Strangle a Customer System?Move customers district by district, with one system of record per account, a way back, and reconciliation every night.p. 08

Bad news, honestly the report, the telling, the change request and the security review

  1. 09What Do We Say When Discovery Changes Everything?A discovery report that leads with findings and numbers, explains what they mean, and offers priced options with a recommendation.p. 09
  2. 10How Do We Tell Them It's Worse Than They Think?Tell bad news in person, in the right order, to the people who own the problem, with evidence and without blame.p. 10
  3. 11How Do We Ask for More Money Honestly?A change request built from the contract's own assumptions and rates, line by line, with options, and room to negotiate in the open.p. 11
  4. 12Why Does Security Take Twelve Weeks?Start the security and data-protection reviews early, bring evidence in their format, and treat the reviewers as designers, not gatekeepers.p. 12

A plan that survives moving customers in waves, being overruled, and getting the design approved

  1. 13How Do We Move 380,000 Customers?A pilot, rehearsals, an exception queue and a reconciliation for every wave, so each move is boring because it was practised.p. 13
  2. 14What If the Client Overrules Our Design?Disagree in writing, with the cost, then commit to the client's decision and make it work; record the dissent, not a grudge.p. 14
  3. 15How Do We Get the Design Approved?Pre-wire each member, send a decision paper, not a deck, and leave the meeting with conditions you can meet.p. 15

Why Does Security Take Twelve Weeks?

TL;DR

A regulated client's security and data-protection reviews take weeks, and they always look like a surprise to the vendor who started them late. Start them in week two, meet the reviewers before you send anything, bring evidence in their format, track the reviews on the plan like any dependency, and treat their findings as design input, not obstacles. They're protecting customers you'll be responsible for too.

  • Family: bad news, honestly
  • Lives in: security/ (architecture pack, DPIA, test reports)
  • Pairs with: the discovery report, design authority day

1The situation The Grid, October 2027 – January 2028

MetroVolt is an electricity network operator holding data on 2.3 million customers, including the Priority Services Register: people on medical equipment, the elderly, households that need warning before a power cut. Any new system needs three reviews: a security architecture review (about 6 weeks), a data protection impact assessment (about 8 weeks) and a penetration test (3 weeks plus fixes).

  • Wrenfield's plan had them starting in week 2 of discovery. They were requested in week 14, after the laptops, the access delays and everything else.
  • The pilot needs all three passed. Finding 6 of the discovery report was this.
  • Hana's view of security reviewers: “people who say no for a living”.

2Both sides of the table the same moment, seen twice

MetroVolt MetroVolt's security and privacy teams

Three people review every system MetroVolt buys. They've seen suppliers send a marketing deck as “architecture”. A breach of the Priority Services Register would hurt real, vulnerable people and bring the regulator in. They aren't there to slow Lodestar; they're there for those customers.

“Show us where the vulnerable customers' data goes.”

Wrenfield Ines and Hana

Wrenfield started late and now needs the reviews done fast without asking anyone to cut corners. The only way to speed a careful reviewer up is to give them exactly the evidence they need, in the form they use.

“We can't make them faster. We can make their job easier.”

Security reviewers are designers who arrive late because they weren't invited early.

3Real-life analogy the building inspector

A builder who calls the building inspector the week before the family moves in will wait, and may have to open walls. One who invites the inspector at the drawing stage, asks what they'll check and builds it in, passes the final inspection in an afternoon. The inspector's rules aren't the builder's enemy; they're why the house doesn't burn down.

4How it works meet them, their format, findings as design, on the plan

Once the mistake is clear, Ines does four things.

  • Meet the reviewers first. An hour with each team, one question: “What evidence do you need, in what form?” The answers are specific: data flows for every personal field, encryption at rest and in transit, who can see vulnerability data, how access is logged.
  • Their format. Hana fills in MetroVolt's own architecture template rather than Wrenfield's. Bilal maps every personal field from MAVIS through Lodestar to Halvorsen for the DPIA.
  • Findings as design. Seven findings. The most important: Priority Services Register flags need field-level encryption and logged access, because they reveal health conditions. It goes into Schedule 2 as a requirement and into the design authority's conditions.
  • On the plan. Each review is tracked like any dependency, with dates and owners on both sides.

All three pass on 12 January 2028, two weeks late, not twelve. Hana's new view: “They found something we'd have regretted.”

  1. Find out which reviews apply in week one

    Security, privacy, testing, regulatory.

  2. Meet each reviewer early

    Ask what evidence they need.

  3. Use their templates

    Not yours.

  4. Map personal data end to end

    Every field, every system.

    Into the design, with owners.

Vocabulary

Security architecture review
A check that a system's design protects data and access properly.
DPIA
Data protection impact assessment: an assessment of risks to people's personal data.
Penetration test
Authorised attempts to break in, to find weaknesses.
Priority Services Register
A list of customers who need extra help, e.g. because of medical equipment.
Field-level encryption
Encrypting particular sensitive fields, not just whole databases.
Special category data
Sensitive personal data, such as health information, with stricter rules.

5What the contract says what the contract and the law require

Lodestar SOW · §10 (security) and Schedule 8signed 30 June 2027
passed

10.2 The Supplier shall comply with MetroVolt's Security Policy and shall not process Personal Data in a new system until MetroVolt's security architecture review and data protection impact assessment have been completed.

no pass, no pilot. that's the whole clause.

Sch. 8, 3 Special category data, including data held on the Priority Services Register, shall be encrypted at field level and access to it logged.

added after finding S3. in the contract now.

In plain words: security and data-protection reviews are usually conditions in the contract, and sometimes in law. Starting them late doesn't make them optional; it makes them the critical path.

9Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Starting late

    Reviews become the critical path. Fix → book them in week one.

  • Your format, not theirs

    Reviewers translate, slowly. Fix → use their templates.

  • Arguing with findings

    Delay and distrust. Fix → treat them as design input.

  • Vague data flows

    The DPIA can't conclude. Fix → map every personal field.

  • Forgetting the vulnerable

    The most sensitive data gets least attention. Fix → PSR first.

10When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Any client with personal or sensitive data.
  • Regulated industries.
  • Any system that needs approval before go-live.

Skip it when…

  • Never skip; a small internal tool may need only a light review.

11Self-check quiz

What three reviews did Lodestar need?

A security architecture review, a data protection impact assessment and a penetration test.

Why do Priority Services Register flags matter so much?

They reveal health conditions and vulnerability; a breach would hurt vulnerable people.

What was the key planning mistake?

Requesting the reviews in week 14 instead of week 2.

What does “findings as design” mean?

Each finding becomes a requirement in the design, with an owner.

What does clause 10.2 prevent?

Processing personal data in a new system before the reviews are complete.

what would you do?The pen test finds a medium-risk issue a week before the pilot. Hana says it's “theoretical”. What do you do?

Fix it or agree a documented risk acceptance with MetroVolt's security owner, with a date. It's their decision, not Wrenfield's, and the pilot shouldn't go ahead with an unowned finding.

try it

List the reviews your current project needs before go-live. When were they requested? Book a one-hour meeting with one reviewer and ask what evidence they need.

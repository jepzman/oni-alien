How Do We Get the Design Approved?

TL;DR

A design authority approves designs it already understands and trusts. So pre-wire: meet each member beforehand and ask what they need to approve. Send a short decision paper, not a deck: the decision asked for, the design on one page, each member's concern and how it's met, and the open points. Use the meeting for questions only, and leave with conditions that each have an owner and a date.

1The situation The Grid, 9 February 2028

Milestone M2, design approved, is worth £720,000: 15 % of the fixed price, and Wrenfield's cash for the spring. It needs the design authority to approve Schedule 2 v1.1 on Wednesday 9 February 2028.

  • Six members: Hollis (chair), Kofi (metering), MetroVolt security, the privacy office, the mainframe lead, and Bea from Vexmoor as Horizon assurance.
  • Callum's plan: a 60-slide deck, presented in 90 minutes.
  • Each member has a different worry. A deck answers none of them specifically.

2Both sides of the table the same moment, seen twice

MetroVolt the six members

Each member approves on behalf of something they'll have to defend later: Hollis, the architecture; Kofi, the meters; security and privacy, the customers' data; the mainframe lead, MAVIS's nights. None of them wants to discover their concern in slide 47.

“Show me my part, and how it's handled.”

Wrenfield Ines and Hana

Wrenfield needs approval, not applause, and approval comes from six separate yeses. A long presentation invites six separate “we'll need to think about it”s, and a second meeting in March.

“Six private conversations, then one short public decision.”

Approval meetings rarely change minds. They record decisions people have already reached, if you've helped them reach them.

3Real-life analogy the planning committee

An architect applying for planning permission doesn't surprise the committee. She talks to the planning officer, the neighbours and the conservation adviser first, adjusts the drawings to answer their concerns, and submits a short application that shows each concern and how it's met. The committee meeting takes fifteen minutes and ends with conditions: “brick to match; no work on Sundays”.

4How it works pre-wire, a decision paper, questions only, conditions

Pre-wire, two weeks before. Ines and Hana meet each member for 20 minutes with one question: “What do you need to see to approve this?” Security: the priority-services encryption. Kofi: the meter fields in the back-feed. The mainframe lead: the MetroBus load on MAVIS's nights. Bea: the reconciliation design and the rollback.

A decision paper, one week before: nine pages, not sixty slides. Page 1: the decision asked for (“approve Schedule 2 v1.1”). Page 2: the design on one page. Pages 3–8: one section per member, their concern in their words and how the design meets it. Page 9: open points and proposed conditions.

The meeting: 45 minutes, questions only. No presentation. Hollis asks the members in turn. Bea asks two questions about rollback; Hana answers both from page 7.

Approved with four conditions, each with an owner and a date: field-level encryption for priority-services data (Hana, before the pilot); MetroBus delay alerts (MetroVolt IT, May); a performance test by July (Oskar); Kofi's sign-off on meter fields (Kofi, March). M2 accepted on 9 February 2028; £720,000 invoiced.

  1. List every approver and their concern

    Ask; don't guess.

  2. Pre-wire each one

    Twenty minutes, one question.

  3. Write a decision paper

    Decision asked, design on a page, a section per concern.

  4. Send it a week ahead

    Time to read, not to be surprised.

  5. Use the meeting for questions

    No presentation.

  6. Propose conditions you can meet

    Owners and dates, in the minutes.

Vocabulary

Decision paper
A short document asking for a specific decision, with what's needed to make it.
Pre-wiring
Talking to decision makers individually before the meeting.
Conditional approval
Approval subject to listed conditions with owners and dates.
Milestone payment
Money released when a defined milestone is accepted.

5What the contract says the milestone, and how it's accepted

Lodestar SOW · Schedule 4 (M2)baseline v2.0
M2 accepted

M2 Design approved: signed design authority minutes and Schedule 2 v1.0 or later.

signed minutes, not a deck. Elaine insisted in June 2027.

5.5 Approval subject to conditions is acceptance of the Milestone where each condition has an owner and a date.

without this, four conditions would have held up £720,000.

In plain words: make sure conditional approval counts as acceptance, as long as each condition has an owner and a date. Otherwise every condition becomes a reason to hold a payment.

9Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • A long deck

    Every concern buried somewhere. Fix → a decision paper with a section per concern.

  • No pre-wire

    Surprises in the meeting. Fix → twenty minutes with each member.

  • Presenting in the meeting

    Time for questions runs out. Fix → questions only.

  • Vague conditions

    Payment held indefinitely. Fix → owners and dates.

  • Ignoring the reviewer

    The outside voice derails it. Fix → answer their concern on paper too.

10When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Any approval forum: design authority, architecture board, investment committee.
  • Several approvers with different concerns.
  • A payment depends on the decision.

Skip it when…

  • A single approver you speak to daily.

11Self-check quiz

What is pre-wiring?

Meeting each decision maker beforehand to learn what they need to approve.

What's in the decision paper?

The decision asked, the design on one page, a section per member's concern, and open points.

Why questions only in the meeting?

Everyone has read the paper; the time is for resolving concerns.

What were the four conditions?

PSR field encryption, MetroBus delay alerts, a performance test by July, and Kofi's sign-off on meter fields.

Why does clause 5.5 matter?

It makes conditional approval count as acceptance, so the £720,000 milestone isn't held by conditions.

what would you do?Bea asks in the meeting for a full independent review of the reconciliation code before approval. What do you say?

Point to page 7 and the M3 and M4 criteria, which test reconciliation on real runs; offer the reconciliation reports from each rehearsal to Horizon assurance. Ask Hollis to decide whether it's a condition with a date.

try it

Think of a decision you need from a group. List each person and their concern. Write a one-page decision paper with a section per concern. Who would you pre-wire, and with what question?

remember!!

Approval meetings record decisions people already reached. Pre-wire, write a paper, leave with conditions you can meet.

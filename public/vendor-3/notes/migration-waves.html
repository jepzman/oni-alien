How Do We Move 380,000 Customers?

TL;DR

A big migration should be the fourth time you've done it, not the first. Start with a pilot on a small, friendly group; run full dress rehearsals on copies of real data, timed; send what the automation can't handle to a staffed exception queue; and reconcile counts and balances after every run. Then the night is just another rehearsal, with customers.

1The situation The Grid, January 2028

Baseline v2.0 is nearly agreed: wave 1, 380,000 accounts, moves on the night of Saturday 23 September 2028. Callum's first migration plan has one line for it: “Wave 1 cutover, 23 Sep.”

  • MetroVolt has never migrated customers between systems. Neither has anyone currently at MetroVolt.
  • The profile says 117,800 wave 1 records fail the address check. After cleansing, thousands will still need a person.
  • Nobody has said who those people are.

2Both sides of the table the same moment, seen twice

MetroVolt Customer Ops

For Customer Operations, migration night is the night their customers' accounts move. If it goes wrong, the phones ring on Monday. They want it boring, and they want to know which of their staff will be working that weekend.

“Make it boring, and tell us who's working.”

Wrenfield Oskar and Bilal

For Wrenfield, the migration is the moment the fixed price is judged. An unrehearsed night is a gamble on 380,000 accounts. Rehearsals cost time and environments; not rehearsing costs much more.

“The night should be the fourth time we've done it.”

Rehearsals feel expensive until the night. Then they're the cheapest thing you did.

3Real-life analogy the theatre's dress rehearsals

A theatre doesn't open with the first full run-through. It does technical rehearsals, then dress rehearsals with costumes and lights, timed, then a preview for a friendly audience. When something goes wrong, the stage manager has a person ready in the wings. By opening night, the show has run four times.

4How it works a pilot, three rehearsals, a staffed queue, a reconciliation

A pilot. Ashgrove, 20,000 accounts in the Northern district, including 340 staff households who'll report problems kindly and fast. Live on Monday 5 June 2028, with the full process.

Three dress rehearsals of the whole of wave 1 on copies of production data: April, July and August. Each is timed end to end, including the 02:10 job and the Halvorsen file. Each must finish inside the window, or the plan changes.

An exception queue. Records the migration can't move automatically (an address it can't resolve, a duplicate it can't merge) go to a queue with the reason. Twelve Customer Ops staff, chosen by Priya's team, are trained in May. Target for the night: under 0.5 % of accounts.

A reconciliation report every run: account counts, balances and open debt, MAVIS against Lodestar, per area. Any unexplained difference fails the rehearsal. The report is a go / no-go criterion.

  1. Pick a small, friendly pilot group

    Staff households help.

  2. Rehearse the whole thing, timed

    Copies of real data, the real batch.

  3. Three rehearsals, at least

    The last one close to the night.

  4. Design the exception queue

    Reasons, screens, who works it.

  5. Train the people early

    Months, not days, before.

  6. Reconcile every run

    Counts, balances, debt; differences fail the run.

Vocabulary

Data migration
Moving data from one system to another, usually with transformation.
Dress rehearsal
A full, timed run of the migration on a copy of real data.
Pilot
A first live migration of a small group.
Exception queue
Records needing a person, with the reason.
Migration window
The time available to migrate without disrupting customers.
Reconciliation report
Proof that counts and totals match after the run.

9Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • The night as the first full run

    Every surprise happens live. Fix → three dress rehearsals.

  • Rehearsing on fake data

    Real data's oddities are missed. Fix → copies of production.

  • Untimed rehearsals

    The night runs over. Fix → time end to end.

  • An unstaffed exception queue

    Records wait for nobody. Fix → trained people, named.

  • Reconciling totals only

    Errors cancel out. Fix → per area, per measure.

10When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Any migration of customer or financial records.
  • First-time migrations for a client.
  • Large volumes in a short window.

Skip it when…

  • A small migration you can verify by hand afterwards.

11Self-check quiz

Why a pilot with staff households?

They report problems kindly and fast, and the group is small.

How many dress rehearsals, and on what data?

Three, on copies of production data, timed end to end.

What is the exception queue?

Records the automation can't migrate, with reasons, worked by trained people.

What makes a rehearsal fail?

Any unexplained reconciliation difference, or running over the window.

Who chose the exception-queue staff?

Customer Ops.

what would you do?Rehearsal 2 finishes in 12 hours 40 minutes against an 11-hour target. What happens?

The plan changes: find the slow steps, parallelise or move work earlier, and prove it in rehearsal 3. Wave 1 doesn't proceed unless the final rehearsal meets the criteria.

try it

Pick a big change you'll make in one go. Plan a pilot group, at least two rehearsals and a check after each. Who handles what the automation can't?

remember!!

The night should be the fourth time, not the first. Rehearse, staff the exceptions, reconcile every run.

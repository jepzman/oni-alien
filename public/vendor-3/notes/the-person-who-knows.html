Who Knows How It Really Works?

TL;DR

Every legacy system has a person who knows how it really works, and every project tries to drink from them at once. Protect them: one door for questions, limited, protected hours, a pair who writes every answer once, recordings of the critical parts, and a succession plan they choose themselves. Treat their knowledge as the client's asset and their goodwill as yours to earn.

  • Family: inside MAVIS
  • Lives in: discovery/berts-book/ (one page per answer)
  • Pairs with: software archaeology, one team

1The situation The Grid, October 2027

Bert Ogundipe has worked on MAVIS for 31 years. He wrote a fifth of it. Since September he's assigned to Lodestar three days a week (dependency D-09), and he retires in March 2029.

  • In the second week of October, Lottie counts Bert's interruptions: 23 “quick questions” on Tuesday, from seven people, including two from Halvorsen.
  • Half are the same question asked by different people.
  • On Wednesday Bert works from home. On Thursday he tells Elaine he's “thinking about going early”.

2Both sides of the table the same moment, seen twice

MetroVolt Bert

Bert has been the only person who knows for twenty years. It used to make him important; now it makes him tired. He's proud of MAVIS and worried that his last two years will be spent being drained by a supplier that's replacing his life's work.

“Everybody wants my head. Nobody wants me.”

Wrenfield Ines

Without Bert, discovery takes twice as long and gets things wrong. If he leaves early, Lodestar loses its best source of truth, and Wrenfield gets blamed for driving him out. His knowledge needs capturing; his goodwill needs earning.

“We need Bert for eighteen months, and he needs to want to stay.”

Key-person risk is usually treated as a risk about knowledge. It's mostly a risk about a person.

3Real-life analogy the village's last thatcher

A village has one thatcher left, seventy years old. Everyone needs a roof fixed. If they all knock on his door at once, he'll stop working. A sensible village gives him one apprentice, a fixed number of jobs a week, writes down what he teaches, and asks him whom he wants to teach. The craft survives because he wanted it to.

4How it works one door, protected hours, written once, his succession

Ines and Elaine sit down with Bert on 14 October, and mostly listen. Then they agree five changes, all of which Bert shapes:

  • One door. All questions for Bert go to Lottie, who batches them, removes duplicates and checks Bert's book first.
  • Two protected hours a day, 10:00 to 12:00. Outside them, nobody interrupts Bert about MAVIS, including Ines.
  • Written once. Lottie writes every answer as one page in Bert's book, which Bert reviews and corrects. By 10 December it has 64 pages.
  • Recorded. Bert walks through the nine nightly jobs on video, in his own words.
  • Succession, chosen by Bert. He picks what he wants to teach and to whom: Lottie, and two MetroVolt engineers he trusts, so the knowledge stays at MetroVolt.

Bert stops talking about going early. In November he asks to be credited by name in the discovery report. He is, on the first page.

  1. Count the interruptions

    Who asks what, how often.

  2. Listen to the expert first

    Ask what would make the next eighteen months good for them.

  3. One door for questions

    Batched, de-duplicated, checked against what's written.

  4. Protected hours

    Fewer, longer sessions.

  5. Write every answer once

    Reviewed by the expert, in a shared place.

  6. Let them choose their successors

    Knowledge stays with the client.

Vocabulary

Key-person dependency
Relying on one person's knowledge to deliver or run something.
Knowledge capture
Turning what's in someone's head into something others can use.
Succession plan
Who will take over someone's role or knowledge, and how.
Tacit knowledge
Know-how people use without being able to easily write it down.
Pairing
Two people working together so one learns from the other.
Bus factor
How many people would have to leave before nobody knows how something works.

5What the contract says the dependency, and what it doesn't say

Lodestar SOW · Appendix C, D-09signed 30 June 2027
a person, not a resource

D-09 MetroVolt provides a named MAVIS expert for three days per week from the Start Date.

the contract counts his days. it can't make him want to come in.

11.2 The Supplier shall transfer knowledge to MetroVolt staff throughout the Services.

Bert's successors at MetroVolt are §11.2 too.

In plain words: a dependency on a named person is fragile: the contract can count their days, not their goodwill. Treat it as a relationship and plan for the day they leave.

6Account health the same move, green, amber and red

Green

the expert is keen to share

  • Still one door and protected hours.
  • Write everything once.
  • Plan succession early.

Amber Lodestar now

the expert is overloaded

  • Listen first; let them shape the rules.
  • Protect their time visibly.
  • Credit them by name.

Red

the expert resists

  • Ask what they fear (often: being discarded).
  • Involve them in choosing successors.
  • Rely more on runtime evidence.

9Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Draining the expert

    They leave early. Fix → one door and protected hours.

  • Asking the same thing twice

    Duplicates waste their time. Fix → check the book first.

  • Capturing knowledge but not credit

    They feel used. Fix → credit them by name.

  • Choosing their successors for them

    Quiet resistance. Fix → let them choose.

  • Keeping the knowledge at the vendor

    The client is dependent on you instead. Fix → successors at the client too.

10When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Any system with one or two people who really know it.
  • The expert is overloaded or near retirement.
  • Several teams need the same person.

Skip it when…

  • Knowledge is already spread and written down.

11Self-check quiz

What is the “one door”?

All questions for the expert go through one person, who batches and de-duplicates them.

Why protected hours?

Fewer, longer sessions instead of constant interruptions, so the expert can work and stay.

What is Bert's book?

One page per answer, written by Lottie and reviewed by Bert.

Why let Bert choose his successors?

Knowledge transfers when the expert wants it to, and stays at MetroVolt.

Why is key-person risk mostly about a person?

The contract counts days; whether they stay depends on how they're treated.

what would you do?Halvorsen's engineer keeps phoning Bert directly. What do you do?

Ask Sven, kindly, to route questions through Lottie under the team's rules, and offer the relevant pages of Bert's book. Tell Bert you've done it.

try it

Name the person on your project whose absence would hurt most. Count how often they're interrupted. Design one door, protected hours and a place where every answer is written once. Ask them who they'd like to teach.

remember!!

Protect the person who knows: one door, protected hours, every answer written once, successors they choose.

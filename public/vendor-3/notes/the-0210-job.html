What Does the 02:10 Job Really Do?

TL;DR

Old batch jobs do more than anyone remembers. Trace them step by step from the scheduler, not from descriptions: each step's program, inputs, outputs and, above all, who uses the outputs. That's where you find the side effects the business quietly depends on and the undocumented consumers you'll have to keep feeding. Then decide, with the client, which to keep.

1The situation The Grid, 9 November 2027

Everyone describes the 02:10 job the same way: “MAVIS sends the customer file to Halvorsen.” One arrow. The interface agreement IA-01 is written around it. Rework of the job is excluded from Wrenfield's scope (exclusion X-02).

  • The scheduler shows fourteen steps.
  • Step 9, ADDRFIX, added in 2003, rewrites postcodes for the Northern district, which is exactly wave 1's district.
  • Step 12, COPYMTR, sends a copy of the file to Gareth's meter system. It's on nobody's list, including IA-01.

2Both sides of the table the same moment, seen twice

MetroVolt Hollis and Gareth

For Hollis, the 02:10 job is plumbing that works. Finding two undocumented steps is uncomfortable. For Gareth's team, COPYMTR is how their meter system learns about new customers; if Lodestar stops it, meters stop matching accounts, and it'll be Wrenfield's fault.

“It's always worked. Don't break it.”

Wrenfield Ines and Bilal

X-02 excludes reworking the job, but wave 1 customers will come from Lodestar, so steps 9 and 12 have to be replaced for them. That's work nobody priced, and ADDRFIX may explain why addresses fail.

“The exclusion assumed one arrow. There are fourteen.”

Side effects are features nobody asked for and everybody uses.

3Real-life analogy the old house's mystery switch

An old house has a switch by the back door that nobody uses, so the new owners plan to remove it. An electrician traces the wire first: it runs the pump that keeps the cellar dry, installed by a previous owner in 2003. Remove it, and the cellar floods in March. Nobody would have known until then.

4How it works each step: reads, writes, and who uses it

Bilal and Lottie trace the job from the scheduler's definition, one step at a time, with Bert's book open. For each step they write four things: the program, what it reads, what it writes, and who uses what it writes. The last column is the one that matters.

Step 9, ADDRFIX (2003) rewrites Northern district postcodes using a lookup table last updated in 2011. Since then, new streets and changed postcodes have been “corrected” into wrong ones. It's one of the causes of failing addresses.

Step 12, COPYMTR writes a copy of the customer file to a folder Gareth's meter system reads at 03:00. Nobody listed it because nobody at Customer Ops knew it existed, and Network Assets assumed it was part of “the file”.

Both go to Elaine the same week, as facts, with a note on X-02: the exclusion priced reworking the job for MAVIS; Lodestar must now replace steps 9 and 12 for migrated customers. Kofi is added to IA-01 for COPYMTR. The cost goes into the discovery report.

  1. Start from the scheduler's definition

    Not from anyone's description.

  2. For each step: program, reads, writes

    From the code and the job control.

  3. Then: who uses each output?

    Follow files and tables to their readers.

  4. Mark side effects and hidden consumers

    Anything that changes data or feeds someone unlisted.

  5. Check with the expert

    Bert's book, then Bert.

  6. Tell the client as facts, with scope impact

    Before the discovery report.

Vocabulary

Batch job
A sequence of steps run on a schedule.
Job control
The definitions that tell the scheduler what to run and in what order.
Side effect
A change a step makes beyond its stated purpose.
Hidden consumer
A system or team using an output nobody listed.
Lineage
Where data comes from and where it goes.
Exclusion
Work explicitly outside the contract's scope.

5What the contract says what the exclusion covered, and what it didn't

Lodestar SOW · Appendix C, X-02signed 30 June 2027
assumption tested

X-02 Rework of the 02:10 MAVIS → Halvorsen batch job is excluded.

we priced one arrow out. wave 1 needs steps 9 and 12 replaced for migrated customers.

3.2 If an Assumption proves materially incorrect, either Party may raise a Change Request under clause 7.

the job's description was an assumption too. we wrote it down. good.

In plain words: an exclusion is based on a description of the thing excluded. If the description was wrong, the exclusion may not protect you, so tell the client early and let the change process handle it.

9Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Trusting the description

    One arrow hides fourteen steps. Fix → trace from the scheduler.

  • Ignoring who reads the outputs

    Hidden consumers break after go-live. Fix → follow every output to its readers.

  • Blaming the 2003 fix

    It solved a real problem then. Fix → describe it neutrally.

  • Hiding the scope impact

    It turns up in the change request as a surprise. Fix → tell the client the same week.

  • Forgetting the interface agreement

    The hidden consumer has no owner. Fix → add them to the RACI.

10When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Any batch job you'll replace or feed.
  • Any interface described as “one file”.
  • Before relying on an exclusion.

Skip it when…

  • The job is fully documented, recent and has tests.

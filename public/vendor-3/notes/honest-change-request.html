How Do We Ask for More Money Honestly?

TL;DR

A change request after bad news is where trust is won or lost. Build it line by line from the contract: each line tied to an assumption, exclusion or rate the client already signed. No padding to “leave room”. Offer options that move scope, not just price, and negotiate in the open by changing what's delivered, never by quietly changing the rates. Then re-baseline.

1The situation The Grid, December 2027 – February 2028

With the discovery report, Wrenfield submits CR-007 on 14 December: +£1.1m and +4 months to deliver wave 1 against what discovery found.

  • Elaine's first words: “Orion's vendor did this after discovery too.” She doesn't say it angrily. That's worse.
  • Graham, from Leeds: “Ask for £1.5m. They'll negotiate down to what we need.”
  • Nkem has to take the number to Horizon's investment board in a 15 % cost-cut year.

2Both sides of the table the same moment, seen twice

MetroVolt Elaine and Nkem

For MetroVolt, a big change request after discovery is the Orion pattern: win cheap, then charge. Elaine needs to see that every pound comes from something MetroVolt already agreed to, and Nkem needs options she can take to a cost-cutting board.

“Show me it's the contract, not a trick.”

Wrenfield Ines and Callum

Wrenfield's fixed price assumed a world that discovery showed isn't real. The contract says so, line by line. If the request is padded, the one thing that separates Wrenfield from Orion's vendor (honesty) is gone.

“Every line from a clause. No line for “room”.”

Padding a change request to leave room for negotiation teaches the client that your numbers aren't real. After that, every number is negotiated.

3Real-life analogy the builder who found asbestos

The builder from the assumptions note opened the wall and found asbestos. An honest builder doesn't add a mystery 30 % “for complications”. He points at the line in the quote (“if found, removal priced separately”), shows the specialist's invoice, and offers options: full removal; seal it and box it in; or do this room now and the rest next year.

4How it works every line from a clause; negotiate scope, not rates

Line by line. CR-007 has four lines, each pointing to the contract: cleansing above A1-07, 26 points at £9,500 (the formula MetroVolt signed): £247k. Replacing steps 9 and 12 of the 02:10 job, which exclusion X-02 never covered: £310k at the rate card. Change capture (the licence and mainframe work, not in Schedule 5): £180k. Reconciliation and exception tooling for 117,800 failing records: £363k. Total £1.1m. No line for contingency or “room”.

Options that move scope. In January, Ines, Nkem and Elaine work through the lines together. MetroVolt's own Customer Ops data team cleanses about 40 % of failing addresses (they know the streets): −£99k. Hollis's decision to use MetroBus instead of the licence (note 14): −£85k, +3 weeks. The self-service portal moves to wave 2: −£216k.

Agreed on 11 February 2028: +£700k, +3 months. Not one rate changed. The baseline becomes v2.0: pilot 5 June 2028, wave 1 night Saturday 23 September 2028. Elaine's comment at steering: “That's the first change request I've understood.”

  1. Tie every line to a clause

    Assumption, exclusion, schedule or rate.

  2. Show the arithmetic

    From the contract's own formulas.

  3. No padding

    Not for contingency, not for room.

  4. Offer options that move scope

    Who does what, what moves to later.

  5. Negotiate in the open

    Change what's delivered, never the rates.

  6. Re-baseline when agreed

    New version, dated, signed.

Vocabulary

Change request (CR)
A formal request to change scope, price or dates.
Padding
Adding unexplained money to leave room for negotiation.
Re-baseline
Agreeing a new baseline after a significant change.
Scope trade
Reducing or moving scope to offset cost.
Client-side work
Work the client takes on to reduce cost.
Win cheap, charge later
A pattern of under-bidding and recovering through changes.

5What the contract says the clauses doing the work

Lodestar SOW · §3.2, §7, Appendix CCR-007 agreed 11 Feb 2028
agreed

3.2 If an Assumption proves materially incorrect, either Party may raise a Change Request under clause 7.

the clause we fought for in June 2027. it paid for itself in one line.

7.2 The Supplier shall provide an impact assessment … priced using the Rates in Schedule 5.

the rates did the arithmetic. nobody argued with them.

DL-031 CR-007 approved at £700,000 and three months. Baseline v2.0 adopted.

In plain words: a change request built from the contract's own clauses and rates is hard to dispute and easy to negotiate in the open. The negotiation moves scope and responsibilities, not the price of a day.

9Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Padding

    Teaches the client your numbers aren't real. Fix → every line from a clause.

  • Haggling over rates

    Future changes all re-argued. Fix → negotiate scope, never rates.

  • One take-it-or-leave-it number

    The client can't find a way to yes. Fix → options that move scope.

  • Late submission

    It looks like failure. Fix → with the discovery report.

  • No re-baseline

    Old dates haunt every status report. Fix → new version, signed.

10When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • After discovery changes the picture.
  • When an assumption or exclusion proves false.
  • Any request for more money or time.

Skip it when…

  • Small changes inside the change pool: the normal process will do.

11Self-check quiz

What does every line of an honest CR point to?

A clause, assumption, exclusion or rate the client already signed.

Why not pad the request?

It teaches the client your numbers aren't real, and every future number gets argued.

How did CR-007 go from £1.1m to £700k?

MetroVolt cleansed about 40 %, MetroBus replaced the licence, and the portal moved to wave 2.

What didn't change in the negotiation?

The day rates.

What are the new dates in baseline v2.0?

Pilot Monday 5 June 2028; wave 1 night Saturday 23 September 2028.

what would you do?Nkem asks for a further 10 % off “as a goodwill gesture for Horizon”. What do you answer?

Offer goodwill in scope or time (a free half-day data workshop, a later invoice date), and explain that a cut without a trade would make every line of the CR look padded. The rates stay.

try it

Take a request for more money or time you need to make. Tie every line to something already agreed. Then write two options that move scope or responsibility instead of price.

remember!!

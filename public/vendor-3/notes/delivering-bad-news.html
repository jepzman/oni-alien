How Do We Tell Them It's Worse Than They Think?

TL;DR

Bad news delivered badly becomes a fight about how it was delivered. Tell it in person, in the right order: the expert first (check the facts, no surprises about their system), then your counterpart, then the people who own the problem, then everyone else. Bring the number, the evidence, what it means and what you propose, and no blame. Nobody important should hear it first in a meeting.

1The situation The Grid, 8–14 December 2027

The discovery report goes to the steering committee on 14 December. Its first line is 31 %. Callum's plan: email the report to all eleven steering members on Friday 10 December, “so they have time to read it”.

  • Priya would read it on her phone, the same evening as Lord Varnish and Oliver Thane. The data is hers.
  • Bert would learn from a report that “37 % of MAVIS never runs” and that a 2003 fix corrupts postcodes.
  • Kofi would learn that his meter system depends on a hidden step, from a document his boss Gareth reads at the same time.

2Both sides of the table the same moment, seen twice

MetroVolt Priya

Priya owns Customer Operations' data. Hearing that a third of it is wrong is bad. Hearing it at the same time as Lord Varnish and the Vexmoor partner, without a chance to think, is humiliating, and in a Horizon year it's dangerous.

“Let me hear it first, from you, in a room.”

Wrenfield Ines

Ines needs the findings accepted, not fought. That depends less on the numbers than on who hears them first and how. An email to eleven people is the fastest way to make Priya defend her data instead of fixing it.

“The order of telling is part of the message.”

People forgive bad news. They don't forgive being the last to know about their own area.

3Real-life analogy a doctor's diagnosis

A doctor with bad test results doesn't email them to the whole family. She checks the results with the lab, tells the patient first, in a room, with time, the facts and a plan, and lets the patient decide who else to tell and when. The facts are the same either way. The order changes everything about what happens next.

4How it works the expert, the counterpart, the owners, then everyone

Ines replaces the Friday email with a telling order, over six days:

  • Bert, Wednesday 8 December: every finding about MAVIS, to check facts. He corrects one number and asks that ADDRFIX be described as “a 2003 fix that outlived its lookup table”, which is fairer.
  • Elaine, Thursday 08:00 at the café: the whole picture, under the no-surprises pact.
  • Priya and Nkem, Thursday 14:00, in Priya's office: the data is theirs and so is the money. Bilal shows thirty failing records on screen. Priya asks for an hour alone, then calls Ines back: “Option B. And I want to tell Rupert myself.”
  • Hollis, Friday: the 02:10 job, change capture, the design. Kofi, Friday: COPYMTR, before Gareth reads it.
  • The steering committee, Tuesday 14 December. Nobody hears 31 % there for the first time. Priya presents finding 1 herself.

In every conversation: the number, the evidence, what it means, no blame, and what Wrenfield proposes.

  1. List who owns each finding

    Data, money, systems, teams.

  2. Check facts with the expert first

    And let them shape fair wording.

  3. Tell your counterpart early

    No surprises.

  4. Tell owners in person

    The number, evidence, meaning, proposal.

  5. Let owners tell their bosses

    If they want to.

  6. Only then the big meeting

    Where nobody hears it first.

5What the contract says the contract's view of bad news

Lodestar SOW · Schedule 7, §6 (early warning)v1.1 · Aug 2027
early warning

6.1 Each Party shall notify the other promptly of any matter which could increase the Charges, delay a Milestone or impair performance.

“promptly” and “notify” don't say “by email to everyone”.

6.3 The Parties shall meet within 5 Working Days of an early warning to consider how to avoid or reduce its effect.

the Thursday meetings were 6.3, early.

In plain words: contracts usually require early warning of anything that could change cost or time. How you warn is up to you; a meeting with the owner meets the clause and the relationship.

9Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Email to everyone at once

    The owner learns with their boss. Fix → owners first, in person.

  • Skipping the expert

    A wrong fact, or an insulted colleague. Fix → check with them first.

  • Blame in the wording

    “Corrupted by a bad fix.” Fix → conditions, not people.

  • Telling the boss first

    Escalation by surprise. Fix → let the owner tell their boss.

  • Surprises at the meeting

    The meeting becomes a fight. Fix → nobody hears it there first.

10When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Any finding that hurts someone's area, budget or pride.
  • Before a big meeting.
  • When the audience includes rivals.

Skip it when…

  • Emergencies, where speed matters more than order: tell everyone at once, then follow up in person.

11Self-check quiz

Why tell the expert first?

To check the facts and agree fair wording, so they aren't surprised about their own system.

Who owns finding 1, and how were they told?

Priya and Nkem (data and money), in person, with thirty failing records on screen.

Why let Priya tell Rupert herself?

It protects her standing; she presents bad news as a leader, not as its victim.

What's in each conversation?

The number, the evidence, what it means, no blame, and a proposal.

What's the rule for the big meeting?

Nobody important hears the bad news there for the first time.

What Do We Say When Discovery Changes Everything?

TL;DR

A discovery report exists to change decisions, and the people who decide will read two pages. Put the findings first, with numbers; then what they mean for the plan and the price; then priced options and a recommendation. The evidence goes behind, one section per finding. Credit the people who helped. If the report doesn't change anything, either discovery didn't look or the report didn't say.

1The situation The Grid, room 27A, 14 December 2027

Discovery ended on 10 December. The report, deliverable D4, goes to the steering committee on Tuesday 14 December. It has six findings, and the first one is 31 %.

  • Callum's first draft is 112 pages: method, interviews, every table. The findings start on page 71.
  • Priya will read two pages. Rupert's delegate, one. Oliver Thane will read all of it, looking for weak points.
  • The draft doesn't say what Wrenfield recommends. Callum thought that would “look like selling”.

2Both sides of the table the same moment, seen twice

MetroVolt Priya and the steering

MetroVolt paid £380k to find out what's really in MAVIS. The steering committee needs to decide one thing: what now? It wants the facts, what they cost, and the vendor's honest advice, in that order, and short.

“Tell me what you found and what you'd do.”

Wrenfield Ines and Callum

Wrenfield needs a report that's impossible to dispute and easy to act on, without looking like a sales document for more money. Callum's instinct is to prove the effort; Ines's is to prove the findings.

“Page one should be enough to decide.”

A recommendation isn't selling. Refusing to give one, after being paid to look, is a kind of hiding.

3Real-life analogy the house survey

A good building survey starts with a one-page summary: “roof sound; damp in the cellar, about £6,000 to fix; wiring needs replacing within five years.” Then the options and the surveyor's advice. The fifty pages of photos and readings come after, for anyone who wants to check. A survey that starts with the methodology is a survey nobody reads.

4How it works findings first, meaning, priced options, a recommendation

Ines rewrites the front: two pages that stand alone.

  • Findings, numbered, each with a number: 31 % of wave 1 addresses fail; the 02:10 job has 14 steps, two undocumented; 37 % of MAVIS never runs; change capture needs a licence; the billing spec missed six behaviours (now fixed in v1.2); the security review started late.
  • What they mean: the baseline dates and price can't hold; three assumptions (A1-07, X-02, D-03) are affected; the rest of the design stands.
  • Three options, each with cost, date and risk. A: as planned, with cleansing at A1-07's rate (+£1.1m, +4 months). B: MetroVolt's own team cleanses a share, portal moved to wave 2 (less cost, +3 months). C: a smaller wave 1 (190,000 accounts) on the original date.
  • A recommendation: B, with one sentence why.

Behind it, 38 pages of evidence, one section per finding. Bert is credited by name on page one. The 112-page draft becomes the appendix. Oliver Thane reads all of it and finds two typos.

  1. Write the two front pages first

    Then build the evidence behind them.

  2. Number the findings, each with a number

    No adjectives without data.

  3. Say what they mean

    For the plan, the price, the assumptions.

  4. Give priced options

    Cost, date and risk for each.

  5. Recommend one

    One sentence why.

  6. Credit the people who helped

    By name, on page one.

Vocabulary

Discovery report
The findings of a discovery phase and their consequences.
Executive summary
The pages that stand alone for decision makers.
Finding
A fact discovered, stated with evidence.
Option
A possible course of action, with its cost, time and risk.
Recommendation
The option the author advises, and why.
Pyramid principle
Lead with the answer; support it with the reasons below.

5What the contract says what D4 is for

Lodestar SOW · Schedule 3, D4 · §4.2.4signed 30 June 2027
D4

D4 A discovery report setting out findings, their effect on the Assumptions, and options.

“and options”. a report without options doesn't meet the deliverable.

4.2.4 Milestone dates will be re-baselined at the end of discovery by agreement.

this report is the start of that agreement.

In plain words: the contract usually defines what the discovery report must contain. Findings without options don't meet it; options without a recommendation leave the client to guess what you think.

9Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Methodology first

    Findings on page 71. Fix → findings first.

  • Adjectives without numbers

    “Significant data issues.” Fix → 31 %, 117,800 records.

  • No recommendation

    The client has to guess. Fix → recommend one, say why.

  • Unpriced options

    Nobody can choose. Fix → cost, date and risk for each.

  • No credit to helpers

    The client's expert feels used. Fix → name them on page one.

10When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • The end of any discovery or assessment.
  • Findings that change price or plan.
  • Readers who won't read past page two.

Skip it when…

  • Never skip the front pages; the evidence can be short if the findings are small.

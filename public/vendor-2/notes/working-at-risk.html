Should We Start Before It's Signed?

TL;DR

Starting before the contract is signed can save weeks, and can also mean lending your client money they never repay. Only do it with a written letter of intent that sets a scope, a cap, an end date and says the client pays your costs if it doesn't proceed. Work inside that fence and nowhere else, report the spend weekly, and stop when the money or the date runs out.

  • Family: the paper
  • Lives in: contract/lodestar/letter-of-intent.pdf
  • Pairs with: the stalled signature, day one

1The situation Wrenfield, 21 May 2027

With the contract stuck until at least 22 June, Wrenfield's six named people are waiting. Each idle week costs Wrenfield about £35k in salaries. Hana has had two other offers inside Wrenfield. Graham wants to start on 1 June anyway, “as a gesture”.

  • If Wrenfield starts without anything in writing and Horizon cancels Lodestar, MetroVolt owes nothing. Clause 5 of the award letter says so.
  • If Wrenfield waits, security vetting and laptop requests (dependency D-05) won't even begin until July.
  • Elaine would like Wrenfield to start too. She can't promise money she doesn't control.

2Both sides of the table the same moment, seen twice

MetroVolt Elaine and Desmond

MetroVolt wants the programme to start the day the contract is signed, not six weeks after. But Desmond can't commit spend before Horizon approves. A small, capped letter is something procurement can sign; an open promise isn't.

“We can cover a small, defined piece. Not a programme.”

Wrenfield Graham and Ines

Wrenfield needs to keep the team together and get the slow things moving (vetting, access, laptops), without lending MetroVolt money. The answer is a letter that pays for preparation, not delivery, up to a cap.

“Prepare at their risk. Build only at ours, and only after signing.”

A letter of intent is a small contract about the waiting. It lets both sides move without pretending the big contract exists.

3Real-life analogy the deposit on the wedding venue

A couple wants a wedding venue, but their budget won't be approved until next month. The venue offers to hold the date and start planning for a small, refundable-to-them deposit, written down: tastings and planning only, no catering ordered, no flowers bought. If the wedding is off, the venue keeps the deposit for the planning it did. Nobody orders the cake.

4How it works a fence: scope, cap, end date, costs covered

Graham asks Desmond for a letter of intent, and Ines drafts what it should cover. Signed on Friday 21 May, it sets:

  • Scope: mobilisation planning, environment and access requests, security vetting, discovery preparation (reading documents MetroVolt shares, planning interviews).
  • Cap: £250k, at the contract's day rates.
  • End: on signature (when the work counts towards discovery) or 30 June, whichever comes first.
  • If MetroVolt doesn't proceed: it pays Wrenfield's costs up to the cap.

Outside the fence: no build work, no access to customer data, no promises to anyone at MetroVolt. Callum reports the spend to Desmond every Friday. By 30 June it's £212k, and the security vetting is done, which is why the team can at least get into the building on 5 July.

  1. Ask for it in writing

    A phone call isn't a letter of intent.

  2. Define the scope narrowly

    Preparation, not delivery.

  3. Set a cap and an end date

    Whichever comes first.

  4. Say who pays if it stops

    Costs up to the cap, at agreed rates.

  5. Report the spend weekly

    To the person who signed it.

  6. Stop at the fence

    No work outside the scope, however tempting.

Vocabulary

Letter of intent (LOI)
A short, limited agreement before the main contract.
Working at risk
Spending before you're sure you'll be paid.
Mobilisation
Getting people, access and tools ready to start.
Cap
The maximum the client will pay under the letter.
Long-stop date
The date the letter ends if the contract isn't signed.
Security vetting
Background checks needed before access to client systems.

5What the contract says the letter of intent

MetroVolt letter of intent · Lodestar21 May 2027
capped

2 MetroVolt authorises the Supplier to carry out the Preparatory Work described in the Annex, up to a maximum of £250,000 at the Rates in the tender response.

the Annex is the fence. read it to the team.

4 If the Contract is not signed by 30 June 2027, MetroVolt shall pay the Supplier's reasonable costs of the Preparatory Work up to the maximum.

this is the clause that makes it a letter, not a hope.

5 Preparatory Work carried out after signature of the Contract counts towards the Discovery Services.

In plain words: a letter of intent is only worth something if it says what happens if the big contract never comes. Without clause 4, it's a polite way of working for free.

9Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Starting on a promise

    Cancelled, and nobody pays. Fix → a letter that says who pays.

  • No cap

    A letter becomes an open cheque. Fix → a cap and an end date.

  • Scope creep inside the letter

    Preparation turns into building. Fix → a narrow annex, read to the team.

  • Touching data early

    Data protection without a contract. Fix → no customer data before signature.

  • Forgetting to report

    The client is surprised by the bill. Fix → weekly spend to the signer.

10When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Signature is delayed but likely.
  • Slow preparation (vetting, access) would delay the start.
  • The team would scatter otherwise.

Skip it when…

  • The client won't put anything in writing: then don't start; offer a free half-day at most.

11Self-check quiz

What makes a letter of intent worth having?

A clause saying the client pays your costs, up to a cap, if the contract isn't signed.

What four things should it set?

Scope, cap, end date, and who pays if it stops.

Why preparation only, not build work?

Build work commits both sides to a design before the contract, and is the most expensive to lose.

Why no customer data before signature?

Data protection terms are in the contract; without them, access is a risk to both sides.

What did the letter achieve for day one?

Security vetting was done, so the team could get into the building on 5 July.

what would you do?On 28 June Elaine asks the team to “just start migrating a test batch” to save time. What do you say?

Not yet: data and build work are outside the letter, and the contract is two days away. Offer to have everything ready to start the day it's signed.

Think of work you've been asked to start before an agreement is final. Write a one-paragraph letter of intent: scope, cap, end date and who pays if it stops. Would the other side sign it?

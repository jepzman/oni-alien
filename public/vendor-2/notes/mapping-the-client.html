Who Really Decides Here?

TL;DR

As a vendor you can see the client's politics but not touch them. The org chart tells you who reports to whom; it doesn't tell you who decides, who can block, and who is listened to. Spend the first weeks listening, then draw the real map: each person's influence on your project, where they stand, and what they need. Keep it private, update it monthly, and use it to decide whom to talk to, never to play games.

1The situation The Grid, 16 July 2027

The contract's governance schedule shows a neat chart: Priya (sponsor) → Elaine (programme director) → Hollis, Nkem, Kofi. Twelve days on floor 19 have shown Ines a different picture.

  • Since Horizon, Rupert Thorne's board approves anything Priya wants, and Vexmoor advises Rupert.
  • Ottoline Grey, the Chief of Staff, isn't on any Lodestar chart, yet every senior meeting Ines hears about was “checked with Ottoline” first.
  • Gareth Pound owns the smart-meter data Lodestar needs, and wanted Lodestar in his own Network Assets programme.
  • Bert Ogundipe has no title worth mentioning. If Bert says MAVIS data can't be trusted, nobody will argue with him.

2Both sides of the table the same moment, seen twice

MetroVolt MetroVolt's people

Inside MetroVolt, everyone knows the real map, and nobody writes it down. People are cautious about what a supplier hears: in a Horizon year, being seen to brief the vendor can be held against them.

“We'll tell you what you need to know. Eventually.”

Wrenfield Ines

Ines needs to know whom to brief before a decision, whose worry will sink a design, and whose support matters. She can't ask directly (“who really decides here?”) without sounding political. So she listens, and draws.

“I don't want to play the politics. I need to not be run over by them.”

A vendor who ignores the client's politics gets surprised. A vendor who plays them gets thrown out. The map is for not being surprised.

3Real-life analogy the new teacher's seating plan

A new teacher gets the official class list on day one. By week two she has her own seating plan in pencil: who the class listens to, who'll derail a lesson, who's quiet but brilliant, who needs to be asked rather than told. She never shows it to the class. She uses it to decide whom to talk to first.

4How it works listen, then map influence, stance and need

For two weeks Ines just notices: who is mentioned before decisions (“have you checked with…?”), who arrives late and is waited for, whose objections end a discussion, who is copied on emails and who is quietly removed from them.

Then she draws a power map: up the side, influence on Lodestar; along the bottom, for, neutral or against. Priya and Elaine: high, for. Rupert, Hollis, Bert: high, neutral. Gareth: high, against. Nkem, Kofi, Ottoline lower; Vexmoor's Oliver lower but leaning against.

The useful part is the third column: what each person needs. Priya needs a visible win for Customer Ops in a cut year. Hollis needs to be asked, not informed. Bert needs respect and a successor before he retires. Gareth needs credit for the meter data. Each need becomes an action: Hollis gets design questions before the design authority; Bert gets Lottie as his apprentice; Gareth's team is named on every Lodestar slide that shows meter data.

The map lives in Ines's own notebook, not in a shared folder. It's updated on the first Friday of each month.

Vocabulary

Power map
A drawing of who influences a decision and how.
Stakeholder
Anyone who can affect, or is affected by, the work.
Influence
How much someone can change the outcome, whatever their title.
Stance
For, neutral or against, and why.
Informal power
Influence that doesn't come from position: knowledge, trust, access.
Gatekeeper
Someone whose approval or knowledge others must pass through.

5What the contract says what the contract says about who decides

Lodestar SOW · §8 governance and Schedule 7signed 30 June 2027
official only

8.1 MetroVolt's Sponsor is the Head of Customer Operations. MetroVolt's Programme Director is MetroVolt's authorised representative for the day-to-day management of the Contract.

Elaine signs. Priya sponsors. Rupert approves. the contract only knows two of them.

8.5 Instructions to the Supplier are valid only if given by MetroVolt's Programme Director or the Steering Committee.

so Gareth can ask. only Elaine can instruct.

In plain words: the contract names who can instruct you, which protects you from everyone else's requests. It doesn't tell you who has influence. You need both maps: one for instructions, one for influence.

9Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Trusting the org chart

    The real decider isn't on it. Fix → listen for who's mentioned before decisions.

  • Asking “who really decides?”

    You sound political. Fix → notice instead.

  • Playing the politics

    Taking sides in a client's fight. Fix → map to avoid surprises, never to win games.

  • Sharing the map

    A list of who's “against” leaks. Fix → your notebook only.

  • Ignoring the experts

    Bert has no title and all the influence on data. Fix → map influence, not rank.

10When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Any client with more than a handful of decision makers.
  • A reorganisation is underway.
  • Someone powerful seems against the project.

Skip it when…

  • A tiny client where you work directly with the owner.

11Self-check quiz

What does a power map show that an org chart doesn't?

Who influences the decision, where they stand, and what they need.

Why is the “needs” column the useful one?

Each need becomes an action that turns opposition into neutrality or support.

Why keep the map private?

A list of who's “against” would damage relationships if it leaked.

Who can give Wrenfield instructions under the contract?

Only Elaine (the programme director) or the steering committee.

Why does Bert rank high on influence?

On MAVIS data, his view ends every argument, whatever his title.

try it

Draw the people around your project on two axes: influence and stance. Add a third column: what each of them needs. Pick the most influential person against you and write one action that meets their need.

remember!!

The org chart shows who reports to whom. Listen until you can draw who decides, who blocks, and what each one needs.

What Are We Measured Against?

TL;DR

By the time a project has really started, the deal has already moved: a late start, relief, traded scope, an assumption that looks shaky. Before the work gets serious, agree a baseline: one page (plus its documents) with the current scope, plan, money, risks and assumptions, signed at steering. Every later change, delay or argument is then measured against the same thing, and changes to the baseline only happen through steering.

  • Family: two firms, one team
  • Lives in: delivery/lodestar/baseline-v1.0.pdf · DL-014
  • Pairs with: change control, the first status report, acceptance

1The situation The Grid, room 27A, 28 September 2027

Twelve weeks in, the deal Wrenfield is delivering isn't quite the one it bid. The start moved from 1 June to 5 July. Discovery now ends on 10 December (relief RN-01). Training became train-the-trainer in the final offer. Bert moved from one day a week to three. And MetroVolt's sample showed 7.8 % of addresses failing, not the 5 % in A1-07.

  • Each change is recorded somewhere: the change register, the decision log, the BAFO letter, a relief notice.
  • Nobody has a single page that says what the deal is now.
  • Vexmoor's first assurance note, due at the September steering, will compare Wrenfield's progress against something. Ines would like it to be the right thing.

2Both sides of the table the same moment, seen twice

MetroVolt Priya and Nkem

MetroVolt needs one set of numbers for Horizon: what Lodestar costs, when the pilot goes live, what's at risk. Finance can't report against five documents. Priya wants the September steering to sign something she can show Rupert.

“Give me the one page I'll be measured against too.”

Wrenfield Ines and Callum

Wrenfield wants every later conversation, especially with Vexmoor, to start from an agreed starting line, not from the bid or a memory. A signed baseline also records, in public, that A1-07 is already “at risk”, three months before discovery tests it.

“Measure us against what we agreed, all of it, on one page.”

A baseline isn't a plan you promise never to change. It's the plan you agree to change only in the open.

3Real-life analogy the navigator's planned line

A ship's navigator draws the planned route in pencil and marks a fix at the start of the voyage. Every later position is compared with that line: ahead, behind, off course. If the route changes, the navigator draws a new line, dates it, and keeps the old one visible. Nobody argues about where the ship should be, because the line is on the chart.

4How it works one page, five parts, signed, versioned

Callum and Ines assemble baseline v1.0 from the documents that already exist, so nothing is new and everything is in one place:

  • Scope: Schedule 2 v1.0 (approved at the design authority on 15 September) plus the change register (CR-001; CN-001).
  • Plan: discovery ends 10 December, report on 14 December; pilot (20,000 accounts) live Monday 6 March 2028; wave 1 (380,000) go-live Saturday 24 June 2028.
  • Money: £5.0m (£380k discovery, £4.62m fixed); £142k left in the change pool.
  • Risks: the top ten, each with an owner. Number one: A1-07 at risk: the sample shows 7.8 %; full profiling in discovery.
  • Assumptions: all 41; two updated (D-09 met at three days; X-04 is now train-the-trainer).

At steering on Tuesday 28 September in room 27A, Priya signs it; it's logged as DL-014. Oliver Thane presents Vexmoor's first assurance note. Its main finding: “the data migration plan is optimistic”. Ines thanks him, and points to risk 1 on the baseline: “We agree. It's the first thing discovery tests, and it's priced in A1-07.” From now on, changes to the baseline happen only at steering, as v1.1, v1.2.

  1. Collect what has already changed

    Start date, relief, traded scope, assumptions.

  2. Assemble one page from existing documents

    Scope, plan, money, risks, assumptions.

  3. Mark what's already at risk

    Say it now, publicly.

  4. Sign it at steering

    Logged, versioned, v1.0.

  5. Report against it every week

    Status compares with the baseline, not with the bid.

  6. Change it only through steering

    New version, dated, old one kept.

Vocabulary

Baseline
The agreed version of scope, plan, cost and risk that progress is measured against.
Re-baseline
Formally agreeing a new baseline after a significant change.
Variance
The difference between where you are and the baseline.
Schedule 2
Lodestar's solution description, now version 1.0.
Assurance note
An independent reviewer's written findings.
Version control
Keeping each version dated and the old ones visible.

5What the contract says what the baseline does to the contract

Lodestar SOW · §4.2.4 and Schedule 4as amended 28 Sep 2027
baselined

4.2.4 Milestone dates will be re-baselined at the end of discovery by agreement.

and we'll be back here in December. A1-07 will decide how.

Sch. 4, 1.3 Baseline v1.0 (DL-014) sets the Milestone dates for the purposes of this Schedule until amended under clause 7.

now there's one set of dates, signed.

In plain words: a signed baseline becomes the reference the contract measures you against. Put in it everything that has already changed, and every risk you already see, so the next argument starts from the truth.

9Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Measuring against the bid

    The deal has moved since. Fix → a baseline with every change in it.

  • A baseline in ten documents

    Nobody can see it. Fix → one page that points to the documents.

  • Hiding known risks

    An assurer finds them first. Fix → name them in the baseline.

  • Changing it quietly

    Nobody knows which version counts. Fix → only through steering, versioned.

  • Treating it as a promise never to change

    Then it's ignored when reality moves. Fix → agree to change it openly.

10When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Once a project's start has settled.
  • After any big change (end of discovery, a major CR).
  • When someone independent will measure you.

Skip it when…

  • A small, short piece of work where the contract is the baseline.

11Self-check quiz

What five parts does Lodestar's baseline have?

Scope, plan, money, risks and assumptions.

Why assemble it from existing documents?

Nothing new needs agreeing; it just puts the current deal on one page.

Why mark A1-07 as risk 1 publicly?

It was already likely; naming it first means the assurer's finding agrees with Wrenfield instead of exposing it.

How can the baseline change?

Only through steering, as a new dated version.

What are the milestone dates in v1.0?

Pilot Monday 6 March 2028; wave 1 go-live Saturday 24 June 2028.

what would you do?Oliver suggests the baseline should say “wave 1 by June 2028, guaranteed”. What do you say?

No: the baseline records the agreed plan and its risks, and the contract already says dates are re-baselined after discovery. Adding “guaranteed” would hide risk 1, which his own note found.

try it

Write a one-page baseline for your current work: scope, plan, money, risks, assumptions, each pointing to where it's recorded. What has already changed since the start? Who would sign it?

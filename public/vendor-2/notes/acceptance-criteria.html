How Will We Know It's Done?

TL;DR

Every milestone payment waits behind an acceptance clause, so write it as a test both sides can run: named criteria for each milestone, the evidence you'll provide, a deadline for the client to decide, rejections only with reasons tied to a criterion, and deemed acceptance if they say nothing. “To our satisfaction” is a door without a handle.

  • Family: the paper
  • Lives in: SOW §5 and Schedule 4 (milestones)
  • Pairs with: the statement of work, the baseline

1The situation The Grid, floor 19, 16 June 2027

Draft clause 5.2 says a milestone is accepted “when completed to MetroVolt's reasonable satisfaction”. There are no criteria, no deadline, and nothing about what happens if MetroVolt says nothing.

  • Milestone M3, the pilot, is worth £1.2m: a quarter of the fixed price, and Wrenfield's cash flow for the spring.
  • Oskar remembers a project where acceptance took five months because the client's tester was on leave. Nobody refused; nobody accepted.
  • Elaine has the opposite memory: Orion's supplier claimed a milestone was “done” because a document existed.

2Both sides of the table the same moment, seen twice

MetroVolt Elaine and Anjali

After Orion, MetroVolt wants to accept working results, not documents, and to be able to say no. “Reasonable satisfaction” feels like protection: MetroVolt decides. Nobody on the client side has thought about what silence means.

“We won't pay for another document.”

Wrenfield Oskar and Callum

Wrenfield wants acceptance to be a test, not an opinion: criteria agreed now, evidence it can produce, a decision by a date. And it wants protection from the slow no: a reviewer on holiday shouldn't stop a payroll.

“Tell us the test now, and we'll pass it.”

Both fears are reasonable: paying for nothing, and doing the work and never being paid. Written criteria and a deadline cure both.

3Real-life analogy the MOT test

A car's MOT isn't “to the inspector's satisfaction”. It's a list of checks (brakes, lights, emissions), each pass or fail, with a written reason for any failure. You can fix the failed item and come back for a re-test. Nobody can fail your car for being the wrong colour, and the garage can't keep it for five months.

4How it works criteria, evidence, a deadline, reasons, and silence means yes

Oskar and Elaine's test manager write criteria for each milestone, each testable by someone who wasn't in the room. For M3, the pilot: 20,000 accounts migrated; account balances reconcile 100 % between MAVIS and the new platform; no more than 0.5 % of records left in the exception queue; two successful billing cycles on Halvorsen.

The process becomes clause 5: Wrenfield submits evidence against the criteria; MetroVolt has 10 working days to accept or reject; a rejection must name the criterion that failed; Wrenfield fixes and resubmits once, then it goes to steering. If MetroVolt says nothing in 10 days, the milestone is deemed accepted. Anjali agrees, on one condition: deemed acceptance doesn't apply over Christmas. Fair.

Elaine gets what she wanted from Orion's lesson: no milestone is a document. Even M2 (design approved) needs the design authority's signed minutes, not just a PDF.

  1. Write criteria per milestone

    Testable by someone who wasn't there.

  2. Agree the evidence

    Reports, reconciliations, signed minutes, test results.

  3. Set a deadline to decide

    Ten working days is common.

  4. Rejections name a criterion

    No criterion, no rejection.

  5. Add deemed acceptance

    Silence after the deadline is a yes; agree exceptions.

  6. Limit the loops

    Fix and resubmit once, then escalate.

Vocabulary

Acceptance criteria
The conditions a deliverable must meet to be accepted.
Acceptance test
Checking the deliverable against the criteria.
Deemed acceptance
Acceptance by default if the client doesn't decide in time.
Milestone
A defined, checkable result that triggers payment.
Exception queue
Records the migration couldn't move automatically, waiting for a person.
Reconciliation
Checking that totals match between the old system and the new one.

5What the contract says the acceptance clause, before and after

Lodestar SOW · draft v0.4 · redlined16 June 2027
agreed

5.2 A Milestone is accepted when completed to MetroVolt's reasonable satisfaction. A Milestone is accepted when the Supplier demonstrates that the Acceptance Criteria in Schedule 4 are met.

5.4 MetroVolt shall accept, or reject with reasons referring to the Acceptance Criteria, within 10 Working Days of receiving the evidence. If MetroVolt does neither, the Milestone is deemed accepted, except between 20 December and 3 January.

Anjali's Christmas exception. fair.

In plain words: acceptance should be a test, not an opinion. Name the criteria, set a deadline, require reasons for a rejection and decide what silence means, so neither side can wait the other out.

9Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • “To our satisfaction”

    Acceptance is an opinion. Fix → criteria in a schedule.

  • Document milestones

    Paid for a PDF; nothing works. Fix → working results only.

  • No deadline

    Acceptance waits for a holiday to end. Fix → ten working days, then deemed accepted.

  • Reasonless rejections

    “Not quite right.” Fix → rejections must name a criterion.

  • Endless loops

    Fix, reject, fix, reject. Fix → one resubmission, then escalate.

10When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Any milestone payment.
  • Fixed-price work.
  • A client burned by paying for documents.

Skip it when…

  • Pure time and materials with no milestones; still agree a “definition of done” for deliveries.

11Self-check quiz

Why is “to MetroVolt's reasonable satisfaction” a problem?

It's an opinion with no criteria or deadline; payment can stall indefinitely.

What is deemed acceptance?

A milestone counts as accepted if the client neither accepts nor rejects by the deadline.

Name two of the M3 acceptance criteria.

20,000 accounts migrated; balances reconcile 100 %; ≤ 0.5 % in the exception queue; two billing cycles on Halvorsen.

Why must a rejection name a criterion?

So rejections are about the agreed test, not new wishes.

Why did Elaine insist no milestone is a document?

Orion's supplier was paid for documents while nothing went live.

what would you do?Day 9 of the review: MetroVolt's tester says she “needs more time to look properly”. What do you do?

Offer to walk her through the evidence that day, and agree in writing a short extension with a new date, rather than relying on deemed acceptance and souring the relationship.

try it

Pick a deliverable you owe someone. Write four acceptance criteria a stranger could test, the evidence you'd give, and how long they have to decide. What happens if they don't?

remember!!

Acceptance is a test, not an opinion: criteria, evidence, a deadline, and silence means yes.

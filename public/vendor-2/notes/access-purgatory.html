Why Can't We Log In?

TL;DR

Laptops, accounts, badges and system access are the first dependency on almost every client project, and the first to slip. Treat them like any other dependency: a tracker with dates promised and delivered, a price per idle day, a calm relief notice under the contract, and visible work-arounds so nobody thinks you were idle. Do it without blame: the person chasing your laptop isn't the person who lost it.

1The situation The Grid, floor 19, 2 August 2027

Dependency D-05 says MetroVolt provides 12 laptops, accounts and VPN within 10 working days of the start date: by Friday 16 July. On day 23, Tuesday 27 July, the laptops arrive. VPN and contractor badges are promised for 4 August. Read access to MAVIS: no date.

  • Discovery is a 14-week plan. Eleven working days are gone. Nobody has written down why.
  • Graham asks from Leeds: “What have they been doing for three weeks?”
  • MetroVolt's IT service desk lost the first laptop request in a queue reorganised by Horizon. The person now chasing it, Yusuf in IT, is apologetic and overloaded.

2Both sides of the table the same moment, seen twice

MetroVolt Elaine and MetroVolt IT

Elaine is embarrassed: Wrenfield's first weeks are being wasted by her own IT. She's also wary: a late dependency is how Orion's vendor started its change requests. She wants it fixed and doesn't want it used as a weapon.

“Please don't make this into an Orion-style claim.”

Wrenfield Callum and Ines

Wrenfield is paying six people to wait, at a fixed price. The contract gives relief, day for day, for late dependencies, but only if Wrenfield claims it promptly and shows the facts. It also needs Graham to see that the team was working.

“Count it, claim it, and keep working where we can.”

A relief notice isn't an accusation. It's how both sides keep the plan honest when one of them is late.

3Real-life analogy the plumber who can't get into the flat

A plumber booked for Monday finds the building's door code doesn't work, and the caretaker is off until Thursday. A good plumber notes the time, texts the customer a photo of the locked door, moves the finish date by the days lost, and meanwhile measures up from outside and orders the parts. Nobody argues on Thursday.

4How it works track, price, claim, and work around

Track it. From day one Callum keeps an access tracker shared with Elaine: each request, the date promised, the date delivered, and who is chasing it at MetroVolt. It's updated every morning at 09:00 and takes four minutes.

Price it. Next to it, one line: six people waiting cost about £4,200 per working day. By 30 July that's about £46k. Wrenfield doesn't invoice it (it's a fixed price); the number is there so everyone understands why the dates must move.

Claim it. On 30 July, Callum sends relief notice RN-01 under clause 3.4: D-05 delivered 11 working days late; discovery end moves from 26 November to 10 December; no extra charge. It's one page, factual, with the tracker attached, and Ines walks Elaine through it the day before it arrives.

Work around, in public. Without systems, the team does 18 interviews, reads 40 MAVIS documents, runs three workshops on paper, and draws the 02:10 batch job by hand with Bert. All of it is in the Friday notes, so Graham's question has an answer.

  1. Start the tracker on day one

    Request, promised, delivered, who's chasing.

  2. Share it with the client lead

    Daily, without commentary.

  3. Put a price on idle days

    Not to invoice: to explain why dates move.

  4. Claim relief promptly

    A short notice under the right clause, after a conversation.

  5. Work around visibly

    List what you did without systems.

  6. Thank the people chasing it

    They're usually as frustrated as you are.

Vocabulary

Dependency
Something the client must provide, by a date, for you to deliver.
Relief
A contractual right to move dates (or costs) when the client is late.
Relief notice
A written claim for relief, with facts and dates.
Idle time
Paid time when the team can't do planned work.
Onboarding
Getting people accounts, devices and access.
Work-around
Useful work that doesn't need the missing thing.

5What the contract says the relief clause, used for the first time

Lodestar SOW · §3.4 and relief notice RN-0130 July 2027
relief claimed

3.4 Where a Dependency is late, the Supplier is entitled to relief day for day on affected Milestones, provided the Supplier notifies MetroVolt within 10 Working Days of becoming aware of the delay.

“within 10 working days”. if we'd waited for the August steering, we'd have lost it.

RN-01 D-05 was delivered 11 Working Days late. The Supplier claims relief of 11 Working Days on the Discovery end date (26 November → 10 December 2027). No change to the Charges is claimed.

£0. the point is the date, not the money.

In plain words: relief usually has a deadline for claiming it. Claim promptly, factually and without blame, and say clearly when you're not claiming money.

9Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Waiting quietly

    Then the lost weeks are yours. Fix → track from day one.

  • Claiming late

    The notice deadline passes. Fix → claim within the contract's window.

  • Claiming with blame

    The client hears Orion. Fix → facts, a conversation first, time not money.

  • Looking idle

    Your own firm assumes nothing happened. Fix → list the work-arounds every Friday.

  • Blaming the person chasing it

    You lose your only ally in IT. Fix → thank them, help them escalate.

10When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Any client site with accounts, devices or access to arrange.
  • Any dependency with a date.
  • Fixed-price work, especially.

Skip it when…

  • Never skip the tracker. Skip the formal notice if a written agreement on the dates is reached anyway.

11Self-check quiz

Why price idle days if you won't invoice them?

To explain why dates must move, in numbers everyone understands.

What did RN-01 claim?

Eleven working days' relief on the discovery end date, and no money.

Why does the relief clause's deadline matter?

Relief must be claimed within 10 working days of becoming aware of the delay, or it may be lost.

What did the team do without systems?

Interviews, document reading, paper workshops, and drawing the 02:10 job with Bert.

Why talk to Elaine before sending the notice?

So it arrives as agreed facts, not as a surprise claim.

what would you do?Graham wants RN-01 to claim the £46k as well. What do you advise?

Not on a fixed price, and not as the first notice of the relationship. Claim time, which protects the dates; record the cost in the tracker in case delays continue and a bigger claim is ever needed.

try it

List what you're waiting for from others on your current project. For each: when it was promised, when it arrived, and who's chasing it. What does a day of waiting cost? Who should know that number?

remember!!

Access is a dependency. Track it, price it, claim it calmly, and show the work you did anyway.

How Do We Earn the Programme Director's Trust?

TL;DR

Your most important relationship on a client project is with the client's programme director, and if they've been burned before, they'll start by expecting the worst. Trust is built in small, repeated proofs: a weekly hour on their agenda, a no-surprises pact, bad news early and small, with a fix, and small promises kept where they can see them. You can't speed it up; you can only start it on day one.

  • Family: two firms, one team
  • Lives in: Thursdays, 08:00, the café on floor 2
  • Pairs with: mapping the client, the first status report

1The situation The Grid, July and August 2027

Elaine Corbett was Orion's last programme manager. She watched four project leads come and go, signed off change requests for four assumptions, and spent forty minutes of her first hour with Ines on day one talking about it.

  • She reads every Wrenfield email twice, looking for the exit Wrenfield is building.
  • She copies Priya on everything, “just so it's on record”.
  • On 12 August Ines learns that MetroVolt has assigned Bert to Lodestar one day a week, not the three in dependency D-09. It's exactly the kind of thing Orion's supplier would have saved for a steering committee.

2Both sides of the table the same moment, seen twice

MetroVolt Elaine

For Elaine, every supplier is guilty until proven otherwise. She's not hostile; she's careful, because the last time she trusted a supplier's reports, she had to explain three years to the board. She'll trust what she sees repeatedly, not what she's told.

“Show me, every week, for months.”

Wrenfield Ines

Ines needs Elaine as a partner: someone who'll fix MetroVolt's side of problems, defend the plan in Horizon meetings and tell Ines what's coming. She can't ask for trust. She can only earn it in small amounts, often.

“Trust is a habit, not a speech.”

A wary counterpart isn't an obstacle. They're the person whose trust, once earned, protects the project more than any clause.

3Real-life analogy the new dog walker

A family hires a new dog walker after the last one lost the dog. They don't trust her on day one, and they're right not to. She texts a photo after every walk, says straight away when the dog cut its paw, and is there at 8:00 exactly, every day. After two months they give her a key. She never asked for it.

4How it works four habits, repeated

A weekly hour, her agenda first. From 15 July: Thursdays, 08:00, the café on floor 2. Elaine talks first. Ines brings one page: what happened, what's coming, what she needs. Ines has never cancelled one.

A no-surprises pact. At the second Thursday they agree: anything Elaine would want to know, she hears from Ines before she hears it anywhere else, including from Wrenfield's own reports. Elaine adds her side: she'll tell Ines about Horizon decisions before they're announced, when she can.

Bad news early, small, with a fix. On Thursday 12 August, Ines tells Elaine about D-09 over coffee: Bert is assigned one day a week; the plan assumes three; the data workstream will slip about two weeks by October unless it changes; here's a proposal (Bert three days through November, Lottie as his apprentice so the load falls later). No notice, no email to Priya.

Small promises, kept where she can see them. The access tracker, updated at 09:00, every day, for six weeks. It's trivial, and Elaine checks it every morning.

The test comes a week later: on 19 August Elaine tells Ines she has moved Bert to three days herself, by talking to his manager. She didn't copy Priya.

  1. Book a weekly hour, and keep it

    Their agenda first.

  2. Agree a no-surprises pact

    Both ways, out loud.

  3. Tell bad news early, small, with a fix

    In person before in writing.

  4. Keep small promises visibly

    Daily, boringly, without fail.

  5. Let them fix their side

    Give them the chance before escalating.

  6. Notice when trust arrives

    Stop copying their boss on everything, if they have.

Vocabulary

Counterpart
The person on the other side responsible for the same thing.
Trust equation
Trust grows with credibility, reliability and intimacy, and shrinks with self-interest.
No-surprises rule
Agreeing that important news is always told first, directly.
Early warning
Raising a likely problem while it's small and fixable.
Escalation
Taking an issue to someone more senior; a last resort between partners.
Relationship capital
Goodwill you build up and can spend when things go wrong.

5What the contract says the contract says who; habits say how

Lodestar SOW · §8.1 and Appendix C (D-09)signed 30 June 2027
fixed in a café

D-09 MetroVolt provides a named MAVIS expert for three days per week from the Start Date.

Bert, one day. fixed by Elaine, not by a notice.

Sch. 7, 4 Disputes … shall be referred to the Steering Committee.

the path exists. the point of Thursdays is to rarely use it.

In plain words: the contract gives you the right to claim when the client's side slips. A good relationship lets the client fix it before you ever need to. Use the right sparingly; build the relationship every week.

9Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Asking to be trusted

    Words don't move a burned client. Fix → small proofs, repeated.

  • Cancelling the weekly hour

    The habit breaks. Fix → never cancel; shorten if you must.

  • Bad news in writing first

    It reads like a claim. Fix → in person, then in writing.

  • Escalating before they can fix it

    They look weak to their boss. Fix → give them the chance first.

  • Surprising them through your own reports

    They read it in the status report first. Fix → the pact covers your reports too.

10When to use it (and when not)

11Self-check quiz

What are the four habits?

A weekly hour on their agenda, a no-surprises pact, bad news early with a fix, and small promises kept visibly.

Why tell bad news in person first?

In writing first it reads like a claim; in person it's a shared problem.

How was D-09 resolved?

Ines told Elaine early with a proposal; Elaine moved Bert to three days herself.

Why let the client fix their side first?

Escalating makes them look weak to their boss and costs trust.

What showed that trust had grown?

Elaine fixed D-09 herself and stopped copying Priya.

what would you do?Elaine asks Ines, off the record, whether Vexmoor's assurance notes are “fair”. What do you say?

Stay factual and generous: point to the decision log and the evidence behind each decision, and offer to answer any specific point. Don't criticise Vexmoor; it would break the trust you're building with her too.

try it

Name your most important counterpart. Write down one weekly habit, one pact, one piece of bad news you should share early, and one small promise you could keep visibly every day.

remember!!

Trust isn't asked for. It's small promises kept, often, and bad news told early.

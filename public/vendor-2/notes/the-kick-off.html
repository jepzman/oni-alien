How Do We Set the Rules at Kick-off?

TL;DR

A kick-off isn't a launch party. It's the one meeting where everyone is in the room, so use it to agree the rules you'll need later: why the project exists (in the client's words), the plan and its gates, the assumptions (with owners standing up), how we work, and how we disagree. When someone senior eats the time, cut the agenda, not the rules.

1The situation The Grid, floor 2 auditorium, 13 July 2027

The Lodestar kick-off is on Tuesday 13 July, 10:00 to 11:30, in the floor 2 auditorium: 34 people from MetroVolt, Wrenfield and Halvorsen. Elaine chairs. At 10:02 Lord Varnish arrives, unannounced, and asks to “set the vision”.

  • His slide is the “Lodestar Value Pyramid”: five layers (people, data, trust, value, synergy), no dates, no names.
  • He speaks for twenty minutes. Wrenfield's agenda had ninety minutes of content for ninety minutes.
  • In the back row, Bert Ogundipe folds his arms. He's sat through the Orion kick-off too. It also had a pyramid.

2Both sides of the table the same moment, seen twice

MetroVolt the 34 in the room

Most of MetroVolt's people want to know three things: what changes for me, when, and whether this one will be different. Senior people want to be seen to own it. Old hands like Bert are deciding, in the first hour, whether to invest in it.

“Is this real, or another pyramid?”

Wrenfield Ines

For Wrenfield, the kick-off is the only time it can say the rules to everyone at once: the assumptions, the change pool, how to raise a disagreement. If those don't land today, they'll be news in month five, and sound like excuses.

“Cut slides. Keep the rules.”

A senior person opening a kick-off is a gift, even with a pyramid: it shows the room the project matters. The job is to make sure the rules still fit in the time that's left.

3Real-life analogy the climbers at the foot of the mountain

A climbing team agrees the important things at the bottom: who leads each section, the signals for stop and go, what happens if someone is hurt, the turn-back time. On the mountain, in the wind, there's no time to discuss them. A team that skips this to start climbing sooner usually regrets it halfway up.

4How it works cut the agenda, not the rules

At 10:22, with 68 minutes left, Ines passes Callum a note: “Cut slides 3–6. Keep 7–12.” What's kept:

  • Why (10 min): Priya, in Customer Operations' words: two of every ten complaint calls are about wrong addresses; MAVIS can't fix them; Lodestar can.
  • The plan (10 min): discovery to December, pilot in March, wave 1 in June, and the pilot gate: nothing goes further until 20,000 customers are live and reconciled.
  • The top eight assumptions (15 min): each read aloud, and the owner stands up. Hollis stands for D-03 (with Sven). Yusuf stands for D-05, a little embarrassed. The room laughs, kindly.
  • How we work (10 min): the Friday note, the access tracker, the change pool and who can instruct Wrenfield.
  • How we disagree (10 min): three steps. Say it in the room; write it in the decision log; escalate within a week. Never in the corridor, never by email to someone's boss.

Questions: Bert asks the first one. “When you find something in MAVIS you don't understand, who do you ask?” Ines: “You. And Lottie will write down the answer so nobody has to ask you twice.” Bert unfolds his arms.

  1. Plan the rules, not the slides

    Purpose, plan, assumptions, ways of working, disagreement.

  2. Let the client say why

    In their words, with their numbers.

  3. Make assumption owners stand up

    A name in a room beats a line in an appendix.

  4. Say how you'll disagree

    Three steps, written down.

  5. When time is eaten, cut slides

    Have the cut list ready before you start.

  6. Leave room for the hard question

    And answer it plainly.

Vocabulary

Kick-off
The first meeting of everyone involved, to agree purpose and rules.
Pilot gate
A point where the programme stops unless the pilot meets its criteria.
Decision log
A dated record of decisions, who made them and why.
Escalation path
The agreed route for a disagreement nobody can settle locally.
Ways of working
The habits and tools a team agrees to use.
Cut list
Slides you'll drop if you run out of time, decided in advance.

13 July is day 7 working. in time.

Sch. 7, 4 Disputes that cannot be resolved by the Programme Director and the Supplier's Engagement Manager within 5 Working Days shall be referred to the Steering Committee.

“escalate within a week”, in plain words.

In plain words: many contracts require a kick-off and say what must be presented. Use the minutes: once the room has heard the assumptions and the escalation path, nobody can later say they didn't know.

9Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • A launch party

    Vision and no rules. Fix → purpose, plan, assumptions, ways of working, disagreement.

  • No cut list

    A senior person over-runs and the rules are dropped. Fix → decide in advance what to cut.

  • The vendor explains the why

    It sounds like a sales pitch. Fix → the client says why, in their words.

  • Assumptions in an appendix

    Nobody remembers them. Fix → read aloud, owners stand.

  • No disagreement rules

    Disagreements go to corridors and bosses. Fix → three steps, minuted.

10When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • The start of any programme with several teams or firms.
  • When people have been burned by a previous kick-off.
  • When the contract requires one.

Skip it when…

  • A two-person engagement: a kick-off conversation and an email of the rules will do.

11Self-check quiz

What five things should a kick-off agree?

Why, the plan, the assumptions, how we work and how we disagree.

Why should the client explain the “why”?

From the vendor it sounds like a pitch; from the client it's a purpose.

Why ask assumption owners to stand up?

A name in a room is remembered; a line in an appendix isn't.

What are the three steps for disagreeing?

Say it in the room, write it in the decision log, escalate within a week.

What did Ines cut when time ran short?

Wrenfield's own slides about itself (3–6); the rules stayed.

what would you do?Lord Varnish asks at the end: “Where does the Value Pyramid fit in your plan?” What do you say?

Thank him, and connect it to something concrete: the pyramid's “data” and “trust” layers are what the pilot gate tests in March. Offer to show him the pilot results first.

try it

Plan a kick-off for your next project in five parts: why, plan, assumptions, how we work, how we disagree. Which slides would you cut if a senior person took twenty minutes? Write the cut list now.

remember!!

A kick-off is where everyone hears the rules once. When time runs out, cut the slides, not the rules.

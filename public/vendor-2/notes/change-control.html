How Do We Change the Deal Without a War?

TL;DR

Every project changes. The question is whether changes are raised early and priced calmly, or argued over late. Make change control fast for small things (a pre-approved change pool), fair for big ones (an impact assessment, then steering), and always written down (a change register). A process too slow for an £8k change teaches everyone to stop asking and start assuming.

1The situation The Grid, floor 19, August 2027

On Tuesday 3 August, MetroVolt's accessibility team asks for its accessibility checker to run in Wrenfield's build pipeline. It's a good idea and a small job, about £8k. It isn't in Schedule 2.

  • The first draft of clause 7 sent every change, however small, to the monthly steering committee, with a 14-page form.
  • The next steering meeting is six weeks away. Hana's instinct is to “just do it”. So is MetroVolt's.
  • Ines has seen where “just do it” goes: forty small favours, nothing written down, and an argument in month ten about why the project is late.

2Both sides of the table the same moment, seen twice

MetroVolt Elaine

For MetroVolt, a change request can feel like the vendor sending a bill for a favour. After Orion, “change request” is a dirty word. Elaine wants small sensible things done without a fight, and big things brought to her early.

“Please don't send me an invoice every time we ask a question.”

Wrenfield Ines and Callum

For Wrenfield, every unrecorded favour is fixed-price time given away, and invisible when the plan slips. But a heavy process makes Wrenfield look petty. It needs changes to be cheap to raise, quick to decide and always written down.

“Small and fast, but never invisible.”

Change control isn't about saying no. It's about making every yes visible, so nobody is surprised by the total.

3Real-life analogy the builder's variation sheet

A good builder keeps a variation sheet taped to the kitchen wall. “Extra socket, £80” gets a line and a signature on the spot. “Move the staircase” gets a proper quote and a conversation. At the end, nobody is shocked by the bill, because every change was written down when it happened.

4How it works a pool for small changes, steering for big ones, a register for all

Back in June, Ines and Anjali rewrote clause 7 around three ideas. Anyone can raise a change on one page. Wrenfield returns an impact assessment within 10 working days: cost (from the rate card), time, risk, and what else it affects. Then the route depends on size: under £25k, Elaine can approve it from a £150k change pool, within a week; £25k and over goes to steering.

Every change, including the free ones, goes in the change register with its cost and days. Zero-cost changes are still recorded: they consume time, and they show goodwill.

CR-001 is the accessibility checker: raised 4 August, assessed 6 August (£8,000, 2 days of Hana, no impact on dates), approved by Elaine on 12 August, done the next week. Hana grumbles about the form. The form is one page.

  1. One page to raise a change

    What, why, who asked.

  2. An impact assessment in 10 days

    Cost from the rate card, time, risk, knock-on effects.

  3. A pool for small changes

    A named approver and a budget.

  4. Steering for big ones

    With the assessment, not a debate.

  5. A register for everything

    Including free changes: they're goodwill, made visible.

  6. Show the running total

    At every steering, so nobody is surprised.

Vocabulary

Change request (CR)
A formal request to change scope, price, dates or approach.
Impact assessment
What a change would cost, take and risk.
Change pool
A pre-approved budget for small changes.
Change register
The list of all changes and their status.
Scope creep
Unrecorded growth of the work.
Early warning notice
Telling the other side as soon as you see something that may change cost or time.

5What the contract says the change clause, as signed

Lodestar SOW · §7 change controlsigned 30 June 2027
signed

7.2 Either Party may raise a Change Request. The Supplier shall provide an impact assessment within 10 Working Days, priced using the Rates in Schedule 5.

7.4 Changes with a value below £25,000 may be approved by MetroVolt's Programme Director, up to a total of £150,000 (the Change Pool). Other Changes require approval by the Steering Committee.

the pool. the best clause we negotiated.

7.6 No Change is binding unless recorded in the Change Register and approved under this clause.

so “just do it” doesn't count. for either side.

In plain words: a good change clause is quick for small things and fair for big ones, and says that only recorded changes count. That protects the client from surprise bills and the vendor from invisible favours.

9Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Everything to steering

    Small changes wait six weeks, so people stop asking. Fix → a change pool with a named approver.

  • “Just do it”

    Invisible favours; a surprise in month ten. Fix → record everything, even free changes.

  • Heavy forms

    Nobody fills them in. Fix → one page.

  • Late assessments

    The client feels stalled. Fix → ten working days, kept.

  • Surprise totals

    Each CR fine, the sum shocking. Fix → show the running total at every steering.

10When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Any contract with a defined scope.
  • Fixed price, especially.
  • From the first change, to set the habit.

Skip it when…

  • Pure time and materials, where the client directs the work daily: still keep a light register.

11Self-check quiz

What is the change pool for?

A pre-approved budget so small changes can be decided quickly by a named person.

Why record free changes?

They use time, show goodwill, and explain the plan later.

What does clause 7.6 say, and why does it help both sides?

Only recorded, approved changes count; no surprise bills, no invisible favours.

What's in an impact assessment?

Cost, time, risk and knock-on effects.

Why make the first change quick and friendly?

It sets the habit; people will use a process that works.

what would you do?A MetroVolt manager asks Lottie directly to “quickly add a field”. Lottie does it. What now?

Record it (as a change note, at no cost if small), thank the manager, and remind both of them gently that requests go through the register, so the work shows up in the plan.

try it

List the last five things you were asked to do that weren't in the original plan. Were they written down? What did they cost in time? Design a one-page change form you'd actually use.

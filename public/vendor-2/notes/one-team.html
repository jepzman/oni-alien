How Do Two Companies Work as One Team?

TL;DR

A programme fails when the vendor's team and the client's team work side by side but apart. Mix them into squads around the work, share one board, one chat and one stand-up, and sit together. But stay honest about the contract: who's paid by whom, who can instruct whom, and what each firm owns. One team in how you work; two firms in what you owe.

1The situation The Grid, floor 19, 9 August 2027

Five weeks in, floor 19 has two Lodestar teams. Wrenfield's six work in room 19C with their own task board and their own chat. MetroVolt's people (Bert, two business analysts, a tester and an engineer) sit at desks outside the glass and use MetroVolt's Jira and Teams.

  • Questions cross the glass by email. Answers take a day.
  • Lunch is at different times. Someone has stuck a note on the glass: “us / them”. Nobody admits to it.
  • MetroVolt's analysts have heard Horizon will cut 600 roles. Some of them think Wrenfield is here to replace them.

2Both sides of the table the same moment, seen twice

MetroVolt MetroVolt's Lodestar staff

MetroVolt's people know the system and the customers. They also know that suppliers come and go and they stay, and that in a Horizon year, the people who “helped the supplier replace the old system” may be next. Being asked their opinion matters more than any tool.

“Are we your team, or your users?”

Wrenfield Ines and Hana

Wrenfield needs MetroVolt's knowledge daily, not by email. It also needs to stay clear about its contract: Wrenfield is accountable for the fixed scope, can't be directed by anyone except Elaine, and can't quietly take on MetroVolt's work.

“Sit together. Owe separately.”

Blending teams is easy to say and hard to do because two employers, two pay scales and two futures don't disappear when you share a board.

3Real-life analogy the hospital and the agency nurses

On a busy ward, agency nurses and staff nurses work one shift, one handover, one patient board. Nobody treats a patient differently because of who pays their nurse. But everyone knows that the ward sister decides, and the agency nurse's contract says what she can and can't do. One team on the ward; two employers on paper.

4How it works mixed squads, shared tools, honest lines

On Monday 9 August, Ines and Elaine run a one-hour session with everyone on the programme and write a team agreement together.

  • Squads around the work, each mixed: data (Bilal, Bert, a MetroVolt analyst), platform (Hana and a MetroVolt engineer), testing (Oskar and MetroVolt's tester).
  • One of everything: MetroVolt's Jira (Wrenfield drops its own board), one Teams channel, one stand-up at 09:30. Wrenfield moves out of 19C to mixed desks.
  • Honest lines, said out loud: Wrenfield owns delivery of the fixed scope; MetroVolt owns decisions, its data and running Lodestar afterwards; only Elaine can instruct Wrenfield; MetroVolt's people are learning the new platform as it's built, because they'll run it.

The last line answers the unspoken fear: MetroVolt's people aren't being replaced by Wrenfield; they're the ones Wrenfield is building it for, and handing it to. Bert says it better: “So the kid learns my COBOL, and I learn her platform.” Lottie and Bert have been doing that since July.

  1. Build squads around the work

    Each with people from both firms.

  2. One board, one chat, one stand-up

    The client's tools, usually.

  3. Sit together

    Leave the glass room.

  4. Say who owns what, out loud

    Scope, decisions, data, running it later.

  5. Address the unspoken fear

    Whose jobs, whose future.

  6. Write it down together

    A one-page team agreement, signed by both leads.

Vocabulary

Blended team
A team with people from client and vendor working as one.
Squad
A small cross-functional team around one area of work.
Team agreement
A short, shared set of rules for how a team works.
Knowledge transfer
Moving know-how from one group to another, ideally all along, not at the end.
Instruction
A contractual direction; only named people can give it.
Disguised employment
Treating a supplier's staff like your own employees; a legal and contractual risk.

5What the contract says where “one team” stops

Lodestar SOW · §8.5 and §11 (exit)signed 30 June 2027
two firms

8.5 Instructions to the Supplier are valid only if given by MetroVolt's Programme Director or the Steering Committee.

so a MetroVolt squad member can ask Hana; only Elaine can instruct her.

11.2 The Supplier shall transfer knowledge to MetroVolt staff throughout the Services, so that MetroVolt can operate the solution at the end of Wave 1.

mixed squads aren't a nicety. they're §11.2.

In plain words: the contract usually supports blended teams (knowledge transfer) and limits them (who can instruct). Say both out loud, so a friendly team doesn't drift into unpriced work or blurred responsibilities.

  • Make knowledge transfer visible.
  • Never let your people be instructed around the contract.

9Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Side by side but apart

    Two boards, two chats, two lunches. Fix → one of everything.

  • Pretending there's one employer

    Blurred instructions, unpriced work. Fix → honest lines about the contract.

  • Ignoring the job fear

    Quiet resistance. Fix → say who'll run it afterwards, and mean it.

  • Vendor tools by default

    The client can't see the work. Fix → use the client's tools.

  • Knowledge transfer at the end

    A handover nobody can absorb. Fix → pairs from the start.

☠
Seen in the GrimoireThe Cult of Family: is the opposite mistake: pretending everyone is family, and forgetting the contract.

10When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Vendor and client staff work on the same system.
  • The client will run what you build.
  • A cut or reorg makes staff anxious.

Skip it when…

  • A fully outsourced job with no client staff involved: still share one board with the client lead.

11Self-check quiz

What does “one team in how we work, two firms in what we owe” mean?

Share squads, tools and rituals, but be clear about scope, instructions and responsibilities.

Why use the client's tools?

The client can see the work, and will own it afterwards.

Who can instruct Wrenfield?

Only Elaine or the steering committee; anyone can ask.

What fear did the honest lines address?

That Wrenfield was there to replace MetroVolt's staff.

Why pair Bert with Lottie?

MAVIS knowledge moves to Wrenfield and platform knowledge to MetroVolt, all along.

what would you do?A MetroVolt analyst in the data squad asks Bilal to also fix a report for her other team. What should Bilal do?

Say he'll help her raise it with Elaine; it's outside the fixed scope and outside what anyone but Elaine can instruct. Offer to show her how, if it's quick, and record it.

try it

Look at a team you share with another firm or department. Count the boards, chats and stand-ups. Write a one-page agreement: squads, tools, seating, and three honest lines about who owns what.

remember!!

Mix the squads, share one board, one chat, one stand-up, and say out loud who owes what.

What Do We Do on Day One?

TL;DR

On day one you're a visitor in someone else's house, and everyone is watching how you behave. Arrive with a first-week plan: meet the client's programme director, meet the people who'll judge your work, spend time listening to the business, start tracking what you're waiting for, and end the week with a three-line note. Learn the building before you try to change anything.

1The situation The Grid, Monday 5 July 2027

The contract was signed on Wednesday 30 June. On Monday 5 July at 08:30, six people from Wrenfield arrive at The Grid's reception: Ines, Callum, Hana, Bilal, Oskar and Lottie. They get visitor passes. Nobody has booked desks.

  • Facilities finds them room 19C, a glass meeting room everyone calls “the goldfish bowl”: eight chairs, no screens, visible from the whole floor.
  • They need an escort to reach the toilets. Laptops “should be ready this week”.
  • Lottie asks what they're supposed to do today. Hana suggests starting the design. Ines says no.

2Both sides of the table the same moment, seen twice

MetroVolt floor 19 watches

Thirty MetroVolt people on floor 19 have heard “Lodestar” for months and “Orion” for years. They're watching six strangers in a glass room. Are these the people who'll replace their system, or their jobs? Do they say good morning?

“Here we go again.”

Wrenfield Ines and the team

Wrenfield has no laptops, no access and no desks. It does have five days of first impressions that can't be repeated, and people it can meet without any system at all.

“No laptops. Perfect week for listening.”

The first week is the only time nobody expects you to know anything. Use it to ask.

3Real-life analogy the new neighbour

When you move into a new street, you don't start by redesigning your neighbour's garden. You learn where the bins go and on which day, say hello to the person who's lived there forty years, and find out who to ask when the water goes off. In a month, people listen to your ideas because they've met you.

4How it works a first-week plan: listen, meet the judges, write three lines

Ines arrived with a first-week plan on one page. Monday: inductions, an hour with Elaine, a walk around floors 19, 14 and 2 (where the customer service teams sit). Tuesday: meet the people who'll judge Wrenfield's work: Hollis (architecture), Bert Ogundipe (31 years on MAVIS), Kofi (metering), MetroVolt's test manager. Each meeting has one question: “What would make Lodestar a success for you, and what are you worried about?”

Wednesday: listening. Bilal and Lottie sit with Customer Operations (Pip's analysts take them) and listen to three customer calls, two of which are about wrong addresses. Thursday: the access tracker (next note) and a rehearsal for the kick-off. Friday: the first Friday note to Elaine, three lines: done / learned / need.

And one rule on the whiteboard in 19C: learn the building before trying to change anything. Hana's design stays in her notebook until she's met Bert.

  1. Write the first-week plan before you arrive

    One page, day by day.

  2. Meet the client lead on day one

    An hour, their agenda.

  3. Meet the people who'll judge your work

    One question: success and worries.

  4. Listen to the business

    Sit with the users of the system you're replacing.

  5. Track what you're waiting for

    Access, desks, passes: from day one.

  6. End the week with three lines

    Done, learned, need.

5What the contract says what the contract says about arriving

Lodestar SOW · §4 and Appendix Csigned 30 June 2027
day 1

4.1 The Services commence on 5 July 2027 (the Start Date). Milestone dates are measured from the Start Date.

dates run from today, laptops or not.

D-05 MetroVolt provides 12 laptops, network accounts and VPN access within 10 Working Days of the Start Date.

Friday 16 July. starting the tracker today.

In plain words: the clock starts on the start date, whether or not you can work. Day one is when you start recording what you're waiting for.

6Account health the same move, green, amber and red

Green

you've worked there before

  • Re-meet people; say what's new.
  • Shorter first week.
  • Still listen first.

Amber Lodestar now

new to the client

  • A day-by-day first week.
  • Meet the judges, one question each.
  • Three lines on Friday.

Red

hostile floor

  • Over-invest in listening.
  • Be visibly polite and visibly useful.
  • No design until people have been heard.

9Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Designing on day one

    A solution before anyone's been heard. Fix → listen first; meet the judges.

  • Waiting for laptops

    Five days lost. Fix → people don't need laptops.

  • Only meeting the sponsor

    The judges are the people below. Fix → meet Hollis, Bert, Kofi, the tester.

  • Behaving like visitors

    Huddled in the glass room. Fix → walk the floors; say good morning.

  • Silence on Friday

    The client doesn't know what happened all week. Fix → three lines.

10When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • The first week on any client site.
  • A new team joining an existing programme.
  • When access is late (it usually is).

Skip it when…

  • Never skip it; shorten it if you know the client well.

11Self-check quiz

What's the one question for each “judge”?

What would make Lodestar a success for you, and what worries you?

Why listen to customer calls in week one?

To learn the business problem the system serves; two of three were about wrong addresses.

What are the three lines of a Friday note?

Done, learned, need.

Why no design until the team had met Bert?

He knows MAVIS best; designing before hearing him risks the wrong design and an offended expert.

When do milestone dates start counting?

From the start date, whether or not the team can work.

what would you do?By Wednesday the team is restless and Hana starts sketching the target architecture on the glass wall of 19C. What do you do?

try it

Plan your first week on a new project or team, day by day. Who will judge your work? What one question would you ask each of them? What would your three-line Friday note say?

remember!!

On day one you're a guest. Listen first, meet the judges, and say what you learned on Friday.

Who Decides What, and Where?

TL;DR

Governance answers one question: where does each kind of decision get made? Give every decision one home: a steering committee for scope, money and milestones; a design authority for architecture; working groups for detail; the two leads for day-to-day. Write the decisions in one log, and agree an escalation path with deadlines. A big room that decides everything decides nothing well.

1The situation The Grid, room 27A, 28 July 2027

The first steering committee meets on Wednesday 28 July in room 27A. Eleven people: Priya (chair), Elaine, Hollis, Nkem, Rupert's delegate, Graham, Ines, Callum, two people from Priya's team, and Oliver Thane of Vexmoor, attending as “Horizon assurance” under the board's condition.

  • The first 40 minutes are spent on which people Wrenfield should interview in discovery. Two people could have decided it in ten minutes.
  • The decision that matters (approving relief notice RN-01) gets eight minutes at the end.
  • Oliver doesn't speak. He writes four pages of notes.

2Both sides of the table the same moment, seen twice

MetroVolt Priya chairs

Priya wants to be seen to be in control in a Horizon year, so everything comes to her steering. But she has 90 minutes a month, and she can't judge interview lists. She needs the right decisions brought to her, prepared, and the rest kept away.

“Bring me the decisions only I can make.”

Wrenfield Ines and Callum

Wrenfield needs decisions fast and in the right place: architecture with people who understand it, money with people who can approve it. And with Vexmoor in the room, it needs every decision written down, with reasons.

“One home per decision. One log for all of them.”

Governance isn't bureaucracy when it's the right size. It's how a sponsor stays in control without being in every meeting.

3Real-life analogy a hospital's decisions

A hospital doesn't send every decision to the board. The board decides budgets and strategy. A clinical committee decides treatments. The ward sister decides who's on shift. Each has a rule for when to pass a problem up, and a timeline. If the board discussed shift rotas, nothing important would ever be decided.

4How it works one home per decision, one log, one escalation path

After the first steering, Ines and Elaine write a one-page decision map, which Priya approves in five minutes:

  • Steering (monthly, Priya): scope, money of £25k or more, milestone acceptance, top risks, relief and changes to the baseline. Vexmoor attends; no vote.
  • Design authority (every other Wednesday, Hollis): architecture, data model, standards. Kofi attends for metering.
  • Elaine and Callum (weekly): day-to-day, the change pool, interview lists, access.
  • Working groups (weekly): data (with Bert), billing (with Sven), testing (with Oskar).

Everything decided anywhere goes in one decision log: number, date, decision, who, why, and what was rejected. DL-001 (28 July): relief RN-01 approved. With Oliver in the room, the log is Wrenfield's best friend: every decision has a reason someone signed.

The escalation path has deadlines: a working group that can't agree passes it to Elaine and Callum, who have five working days; then it goes to the next steering. Nothing waits more than a month.

  1. List the kinds of decision

    Scope, money, architecture, day-to-day, detail.

  2. Give each one home

    A forum with a chair and a frequency.

  3. Keep the steering small and senior

    Only decisions only they can make.

  4. One decision log for everything

    Number, date, decision, who, why, rejected options.

  5. Escalation with deadlines

    Five days, then steering.

  6. Prepare every steering decision

    A one-page paper, sent two days before.

Vocabulary

Steering committee
The senior group that owns scope, money and milestones.
Design authority
The group that approves architecture and standards.
Working group
A small team that works out detail and recommends.
Decision log
The dated record of all decisions and their reasons.
Assurance
An independent check on how the programme is run, for the sponsor.
Terms of reference
A forum's purpose, members, decisions and frequency.

5What the contract says the governance schedule

amended

2.1 The Steering Committee meets monthly, chaired by the Sponsor, and decides matters reserved to it in the Decision Map (Annex A).

Annex A is the one-page map. added 2 Aug.

2.6 A representative of Horizon Assurance may attend the Steering Committee and receive its papers, without a vote.

Vexmoor, written in, with no vote. read every word of every paper as if they will.

3.2 All decisions are recorded in the Decision Log, which is a Contract document.

the log is contractual. good.

In plain words: governance belongs in the contract: who decides what, and that the decision log counts. If a third party attends, write down its role and that it doesn't vote.

9Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Everything to steering

    Senior time spent on detail. Fix → a decision map.

  • Too many forums

    Six committees, nobody knows which. Fix → four homes, one page.

  • Decisions without reasons

    Re-argued every month. Fix → log why, and what was rejected.

  • No escalation deadline

    Disagreements wait forever. Fix → five days, then steering.

  • Unprepared papers

    Discussion instead of decision. Fix → options and a recommendation, sent two days before.

10When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Any programme with a sponsor and more than one team.
  • When a steering committee is drowning in detail.
  • When an outside party watches the programme.

Skip it when…

  • A small project with one decision maker: a log is still worth it.

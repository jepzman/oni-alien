Signing and Starting

The statement of work, acceptance criteria, change control, a stalled signature, working at risk, day one on site, access, the client's real power map, the kick-off, governance, a multi-vendor RACI, the client's programme director, one team, the first status report and the baseline.

The paper what we signed, how it changes, and what to do while it isn't signed

  1. 01What Does the Contract Actually Say?Read the statement of work clause by clause, with the delivery team, and fix the five clauses that decide every future argument.p. 01
  2. 02How Will We Know It's Done?Acceptance criteria both sides can test: what is checked, by whom, how long they have, and what happens if they say nothing.p. 02
  3. 03How Do We Change the Deal Without a War?A change process that's quick for small things and fair for big ones, so changes are raised early instead of fought over late.p. 03
  4. 04What If the Signature Stalls?When a reorg freezes your contract, find the new decision path, give the new people what they need, and keep the team together.p. 04
  5. 05Should We Start Before It's Signed?Work at risk only with a written letter of intent, a cap, a scope and a date you stop; otherwise you're lending money to a client.p. 05

Arriving day one, access, the real power map, the kick-off and who decides

  1. 06What Do We Do on Day One?Arrive with a first-week plan, meet the people who'll judge you, and learn the building before you try to change anything.p. 06
  2. 07Why Can't We Log In?Treat laptops, accounts and badges as a dependency with dates, track the cost of every idle day, and work around it in public.p. 07
  3. 08Who Really Decides Here?Draw the client's real power map from outside: who decides, who blocks, who's listened to, and what each of them needs.p. 08
  4. 09How Do We Set the Rules at Kick-off?Use the kick-off to agree how you'll work, decide and disagree, and walk through the assumptions in front of everyone.p. 09
  5. 10Who Decides What, and Where?One steering group, one design authority, working groups and a decision log, with clear rights and an escalation path.p. 10

Two firms, one team other vendors, the client's people, honest reporting and the baseline

  1. 11Who Does What When Three Firms Share a Project?A responsibility chart for every shared interface, written with the other vendors, so a failure has an owner and not a blame war.p. 11
  2. 12How Do We Earn the Programme Director's Trust?No surprises, early bad news, kept small promises and a weekly hour together turn a wary client into a working partner.p. 12
  3. 13How Do Two Companies Work as One Team?Mixed teams, shared tools and one set of rituals, while staying honest about who is paid by whom.p. 13
  4. 14What Goes in the First Status Report?The first report sets the tone: honest colours, facts before adjectives, and the bad news in the first line.p. 14
  5. 15What Are We Measured Against?Agree the baseline in writing: scope, plan, price, risks and assumptions, signed, so every later change is measured against the same thing.p. 15

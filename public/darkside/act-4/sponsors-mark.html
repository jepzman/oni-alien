The Sponsor's Mark

Mentors give you a map. Sponsors open a door, in a room you're not in.

Threat

IA sighting

2027 · three mentors

Pip had mentors: the Chronicler, the Annotator, Jules. They gave him excellent advice. None of them was in the rooms where decisions about him were made.

Tuesday 14 December · calibration

In room 27A, Priya read four lines from Pip's Book of Deeds and said, “He should be considered for the Head role. I've seen him run the planning review. I'll vouch for him.” Pip was at his desk, three floors down, building a model.

February · the shortlist

When the panel shortlisted for Head of Customer Insight, Ottoline asked who would vouch for the internal candidates. Priya spoke for Pip. Nobody spoke for Dorian. Dorian had several mentors.

May · the new track

When Pip proposed a Principal Analyst track (see the Peter Principle), it was Priya who took it to Ottoline, and Ottoline who took it to Dame Helena. Pip never met Dame Helena. His idea did.

IIOrigin

flashbackHow Priya became a sponsor

  1. 2014 · Priya's first big role

    Priya, then a team lead, is promoted to manager because a director she'd never met said her name in a talent review. She finds out a year later.

  2. 2014 · the debt

    She asks the director why. “You fixed my call-centre numbers in 2013. I remember people who make my life easier.”

  3. 2015 to 2026 · paying it forward

    Priya decides to do the same: watch for people who deliver, then spend her reputation on them, in rooms they're not in.

  4. 2027 · Pip

    Pip gave her the Horizon submission that kept her team intact and a page that made calibration easy. She remembers people who make her life easier.

The turning point. Being sponsored once by a stranger, and deciding to become that stranger for others.

What might have saved them. Nothing needed saving. Sponsorship is a chain; Priya just kept it going.

— the AnnotatorMost sponsors were sponsored once. Ask yours who did it for them. It's a lovely conversation.

IIIKnow thy enemy

Mentors advise you. Sponsors act for you: they spend their reputation to put your name forward for opportunities, often in rooms you'll never enter. Many talented people have mentors and no sponsor, and wonder why they don't move.

What sponsors want

People who make them look good: reliable, visible results, and no embarrassments after they've vouched.

Why sponsorship matters

Because the decisive moments (calibration, shortlists, talent reviews) happen without you. Someone has to be there for you.

How to earn one

  • Deliver something important for them.
  • Be reliable and visible.
  • Ask, specifically.
  • Give them sentences to say.

Three kinds of supporter

The Mentor

Gives advice. Valuable. Not enough.

The Advocate

Speaks well of you when asked. Helpful.

The Sponsor

Puts your name forward unasked, and stakes their reputation. Rare.

— the AnnotatorMentors give you a map. Sponsors open a door. You need both, but only one gets you through.

IVSigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • You have mentors but nobody senior who'd put their name to you.
  • Opportunities go to people “someone vouched for”.
  • Your name never comes up in rooms you're not in.
  • You've never asked anyone to sponsor you.

VCounter-spells

  1. 1The Earned Mark

    Deliver something important for a senior person, reliably and visibly. Sponsorship follows trust.

  2. 2The Ask

    Ask a senior person who knows your work to advocate for a specific opportunity.

    incantation“I'd like to be considered for the Head role. Would you be willing to speak for me if my name comes up?”

  3. 3The Ammunition

    Make sponsorship easy: give your sponsor three sentences they can say about you.

    incantation“If it helps: the complaint model, the Horizon submission, and onboarding four people this year.”

VIForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Collector

    Asking everyone senior to sponsor you. Sponsorship is spent carefully; scattered asks look careless.

  • The Embarrassed Sponsor

    Letting your sponsor down after they vouched for you. They won't do it twice.

  • The Silent Hope

    Hoping someone will notice and speak up. Some will. Most need to be asked.

VIIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

You have a natural sponsor in your manager.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Your manager speaks up for you.
  • They know your work.
  • They're respected.

Do

  1. Give them ammunition (three sentences).
  2. Tell them what you want next.
  3. Never surprise them.

Don’t: The silent hope.

If it gets worse: If nobody senior would vouch for you, you're at Nightmare.

Nightmare

Mentors, but no sponsor.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • You get good advice, but no doors open.
  • Your manager is weak or absent.
  • Senior people don't know your name.

Do

  1. Deliver something important for a senior person.
  2. Then ask them, specifically, for one opportunity.
  3. Give them the Ammunition.
  4. Keep your mentors too.

Don’t: The collector: asking everyone.

If it gets worse: If no senior person will engage with you at all, you're at Hell.

Hell

No access to anyone senior.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Your manager blocks contact upwards.
  • Opportunities are decided in a closed circle.
  • Nobody senior knows you exist.

Do

  1. Find cross-team projects with senior visibility.
  2. Build a skip-level relationship openly (Act III).
  3. Look for sponsors outside your line.
  4. Consider where you'd be seen.

Don’t: The embarrassed sponsor: overpromising to someone who vouches for you.

If it gets worse: If you can't be seen here, go where you can.

VIIIWho says your name?

Tick honestly.

  • You couldn't name one senior person who'd put you forward unasked.
  • You've never asked anyone to sponsor you.
  • You think mentors and sponsors are the same thing.
  • Your name never comes up in rooms you're not in.
  • You've never sponsored anyone yourself.

The cure, if you ticked two: Name one senior person whose work you could make easier this quarter. Do it. Then, in a few months, ask them for one thing.

IXThe rehearsal

The Ask, and the Ammunition.

Scene: Monday 6 December, Pip asks Priya directly.

PipPriya, I'd like to be considered for the Head role. Would you be willing to speak for me if my name comes up in calibration?

PriyaI was going to anyway. But I'm glad you asked; it tells me you want it.

PipIf it helps, here are three sentences: the complaint model, the Horizon submission, and onboarding four people.

PriyaPerfect. And Pip? One day you'll do this for someone else. Ask me who did it for me, some time.

— the AnnotatorPriya would have spoken for Pip anyway. The ask still mattered: it told her what he wanted, and gave her the words.

What's the difference between a mentor and a sponsor?

A mentor advises you. A sponsor spends their reputation to open doors for you, often in rooms you're not in.

How do you get a sponsor?

Deliver something important for them, then ask, specifically, for one opportunity.

What do you owe your sponsor?

Results, honesty, and never making them regret it. And, one day, sponsoring someone else.

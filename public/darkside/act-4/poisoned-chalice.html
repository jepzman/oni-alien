The Poisoned Chalice

An acting role: double the work, the same pay, and no promise of the real one.

Threat
Habitat
acting appointments, “interim” titles, roles held open for months
Weakness
terms: an end date, an allowance, and an honest answer about the permanent role

IA sighting

Monday 1 November 2027 · the cup

Pip became acting Head of Customer Insight “while we recruit, just for a couple of months”. Nine people instead of two. His old job didn't go anywhere. Neither did his salary.

November to January

Pip ran the joint planning review, the Horizon follow-ups and nine 1:1s a week, then did the regulator dashboard on Thursday nights. His Friday lines were the best they'd ever been. So were the bags under his eyes.

February · the extension

The interviews slipped. “Could you carry on until March?” The allowance was “being looked at” (see the Raise Duel). Pip noticed he'd been acting for longer than the original “couple of months” with no end date in writing.

March · the result

The permanent role went to someone else (see Passed Over). Pip handed back nine people on a Monday and returned to two. It was the most valuable five months of his career, and the most exhausting, and nobody had agreed the terms first, except for one thing: he'd asked, in October, whether acting would count in the process. It did. It just wasn't enough.

— the AnnotatorRead the label before you drink. If there isn't one, ask for one.

IIOrigin

flashbackHow acting roles became a trial

  1. 2016 · a sensible practice

    When a manager leaves, someone acts up for a few weeks. It's a good development chance, and short.

  2. 2020 · the freeze

    During the hiring freeze, acting roles stretch to months, because recruiting is slow and acting is free.

  3. 2023 · the audition

    Leaders start using long acting periods as unpaid auditions: “let's see how they do before we decide”.

  4. 2027 · Pip

    Pip's “couple of months” becomes five. It's a real opportunity and an unpaid trial at the same time.

The turning point. A short development step that turned into a free, open-ended audition.

What might have saved them. Terms agreed on day one: an end date, an allowance, cover, and clarity on the permanent role.

— the AnnotatorActing roles can be gifts. They're poisoned only when nobody writes down the terms.

IIIKnow thy enemy

Acting or interim roles can be great: you get experience and evidence for promotion. They become poisoned when they have no end date, no extra pay, no cover for your old job, and no honest answer about whether you're a real candidate for the permanent role.

What the organisation wants

Continuity while it recruits, at no extra cost, and a chance to see you in the job.

Why the poison spreads

Because recruitment slips, acting is free, and nobody revisits the terms once you've started.

The antidote

  • An end date.
  • An acting allowance.
  • Cover for your old job.
  • An honest answer on the permanent role.

Three kinds of chalice

The Honest Cup

Short, paid, clear odds. Drink and learn.

The Bottomless Cup

No end date. Ask for one before it's empty.

The Audition Cup

An unpaid trial for the real role. Know your odds.

— the AnnotatorIt's still worth drinking sometimes. Just know what's in the cup.

IVSigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • You're asked to “act up” while the role is recruited.
  • There's no end date.
  • There's no extra pay.
  • You still do your old job too.

VCounter-spells

  1. 1The Chalice Terms

    Agree an end date, an acting allowance, who covers your old job, and how the permanent role will be decided.

    incantation“Happy to act up. Can we agree: until 31 March, an acting allowance, Asha covering my analysis, and whether acting counts in the permanent process?”

  2. 2The Honest Odds

    Ask directly whether you're a real candidate for the permanent role.

    incantation“Honestly, am I a realistic candidate for the permanent role, or am I holding the seat?”

  3. 3The Evidence Harvest

    Treat the acting period as evidence: record decisions, results and what you learned.

VIForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Bottomless Cup

    Doing both jobs indefinitely without terms. You'll burn out, and the acting will be “just how it is”.

  • The Presumption

    Acting as if the permanent job is yours. If it isn't, the fall is public.

  • The Half-Act

    Doing the acting role half-heartedly because it's unpaid. It still becomes your evidence, good or bad.

VIIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

The Honest Cup.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • There's an end date.
  • There's an allowance.
  • You know your odds.

Do

  1. Accept and learn.
  2. Record your results in the Book of Deeds.
  3. Ask for feedback at the end.

Don’t: The half-act.

If it gets worse: If there's no end date or pay, you're at Nightmare.

Nightmare

The Bottomless Cup.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • “Just a couple of months” with no date.
  • No allowance.
  • You're doing both jobs.

Do

  1. Agree the Chalice Terms now, even mid-way.
  2. Ask for the allowance backdated.
  3. Hand over part of your old job.
  4. Ask the Honest Odds question.

Don’t: The presumption: acting as if the job is yours.

If it gets worse: If it's an unpaid audition and you're not a real candidate, you're at Hell.

Hell

The Audition Cup, with poor odds.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Leadership wants an outsider.
  • You're holding the seat.
  • Your health is suffering.

Do

  1. Ask directly about your odds.
  2. Set an end date yourself.
  3. Harvest the evidence for your next role.
  4. Protect your health.

Don’t: The bottomless cup: carrying on indefinitely because nobody stopped you.

If it gets worse: When it ends, read Passed Over before you decide anything.

VIIIIs your cup labelled?

If you're acting in a role, tick honestly.

  • Your acting role has no end date in writing.
  • You're not paid extra for it.
  • You're still doing all of your old job.
  • You don't know if you're a real candidate for the permanent role.
  • It was meant to be “a couple of months” and it's been longer.

The cure, if you ticked two: Send one email this week asking for the four terms: end date, allowance, cover, and how the permanent role will be decided.

IXThe rehearsal

The Chalice Terms and the Honest Odds.

Scene: Thursday 28 October, Pip's 1:1 with Priya, before saying yes.

PipI'd love to act up. Can we agree four things? An end date, say 31 March. An acting allowance. Asha covering the analysis. And will acting count in the permanent process?

PriyaEnd date, yes. Asha, yes. Acting counts, yes. The allowance I'll have to fight for.

PipAnd honestly: am I a realistic candidate, or holding the seat?

PriyaRealistic. But Ottoline wants to look outside too. I won't pretend otherwise.

PipThank you for being straight. I'll take it.

— the AnnotatorPip got three of four terms and an honest answer. The fourth (the allowance) he won later in the Raise Duel.

XThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

You're asked to act up while they recruit. What do you agree first?

An end date, an allowance, cover for your old job, and how the permanent role will be decided.

Five months later there's still no decision. What do you ask?

For a decision date in writing, and the allowance backdated if it was promised.

Is an acting role ever worth it unpaid?

Sometimes, for experience, if it's short and you know the odds. Never indefinitely.

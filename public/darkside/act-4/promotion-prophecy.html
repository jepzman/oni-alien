The Promotion Prophecy

Promotions are decided long before the process opens. The process just reads the prophecy aloud.

Threat
Habitat
sponsor conversations, talent reviews, the months before any job is advertised
Weakness
being known, by the right people, for the next job, before it exists

IA sighting

Monday 4 October 2027 · the announcement

Priya was promoted to Director of Customer. In the same email: a new role, Head of Customer Insight, would be “advertised in the new year through our usual fair process”.

Tuesday 5 October · floor 27

Tamsin, bringing tea into Ottoline's office, overheard two names in the first minute: Priya said “Pip”, Ottoline said “and the woman from Northgate Energy, if we can get her”. The formal process was three months away. The prophecy was already being written.

October to December · the visible slice

Pip, who had read this entry, told Priya that week that he wanted the role. She made him acting head (see the Poisoned Chalice). Dorian told nobody he wanted it, and spent October building a beautiful deck for an interview that wouldn't happen until February.

January · the advert

The role was advertised. By then three people already knew who the real candidates were. None of them was on the interview panel. All of them were in the room where the panel's recommendation would be discussed.

— the AnnotatorThe job advert is the last page of the prophecy. Get into the first chapter.

IIOrigin

flashbackHow MetroVolt's promotions came to be decided in advance

  1. 2012 · the open competition

    MetroVolt runs a fully open process for a senior role. The best interviewee wins, then fails within a year. Leadership decides interviews alone aren't enough.

  2. 2014 · talent reviews

    Twice a year, leaders discuss “who's ready for what”. It's sensible succession planning. It also means names are attached to roles long before they exist.

  3. 2019 · the CEO's circle

    Dame Helena and Ottoline start reviewing every senior appointment informally first. Formal processes continue, but their outcome is usually known.

  4. 2027 · the prophecy

    By the time Head of Customer Insight is advertised, it has been discussed in four talent reviews and two cups of tea. The process is real. It just isn't where the decision starts.

The turning point. A sensible fix (don't decide on interviews alone) that moved the real decision months earlier, where most candidates never see it.

What might have saved them. Nothing needs saving; succession planning is normal. What helps is candidates knowing it happens, and declaring early.

— the AnnotatorThe process isn't fake. It's just the second half. The first half happens in talent reviews, sponsors' conversations and cups of tea.

IIIKnow thy enemy

Most organisations decide promotions in two stages: an informal one (talent reviews, sponsors, “who's ready?”) months before, and a formal one (advert, interviews, panel) that mostly confirms it. You can't skip the formal stage. But the informal one decides who's seriously considered.

What the deciders want

Confidence. They want someone they've seen doing parts of the job already, recommended by someone they trust.

Why it works this way

Because senior appointments are risky and interviews are poor predictors. People trust what they've seen over what they've heard in an hour.

What gets you into the prophecy

  • Declaring your interest early.
  • Doing a visible slice of the next job.
  • A sponsor who says your name.
  • Evidence your manager can repeat.

Three kinds of prophecy

The Open Field

Genuinely undecided. The process matters a lot.

The Favourite

A name is already attached. Make sure yours is too.

The Outsider

Leadership wants someone from outside. Know this early; it changes your odds.

— the AnnotatorAsk your manager: “Who else is being talked about?” Many will tell you, and it's the most useful thing you'll hear.

IVSigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • A role is “coming soon” and people already talk about who'll get it.
  • Your manager says “let's see what the process says”.
  • Someone is given “stretch” work that looks like the future job.
  • Senior people mention a name in passing, never yours.

VCounter-spells

  1. 1The Early Declaration

    Tell your manager and sponsor, early and plainly, which role you want and why.

    incantation“I'd like to be considered for Head of Customer Insight. Can we talk about what you'd need to see?”

  2. 2The Future-Job Evidence

    Start doing a visible slice of the next job now, with your manager's blessing.

    incantation“Could I run the joint planning review next quarter? It's the part of the Head role I haven't done yet.”

  3. 3The Prophet's Ear

    Find out who shapes the decision and make sure they know your work, through your sponsor, openly.

VIForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • Waiting for the Advert

    Doing nothing until the role is advertised. By then the prophecy is written.

  • The Self-Coronation

    Telling colleagues the job is yours. It reaches the people deciding, and it reads as arrogance.

  • The Rival's Rumour

    Quietly undermining the other likely candidate. See Act II, the Doppelgänger. Don't be the Doppelgänger.

VIIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

The Open Field.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Nobody has been named.
  • The process is new.
  • Your manager is neutral.

Do

  1. Declare early.
  2. Ask what they'd need to see.
  3. Prepare well for the formal process.

Don’t: Waiting for the advert.

If it gets worse: If a favourite exists and it isn't you, you're at Nightmare.

Nightmare

The Favourite, and it's not you yet.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Another name keeps coming up.
  • They're doing the future job's visible work.
  • Your manager is vague.

Do

  1. Do the Early Declaration plainly.
  2. Ask for a visible slice of the job.
  3. Find a sponsor.
  4. Ask who else is being talked about.

Don’t: The rival's rumour.

If it gets worse: If leadership wants an outsider, you're at Hell.

Hell

The Outsider: leadership wants someone from outside.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • An external candidate is being courted.
  • Your sponsor says “it's complicated”.
  • The role's description fits someone else's CV.

Do

  1. Go for it anyway; it's good evidence for next time.
  2. Ask your sponsor honestly about the odds.
  3. Plan what you'll learn either way.
  4. Read Passed Over before the decision.

Don’t: The self-coronation.

If it gets worse: If you're passed over, read Passed Over: The Resurrection.

VIIIAre you in the prophecy?

Check whether anyone deciding knows you want the next job. Tick honestly.

  • You've never told your manager which role you want next.
  • You're waiting for roles to be advertised.
  • No senior person has seen you do any part of the next job.
  • You don't know who decides promotions in your area.
  • You think the formal process is the whole story.

The cure, if you ticked two: This week, tell your manager the next role you want, and ask what they'd need to see. That one sentence puts you in the first chapter.

IXThe rehearsal

The Early Declaration and the Future-Job Evidence.

Scene: Wednesday 6 October 2027, Pip's 1:1 with Priya.

PipPriya, I'd like to be considered for Head of Customer Insight. Can we talk about what you'd need to see?

PriyaI'm glad you asked. Honestly, the gap is the planners. You've never led Dorian's team.

PipCould I run the joint planning review next quarter? It's the part I haven't done.

PriyaBetter: act up as head from November while we recruit. You'd lead both teams.

PipI'd love that. Can we agree terms? I've read about poisoned chalices.

PriyaOf course you have. Send me what you need.

— the AnnotatorPip's sentence put his name next to Priya's, in her talks with Ottoline, three months before the advert.

XThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

A role you want will open “early next year”. What do you do this month?

Tell your manager you want it, ask what they'd need to see, and start doing a visible slice of it.

Everyone assumes a colleague will get it. Is it worth trying?

Yes, if you declare early and build evidence. Assumptions change; silence doesn't.

Is it unfair that promotions are decided before the process?

Often. But processes rarely change fast. Being known early is how you take part.

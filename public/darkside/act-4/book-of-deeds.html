By December, nobody remembers what you did in March. Including you.

IA sighting

Every Friday since March · 17:55

Since the Chronicler's advice (see Act V, the Go-Bag Scroll), Pip had written one line every Friday: what he'd done, what it changed, and proof. Some weeks the line was small (“Fixed the dictionary join; Asha's report 4× faster”). Some were big (“Horizon submission: kept the team; 5% cut instead of 15%”).

Thursday 2 December · review season

Pip's self-assessment took forty minutes instead of four evenings. It had numbers, dates and three thank-you emails. Dorian's said “various strategic contributions”.

Monday 6 December · before calibration

Pip sent Priya one page: the year in eight lines. She used five of them, word for word, in the calibration meeting.

January · the interview

For the Head of Customer Insight interview, Pip's examples were dated, measured and true. He didn't have to remember anything. He just had to read.

— the AnnotatorOne line a week is fifty-two lines a year. That's a promotion case, already written.

IIOrigin

flashbackHow the Book of Deeds was first written

  1. 2011 · the Chronicler's first calibration

    The Chronicler is rated “meets expectations” after their best year, because their manager could only remember the last two months.

  2. 2011 · the notebook

    The Chronicler starts a notebook: one line every Friday. It feels silly. It takes two minutes.

  3. 2012 · the next review

  4. 2027 · passing it on

    The Chronicler tells Pip about it in a café (Act V). Pip starts that Friday.

The turning point. A great year forgotten by a manager with a two-month memory.

What might have saved them. Nothing; this is the cure. The only thing to save is your future self's December.

— the AnnotatorYour manager has eight people, two hundred emails a day and a two-month memory. Help them.

IIIKnow thy enemy

Reviews, promotions and pay negotiations all run on evidence, and memory is terrible evidence. A Book of Deeds (sometimes called a brag document) is a running record of what you did and what it changed, written weekly while it's fresh.

What goes in it

One line a week: what you did, its result (with a number if possible), and proof (a link, an email, a metric).

Why it works

Because recency bias is real: managers remember the last month. A year of lines corrects it, and gives them sentences they can use.

Where it pays off

  • Self-assessments.
  • Calibration (via your manager).
  • Promotion interviews.
  • Pay negotiations and CVs.

Three kinds of entry

The Delivery

Shipped something. Add the result.

The Rescue

Fixed something or prevented a problem. Add what would have happened.

The Multiplier

Helped others succeed. Add whose success, and how.

— the AnnotatorWrite it on Friday at 17:55. It's the most valuable five minutes of the week, and the least glamorous.

IVSigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • Review season arrives and you can't remember the spring.
  • Your self-assessment says “various projects”.
  • Your manager remembers your last month, not your year.
  • You've never kept a list of your work.

VIForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The December Scramble

    Trying to remember a whole year in one evening. You'll remember the last month and a vague feeling.

  • The Inflated Deed

    Exaggerating results in the book. One check and your whole book loses credibility.

  • The Shared Credit Theft

    Writing team results as yours. Name who helped. It's more credible anyway.

VIIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

No book yet.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Your last self-assessment was painful.
  • You can't remember the spring.
  • You have evidence scattered in emails.

Do

  1. Start this Friday: one line.
  2. Backfill the last month from your sent items.
  3. Set a recurring reminder.

Don’t: The December scramble.

If it gets worse: If review season is close and the book is empty, you're at Nightmare.

Nightmare

Review season, empty book.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Reviews are in two weeks.
  • You can't remember most of the year.
  • Your manager is busy.

Do

  1. Mine your sent items, calendar and chats for the year.
  2. Write ten lines with numbers.
  3. Send your manager one page.
  4. Start the weekly habit afterwards.

Don’t: The inflated deed: rounding up when you're not sure.

If it gets worse: If your manager ignores your evidence, you're at Hell.

Hell

Your evidence is ignored.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Your review contradicts your book.
  • Your manager won't discuss it.
  • Calibration went badly.

Do

  1. Ask, in writing, how your evidence was considered.
  2. Share it with your skip-level or sponsor.
  3. Use the formal review appeal if there is one.
  4. Use the book for your CV: it's yours.

Don’t: The shared credit theft: claiming team results as yours to compensate.

If it gets worse: If your work is never recognised, the book is also your next job's evidence.

VIIIIs your book open?

Tick honestly.

  • You couldn't list ten things you did this year with numbers.
  • Your last self-assessment took more than an evening.
  • You rely on your manager to remember your work.
  • You've never sent your manager a summary before a review.
  • You think writing it down is bragging.

The cure, if you ticked two: Set a reminder for Friday, 17:55: “one line”. Do it for a month. Then never stop.

PipPriya, in case it helps you in the room: 1) Complaint model: repeat calls −9% (£410k). 2) Horizon: team kept; 5% cut not 15%. 3) Acting head since November: joint planning review delivered. 4) Onboarded four people in two weeks each (with Asha). Full list attached.

PriyaThis is exactly what I needed. I'm using lines 1 to 4 word for word.

In calibration, Priya reads the four lines in under a minute. Ottoline writes one of them down.

— the AnnotatorPip didn't argue for himself in the room. His book did, through the person who was there.

XThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

What goes in a Friday line?

What you did, its result, and evidence: “Shipped forecast v4; peak within 2%; Ops thanked us (email 12 Mar).”

When do you share it with your manager?

Before reviews and promotion discussions, as a one-page summary.

Isn't it bragging?

It's record-keeping. Your manager has many people to remember. Help them remember you accurately.

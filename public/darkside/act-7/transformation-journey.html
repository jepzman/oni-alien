The Transformation Journey

Every few years the kingdom sets out on a holy pilgrimage to a new way of working. It's the fourth one. The road looks familiar.

Threat
Habitat
operating-model decks, renamed teams, new job titles, coaches, sticky notes
Weakness
finding the real problem underneath, keeping what works, and helping the good coaches

IA sighting

Monday 3 September 2029 · the pilgrimage begins

Ascend 2030's centrepiece was a new Product Operating Model. Customer Insight became the “Insight Squad”, part of the “Customer Value Stream”. Ruth became a “Value Stream Lead”. Pip became a “Principal Insight Practitioner”. Nothing else about anyone's job changed on day one.

The ghosts on the road

The Chronicler had walked this road three times. Lean, 2014: kaizen boards, still on a wall on floor 9, last updated in 2015. Agile, 2018: stand-ups, sprints and a team renamed “tribe” for a year. SAFe, 2022: release trains and quarterly planning events in a hotel; the Chronicler was a “Release Train Engineer” for eight months and never found out what it meant.

Tuesday 11 September · the fourth guide

Juno Ferrell, Head of Ways of Working, ran the Insight Squad's first session. Pip expected sticky notes. Juno asked one question: “What takes too long here?” Pip did the Real Problem Hunt and found it: a piece of analysis took eleven weeks from idea to approved work, mostly waiting for sign-offs. Juno's eyes lit up. “That. Let's fix that.”

March 2030 · the destination

Pip measured it monthly. Juno removed three sign-off steps and introduced a weekly decision meeting with Ruth. By March, idea to approved work took four weeks. Pip had kept the Keep List: Ruth's monthly top three, the Friday notes, the team's Tuesday lunch. All survived. The names didn't: by spring, everyone was quietly calling it Customer Insight again. Juno didn't mind. “The names were never the point.”

— the AnnotatorEvery transformation promises a new world. Ask which problem it solves, and help solve that one.

IIOrigin

flashbackHow MetroVolt came to transform four times

  1. 2014 · Lean

    A manufacturing method applied to an office. Real improvements in billing; boards everywhere else. The CEO leaves; it fades.

  2. 2018 · Agile

    A new CTO brings agile from tech. It works in software teams. It's rolled out to Finance. It doesn't.

  3. 2022 · SAFe

    The parent group wants agile at scale. Consultants arrive. A great deal of planning happens. Rather less else.

  4. 2029 · the Product Operating Model

    Rupert brings the fourth. This time, by luck or design, the coach is good and asks about real problems.

The turning point. Each transformation being sold as the answer before anyone asked what the question was.

What might have saved them. Starting with a measured problem (eleven weeks from idea to work) rather than a method.

— the AnnotatorTransformations fail when the method is the goal. They work when a real problem is the goal, and the method is just a tool.

IIIKnow thy enemy

A transformation is a large programme to change how an organisation works: new structures, new roles, new rituals, often a new vocabulary. Many fail, not because the methods are bad but because they're imposed as rituals rather than used to fix specific problems. The fourth one is usually met with a sigh. Sometimes it's the one that works.

What it wants

Faster, better work. Sometimes just the look of change, for a board or a parent company.

Why they repeat

Because each one changes names and rituals but not decisions, so the problems stay, and the next one is announced.

What makes one work

  • A specific, measured problem.
  • Good coaches with allies.
  • Keeping what already works.
  • Changing decisions, not just names.

Three kinds of pilgrimage

The Real Journey

A real problem, measured. Join in and help.

The Renaming

New words, same work. Be polite; protect your Keep List.

The Consultant Crusade

Imported wholesale, at great expense. Find the real problem inside it anyway.

— the AnnotatorAsk the coach what problem they've been asked to solve. Good ones will tell you, and be grateful you asked.

IVSigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • Teams are renamed squads, pods or tribes.
  • Everyone gets a new job title that means the same thing.
  • Consultants arrive with sticky notes.
  • Nobody can say what the last transformation achieved.

VCounter-spells

  1. 1The Real Problem Hunt

    Ask what problem this transformation is meant to solve, specifically. Then help solve that.

    incantation“What's the one thing that should be different in a year? I'd like to help measure it.”

  2. 2The Keep List

    Write down what works now and argue, kindly, to keep it through the change.

  3. 3The Coach's Ally

    Find the coaches who are actually good, and help them. They need allies more than sceptics.

VIForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Eye-Roll

    Visible cynicism about every change. You'll be right twice and ignored forever.

  • The True Believer

    Adopting every ritual without asking why. Stand-ups with nothing to say are still meetings.

  • Renaming Instead of Changing

    If you lead it: changing the names and the slides but not the decisions. Everyone notices.

VIIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

The Real Journey.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • A clear problem is named.
  • The coach asks good questions.
  • Leaders change their own habits too.

Do

  1. Help measure the problem.
  2. Share your Keep List.
  3. Be the coach's ally.

Don’t: The eye-roll.

If it gets worse: If it's mostly new names, you're at Nightmare.

Nightmare

The Renaming.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • New titles, same jobs.
  • New rituals, same decisions.
  • Nobody can say what problem it solves.

Do

  1. Do the Real Problem Hunt yourself.
  2. Propose a measure.
  3. Protect what works.
  4. Go along with the names politely.

Don’t: The true believer.

If it gets worse: If it's breaking what works, you're at Hell.

Hell

The Consultant Crusade.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • A huge programme, imported wholesale.
  • Teams that worked are being broken up.
  • Dissent is called resistance.

Do

  1. Document what's working, with evidence.
  2. Take the Keep List to the most senior sponsor you can.
  3. Find allies inside the programme.
  4. Pick your battles.

Don’t: Open war with the programme.

If it gets worse: It will pass. Keep good records for the fifth one.

VIIIAre you a transformation ghost?

Tick honestly.

  • You roll your eyes whenever a change programme is mentioned.
  • You've adopted a ritual without knowing why.
  • You can't say what problem the current change is solving.
  • You've renamed something and called it change.
  • You've never helped a coach, only judged them.

The cure, if you ticked two: Ask the next coach you meet: “What problem are you here to fix?” Then offer to help measure it.

IXThe rehearsal

The Real Problem Hunt.

Scene: Tuesday 11 September 2029, the Insight Squad's first session with Juno.

JunoI'm the fourth agile coach you've had. I know. What takes too long here?

PipHonestly? Getting work approved. I measured it once: eleven weeks from idea to start.

JunoEleven weeks. Where does it go?

PipThree sign-offs and a monthly committee.

JunoThat's what we fix. Will you measure it every month with me?

PipIf we can keep Ruth's monthly top three and the Tuesday lunch.

JunoKeep everything that works. I'm here for the eleven weeks.

— the AnnotatorJuno is the first coach the Chronicler has ever invited to lunch.

XThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

It's the fourth transformation. Is it worth engaging?

Yes, with the Real Problem Hunt. Some transformations work. The ones with a real problem and allies.

Your team works well. The transformation will change it. What do you do?

Make the Keep List and take it to the people leading the change, early.

Is agile nonsense?

No. Agile done as a ritual is nonsense. Agile done to solve a real problem is often very good.

The Annotator's last wordEvery transformation promises a new world. Ask which problem it solves, and help solve that one.

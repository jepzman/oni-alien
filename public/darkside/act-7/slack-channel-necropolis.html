The Slack Channel Necropolis

Four hundred and twelve channels. Twelve are alive. The rest are tombs that still send notifications.

Threat
Habitat
chat tools, @channel pings, unread badges, “let's spin up a channel for this”
Weakness
ruthless muting, clear channel purposes, and someone willing to archive

IA sighting

Wednesday 2 January 2030 · the census

Back from Christmas, Pip had 1,402 unread messages. He counted his channels: 183. Twelve had a message in the last month that mattered to him. The rest were tombs: #proj-lumen-old-DO-NOT-USE, #random-2, #ascend2030-announcements-official-v2, #cake-floor-22 (floor 22 had closed in September). Hugo was in 412.

January · the missed decision

Rafi spent a week building a forecast on May's data. The decision to use June's had been made in a thread in #insight-squad-general on 19 December, at 17:48, by two people, and never written anywhere else.

Friday 4 January · the Great Muting

Pip left 160 channels in an afternoon. Nothing bad happened. He proposed to the team: every new channel gets a purpose line and an end date; decisions in chat get written in the project log, with a link. Juno liked it so much they made it part of Ascend's guidance.

Friday 14 February · the day of the dead

Juno and IT archived 2,600 dead channels across MetroVolt in one day. Hugo's @channel to 3,000 people about cake in kitchen 14 was, by consensus, the last one. He has not used @channel since.

— the AnnotatorMost channels are tombs. Leave the dead in peace and spend your attention on the living.

IIOrigin

flashbackHow the necropolis grew

  1. 2014 · the first channels

    Slack arrives. Ten channels, all lively. It's faster than email. Everyone loves it.

  2. 2018 · a channel per project

    Every project gets a channel. Projects end. Channels don't. Nobody has the job of closing them.

  3. 2022 · the sprawl

    Thousands of channels. People join everything, in case. Decisions happen in threads that half the team never sees.

  4. 2030 · the census

    Pip counts 183. Hugo counts 412. MetroVolt has 4,100. About 400 are alive.

The turning point. Making channels free and easy to create, and nobody's job to close.

What might have saved them. A purpose line and an end date on every channel from the start, and one person per project who archives.

— the AnnotatorTools that are easy to start and nobody's job to end always become graveyards. Give endings an owner.

IIIKnow thy enemy

Chat tools make it easy to create channels for every project, team and topic. Over time most go quiet but stay open, still notifying, still holding old decisions. People join everything for fear of missing out, then miss what matters in the noise.

What it wants

Your attention, constantly, in small pieces.

Why it grows

Because creating a channel is free, closing one is nobody's job, and leaving one feels risky.

What tames it

  • Leaving and muting freely.
  • A purpose and an end date.
  • Decisions written somewhere permanent.
  • Rare, careful @channel.

Three kinds of channel

The Living

Active, purposeful. Stay.

The Undead

Quiet but still notifying. Mute.

The Tomb

Finished, abandoned. Leave, and suggest archiving.

— the AnnotatorIf something matters to you, someone will tell you directly. If they won't, a channel won't save you.

IVSigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • You're in more channels than you have colleagues.
  • Every project gets a channel. No project closes one.
  • @channel is used for lunch.
  • Important decisions happen in threads you missed.

VCounter-spells

  1. 1The Great Muting

    Once a quarter, leave or mute every channel you haven't read in a month. Nothing bad will happen.

  2. 2The Purpose Line

    Every channel you create gets a one-line purpose and an end date. When it's done, archive it.

    incantation“#tariff-study: for the 2029 tariff study only. Archived when the report ships in March.”

  3. 3The Decision Rule

    Decisions made in chat get written down somewhere permanent, with a link back.

    incantation“Decision: we're using the June data. I've added it to the project log.”

VIForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The @channel Curse

    Pinging three hundred people for something that matters to five.

  • The Channel Spawn

    Creating a new channel for every conversation. You're building tombs.

  • Living in the Tombs

    Reading every channel to avoid missing anything. You'll miss your actual work.

VIIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

A few too many channels.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • You're in dozens.
  • Most are quiet.
  • Notifications are manageable.

Do

  1. Do the Great Muting once a quarter.
  2. Turn off notifications for most.
  3. Keep a short list of the living.

Don’t: Living in the tombs.

If it gets worse: If you're missing decisions, you're at Nightmare.

Nightmare

Decisions in the noise.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Important things are decided in threads.
  • People miss them.
  • Work is redone.

Do

  1. Push for the Decision Rule.
  2. Write decisions in one permanent place.
  3. Ask for a weekly summary.
  4. Say when you've missed one, calmly.

Don’t: The channel spawn.

If it gets worse: If the noise is constant and expected, you're at Hell.

Hell

The Always-On Necropolis.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Instant replies are expected.
  • Hundreds of channels, all “important”.
  • Your focus time is gone.

Do

  1. Agree response-time norms with your manager.
  2. Block focus time.
  3. Batch chat twice a day.
  4. Read the Green Dot of Doom.

Don’t: The @channel curse.

If it gets worse: If it's damaging your health, read Act VIII.

VIIIAre you building the necropolis?

Tick honestly.

  • You've created a channel without a purpose line.
  • You've never archived a channel you started.
  • You've used @channel for something that mattered to fewer than ten people.
  • You've made a decision in a thread and not written it anywhere else.
  • You're in more channels than you can name.

The cure, if you ticked two: Archive one channel you created that's finished. Then leave five you haven't read this month.

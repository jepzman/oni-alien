The Return-to-Office Decree

A proclamation from on high: thou shalt be seen, three days a week, mostly on video calls with people at home.

Threat
Habitat
turnstiles, badge reports, all-staff emails, empty floors on Fridays
Weakness
making office days worth the commute, honest conversation, and knowing what's actually enforced

IA sighting

Monday 14 May 2029 · the decree

An email from Imogen Hale and Rupert Thorne, titled “The way we work together”: from 3 September, everyone in the office three days a week, Tuesdays included (“Collaboration Tuesdays”). Badge data would be “shared with managers to support conversations”. Floor 22 would close for “refurbishment”. It has not reopened.

Tuesday 4 September · the first Collaboration Tuesday

Pip left home at 06:00, seventy minutes each way. His team had all picked different days. He spent the day on video calls with people at home, in a booth, wearing headphones. At 17:00 he worked out he'd spoken to one person in the room: the man who refilled the coffee machine.

Wednesday 5 September · the Overlap Pact

Pip proposed that the team all come in Tuesdays and Thursdays, and save planning, workshops and lunch for those days. Everyone agreed in under a minute. Nobody had asked before. Then he made the Honest Ask to Ruth about his third day: fixed Tuesdays and Thursdays, the third when there was a workshop. Ruth said yes, and that he wasn't the first to ask.

October · the badge report

The first report said Pip averaged 2.6 days. Ruth looked at it, looked at the Overlap Pact, and filed it. In the same report, three people in another team badged in at 08:58 and out at 09:20 every Friday. Everyone knew. Nobody said anything. In November their manager did.

— the AnnotatorA mandate tells you how often to come in. It can't tell you why. Find a why, or make the honest case that there isn't one.

IIOrigin

flashbackHow the decree came down from floor 27

  1. 2020 · the great scattering

    Like everyone, MetroVolt sends everyone home. Work mostly carries on. Some things (onboarding, mentoring, chance conversations) quietly get worse.

  2. 2022 · the hybrid truce

    Teams choose their own patterns. Some thrive. Some never meet. Nobody measures which.

  3. 2028 · the lease

    MetroVolt is paying for thirty floors and using fourteen. The parent group wants either fewer floors or fuller ones. Both, ideally.

  4. May 2029 · the decree

    Rupert and Imogen combine three real problems (lonely new joiners, empty floors, a CEO who misses the buzz) into one rule for everyone.

The turning point. Three different problems, solved with one blunt rule, so it solves none of them well.

What might have saved them. Asking teams what they need the office for, and setting the rule around that.

— the AnnotatorMost mandates mix a real problem (people not meeting) with a cost problem (empty floors). Help with the first. Be honest about the second.

IIIKnow thy enemy

Return-to-office mandates require people to be in the office a set number of days. Behind them are usually several reasons at once: collaboration, culture, new joiners, property costs, leadership instinct. The mandate is the same for everyone; the reasons aren't. Knowing which reason applies to you changes how you should respond.

What it wants

People together, visibly. Sometimes for collaboration; sometimes for the building; sometimes because leaders feel better seeing people.

Why it's enforced with badges

Because it's easy to count. Collaboration isn't. So the measure becomes the goal.

What makes it bearable

  • Team overlap days.
  • Office days planned for in-person work.
  • Honest, early exceptions.
  • Managers who look at work, not badges.

Three kinds of decree

The Collaboration Decree

Genuinely about working together. Make the days count.

The Property Decree

About filling floors. Comply sensibly; don't pretend it's about you.

The Control Decree

About watching people. Do the work, keep records, and think about the long term.

— the AnnotatorIf you have caring responsibilities, a disability or a health condition, ask about adjustments early. Many workplaces must consider them.

IVSigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • An email arrives titled “The way we work together”.
  • Somebody mentions “badge data”.
  • A whole floor is closing for “refurbishment”.
  • Your team's office days don't overlap.

VCounter-spells

  1. 1The Overlap Pact

    Agree with your team which days you're all in, and make those the days for workshops, planning and lunch.

    incantation“If we're coming in three days anyway, can we all pick Tuesday and Thursday? Let's save the thinking work for those.”

  2. 2The Honest Ask

    If the mandate genuinely doesn't work for you (care, health, distance), say so early, privately and with a proposal.

    incantation“My commute's seventy minutes each way. Could I do two fixed days, and the third when there's a workshop?”

  3. 3The Purpose Test

    Before each office day, plan one thing that's genuinely better in person. If there's nothing, say so.

VIForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • Coffee Badging

    Swiping in, getting a coffee, going home. Badge data shows it. So do your colleagues.

  • The Loud Rebellion

    Ranting about the mandate in a public channel. It changes nothing and gets remembered.

  • Commuting to Sit on Calls

    Coming in to spend all day on video calls with remote people. Plan your days better.

VIIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

The Collaboration Decree, sensibly applied.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • The reasons are explained.
  • Exceptions are possible.
  • Managers are reasonable.

Do

  1. Agree the Overlap Pact.
  2. Plan in-person work for office days.
  3. Ask early if it doesn't fit.

Don’t: Commuting to sit on calls.

If it gets worse: If badges are counted and judged, you're at Nightmare.

Nightmare

The Badge-Counting Decree.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Badge data is reported monthly.
  • Managers are told to “have conversations”.
  • Exceptions are rare.

Do

  1. Comply where you can.
  2. Make the Honest Ask where you can't, in writing.
  3. Keep your outcomes visible.
  4. Ask your manager how they'll use the data.

Don’t: Coffee badging.

If it gets worse: If attendance becomes a performance issue, you're at Hell.

Hell

The Control Decree, with consequences.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Attendance affects reviews or pay.
  • No exceptions are granted.
  • People are being managed out over it.

Do

  1. Know your contract and rights.
  2. Document any adjustments you've asked for.
  3. Talk to HR or a union if needed.
  4. Decide honestly if this place still works for you.

Don’t: The loud rebellion.

If it gets worse: If it doesn't work for your life, read Act VIII's chapter on leaving.

VIIIAre you making the decree worse?

As a colleague or a manager. Tick honestly.

  • You come in on different days from your team and complain the office is pointless.
  • You judge colleagues by how often you see them.
  • You book in-person days and spend them on calls.
  • You've coffee-badged.
  • As a manager, you've looked at badge data before looking at work.

The cure, if you ticked two: Ask your team which two days you'll all be in, and make one of them the day you do your best thinking together.

IXThe rehearsal

The Honest Ask.

Scene: Wednesday 5 September 2029, Pip's 1:1 with Ruth.

PipCan I be honest about the three days? I'm seventy minutes each way. Yesterday I came in and sat on calls.

RuthI saw. Sorry. What would work?

PipThe team's agreed Tuesdays and Thursdays. Could my third day be flexible, for workshops and planning?

RuthYes. I'd rather you came in for a reason than to badge.

PipAnd if the badge report flags me?

RuthThen I'll show them the Overlap Pact. You're not the first to ask, Pip.

— the AnnotatorFour other people asked Ruth the same thing that month. All got a version of yes.

XThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

The mandate says three days. Your team never overlaps. What do you do?

Propose the Overlap Pact. Same days, real collaboration.

The mandate doesn't work for your circumstances. Should you just comply?

Make the Honest Ask first, early and privately, with a proposal. Many exceptions exist; few are advertised.

Are office mandates always about control?

Sometimes. Sometimes it's real estate or real collaboration problems. Find out which; it changes your approach.

The Annotator's last wordIf you must commute, make the office worth it. If it can't be, say so, kindly, with a proposal.

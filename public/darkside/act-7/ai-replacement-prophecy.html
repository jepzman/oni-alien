The AI Replacement Prophecy

A prophecy that the machine will take your job. Half true, half hype, entirely terrifying at 3 a.m.

Threat
Habitat
strategy memos, conference keynotes, LinkedIn, the quiet worry before sleep
Weakness
knowing which parts of your job are judgment, learning the tools, and showing what you add

IA sighting

Monday 4 June 2029 · the awakening

MetroVolt launched VoltMind, an AI assistant, for every employee. It drafted emails, summarised meetings, wrote code and, it turned out, produced Customer Insight's weekly churn report in thirty-eight seconds. That report took an analyst most of a Monday.

Tuesday 12 June · the memo

Rupert Thorne's memo on Ascend 2030 said AI would let MetroVolt deliver “30% more output with 20% fewer people by 2031”. It didn't say which people. Pip's five analysts read it at their desks in total silence. That evening two of them updated their CVs. One of them was Pip.

June · the Task Map

Pip stopped reading predictions and made a list of everything his team did. About 40% (weekly reports, data pulls, first drafts) VoltMind could already do. About 35% it helped with. About 25% was judgment: which question to ask, whether a number was believable, what to tell Ruth. Nobody had ever measured that 25%. It was most of what made the team useful.

Thursday 19 July · the heatwave

VoltMind's demand forecast for the heatwave week was smooth, confident and wrong: it had never seen a heatwave with heat pumps on this scale. Rafi, one of Pip's analysts, spotted it, reworked it, and the control room adjusted in time. Pip wrote it up for Ruth: the machine drafted, the human caught. Ruth sent it to Rupert.

March 2030 · the outcome

Customer Insight wasn't cut in Ascend 2030. The weekly reports were automated, and the Monday they'd freed went on the questions nobody had had time for. Rafi became the team's first “insight engineer”. Honestly told: elsewhere, two report-production roles in Finance weren't replaced when their holders left. The prophecy wasn't false. It just wasn't as simple as the memo.

— the AnnotatorThe machine has no opinion about your job. Your manager does. Make sure they know what you add.

IIOrigin

flashbackHow the prophecy was written

  1. 2023 · the demos

    Everyone sees AI write essays and code. Leaders at every company are asked by their boards: what's your AI strategy?

  2. 2025 · the consultants

    Reports promise huge productivity gains. Numbers like “30%” start appearing in parent-group strategy decks.

  3. 2028 · the target

    The parent group sets Rupert a cost target for 2031. AI is the most convenient way to explain how it'll be met.

  4. June 2029 · the memo

    A real technology, a real cost target and a confident number become one sentence. Every analyst reads it as being about them.

The turning point. Mixing a genuine new tool with a cost target, and announcing both in one number.

What might have saved them. Asking each team to map its own tasks first, then deciding what AI changes. Bottom-up instead of top-down.

— the AnnotatorAI changes jobs; that part is real. But a percentage in a memo is a hope about money, not a measurement of your work.

IIIKnow thy enemy

The AI replacement prophecy is the fear (and sometimes the plan) that AI will take your job. Some of it is real: tasks that are routine, repeatable and text- or data-based are being automated. Some of it is hype. Most jobs are a mix of tasks, and AI usually takes some of them and changes the rest.

What it wants

The prophets want attention or savings. The machine wants nothing. Your leaders want to show they have a plan.

Why it terrifies

Because it's vague. “20% fewer people” could be anyone. Uncertainty is worse than bad news.

What it can't easily replace

  • Choosing the right question.
  • Knowing when a number is wrong.
  • Trust, relationships, persuasion.
  • Taking responsibility for a decision.

Three kinds of prophecy

The Hype Prophecy

Loud predictions, no plan. Learn the tools; ignore the noise.

The Change Prophecy

Your job will change. Move towards the judgment parts.

The Cut Prophecy

Roles really will go. Read Act V; prepare honestly.

— the AnnotatorThe people who learn the tools well early tend to become the people who decide how they're used. Be one of them.

IVSigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • A memo mentions AI and headcount in the same sentence.
  • A task you did weekly now takes VoltMind thirty seconds.
  • Leadership talks about “capacity released”.
  • Colleagues stop sharing how they do things.

VCounter-spells

  1. 1The Task Map

    List everything you do. Mark each: the machine can do it, the machine helps, or it needs human judgment. Be honest.

  2. 2The Apprentice Rite

    Learn the tools properly, early. The people who use AI well become the people who decide how it's used.

    incantation“Could I spend Friday afternoons learning VoltMind properly, and share what works with the team?”

  3. 3The Judgment Showcase

    Make the human part visible: the question you asked, the error you caught, the decision you made.

    incantation“VoltMind drafted the forecast in a minute. I spent the morning finding why it was wrong about the heatwave.”

VIForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Ostrich

    Refusing to touch the tools, hoping it'll pass. It won't, and you'll be the one who can't use them.

  • The Doom Scroll

    Reading every AI prediction at midnight. Prophets are paid to frighten you.

  • Hiding Your Use

    Secretly using AI and passing the output off as all yours. See the Copilot Credit Grab.

VIIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

The Hype Prophecy.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Lots of talk, no specifics.
  • Tools are new and optional.
  • Nobody's role has changed yet.

Do

  1. Learn the tools properly.
  2. Do the Task Map for yourself.
  3. Share what works.

Don’t: The doom scroll.

If it gets worse: If parts of your job are being automated, you're at Nightmare.

Nightmare

The Change Prophecy.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • A real chunk of your work is now automated.
  • Your manager is asking what you'll do instead.
  • Colleagues are anxious.

Do

  1. Do the Task Map with your manager.
  2. Propose what to do with the time freed.
  3. Do the Judgment Showcase.
  4. Help your team learn, not hide.

Don’t: The ostrich.

If it gets worse: If roles are actually being cut, you're at Hell.

Hell

The Cut Prophecy.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Headcount targets name AI.
  • Roles like yours are going.
  • Timelines are announced.

Do

  1. Ask directly how your role is affected.
  2. Build skills that move you towards judgment work.
  3. Update your CV quietly.
  4. Read Act V, the Famine.

Don’t: Pretending it isn't happening.

If it gets worse: If your role goes, read the Go-Bag Scroll.

VIIIAre you feeding the prophecy?

Tick honestly.

  • You haven't tried the AI tools your company offers.
  • You read AI predictions late at night.
  • You can't say which parts of your job need your judgment.
  • You've hidden how you do your work so nobody can automate it.
  • You've told someone “AI will take your job” as a joke, and they didn't laugh.

The cure, if you ticked two: This week, write your own Task Map: what the machine can do, what it helps with, what needs you. Then show your manager the last column.

IXThe rehearsal

The Task Map, out loud.

Scene: Tuesday 26 June 2029, Pip and his analysts.

PipI read Rupert's memo too. I updated my CV. I'm saying that so you know I'm not pretending.

RafiSo are we going?

PipI don't know. But I've mapped what we do. VoltMind can do about forty percent of it already.

Rafi…That's the reports.

PipYes. And the other quarter is judgment, which nobody's ever measured. I want to measure it, and show Ruth.

RafiThen let's give the reports to the machine and do the questions we never had time for.

— the AnnotatorThree weeks later Rafi caught the heatwave error. It was the best evidence Pip ever wrote up.

XThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

A memo says AI will cut 20% of roles. What do you do first?

The Task Map. Find out which of your work is at risk, honestly, before panicking.

A big part of your job can now be automated. Is your job gone?

Not necessarily. The job usually changes. Move towards the judgment parts, and say so to your manager.

Should you be afraid?

Some, and usefully. Ignore the prophets; learn the tools; show your judgment.

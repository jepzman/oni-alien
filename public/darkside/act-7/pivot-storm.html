The Pivot Storm

The strategy changes every quarter. The wind blows a different way each time. The work stays half-finished in every direction.

Threat
Habitat
quarterly planning, strategy decks, new slogans, roadmaps rewritten from scratch
Weakness
finding the work that matters in every direction, finishing small things, and asking what's really changed

IA sighting

July 2029 · the first wind

Ascend 2030's Q3 priority: Customer First. Pip's team built a roadmap around complaints, churn and satisfaction. Ruth presented it in August. It was praised.

October · the second wind

Q4's priority: Cost Leadership. Every roadmap was to be rebuilt around savings. Pip's team rebuilt theirs in a week. The complaints work was paused, half-finished. The churn model, half-built, was moved to “later”.

January 2030 · the third wind

Q1's priority: AI First. A third roadmap. Rafi asked, quietly, whether there was any point finishing anything. Pip asked Ruth the What-Changed Question: of the team's seven projects, which actually stopped under AI First? They went through it line by line. One. The slides had changed completely. The work had barely changed at all.

February · the shelter

Pip picked the work that mattered under all three winds: the churn model (customers, costs and AI, all at once). The team finished it in five weeks, in small pieces. It was used in all three strategies' reports. Ruth stopped passing each new slogan straight down; she started translating it first. In March, Dame Helena announced the 2030 strategy would “stay stable for at least a year”. The Chronicler said they'd believe it in April 2031.

— the AnnotatorFind the work that matters in every wind. Finish it. Storms can't move finished things.

IIOrigin

flashbackHow the storm began

  1. 2028 · the parent group

    The parent group reviews strategy quarterly, for every company it owns. Each review produces a new priority.

  2. 2029 · Rupert's slogans

    Rupert turns each quarter's priority into a slogan for MetroVolt: Customer First, Cost Leadership, AI First.

  3. The cascade

    Every layer passes the slogan down unchanged. Teams rebuild roadmaps from scratch, each quarter.

  4. The truth beneath

    The underlying work barely changes. Customers still leave; costs still matter; AI still needs data. Only the slides change.

The turning point. Treating every quarterly emphasis as a new strategy, and passing it down without translation.

What might have saved them. One manager at each level asking “What actually changes for our work?” before passing the wind on.

— the AnnotatorMost pivots change the words more than the work. Find the work that doesn't change, and protect it.

IIIKnow thy enemy

A pivot storm is when an organisation's priorities change so often that work is constantly restarted and rarely finished. Sometimes the changes are real and necessary. Often they're changes of emphasis, amplified into full rewrites as they pass down the layers. Teams lose faith in plans, and in finishing anything.

What it wants

To look responsive. Sometimes it genuinely is responding to a changing world.

Why it's so damaging

Because half-finished work in every direction delivers nothing, and teams that never finish stop believing.

What survives it

  • Work that matters under every strategy.
  • Small pieces, finished.
  • Managers who translate, not relay.
  • Asking what really changed.

Three kinds of storm

The Real Change

The world changed; so must you. Adapt, and finish what still matters.

The Slogan Storm

New words, mostly the same work. Translate it; protect the shelter work.

The Chaos Storm

Nobody knows what matters. Finish small things and ask for a priority in writing.

— the AnnotatorIf you manage a team: you are the roof. Let some rain through, not all of it.

IVSigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • The strategy slide has a new slogan every quarter.
  • Your roadmap has been rewritten three times this year.
  • Nothing you started has finished.
  • People stop believing the next plan.

VCounter-spells

  1. 1The Shelter Work

    Find the work that matters under every strategy and protect it. There's always some.

    incantation“Whatever the priority, we'll need a good churn model. Let's finish that whatever happens.”

  2. 2The What-Changed Question

    When a pivot comes, ask what actually changes for your work, and what doesn't. Often less than the slides say.

    incantation“Under AI First, which of our current projects stop? Which carry on?”

  3. 3The Small Finish

    Break work into pieces small enough to finish within a quarter. Storms can't blow away finished things.

VIForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Instant Rebuild

    Rewriting your whole roadmap the day the slogan changes. Wait a week; ask what changed.

  • The Cynical Freeze

    Doing nothing because “it'll change again”. Some things still need finishing.

  • The Team Whiplash

    If you lead: passing every pivot straight to your team. Absorb some of the wind.

VIIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

The Real Change.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • The reason for the pivot is clear.
  • It happens rarely.
  • Some work genuinely stops.

Do

  1. Ask what changes for you.
  2. Finish what still matters.
  3. Adapt the rest calmly.

Don’t: The cynical freeze.

If it gets worse: If it happens every quarter, you're at Nightmare.

Nightmare

The Slogan Storm.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • A new priority every quarter.
  • Roadmaps rewritten each time.
  • Little gets finished.

Do

  1. Ask the What-Changed Question.
  2. Find the Shelter Work.
  3. Break work into small finishes.
  4. Show leaders what's been finished.

Don’t: The instant rebuild.

If it gets worse: If nobody knows what matters at all, you're at Hell.

Hell

The Chaos Storm.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Conflicting priorities at once.
  • Nobody can say what matters most.
  • Your team has stopped believing.

Do

  1. Ask your manager for the top three, in writing.
  2. Finish small things anyway.
  3. Protect your team from whiplash.
  4. Tell leaders kindly what it's costing.

Don’t: The team whiplash.

If it gets worse: If it never settles, ask whether this is a place you can do good work. Read Act VIII.

VIIIAre you the wind?

If you lead anything. Tick honestly.

  • You've passed a new priority to your team without translating it.
  • Your team has rebuilt its plan more than twice this year.
  • You can't name what your team finished last quarter.
  • You've changed direction because of a slide, not a fact.
  • Your team has stopped asking about long-term plans.

The cure, if you ticked two: Before passing on the next change, ask: “What actually changes for us?” Pass on that, and nothing else.

IXThe rehearsal

The What-Changed Question.

Scene: Tuesday 7 January 2030, Pip's 1:1 with Ruth.

PipAI First. That's our third roadmap in six months. Rafi asked if there's any point finishing anything.

RuthFair question. What do you suggest?

PipLet's go through our seven projects. Which actually stop under AI First?

Ruth…Only the satisfaction survey redesign, I think.

PipSo six carry on. And the churn model matters under all three strategies. Can we finish it, whatever?

RuthYes. And from now on I'll translate the slogans before they reach you.

— the AnnotatorThe churn model shipped on 21 February. It's the only piece of work cited in all three quarters' strategy reports.

XThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

The strategy changed again. What do you do first?

Ask the What-Changed Question. Then protect the Shelter Work.

Your team has stopped believing in plans. What can you do?

Finish small things. Belief comes back with finished work.

Is it ever right to pivot every quarter?

Rarely. If you can, say gently to your leaders what it's costing, with examples.

The Annotator's last wordFind the work that matters in every wind. Finish it. Storms can't move finished things.

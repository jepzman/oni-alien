The Hot-Desk Hunger Games

Two hundred tributes, a hundred and forty desks, and a booking app that opens at midnight on Sunday.

Threat
Habitat
booking apps, clear-desk policies, lockers that don't lock, the last monitor that works
Weakness
team neighbourhoods, a portable kit, and a calm word with facilities

IA sighting

September 2029 · floor 14

Floor 22 closed. Customer Insight moved to floor 14: two hundred people, a hundred and forty desks, one booking app called DeskIt, which released the next week's desks at 00:00 on Sunday. Lockers were provided. About a third of them locked.

Tuesday 18 September · the arena

By 09:00 every window desk had a jumper on it. By 10:00 Pip's team was scattered across three zones and a booth. Rafi, who has a bad back, had booked the one sit-stand desk at 00:01; someone without a booking was sitting at it. Pip worked from a sofa with his laptop on a cushion.

Wednesday 19 September · the Neighbourhood Claim

Pip emailed Femi Adeyemi in facilities, politely: the team was in on Tuesdays and Thursdays; could they have a block of eight desks on those days? Femi replied within an hour: yes, and why hadn't anyone asked before? Neighbourhoods existed; they just weren't advertised.

October · the kit and the right

Pip assembled a Portable Kit in a tote bag: charger, mouse, headset, the good pen, a photo of his dog. Rafi asked for a reasonable adjustment for his back and got a sit-stand desk booked permanently for his office days. The jumpers continued elsewhere. Femi put up a cheerful sign. It didn't help.

— the AnnotatorA desk is not a home. Carry your home in a bag, and ask for a neighbourhood.

IIOrigin

flashbackHow the Hunger Games began

  1. 2019 · assigned desks

    Everyone has a desk, a drawer and a plant. Utilisation, measured once, is 54%.

  2. 2021 · the closure plan

    With hybrid work, property teams see empty desks. Hot-desking is planned at a ratio of 7 desks per 10 people.

  3. 2029 · the squeeze

    Floor 22 closes just as the three-day mandate starts. Tuesdays and Thursdays are now at 115% of capacity.

  4. The arena

    Nobody planned for everyone choosing the same days. The app opening at midnight makes it a contest.

The turning point. Two decisions (fewer floors, more office days) made by different people in the same year.

What might have saved them. Facilities and the mandate team talking to each other, and team neighbourhoods offered from day one.

— the AnnotatorHot-desking works when there are enough desks on busy days. When there aren't, it's a game. Ask the people running it to change the rules.

IIIKnow thy enemy

Hot-desking means nobody has an assigned desk; you book one each day. It saves space when attendance is spread out. When everyone comes in on the same days, it turns into a competition: early bookings, reserved-by-jumper desks, scattered teams and the daily hunt for a working monitor.

What it wants

Fewer desks, used more. It's a property decision, not a personal one.

Why it hurts

Because it removes small comforts (a place, your things, your team nearby) without anyone deciding that's the goal.

What helps

  • Team neighbourhoods.
  • A portable kit.
  • Reasonable adjustments.
  • Telling facilities what's broken.

Three kinds of arena

The Spacious Arena

Enough desks. Mildly annoying. Pack a kit.

The Crowded Arena

Too few desks on busy days. Ask for neighbourhoods and staggered days.

The Broken Arena

No desks, broken kit, no lockers. Escalate, with numbers.

— the AnnotatorIf you need a specific setup for a health condition or disability, ask for an adjustment formally. It's not a favour.

IVSigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • The booking app opens at an odd hour.
  • People leave jumpers on desks to “reserve” them.
  • Your team is scattered across three floors.
  • There are fewer desks than people on Tuesdays.

VCounter-spells

  1. 1The Neighbourhood Claim

    Ask facilities for a team zone on your overlap days. It's usually allowed, just never offered.

    incantation“We're all in on Tuesdays and Thursdays. Could we have a block of eight desks near the window on those days?”

  2. 2The Portable Kit

    Keep everything you need in one bag: charger, mouse, headset, the good pen. Be at home anywhere.

  3. 3The Access Rite

    If you need a particular setup for health reasons, ask for a reasonable adjustment. That's not a perk; it's a right in many places.

VIForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Jumper Claim

    Leaving a jumper on a desk all day to hold it. Everybody knows. Everybody minds.

  • The Midnight Grab

    Booking the best desks at 00:00 for people who might come in. It's a desk, not a festival ticket.

  • The Silent Suffering

    Working at a broken desk for months instead of asking for help. Facilities often don't know.

VIIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

The Spacious Arena.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Enough desks most days.
  • Some kit issues.
  • Teams can sit together if they try.

Do

  1. Build a Portable Kit.
  2. Book with your team.
  3. Report broken kit.

Don’t: The jumper claim.

If it gets worse: If there aren't enough desks on your days, you're at Nightmare.

Nightmare

The Crowded Arena.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Booking at midnight.
  • Your team is scattered.
  • People sit on sofas.

Do

  1. Make the Neighbourhood Claim.
  2. Suggest staggered team days to facilities.
  3. Count the problem, kindly, with dates.
  4. Ask for adjustments where needed.

Don’t: The midnight grab.

If it gets worse: If people can't work at all, you're at Hell.

Hell

The Broken Arena.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Not enough desks for the mandate.
  • Kit is broken.
  • Nothing changes when you report it.

Do

  1. Raise it with your manager, with numbers.
  2. Ask whether the mandate can flex.
  3. Use health and safety routes if needed.
  4. Keep your outcomes visible either way.

Don’t: The silent suffering.

If it gets worse: If the office makes your work impossible, make the Honest Ask (see the Decree).

VIIIAre you a Hunger Games tribute?

Tick honestly.

  • You've left a jumper on a desk to hold it.
  • You've booked desks for people who might come in.
  • You've sat at someone's booked desk.
  • You've never asked facilities for anything.
  • You complain about hot-desking but never say what would help.

The cure, if you ticked two: Ask facilities one specific thing this month: a neighbourhood, a fixed monitor, a locker that locks. You may be the first to ask.

IXThe rehearsal

The Neighbourhood Claim.

Scene: Wednesday 19 September 2029, Pip emails Femi in facilities.

PipHi Femi, Customer Insight are all in on Tuesdays and Thursdays. Could we have a block of eight desks on those days?

FemiYes. We've got neighbourhoods. Nobody asks for them.

PipBrilliant. And Rafi needs a sit-stand desk for his back. How does he ask properly?

FemiReasonable adjustment form; I'll fast-track it. Anything else?

PipSome lockers don't lock.

FemiI know. Two-thirds of them. I've asked for a budget since June.

— the AnnotatorThe lockers were fixed in February 2030. Femi sent the team a photo of the receipt.

XThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

Your team is scattered across floors. What do you do?

Ask facilities for a neighbourhood on your overlap days.

You need a specific chair for your back. Is it fair to ask?

Yes. Ask for a reasonable adjustment through the proper route.

Someone always takes the window desk with a jumper. What do you do?

Mention the booking system, cheerfully, once. Then let facilities handle it.

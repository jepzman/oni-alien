The Art of Plausible Silence

Sometimes the most powerful thing you can say is nothing. For now.

Threat
Habitat
heated meetings, the after-meeting drink, a VP's leading question
Weakness
the pause, held calmly

IA sighting

At the budget review, Lord Varnish asked the room: “Which team could give up 10% of its budget?” Gareth said nothing. Priya said nothing. Pip, eager, said: “Grid Planning could probably find some savings in the—”

By Friday, Grid Planning's budget was 12% smaller. Marcus found out from Finance. He was very kind about it, which was somehow worse.

IIKnow thy enemy

In politics, what you say is on record and what you don't say mostly isn't. Every offhand remark can become a commitment, a quote or a weapon. Experienced people pause, ask questions and say “let me come back to you” far more than juniors do. It isn't evasion. It's thinking before committing.

Silence is not lying, and it's not cowardice. If you know something serious (harm, fraud, danger), silence is wrong. But for most questions, a short pause and a considered answer beats a fast, regrettable one.

— the AnnotatorJuniors fill silences. Seniors let them sit. The person who speaks first in an awkward silence usually just volunteered for something.

IIISigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • A senior person asks an open question to the room.
  • You feel an urge to fill an awkward pause.
  • “Off the record…” “Just between us…”
  • A question that would commit your team or your manager.

IVCounter-spells

  1. 1The Held Pause

    Count to three before answering a loaded question. Often someone else answers, or the question changes.

  2. 2The Time Charm

    Buy time without looking evasive: promise a considered answer by a specific time.

    incantation“Good question. I'd rather give you a proper answer than a quick one. Can I come back by Thursday?”

  3. 3The Manager's Seal

    Don't commit your team or your manager's resources without them. Say so plainly.

    incantation“That's Marcus's call. I'll make sure he has the numbers today.”

  4. 4The Question Back

    Answer a vague question with a clarifying one.

    incantation“When you say savings, do you mean this year's budget or next year's plan?”

VForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Stone Wall

    Saying nothing, ever, about anything. You'll seem evasive or uninterested, and people stop asking you.

  • The Silent Witness

    Staying quiet about real wrongdoing to stay safe. That's not strategy, it's complicity. See Act VIII, the Dark Pact.

  • The Mysterious Hint

    Hinting that you know more than you say, to seem important. People will assume you're the leak.

VIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

An eager habit of answering too fast. Practise the Held Pause for a month.

Nightmare

Leadership fishes for commitments in public. Use the Time Charm and the Manager's Seal every time.

Hell

Anything you say is used against you. Say less, write carefully, and save your voice for the things that truly matter.

⚿Five Ways to Say Nothing (Politely)

For when the honest answer is “not yet”.

1. "Good question. Let me come back to you by Thursday."
2. "That's Marcus's call. I'll get him the numbers today."
3. "When you say X, do you mean A or B?"
4. "I don't know yet, and I'd rather not guess."
5. "Can I think about that and send you something short?"

IXThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

A VP asks the room which team could cut 10%. What do you say?

Nothing, or a clarifying question. Never volunteer your team's budget without your manager.

Someone says “off the record, what do you think of your manager?” What now?

Nothing off the record exists. Say something true and fair you'd be happy to see quoted.

When is silence wrong?

When you know about serious harm, fraud or danger. Then speaking up (carefully, in the right channel) is the duty.

The Annotator's last wordSilence can't be quoted. Use that, but never to hide harm.

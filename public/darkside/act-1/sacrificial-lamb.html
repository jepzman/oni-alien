The Sacrificial Lamb

“A great opportunity for you.” (It is doomed. You are the opportunity.)

Threat
Habitat
failing projects, stretch assignments offered with unusual warmth
Weakness
questions asked before you say yes, and agreements in writing

IA sighting

Monday 17 November · 15:00 · floor 27

Lord Varnish called Pip to floor 27 for the first time. There was coffee. There was warmth. There was a chair that was more comfortable than Pip's own.

15:05 · the offer

There was Project Orion: a new billing integration, eighteen months late, £2.4 million over budget, already on its third project lead. “It needs fresh eyes,” said Lord Varnish. “Real visibility for you. A great opportunity.”

15:20

Tamsin walked past the glass door with a folder and met Pip's eye for exactly one second. In that second, Pip learned more than in the whole meeting.

15:45 · the corridor

Pip looked up Orion's previous leads on the intranet. The first had been “moved to a strategic role”. The second was on long-term sick leave. The third had “left to pursue other opportunities”. Their photos were still on the project page, smiling, like a memorial.

— the AnnotatorIf you're offered a project with unusual warmth, read the project's obituary first. Ask who led it before you, and where they are now.

IIOrigin

flashbackHow Project Orion became an altar

  1. January 2024 · the promise

    To impress Dame Helena, Lord Varnish launches Orion: a new billing system in twelve months, for £3 million. Engineers say 24 months and £5 million. The engineers aren't in the room.

  2. 2024 · the first lead

    Lead #1 reports that the plan is impossible. She is moved to a “strategic role” within a month.

  3. 2025 · leads two and three

    Lead #2 tries to deliver the impossible and burns out. Lead #3 resets the plan honestly, which makes Lord Varnish look bad; he “leaves to pursue other opportunities”.

  4. November 2026 · the fourth lamb

    Orion now needs someone junior, eager and new enough not to know the history. Someone who'll carry the old date and the old budget, and the blame. Someone like Pip.

The turning point. A promise made to impress a CEO, with the engineers out of the room, which nobody senior was ever willing to take back.

What might have saved them. Lord Varnish resetting the plan himself in 2024. One honest re-baseline would have saved three careers.

— the AnnotatorSacrifices happen when a senior person can't admit a mistake. The cure is a reset in writing, with their name on it, not yours.

IIIKnow thy enemy

When a project is failing, leaders need someone to own it, and sometimes to be blamed for it. The task goes to someone junior and eager, who can't refuse and won't know the history. Not every hard assignment is a trap. The difference is in the terms: authority, resources, a reset plan, and a sponsor who'll stand next to you.

What the priests want

Someone else's name on the failure, or a miracle. Ideally both: if it succeeds, it was their vision.

Why it thrives here

Because admitting a project's original plan was wrong would embarrass its sponsor. It's cheaper, for them, to replace the lead than the plan.

What protects the lamb

  • Asking for time to understand before saying yes.
  • Talking to the previous leads.
  • A published baseline on day one.
  • Terms in writing, with the sponsor's name on them.

Three kinds of offer

The Rescue

A hard project with a real sponsor. A genuine career chance, on the right terms.

The Scapegoat

A doomed project needing someone to blame. Decline, or demand written terms.

The Holding Pattern

Nobody expects success; they need a name on it until it's cancelled. Know which it is.

— the AnnotatorBefore you pick up the knife, check whose neck the ceremony is for.

IVSigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • Unusual warmth from someone senior who's never spoken to you.
  • “Fresh eyes”, “real visibility”, “a great opportunity for you”.
  • Previous leads have left, been moved, or gone quiet.
  • No one can tell you the real budget or the real date.
  • The sponsor's name is nowhere on the project.

VCounter-spells

  1. 1The Autopsy First

    Before you answer, learn the history: previous leads, real status, why it failed. Ask them.

    incantation“I'm honoured. Before I commit, could I spend a few days understanding where it stands? I want to do it properly.”

  2. 2The Terms of Sacrifice

    Agree, in writing, what you need: a reset of date and budget, decision rights, people, and a named sponsor.

    incantation“I'll take it on with a reset plan by the 15th, authority over scope, two engineers, and you as visible sponsor. Can we agree that in writing?”

  3. 3The Honest Baseline

    On day one, publish the true state of the project. Anything that was broken before you is now on record as before you.

    incantation“Status on taking over: 18 months late, £2.4m over, three open blockers. Recovery plan to follow in two weeks.”

  4. 4The Graceful No

    If the terms aren't given, decline politely, with reasons.

    incantation“I'd love to help, but without a reset of date and budget I couldn't succeed, and I don't want to fail you.”

VIForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Eager Yes

    Saying yes on the spot, flattered. You've just signed up for the old date, the old budget and all the old blame.

  • The Silent Takeover

    Taking over without publishing the baseline. In six months, the delay will be yours.

  • The Blame Return

    Spending your time blaming previous leads in public. True or not, it looks like excuse-making.

VIIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

The Rescue. A hard project with a real sponsor.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • The sponsor will put terms in writing.
  • Previous leads left for normal reasons.
  • There's budget for a reset.

Do

  1. Ask for a few days to understand it.
  2. Agree the Terms of Sacrifice in writing.
  3. Publish the honest baseline on day one.

Don’t: The eager yes in the room.

If it gets worse: If the sponsor is vague and terms are dodged, you're at Nightmare.

Nightmare

A failing project with a vague sponsor.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Nobody can tell you the real date or budget.
  • The sponsor says “let's not get bogged down in details”.
  • Previous leads won't talk about it.

Do

  1. Talk to the previous leads before deciding.
  2. Insist on the baseline and terms in writing.
  3. Report honestly every fortnight.
  4. Copy your own manager on everything.

Don’t: The silent takeover: taking over without publishing the baseline. In six months the delay will be yours.

If it gets worse: If everyone knows it's a sacrifice but you, you're at Hell.

Hell

The Scapegoat. A political sacrifice.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Three leads have already gone.
  • The sponsor won't put anything in writing.
  • People look at you with pity.

Do

  1. Decline gracefully, with reasons.
  2. Or accept only with written terms and a sponsor who has something to lose.
  3. Keep a private, dated record.
  4. Line up allies before you start.

Don’t: The blame return: spending your time blaming previous leads in public.

If it gets worse: If you accepted and the terms are broken, raise it immediately, in writing, to your manager and the sponsor.

VIIIHave you ever lit the candles?

If you lead anything, you may have offered a lamb. Tick honestly.

  • You've handed a failing project to someone junior with “great opportunity”.
  • You've kept a plan you knew was wrong to avoid admitting it.
  • You've replaced a person instead of a plan.
  • You've praised someone's “fresh eyes” on something you'd given up on.
  • You weren't in the room when the original promise was made, but you've defended it.

The cure, if you ticked two: If a plan is wrong, reset it yourself, publicly, with your name on the reset. It costs a bit of face. It saves people.

IXThe rehearsal

The Autopsy First and the Terms of Sacrifice: grateful, calm, and never yes in the room.

Scene: Monday 17 November, 15:10. Floor 27. Lord Varnish has just made the offer.

Lord VarnishSo, Orion. Real visibility. What do you say?

PipI'm honoured, genuinely. Before I commit, could I spend a few days understanding where it stands? I want to do it properly.

Lord VarnishOf course, of course. But we need someone by the end of the month.

PipUnderstood. If I take it on, I'd need a reset plan by mid-December for your sign-off, authority over scope, two engineers, and you as visible sponsor at the steering group. Could we agree that in writing?

Lord Varnish…Let's discuss the details next week.

Pip spends Thursday with Orion's three previous leads. Friday, he sends the Terms of Sacrifice email. It is never answered. Pip declines, politely, with reasons. In January, Orion is quietly cancelled.

— the AnnotatorThe unanswered email was the answer. Pip learned what he needed without becoming the fourth photo on the memorial page.

XThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

A VP offers you a failing project with great warmth. What do you say in the meeting?

Thank them and ask for a few days to understand it: the Autopsy First. Never say yes in the room.

You agree to take it on. What's the first thing you publish?

The Honest Baseline: the true state on the day you took over.

The VP won't put the terms in writing. What does that tell you?

That they want the option to blame you. Decline gracefully, or proceed knowing exactly what you're risking.

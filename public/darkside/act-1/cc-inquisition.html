The CC Inquisition

Every name on the CC line is a witness. Or a threat.

Threat
Habitat
email, especially Friday at 16:58
Weakness
the phone call, and the courage to take people off the thread

IA sighting

Pip asked Gareth's team a simple question about asset data. Gareth replied, copying Pip's manager, his own director and Lord Varnish: “Looping in leadership for visibility, as this seems to be a recurring misunderstanding.” It was not recurring. It was the first time.

Pip replied to all, explaining at length. Gareth replied to all. By Friday there were fourteen people on a thread about one spreadsheet column, and Lord Varnish had replied with a single line: “Can we take this offline?” which, in MetroVolt, means “you have both embarrassed me”.

IIKnow thy enemy

The CC line is power made visible. Adding someone's boss turns a question into an escalation. Adding your own boss says “I have a witness”. BCC says “I'm showing someone this behind your back”. Reply-all on a dispute turns a disagreement into theatre.

Most CC escalations come from anxiety, not malice: people want cover. The counter is to lower the temperature: take it off the thread, solve it, then report the solution to the same audience.

— the AnnotatorBCC is the single most dangerous button in Outlook. Someone always replies-all from the BCC line. Always.

IIISigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • Your boss appears on a thread you started.
  • “For visibility.” “Looping in.” “Adding X for awareness.”
  • Long replies-to-all on a disagreement.
  • You're BCC'd on something. (Don't reply.)

IVCounter-spells

  1. 1The Phone Exorcism

    When a thread heats up, call or walk over. Solve it in person.

    incantation“Gareth, rather than fill everyone's inbox, can I call you for five minutes? I think we can sort this quickly.”

  2. 2The Clean Report

    After solving it, reply once to the full audience with the outcome, crediting the other person.

    incantation“Quick update for everyone: Gareth and I spoke, the column means X, all sorted. Thanks Gareth.”

  3. 3The Gentle Uncopy

    When you start a thread, copy only who needs to act. Move escalations to a new, smaller thread.

VForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Counter-Escalation

    Copying even more senior people back. Now it's a war with an audience. Everyone loses, especially the audience.

  • The BCC Snare

    Secretly BCC'ing your boss to show them someone's bad behaviour. One accidental reply-all, and you're the snake.

  • The Essay of Righteousness

    A long reply-all proving you were right. Nobody reads past line three. Everyone remembers the length.

VIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

An anxious colleague copying their boss for cover. Call them, solve it, report it.

Nightmare

CC escalation is the house style. Keep threads small, solve things by phone, and agree with your manager how to handle it.

Hell

Someone uses CC lines to build a case against you. Stay factual, keep copies, never match the tone, and brief your manager before the threads reach them.

VIIILoot drop

⚿The De-Escalation Email

Use after a call. Same audience as the angry thread. Short, factual, generous.

Subject: RE: Asset data question (resolved)

Hi all,

Gareth and I spoke this morning. Summary:
  • "In service date" = commissioning date, not install date
  • I'll update the model by Thursday
  • No other changes needed

Thanks Gareth for the quick help.
Pip

IXThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

Someone copies your manager and theirs on a small issue. What's your first move?

Call them. Solve it off the thread. Then send one short, generous resolution to the same audience.

You're BCC'd on a dispute between two colleagues. What do you do?

Nothing on the thread. If it matters, talk to the person who BCC'd you, in person.

Should you ever copy your own manager?

Yes, when they need to act or decide, and openly. Not as a shield.

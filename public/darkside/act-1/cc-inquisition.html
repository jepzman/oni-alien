The CC Inquisition

Every name on the CC line is a witness. Or a threat.

Threat
Habitat
email, especially Friday at 16:58
Weakness
the phone call, and the courage to take people off the thread

IA sighting

Monday 27 October · 10:02 · a simple question

Pip asked Gareth's team a question about one column in the asset data: did “in service date” mean install date or commissioning date?

10:41 · the reply

Gareth replied, copying Pip's manager, his own director and Lord Varnish: “Looping in leadership for visibility, as this seems to be a recurring misunderstanding.” It was not recurring. It was the first time.

Monday to Thursday · the thread

Pip replied to all, explaining at length. Gareth replied to all, at greater length. Others joined “for awareness”. By Thursday there were fourteen people on a thread about one spreadsheet column.

Friday 31 October · 16:58

Lord Varnish replied with a single line: “Can we take this offline?” which, at MetroVolt, means “you have both embarrassed me”.

— the AnnotatorBCC is the single most dangerous button in Outlook. Someone always replies-all from the BCC line. Always.

IIOrigin

flashbackHow Gareth learned to copy everyone

  1. 2018 · the warning

    Gareth, then an asset engineer, spots a failing cable joint. He emails one person in Network Operations. That person is on leave. The email sits unread.

  2. 2018 · the outage

    The joint fails. Twelve thousand homes lose power for a night. The review asks why nobody warned anyone. Gareth's email is found, but only one person had received it, so the review says the warning was “not escalated appropriately”.

  3. 2019 onwards · the armour

    Gareth never sends an important email to one person again. He copies managers, directors, anyone who might later ask “why wasn't I told?” It's armour, not aggression.

  4. 2025 · the habit

    The armour is now on every email, even about spreadsheet columns. To others it looks like escalation. To Gareth it looks like safety.

The turning point. An outage where a warning existed but only one person had seen it, and the blame landed on the person who wrote it.

What might have saved them. A shared risk log in 2018, so warnings went somewhere visible instead of into one inbox.

— the AnnotatorMost compulsive CC-ers were burned once by a warning nobody saw. Give them a shared place to put things, and the CC line often shrinks.

IIIKnow thy enemy

The CC line is power made visible. Adding someone's boss turns a question into an escalation. Adding your own boss says “I have a witness”. BCC says “I'm showing someone this behind your back”. Reply-all on a dispute turns a disagreement into theatre.

What it wants

Cover. Most CC escalations come from anxiety, not malice: people want proof that they told someone.

Why it thrives here

Because MetroVolt reviews ask “who was told?” after every incident, so people over-tell. And because copying a manager costs one click, while walking over costs five minutes and some courage.

What it fears

  • A phone call.
  • A short, generous resolution email to the same audience.
  • Shared logs that make CC armour unnecessary.
  • Being asked, kindly, “shall we sort this between us?”

Three kinds of CC

The Armourer

Copies people for cover. Calm down the thread; give them a log.

The Escalator

Copies your boss to pressure you. Call them; solve it.

The Audience Builder

Copies everyone to perform. Take the show off the stage.

— the AnnotatorFourteen people on a thread is not a discussion. It's a crowd watching a fight.

IVSigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • Your boss appears on a thread you started.
  • “For visibility.” “Looping in.” “Adding X for awareness.”
  • Long replies-to-all on a disagreement.
  • You're BCC'd on something. (Don't reply.)

VCounter-spells

  1. 1The Phone Exorcism

    When a thread heats up, call or walk over. Solve it in person.

    incantation“Gareth, rather than fill everyone's inbox, can I call you for five minutes? I think we can sort this quickly.”

  2. 2The Clean Report

    After solving it, reply once to the full audience with the outcome, crediting the other person.

    incantation“Quick update for everyone: Gareth and I spoke, the column means X, all sorted. Thanks Gareth.”

  3. 3The Gentle Uncopy

    When you start a thread, copy only who needs to act. Move escalations to a new, smaller thread.

VIForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Counter-Escalation

    Copying even more senior people back. Now it's a war with an audience. Everyone loses, especially the audience.

  • The BCC Snare

    Secretly BCC'ing your boss to show them someone's bad behaviour. One accidental reply-all, and you're the snake.

  • The Essay of Righteousness

    A long reply-all proving you were right. Nobody reads past line three. Everyone remembers the length.

VIIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

The Armourer. An anxious colleague copying managers for cover.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • It's their habit with everyone.
  • The tone is defensive, not aggressive.
  • They're friendly on the phone.

Do

  1. Call them and solve it.
  2. Send one short resolution email to the same audience, crediting them.
  3. Copy only who needs to act on your own emails.

Don’t: The essay of righteousness: a long reply-all proving you were right.

If it gets worse: If CC escalation is the house style, you're at Nightmare.

Nightmare

The Escalator. CC wars are how teams fight.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Every disagreement gets managers copied.
  • Threads reach ten people regularly.
  • Your manager is tired of being copied.

Do

  1. Move disputes off email within one reply.
  2. Start new, smaller threads for escalations.
  3. Agree with your manager how you'll handle CC'd disputes.
  4. Propose a shared log for warnings and decisions.

Don’t: The counter-escalation: copying even more senior people back.

If it gets worse: If someone is building a case against you in CC lines, you're at Hell.

Hell

Someone using CC lines to build a case against you.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Your mistakes are copied to senior people.
  • Your good work isn't.
  • HR appears on threads.

Do

  1. Stay factual, short and calm on every reply.
  2. Keep copies.
  3. Brief your manager before the threads reach them.
  4. If it's targeted, raise it formally.

Don’t: The BCC snare: secretly showing your boss their behaviour. One reply-all and you're the snake.

If it gets worse: If it continues, treat it as a conduct issue and take it to HR with your copies.

VIIIAre you an Inquisitor?

Check your sent items. Tick honestly.

  • You've copied someone's manager on a first, small issue.
  • You've used “for visibility” or “looping in” this month.
  • You've used BCC to show someone a conversation.
  • You've replied-all to a disagreement.
  • Your average email has more than five people on it.

The cure, if you ticked two: On your next five emails, remove everyone who doesn't need to act. And when something heats up, pick up the phone before you pick up the CC line.

IXThe rehearsal

The Phone Exorcism, then the Clean Report.

Scene: Monday 27 October, 10:45. Pip calls instead of replying.

PipGareth, it's Pip. Rather than fill everyone's inbox, can we sort the asset question in five minutes?

Gareth…Sure. Look, it's commissioning date. We've had people get it wrong before and it caused problems.

PipMakes sense. Thanks, that's all I needed. I'll send a quick note to everyone you copied so they know it's sorted.

GarethFine. Good.

10:52. To all fourteen: “Gareth and I spoke. ‘In service date’ is the commissioning date. I'll update the model by Thursday. Thanks Gareth for the quick help.”

Lord Varnish replies with a thumbs-up. It is the shortest thread in MetroVolt history.

— the AnnotatorThe resolution went to the same audience as the escalation. That's what turns a public fight into a public fix.

XThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

Someone copies your manager and theirs on a small issue. What's your first move?

Call them. Solve it off the thread. Then send one short, generous resolution to the same audience.

You're BCC'd on a dispute between two colleagues. What do you do?

Nothing on the thread. If it matters, talk to the person who BCC'd you, in person.

Should you ever copy your own manager?

Yes, when they need to act or decide, and openly. Not as a shield.

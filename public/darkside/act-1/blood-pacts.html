Blood Pacts

Alliances are made in peacetime. In war, it's too late.

Threat
Habitat
lunches, cross-team projects, the favour nobody asked for
Weakness
generosity, given early and kept up

IA sighting

Tuesday 30 September · the rumour

Word spread that a reorg list was being drawn up. Pip realised, with a slow chill, that every useful person he knew in MetroVolt was in Grid Planning. Outside his own team he knew the barista, the IT helpdesk queue and Dorian (unfortunately).

Thursday 2 October · the comparison

Jules, meanwhile, had lunch with someone new every Thursday for two years. Field Ops, Finance, Customer Ops, even Facilities. Jules had helped Priya's team with a dashboard, helped Felicity with a booking report, and fixed Tamsin's spreadsheet macro once, at 17:55 on a Friday.

Friday 10 October · the leaked list

When the rumoured reorg list leaked, three different directors had already asked for Jules by name. Nobody had asked for Pip. Nobody outside Grid Planning knew what Pip did.

That evening

Pip asked Jules how. Jules shrugged. “I helped people. Then they remembered.”

— the AnnotatorNetworking is a horrible word for a lovely thing: being useful to people you like, before you need anything.

IIOrigin

flashbackHow Jules learned to dig the well

  1. 2021 · Jules's first job

    Jules works at a small energy start-up, heads down, brilliant, known only to their own team.

  2. 2022 · the layoff

    The start-up runs out of money. Jules is laid off on a Tuesday with no warning and no network. Three months of job applications go nowhere.

  3. 2022 · two phone calls

    Jules finally gets a job because two people they'd once helped (a former client, a peer from a course) put their name forward. Nobody else did.

  4. 2022 onwards · the vow

    Jules joins MetroVolt and decides never to be invisible again. One lunch a week. One favour a week. Asking nothing. It isn't strategy any more. It's just who they are.

The turning point. Three months of silence after a layoff, broken only by the two people Jules had helped without being asked.

What might have saved them. Nothing needed saving. This is the origin of a good habit, the opposite of a curse. Pip is the one who needs one.

— the AnnotatorMost people with great networks learned it the hard way. You can learn it the easy way: start this Thursday.

IIIKnow thy enemy

Alliances at work are built on reciprocity: people help those who helped them. But the help has to come first and without strings, or it feels like a transaction. The best allies are outside your own team: they see different information and can speak for you in rooms you'll never enter.

What allies want

Someone reliable, useful and pleasant, who doesn't only appear when they need something.

Why it matters here

Because at MetroVolt, reorgs, projects and promotions are decided by people who mostly know you second-hand. Your name needs to come up in rooms you're not in, said by people who've seen you be helpful.

What breaks pacts

  • Keeping score out loud.
  • Disappearing when an ally is out of favour.
  • Broken promises, even small ones.
  • Asking for big favours before giving small ones.

Three kinds of ally

The Weak Tie

Someone you know slightly. Surprisingly powerful: they hear things you don't.

The Ally

Someone who'd help you and whom you'd help. Five to ten is plenty.

The Sponsor

Someone senior who says your name in rooms you're not in. The rarest. Earned by results.

— the AnnotatorDig the well before you're thirsty. Nobody lends a bucket during a drought.

IVSigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • Everyone you know at work is in your own team.
  • You only contact people when you need something.
  • You learn about reorgs from the announcement email.
  • Nobody outside your team would recognise your name.

VCounter-spells

  1. 1The First Gift

    Help someone in another team with something small and useful. Ask nothing back.

    incantation“I saw your team's struggling with the outage data. I've got a cleaned version. Want it?”

  2. 2The Thursday Rite

    One coffee or lunch a week with someone outside your team. Ask what they're working on and what's hard.

    incantation“I'm trying to understand how Customer Ops works. Could I buy you a coffee and hear what drives you mad?”

  3. 3The Kept Word

    Do exactly what you said, by when you said. Trust compounds faster than anything else.

VIForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Favour Ledger

    Keeping score out loud. “After everything I did for you…” turns an ally into a debtor, and debtors avoid you.

  • The Fair-Weather Pact

    Dropping allies when they're out of favour. The building notices, and you'll be next.

  • The Cabal

    Forming a secret group to control decisions. Cabals get found out. They always get found out.

VIIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

You're new or quiet, and the organisation is friendly.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • People are open to coffee.
  • Cross-team work exists.
  • You simply haven't started.

Do

  1. One coffee a week with someone outside your team.
  2. Make one small, useful offer a week.
  3. Keep every small promise.

Don’t: Networking only when you need something. People notice the timing.

If it gets worse: If teams are siloed and suspicious, you're at Nightmare.

Nightmare

A siloed organisation where teams compete.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Teams guard their data.
  • Cross-team help is rare.
  • People are suspicious of offers.

Do

  1. Start with shared problems (data, outages, customers), not friendship.
  2. Help with something concrete and small.
  3. Credit other teams publicly.
  4. Keep an Ally Garden list and water it monthly.

Don’t: Forming a cabal. Secret groups get found out.

If it gets worse: If any ally can become a liability overnight, you're at Hell.

Hell

A blame culture where alliances are risky.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • People are punished for who they're seen with.
  • Favour shifts suddenly.
  • Allies get dropped when they fall.

Do

  1. Build many light alliances rather than a few deep ones.
  2. Keep your own record clean.
  3. Help in the open.
  4. Stay loyal to people, quietly, when they fall.

Don’t: The fair-weather pact. If you drop allies when they're out of favour, you're next.

If it gets worse: If you can't build trust anywhere here, build it outside, for your next job.

VIIIIs your well dry?

Most people only find out when they're thirsty. Tick honestly.

  • Everyone you know well at work is in your own team.
  • You last helped someone in another team months ago.
  • You only contact people when you need something.
  • Nobody outside your team would recognise your name.
  • You learned about the last reorg from the announcement email.

The cure, if you ticked two: Pick one person outside your team this week. Find out what's hard for them. Help with one small thing. Ask for nothing. Repeat next week.

IXThe rehearsal

The Thursday Rite and the First Gift: curious, useful, asking nothing.

Scene: Thursday 16 October, 12:30, the canteen. Pip asks Priya to lunch for the first time.

PipThanks for coming. I realised I don't really know how Customer Ops works. What drives you mad?

PriyaEstimated bills. We get 2,000 complaints a month and I can't tell which meters are actually broken.

PipOh, I have a cleaned list of non-reporting meters. I made it for the forecast. Want it?

PriyaYou have what?

PipI'll send it this afternoon. No strings. Happy to update it weekly if it helps.

Three months later, in Act III, Priya stops Pip in a lift with a job offer. She remembers who sent the list.

— the AnnotatorPip didn't plan the job offer. He just helped first. That's the only way blood pacts work.

XThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

Rumours of a reorg start tomorrow. Is now the time to network?

It's late, but not too late. Help first, ask nothing. In panic season, the person who gives instead of asks stands out.

A colleague helped you once. They now ask for a big favour you can't do. What do you say?

Be honest and offer what you can: “I can't do the whole thing, but I can do the data part by Friday.”

How many allies do you need?

Enough that your name comes up in rooms you're not in. For most people, five to ten real relationships outside their team.

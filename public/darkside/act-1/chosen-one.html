The Chosen One

Every court has a favourite. It is rarely you.

Threat
Habitat
talent reviews, stretch assignments, the VP's “quick coffee”
Weakness
your own visible results, and a sponsor of your own

IA sighting

Jules got the Lumen board presentation. Then the trip to the regulator. Then the “high potential” programme, which nobody had told Pip existed. Every time Lord Varnish needed someone, he said “ask Jules”.

The worst part was that Jules was good. Kind, fast, clear. Pip couldn't even hate them properly. He tried once, for an afternoon, and Jules brought him a coffee and asked how his model was going. It was exhausting.

IIKnow thy enemy

Favouritism is partly human nature: leaders trust people who are like them, who've succeeded before, or who make their lives easier. Once someone is chosen, they get more chances, so they succeed more, so they get chosen more. It compounds.

Sometimes it's unfair. Sometimes it's earned, and simply more visible than your work. Before you decide which, ask honestly: what does the favourite do that you don't? Visibility? Speed? Making the leader's life easier?

— the AnnotatorMost favourites aren't chosen for being brilliant. They're chosen for being reliable in front of the person choosing. You can learn that.

IIISigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • The same name comes up for every opportunity.
  • Programmes and trips you hear about after they're full.
  • Your work is praised; theirs is rewarded.
  • You catch yourself counting their wins.

IVCounter-spells

  1. 1The Honest Mirror

    Ask your manager what the favourite does that you could do more of. Listen without defending.

    incantation“Jules gets a lot of the visible work. What would I need to show for you to put me forward too?”

  2. 2The Direct Ask

    Ask for specific opportunities, by name. Leaders often simply don't think of the quiet ones.

    incantation“I'd like to present the meter data at the next board. Could I take that one?”

  3. 3The Own Sponsor

    Find someone senior who values your work and will put your name forward. Different courts have different favourites.

  4. 4The Fairness Check

    If favouritism crosses into unfair treatment (pay, promotion, protected traits), document it and raise it formally.

VForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Usurper's Plot

    Undermining the favourite to take their place. You'll be seen as bitter, and the leader will protect their choice.

  • The Sulk

    Withdrawing and doing the minimum because it's unfair. It confirms the leader's choice.

  • The Copycat

    Imitating the favourite's style. You'll be a weaker version of someone who's already there.

VIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

A leader who leans on one reliable person. Make yourself visible and ask directly.

Nightmare

A clear inner circle. Find your own sponsor, build visibility outside your manager's view, and keep delivering.

Hell

Favouritism tied to personal relationships, or to things like gender, age or background. Keep records and use formal channels; this is a fairness issue, not a career tip.

⚿The Visibility Ask

A short note to your manager. Specific, positive, about the future.

Hi Marcus,

I'd like to grow my visibility this year. Three things I'd love
to do:
  1. Present the meter-failure data at the Jan board
  2. Join the regulator prep for Lumen
  3. Be considered for the high-potential programme

What would you need to see from me to put me forward?

Pip

IXThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

The favourite gets an opportunity you wanted. What's your first move?

Ask your manager what you'd need to show to be considered next time, and ask for a specific opportunity.

You become the favourite. Is that a problem?

Enjoy it, and share it: bring others into the work and credit them. Courts change; allies stay.

When is favouritism a formal issue?

When it affects pay, promotion or treatment unfairly, especially linked to protected characteristics. Document and raise it.

The Annotator's last wordDon't wait to be chosen. Ask to be considered. It's shocking how often that works.

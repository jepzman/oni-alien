The Ring-Kissing Ritual

Some kneel. Some manage up. Learn the difference before your knees go.

Threat
Habitat
leadership meetings, LinkedIn comments under the CEO's posts, the lift with Lord Varnish
Weakness
usefulness: the boss who has a choice between flattery and help picks help, eventually

IA sighting

Every Monday at the leadership stand-up, Dorian opened with praise for Lord Varnish. “Great point in your email.” “Love how you framed that.” “As you said last week…” Lord Varnish glowed. Dorian got the good projects.

Pip tried it once. “Great framing, Lord— er, Simon.” It came out like a man reading a hostage statement. Lord Varnish looked at him with mild alarm. Afterwards Marcus took Pip aside. “Don't do that,” he said gently. “Just be useful to him. It lasts longer.”

IIKnow thy enemy

Flattery works because leaders are human: they're insecure, under pressure, and rarely praised themselves. Someone who makes them feel good gets their attention. That's why the ritual exists.

But flattery has a short shelf life and a long cost. People notice it, peers resent it, and the leader learns nothing true from you. Managing up is different: you understand your boss's goals and pressures, and help them succeed, honestly. That includes telling them things they don't want to hear, kindly.

— the AnnotatorKissing up: making the boss feel good. Managing up: making the boss do well. The first gets you noticed. The second gets you trusted. Trusted lasts.

IIISigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • Every meeting starts with praise for the most senior person.
  • Bad news never reaches the top.
  • The same few people always get the good projects.
  • You feel tempted to say “great point” before they've finished the point.

IVCounter-spells

  1. 1The Goal Reading

    Learn what your boss's boss is measured on and worried about. Then make your work help with that.

    incantation“What's the one thing that would make this quarter a win for you? I'd like my work to help with it.”

  2. 2The Specific Praise

    Praise is fine when it's true and specific. It's flattery when it's vague and constant.

    incantation“The way you reframed Lumen as a customer-billing fix got Finance on board. That was smart.”

  3. 3The Honest Warning

    Bring bad news early, with a suggestion. Leaders remember who warned them.

    incantation“Heads-up before the board: the meter numbers will look bad. Here's why, and here's what we're doing.”

VForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Full Prostration

    Agreeing with everything the boss says. When their idea fails, you'll be the one who said “brilliant”.

  • The Public Groveling

    Liking every one of the CEO's posts within four minutes. Everyone can see it. Everyone.

  • Kissing Up, Kicking Down

    Charming to those above, cruel to those below. The people below talk. Eventually upwards.

VIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

A boss who likes praise but values results. Be specific, be useful, be honest.

Nightmare

Flatterers are winning. Keep delivering, make your results visible, and build a reputation as the person who tells the truth kindly.

Hell

Only loyalty is rewarded, and honesty is punished. Protect yourself, document your work, find a sponsor elsewhere, and consider whether you want to rise in this court.

⚿The Manage-Up Card

Five lines about your boss (and your boss's boss). Update them every quarter.

THEIR TOP GOAL      Lumen rollout on time for the regulator (Mar)
THEIR WORRY         billing complaints reaching the CEO
HOW THEY LIKE NEWS  short, early, with a fix
WHAT I CAN DO       weekly meter-failure numbers before the board
WHAT I MUST NOT DO  surprise them in front of Dame Helena

IXThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

Your peer flatters the VP and gets the best projects. Should you do the same?

No. Learn the VP's goals and make your work help with them. Specific, honest praise when it's earned.

The VP's pet idea has a real flaw. What do you do?

Tell them privately, early, with respect and a suggestion: the Honest Warning.

Is it OK to praise your boss at all?

Yes, when it's true and specific. Leaders get little real feedback; good, honest praise is rare and valued.

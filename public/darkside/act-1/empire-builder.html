The Empire Builder

Collects headcount like dragon gold. Sleeps on it.

Threat
Habitat
budget season, reorg slides, anything with “centre of excellence” in the title
Weakness
questions about outcomes, not size

IA sighting

January 2024 · forty people

Gareth Pound began 2024 with 40 people in Network Assets, one deputy and one slide titled Our Purpose.

Autumn 2026 · 112 people

By autumn he had 112, a “Centre of Excellence for Asset Intelligence”, two deputies and a slide titled Our Expanding Footprint, in which the footprint was drawn as a map with his department's colour spreading across it.

Meanwhile · the data

Asset data quality had not improved. It had, in fact, got worse: there were now three teams cleaning it, each in a different way, and the CEO's dashboard showed three different counts of transformers.

Tuesday 18 November · the proposal

Gareth proposed absorbing Grid Planning's data work. “Synergies,” he explained. Pip asked which problem it would solve. Gareth looked at him as a dragon looks at someone asking why it needs so much gold.

— the AnnotatorA centre of excellence is a team that has not yet been asked what it's excellent at.

IIOrigin

flashbackHow a small team leader became the Empire Builder

  1. 2015 · a team of six

    Gareth leads six asset engineers. They're good. They're also small, and in the 2015 reorg small teams are merged away. Gareth's team survives by one vote.

  2. 2017 · the lesson repeated

    In the layoffs, the departments with the most people lose the smallest share. Size, Gareth notices, is safety.

  3. 2021 · the restructure

    Department heads are given status by headcount and budget. Gareth's title, pay band and seat at the table all depend on how big Network Assets is.

  4. 2024 onwards · the hoard

    Gareth grows the department relentlessly. Each new team makes him safer and more important. Whether it makes asset data better is a question nobody asks him.

The turning point. Surviving a reorg by one vote, and concluding that the only safe team is a big one.

What might have saved them. A company that measured departments on outcomes rather than size, and didn't punish small, excellent teams in every reorg.

— the AnnotatorEmpire builders are often frightened people. Size feels like armour. Offer them a different kind (outcomes they can show) and some will put the gold down.

IIIKnow thy enemy

In many companies, size is status: bigger teams mean bigger titles, bigger budgets and a louder voice. Empire builders aren't always bad managers; some genuinely believe in their area. The test is simple: does the empire produce better outcomes, or just more empire?

What it wants

Safety, status and a seat at the top table. Headcount delivers all three at MetroVolt.

Why it thrives here

Because pay bands and influence are tied to team size, and reorgs punish small teams. Proposals to “consolidate” sound efficient. Nobody measures what happens after the consolidation.

What it fears

  • Questions about outcomes.
  • Budget owners spotting duplication.
  • Service offers that keep work where it is.
  • Results that can't be absorbed.

Three kinds of empire

The Believer

Really thinks centralising is better. Responds to outcome evidence.

The Fortress Builder

Grows for safety. Responds to a different kind of security.

The Status Collector

Grows for status. Needs finance and leadership to ask hard questions.

— the AnnotatorAsk a dragon what the gold is for. It hates that.

IVSigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • Every problem is solved by moving it into their team.
  • Their slides show headcount, not results.
  • “Centre of excellence”, “hub”, “one front door”.
  • Their team duplicates work done elsewhere.

VCounter-spells

  1. 1The Outcome Question

    Ask what will be measurably better, and by when, if the work moves.

    incantation“If the data work moves to Network Assets, what will be better in six months, and how will we know?”

  2. 2The Service Offer

    Offer a clear service instead of a takeover: you keep the work, they get what they need.

    incantation“What if we keep the model, and give your team a weekly data feed with an agreed quality bar?”

  3. 3The Ally in Finance

    Budget owners care about duplication. Show them the three teams doing the same thing.

VIForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Rival Empire

    Building your own empire to fight theirs. Now there are two dragons and the gold is still dirty data.

  • The Sabotage Hoard

    Withholding data or help to make their team fail. They'll fail loudly, and point at you.

  • The Mocking Song

    Sneering about “Gareth's kingdom” in meetings. Dragons have long memories and senior friends.

VIIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

The Believer. An ambitious manager with a genuine idea.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Their proposal names a real problem.
  • They welcome questions.
  • They'll agree to measures.

Do

  1. Ask the Outcome Question: what will be better, by when?
  2. Agree measures together.
  3. Support it if the case holds.

Don’t: Opposing everything they propose on principle.

If it gets worse: If work keeps flowing to them without results, you're at Nightmare.

Nightmare

The Fortress Builder. They're winning, and nothing's improving.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Every problem is solved by moving it into their team.
  • Their slides show headcount, not results.
  • Work is being duplicated.

Do

  1. Offer a service instead of a takeover.
  2. Track and publish outcomes, theirs and yours.
  3. Show finance the duplication.
  4. Use the Outcome Test on every proposal.

Don’t: The rival empire: building your own to fight theirs.

If it gets worse: If leadership rewards size above all, you're at Hell.

Hell

The Status Collector, rewarded from the top.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Size is explicitly rewarded.
  • Your team is next on their list.
  • Leadership loves consolidation slides.

Do

  1. Protect your team's core work in writing.
  2. Build a reputation on results that can't be absorbed.
  3. Make allies in finance.
  4. Help your people stay visible.

Don’t: The sabotage hoard: withholding data so their team fails. They'll fail loudly, and point at you.

If it gets worse: If your team is absorbed anyway, negotiate the terms (see the Ghost Decision).

VIIIAre you building an empire?

It starts small. Tick honestly.

  • You've asked for headcount without saying what would improve.
  • You've proposed moving work into your team “for synergies”.
  • Your slides show your team's size.
  • You feel safer with a bigger team.
  • You've never proposed giving work away.

The cure, if you ticked two: Next time you want more people, write the case in outcomes only: what will be better, by how much, by when. If you can't, you don't need them yet.

IXThe rehearsal

The Outcome Question and the Service Offer: polite, specific, and an alternative they can accept.

Scene: Tuesday 18 November, the proposal meeting. Pip asks the question.

GarethMoving Grid Planning's data work into Network Assets gives us synergies across the asset estate.

PipThat could be good. What will be measurably better in six months, and how will we know?

GarethData quality, obviously.

PipGreat. Today three teams count transformers differently. Would success be one count, agreed by March?

Gareth…Something like that.

PipWhat if we keep the model where it is, and give your team a weekly feed with an agreed quality bar? You'd get the single count without the move.

Benedict, chairing, says: “Let's try the feed first and review in March.” Gareth agrees. In March, there is one count.

— the AnnotatorPip didn't fight the dragon. He asked what the gold was for, then offered a cheaper way to get it.

XThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

An empire builder proposes absorbing your team's work. What's your reply in the meeting?

The Outcome Question: what will be measurably better, by when? Then offer a service instead.

Your own manager wants more headcount. Is that empire building?

Only if it's not tied to outcomes. Help them write the case in outcomes, not size.

Is it ever right to grow a team fast?

Yes, when demand is real and results follow. The test is outcomes, not intentions.

The Rumour Mill Familiar

Small, fast, everywhere. Feeds on half a sentence.

Threat
Habitat
the kitchen, the smoking area, private chats with names like “the real team”
Weakness
facts, shared early, by the people who know them

IA sighting

At 09:10 on a Wednesday, Pip mentioned to one person that Marcus had “looked stressed after the budget meeting”. By 11:30, Customer Ops believed Grid Planning was being shut down. By lunch, two people had asked Pip whether he was being made redundant. By three, Marcus had heard that Pip was spreading rumours about him.

Marcus had, in fact, been stressed because the coffee machine on 9 was broken.

IIKnow thy enemy

The rumour mill grows in the dark: uncertainty plus silence from leadership. People fill gaps with guesses, and guesses travel faster than facts because they're more interesting. Each retelling adds a little.

The grapevine is also genuinely useful: it often knows things weeks before the announcement. The skill is to listen without feeding it. Hear everything. Repeat nothing you haven't checked. Never be the source.

— the AnnotatorA rumour needs three things: an anxious audience, a silent leadership, and you, saying “I probably shouldn't say this, but…”. Remove yourself from the recipe.

IIISigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • “I probably shouldn't say this, but…”
  • Leadership has been quiet for weeks during a budget season.
  • Private chats are busier than team channels.
  • You hear your own words back, changed.

IVCounter-spells

  1. 1The Listening Ear

    Listen to rumours, thank people, and treat them as weak signals, not facts. Check with someone who knows.

    incantation“Interesting, thanks. I'll wait to hear it from Marcus before I believe it.”

  2. 2The Sealed Lips

    Never repeat anything about a person you wouldn't say with them in the room.

  3. 3The Fact Lantern

    If you lead people, share what you know early and say what you don't. Silence is the rumour mill's food.

    incantation“Here's what I know: budgets are being reviewed. Here's what I don't: whether it affects us. I'll update you on Friday either way.”

VForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • Feeding the Familiar

    Passing on a juicy rumour to be interesting. You become the source, and sources get remembered.

  • The Strategic Leak

    Planting a rumour on purpose to shape a decision. It's traceable, it's unethical, and it's how careers end quietly.

  • The Total Denial

    Ignoring the grapevine entirely. You'll be the last to know everything, including the things that matter.

VIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

An anxious week. Listen, don't repeat, ask your manager.

Nightmare

The grapevine is faster than every official channel. Build trusted sources, and if you lead people, run weekly fact updates.

Hell

Rumours are weaponised: people plant stories about rivals. Keep your own conduct spotless, keep records, and talk directly to the people involved.

VIIILoot drop

⚿The Rumour Filter

Three questions before you repeat anything.

1. Do I KNOW it, or did I hear it?
2. Would I say it with the person it's about standing here?
3. Does repeating it help anyone except my need to be interesting?

If any answer is no: don't.

IXThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

A colleague says your team will be cut next month. What do you do?

Thank them, don't repeat it, and ask your manager directly: “I've heard this. Is there anything you can tell me?”

You let slip that your manager looked stressed and it's grown into a story. Now what?

Go to your manager first and own it: “I said something careless and it grew. I'm sorry. Here's what I actually said.”

You manage a team during a budget review and know very little. What do you tell them?

The Fact Lantern: what you know, what you don't, and when you'll update them. Even “nothing new” beats silence.

The Annotator's last wordHear everything. Repeat nothing you couldn't sign.

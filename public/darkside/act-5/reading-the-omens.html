Reading the Omens

Famines announce themselves. Quietly. Weeks early. To anyone paying attention.

Threat
Habitat
floor 27's room bookings, cancelled off-sites, the visitor badges at reception
Weakness
a calm list of signals, checked weekly, and a plan that doesn't depend on being wrong

IA sighting

Monday 1 February · the off-site

The leadership off-site in March was “postponed”. The Christmas party had already been “simplified” to drinks. Nobody connected the two, except the Chronicler, who wrote both in a notebook.

Monday 15 February · reception

Six people with long-term visitor badges arrived on floor 27. Their lanyards said Vexmoor Consulting. They were polite, wore excellent shoes and booked meeting rooms for eight weeks.

Friday 26 February · the all-hands

Asked about rumours, Dame Helena said there were “no plans to make large-scale changes at this time”. The Chronicler underlined three words: no plans to, large-scale, at this time.

Tuesday 9 March · the room

Tamsin booked the biggest room on the ground floor for the first week of June: HR, all day. She said nothing. Her face said everything. Pip, who was about to accept Priya's offer, noticed. He accepted anyway, and updated his CV the same evening.

— the AnnotatorFamines are rarely a surprise. Every one of these signals was visible three months before the announcement. Write them down; the pattern is obvious on paper.

IIOrigin

flashbackHow MetroVolt learned to read the sky

  1. 2017 · the layoffs

    MetroVolt cuts 300 jobs. Staff read about it in the newspaper two hours before the internal email (see Act I, the Rumour Mill).

  2. 2017 · the post-mortem nobody wrote

    Afterwards, people compare notes. Every sign had been there: cancelled events, consultants, room bookings, careful phrasing. Nobody had put them together.

  3. 2018 to 2026 · the folk wisdom

    Long-serving staff quietly learn the signs and teach them to friends. The Chronicler keeps a list in a notebook, updated after each reorg.

  4. 2027 · the signs return

    When Horizon comes, the signs are exactly the same as in 2017. Only the newcomers don't know how to read them.

The turning point. A layoff where everyone saw the signs separately and nobody saw them together.

What might have saved them. Leaders being honest earlier. Failing that, someone writing the signs down in one place, which is what this entry is for.

— the AnnotatorThe omens aren't secret. They're just scattered. The Chronicler's notebook was the only place they were ever in one list.

IIIKnow thy enemy

Organisations rarely announce cuts without warning. They prepare, and preparation leaves traces: spending stops, consultants arrive, phrasing changes, rooms get booked. Reading them isn't paranoia. It's buying yourself time.

What the omens are

Signals of preparation: money being saved, analysis being done, legal processes being planned, and leaders choosing their words very carefully.

Why they're easy to miss

Because each one alone has an innocent explanation. Off-sites get postponed. Consultants do all kinds of work. It's the combination, over a few weeks, that tells you something.

What they don't tell you

  • Who will be affected.
  • How many.
  • Whether it's you.
  • Exactly when.

Three kinds of omen

The Money Omen

Spending stops: events, travel, hiring, training.

The Analysis Omen

Consultants, “activity analysis”, requests for headcount data.

The Process Omen

HR rooms booked, legal advisers visiting, careful phrasing at all-hands.

— the AnnotatorOne omen is weather. Three omens in a month is a forecast. Five is a calendar.

IVSigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • The off-site, the conference budget or the Christmas party is cancelled “for now”.
  • Consultants appear on the executive floor with long-term visitor badges.
  • A hiring freeze arrives with no end date.
  • Leaders start saying “no plans to” instead of “no”.
  • Large rooms are booked all day by HR, with no meeting title.

VCounter-spells

  1. 1The Omen Ledger

    Write down the signals you see, with dates. Patterns appear on paper that panic hides.

  2. 2The Quiet Preparation

    While nothing has happened: update your CV, save some money, write down your achievements, warm your network.

    incantation“Just updating my CV. I do it every spring.” (Say this whether or not it's true. It's good practice either way.)

  3. 3The Straight Question

    Ask your manager directly what they know. Most will tell you more than they're supposed to, if you ask calmly.

    incantation“I'm hearing a lot of rumours. Is there anything you can tell me, even if it's ‘I can't say’?”

  4. 4The Value Lantern

    Make your work's value visible in numbers, now. When the spreadsheets come, people with numbers fare better.

    incantation“Quick note for the record: the complaint model cut repeat calls by 9% this quarter, about £410k a year.”

VIForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • Prophecy by Panic

    Telling the whole team “we're all going to be fired” based on a room booking. You'll be wrong about the details and right about the stress.

  • The Ostrich

    Ignoring every sign because it's too frightening. The famine comes anyway; you just meet it unprepared.

  • The Early Exit Stampede

    Resigning at the first omen. Sometimes right. Often it means walking away from redundancy pay you'd have been owed.

VIIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

A slowdown: one or two money omens.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Spending is being trimmed.
  • No consultants, no HR rooms.
  • Leaders say “no” plainly.

Do

  1. Note it; don't panic.
  2. Keep your CV current anyway.
  3. Make your work's value visible.

Don’t: Prophecy by panic: alarming your team over a cancelled party.

If it gets worse: If analysis omens appear (consultants, headcount requests), you're at Nightmare.

Nightmare

Preparation: money and analysis omens together.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Consultants are on the executive floor.
  • Hiring is frozen.
  • Leaders say “no plans to”.

Do

  1. Start the Omen Ledger.
  2. Do the Quiet Preparation: CV, savings, achievements, network.
  3. Ask your manager a straight question.
  4. Light the Value Lantern on your work.

Don’t: The ostrich: ignoring it because it's frightening.

If it gets worse: If process omens appear (HR rooms, legal advisers), you're at Hell.

Hell

Imminent: all three kinds of omen.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • HR has booked rooms for whole days.
  • Legal advisers are visiting.
  • Your manager can't meet your eye.

Do

  1. Make sure your go-bag is packed (see the Go-Bag Scroll).
  2. Learn your rights before the day (see the Day of the Calendar Invite).
  3. Keep doing good work; it matters for references.
  4. Look after yourself and your team.

Don’t: The early exit stampede: resigning before you know the terms. You may lose pay you're owed.

If it gets worse: When the invite comes, read the Day of the Calendar Invite before you go in.

VIIIAre you reading the sky?

Most people only look up when it's already raining. Tick honestly.

  • You'd only notice layoffs coming when they were announced.
  • Your CV is more than a year old.
  • You've dismissed signals because each one had an innocent explanation.
  • You've never asked your manager a straight question about the future.
  • You assume it won't be you.

The cure, if you ticked two: Keep a simple list of signals and look at it monthly. And keep your CV current whatever the sky looks like.

IXThe rehearsal

The Straight Question: calm, direct, and easy to answer partially.

Scene: Thursday 11 March, Pip's last 1:1 with Victor before he moves.

PipVictor, I'm hearing a lot of rumours, and there are consultants on 27. Is there anything you can tell me, even if it's “I can't say”?

Victor…I can't say much. There's a review. Strategy is in scope.

PipThank you. That's helpful. Is Customer Ops in scope too?

VictorEverything's in scope. But Ops has numbers. Strategy has slides.

PipUnderstood. I'll make sure the complaint model's numbers are visible before I go.

That evening Pip writes four lines in his Omen Ledger and sends Priya a one-line note about the model's value. In May, it's quoted in her Horizon submission.

— the AnnotatorVictor said almost nothing. “Ops has numbers, Strategy has slides” was the most useful sentence anyone said to Pip all year.

XThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

The off-site is cancelled and consultants are on 27. What do you do this week?

Start the Omen Ledger, update your CV quietly, and ask your manager a calm, straight question.

Should you resign before the layoffs to “get ahead”?

Usually not without another job. If you're made redundant you may be owed pay you'd lose by resigning. Check your rights first.

Your team asks you if layoffs are coming. You don't know. What do you say?

The truth: what you know, what you don't, and when you'll update them. Never a promise you can't keep.

The Annotator's last wordFamines are rarely a surprise. They're usually just a thing nobody wanted to look at.

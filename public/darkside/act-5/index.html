The Darkside of the Corporation · Act V

The Famine

Winter comes for every budget

Every company has a famine eventually. At MetroVolt it was called Project Horizon, which is what large organisations call a famine when they'd like it to sound like a sunrise.

On the 6th of May 2027 the parent group announced it: a 15% cost reduction across MetroVolt, “around 600 roles affected”, a new structure by September. Rupert Thorne would lead it. Vexmoor Consulting would do the analysis. The announcement used the word journey eleven times and the word redundancy once, in a footnote.

By then Pip had made his decision. In March he accepted Priya's offer, and on the 1st of April he became analytics lead in Customer Operations: level 5, two analysts, a manager who went home at six. He had been in the job five weeks when the famine came.

— the AnnotatorThis Act is the one people need most and want to read least. Read it in good times. The counter-spells work far better before the calendar invite arrives than after.

One important thing before you start. Redundancy is law, not folklore. Your rights to consultation, notice, pay and selection criteria depend on your country and your contract. The counter-spells here are about how to act; for what you're owed, talk to a lawyer, your union or an official advice service.

How to read an entry

  1. A sighting: the creature in the wild, at MetroVolt Electric Corporation, scene by scene.
  2. Origin: how it came to be. Most monsters were bitten once, and the company rewarded what happened next.
  3. Know thy enemy: what it wants, why it thrives, what it fears, and its three breeds.
  4. Signs and omens, counter-spells (with the exact words: the incantation) and the forbidden spells you must not cast.
  5. Difficulty (Normal, Nightmare, Hell): how to tell which you're facing, and what to do at each.
  6. Are you the creature? A mirror. Then the rehearsal (the counter-spells, said out loud) and the trial.

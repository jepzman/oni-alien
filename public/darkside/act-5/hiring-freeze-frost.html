The Hiring Freeze Frost

“A temporary pause.” Three years later, the chair is still empty.

Threat
Habitat
headcount spreadsheets, approval forms, the desk nobody sits at
Weakness
written trade-offs: what stops if the chair stays empty

IA sighting

Monday 15 February · the email

MetroVolt announced “a temporary pause on external hiring while we review our resourcing needs”. It had no end date.

Thursday 1 April · Pip's first day

Pip arrived in Customer Ops to find a team of three: himself, Asha and Tomasz. There should have been four. The fourth role, a data engineer, had been approved in January and frozen in February.

April and May · the absorption

The data engineer's work did not freeze. Asha absorbed the data pipelines. Tomasz absorbed the weekly reports. Pip absorbed the regulator dashboard at 21:00 on Thursdays. The work got done. Nobody upstairs noticed anything was missing, which was, of course, the problem.

Monday 7 June

The Horizon template asked Pip to list his team. He listed three people. The fourth chair had been frozen so long it had vanished from the spreadsheet entirely.

— the AnnotatorA frozen role that's quietly covered doesn't stay frozen. It disappears.

IIOrigin

flashbackHow the frost settled on MetroVolt

  1. March 2020 · the first freeze

    At the start of the pandemic, MetroVolt pauses all hiring “for three months”. It's sensible. Nobody knows what's coming.

  2. 2021 · the thaw that didn't come

    The freeze is never formally lifted. Hiring restarts by exception, with a director's approval, then a VP's, then a form.

  3. 2022 to 2026 · the habit

    Every vacancy now needs a fight. Managers learn it's easier to absorb the work than to ask. Teams quietly shrink.

  4. February 2027 · the deep frost

    Horizon begins with a new freeze on top of the old one. The exception process now needs Rupert's signature. He's in London.

The turning point. A three-month emergency measure that nobody ever officially ended, so it became the culture.

What might have saved them. An end date on the first freeze. And managers who wrote down, every time, what the empty chair was costing.

— the AnnotatorEvery freeze is temporary in the email. It's permanent in the spreadsheet unless someone keeps the empty chair visible.

IIIKnow thy enemy

Hiring freezes save money in the short term, often sensibly. The damage comes when they last, and teams quietly absorb the work. The empty chair disappears, the overwork becomes normal, and the next budget round treats the smaller team as the right size.

What a freeze is for

To save money quickly while leaders decide what the organisation needs. It's a pause, in theory, not a plan.

Why the frost spreads

Because absorbing is easier than asking. Each manager covers one gap; nobody adds up the gaps. From above, everything looks fine.

What melts it

  • Written trade-offs.
  • Payback numbers for specific roles.
  • Priorities agreed with your manager.
  • Making the empty chair visible.

Three kinds of frost

The Pause

Genuinely temporary, with a date. Absorb carefully; track the cost.

The Drift

No end date, no review. Make the trade-offs visible now.

The Prelude

A freeze before cuts. Your empty chair is the first thing to go.

— the AnnotatorA freeze is only temporary if someone measures what it's costing.

IVSigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • A vacancy is “paused” with no end date.
  • Leavers aren't replaced; their work is “shared out”.
  • Every approval needs a director, then a VP, then a form.
  • The phrase “temporary pause” is more than six months old.

VCounter-spells

  1. 1The Trade-off Scroll

    When a role is frozen, write down what won't get done. Make the cost of the empty chair visible.

    incantation“With Tomasz's old role frozen, we'll drop the weekly complaints deep-dive. If that matters, we need the role or a priority call.”

  2. 2The Thaw Request

    Ask for an exception with numbers, for one role, and show what it pays back.

    incantation“One analyst at £48k would cut estimated-bill complaints by about £400k a year. Can we request an exception?”

  3. 3The Priority Pact

    Agree with your manager, in writing, what the team stops doing.

    incantation“Of our six commitments, which two do we drop while we're one person short?”

VIForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Silent Absorption

    Quietly doing the missing person's job on top of yours. The freeze becomes permanent, because it's working.

  • The Shadow Hire

    Bringing in a contractor through a side budget to get around the freeze. Finance finds it. Finance always finds it.

  • The Martyr's Spread

    Spreading the work thinly across everyone so nothing is done well. Everyone burns, quietly.

VIIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

The Pause: a short freeze with an end date.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • There's a review date.
  • Exceptions are possible.
  • Your team is only slightly stretched.

Do

  1. Agree what to pause with your manager.
  2. Track what the empty role costs.
  3. Ask for an exception if the payback is clear.

Don’t: The silent absorption: covering the gap without telling anyone.

If it gets worse: If the freeze has no end date, you're at Nightmare.

Nightmare

The Drift: no end date, no review.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • The freeze is months old.
  • Leavers aren't replaced.
  • Your team is working evenings.

Do

  1. Write the Trade-off Scroll and send it.
  2. Agree a Priority Pact: what stops.
  3. Put in a Thaw Request for the role with the clearest payback.
  4. Protect working hours.

Don’t: The shadow hire: contractors through a side budget.

If it gets worse: If the freeze is the prelude to cuts, you're at Hell.

Hell

The Prelude: a freeze before layoffs.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Consultants are counting heads.
  • Your empty roles are being removed from plans.
  • Exceptions need the parent group's signature.

Do

  1. Make sure your submission shows the true workload, including frozen roles.
  2. Show what the work is worth in money.
  3. Re-plan honestly for the team you'll actually have.
  4. Look after your people's wellbeing.

Don’t: The martyr's spread: everyone doing a bit of the missing job, badly.

If it gets worse: Read the Budget Bloodletting before your submission.

VIIIAre you feeding the frost?

Good managers often make freezes worse. Tick honestly.

  • You've covered a vacancy without telling your manager what it costs.
  • Your team works evenings to cover a frozen role.
  • You've stopped asking for exceptions because it's too much hassle.
  • Your plan assumes the empty role will be filled “soon”.
  • Your headcount sheet no longer shows the frozen role.

The cure, if you ticked two: Write down, this week, what each empty chair is costing. Send it to your manager. A visible gap can be argued for; an invisible one can't.

IXThe rehearsal

The Trade-off Scroll and the Priority Pact: make the empty chair visible.

Scene: Monday 12 April, Pip's 1:1 with Priya.

PipPriya, the data engineer role's frozen. Asha, Tomasz and I are covering it, but it's costing us. Here's a one-page list of what's slipping.

PriyaThe regulator dashboard is on here.

PipYes. I'm doing it on Thursday nights. Of our six commitments, which two do we drop while we're one short?

PriyaDrop the weekly deep-dive and the board appendix. Keep the regulator dashboard; I'll ask for an exception for the role.

PipThank you. And I'll keep the frozen role in our headcount sheet so it doesn't disappear.

In June, Pip's Horizon submission shows four roles, one frozen, and what the frozen one costs. It's one of the few submissions that does.

— the AnnotatorThe exception was refused. But the empty chair was in the spreadsheet, and that changed the next conversation.

XThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

Your team loses a person and the role is frozen. First thing you do?

Write the Trade-off Scroll: what stops, and the cost. Agree it with your manager.

Your manager says “just absorb it for now”. What do you ask?

“For how long, and what can we drop in the meantime?” Get a date and a priority list.

When is an exception worth asking for?

When the role pays back clearly in money or risk. One role, numbers, payback period.

The Annotator's last wordA freeze is only temporary if someone measures what it's costing.

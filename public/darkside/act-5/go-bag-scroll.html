The Go-Bag Scroll

Pack it in the good times. Unpack it never, if you're lucky.

Threat
Habitat
your own desk drawer, a cloud folder called “me”, a savings account
Weakness
none. This one's a counter-spell, not a curse.

IA sighting

2008 · the Chronicler's drawer

In the 2008 financial crisis, the Chronicler watched colleagues lose jobs with no savings, no CV and no contacts outside MetroVolt. They went home and packed a go-bag: not a physical one, but close.

Every spring since

Every April, the Chronicler updates a CV, adds the year's achievements to a running list, checks the savings account, and has coffee with three people outside MetroVolt. It takes one afternoon. They have done it nineteen times.

Tuesday 9 March 2027 · Pip

The night he noticed Tamsin's room booking, Pip updated his CV for the first time since joining MetroVolt in 2022. It took four hours, because he had to remember five years of achievements from scratch.

June · Marcus

When Marcus was told his role was at risk, he had no CV, no savings buffer and no contacts outside MetroVolt. He had been loyal for 23 years. It took him five months to find work. He told Pip, afterwards, that the worst part wasn't losing the job. It was having nothing ready.

— the AnnotatorThe people who survive famines aren't the luckiest. They're the ones who packed.

IIOrigin

flashbackHow the Chronicler learned to pack

  1. 2008 · the crisis

    MetroVolt cuts 150 jobs in the financial crisis. The Chronicler, five years in, keeps their job. Their friend, with twenty years' service and no CV, takes eleven months to find work.

  2. 2008 · the first bag

    The Chronicler writes a CV, a list of achievements, and a list of five people outside MetroVolt. They open a savings account labelled “just in case”.

  3. 2009 to 2026 · the ritual

    Every spring, one afternoon: update, add, check, call. Seven reorgs, three rounds of cuts. The bag is never needed. The Chronicler is calmer than anyone in every one.

  4. 2027 · passing it on

    The Chronicler writes this entry because the bag is the only counter-spell in this Act that works before the famine begins.

The turning point. Watching a loyal friend with nothing ready take eleven months to recover.

What might have saved them. Not needed: this is a habit, not a curse. The only thing to save is your future self an awful afternoon.

— the AnnotatorI've never used my go-bag. It has still been the most useful thing I've packed in nineteen years, because it let me stop being afraid.

IIIKnow thy enemy

A go-bag is everything you'd need if your job disappeared tomorrow, prepared while it hasn't: a current CV, a list of achievements with numbers, savings, contacts outside your company, and a basic understanding of your rights. It takes an afternoon a year. Its main effect is to make you less afraid.

What goes in it

Your CV, your Brag Scroll (achievements with dates and numbers), a savings buffer, outside contacts, and your contract terms.

Why people don't pack

Loyalty, optimism, and the feeling that preparing is disloyal or tempts fate. It does neither.

What it gives you

  • Calm in uncertain times.
  • Speed if the worst happens.
  • Better negotiation (you have options).
  • Better reviews (you remember your achievements).

Three kinds of bag

The Empty

Nothing ready. Start with the CV and the list tonight.

The Half-Packed

A CV from three years ago. Update it; add numbers.

The Ready

Packed and refreshed yearly. Carry it lightly.

— the AnnotatorPack it in the good times. Unpack it never, if you're lucky.

IVSigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • Your CV was last updated when you joined.
  • You couldn't cover three months without a salary.
  • Your achievements live only in your manager's memory.
  • Your network is entirely inside one company.

VCounter-spells

  1. 1The Living CV

    Update your CV twice a year with numbers, whether or not you're looking.

  2. 2The Brag Scroll

    Keep a running list of your achievements with dates and numbers (see Act II, the Weekly Deeds Scroll).

  3. 3The Buffer

    Build savings towards three to six months of essentials, however slowly.

  4. 4The Outside Web

    Keep a few relationships outside your company warm: ex-colleagues, peers, a recruiter you trust.

    incantation“Coffee next month? I'd love to hear how the new place is going.”

VIForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • Packing in Panic

    Doing all of this the night of the announcement. It's still worth doing, but it's ten times harder.

  • The Loyalty Vow

    Believing the company will look after you because you looked after it. It may. Pack the bag anyway.

  • The Company Archive

    Keeping company data in your go-bag. Your achievements are yours; their data isn't.

VIIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

Good times, empty bag.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • No omens.
  • Your CV is old.
  • You've never needed it.

Do

  1. Spend one afternoon: CV, achievements, contacts.
  2. Start a savings buffer, however small.
  3. Find your contract and note your terms.

Don’t: The loyalty vow: “they'll look after me”.

If it gets worse: If omens appear and your bag is empty, you're at Nightmare.

Nightmare

Omens are visible; the bag is half-packed.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Consultants are in.
  • Your CV is out of date.
  • Savings are thin.

Do

  1. Update your CV this week, with numbers.
  2. Write your achievements list now, while you still have access to the evidence.
  3. Warm three outside contacts.
  4. Trim spending where you can.

Don’t: Packing in panic at 2 a.m. Do it calmly, in daylight, this week.

If it gets worse: If the calendar invite arrives with an empty bag, you're at Hell.

Hell

The invite arrived; the bag is empty.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • You're at risk now.
  • Nothing is ready.
  • You're frightened.

Do

  1. Don't panic-sign anything (see the Calendar Invite).
  2. Use the consultation period to pack: CV, achievements, references.
  3. Ask colleagues for LinkedIn recommendations now.
  4. Talk to people; ask for introductions.

Don’t: The company archive: copying company data “for your portfolio”.

If it gets worse: You'll get through it. Then pack the bag properly for next time.

VIIIIs your bag packed?

Tick honestly. Nobody's looking.

  • Your CV is more than a year old.
  • You couldn't list five achievements with numbers right now.
  • You have less than three months of essentials saved.
  • Every professional contact you have works at your company.
  • You don't know your notice period.

The cure, if you ticked two: Book one afternoon this month in your calendar, titled “go-bag”. Do the CV, the list and three coffees. Then forget about it until next spring.

IXThe rehearsal

Packing, with someone who's done it nineteen times.

Scene: Saturday 13 March, 14:00. Pip and the Chronicler, a café near The Grid.

PipI updated my CV on Tuesday. It took four hours. I couldn't remember anything I'd done.

ChroniclerThat's why you keep the list. Every Friday, one line. Then the CV takes twenty minutes.

PipAnd savings?

ChroniclerWhatever you can. A month's essentials is a start. Three is calm. Six is sleep.

PipWho should I call outside MetroVolt?

ChroniclerYour old manager from your first job. A recruiter you trust. Someone from your graduate intake who left. Coffee, not favours.

Pip sets a recurring Friday reminder: “one line”. By the time Horizon is announced in May, the list has eight new lines and the bag is packed.

— the AnnotatorThe go-bag isn't about expecting the worst. It's about not having to be afraid of it.

XThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

Things are fine. Why pack a go-bag?

Because it's cheap in good times and priceless in bad ones. And it makes you calmer either way.

What goes in it?

An updated CV, an achievements list, savings, outside contacts and a basic understanding of your rights.

Isn't it disloyal?

No. Loyalty is doing good work. Preparation is looking after the people who depend on you.

The Annotator's last wordThe people who survive famines aren't the luckiest. They're the ones who packed.

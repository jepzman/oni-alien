The Ghost Ship

Your department is sinking. The captain has left. The band is still playing.

Threat
Habitat
departments under “strategic review”, leaders' LinkedIn profiles, empty corner offices
Weakness
an honest look at the waterline, and a plan made while the lifeboats are still there

IA sighting

April · the first lifeboat

Benedict Crane, Director of Strategy, announced he was “leaving to pursue an exciting new chapter”. His farewell email was three lines long. His calendar had been empty for two weeks before it.

May · the second

Victor Kraal, Head of Grid Analytics, updated his LinkedIn headline to “open to new opportunities” on a Sunday. On the Monday he told his team nothing had changed. In June, he joined Vexmoor Consulting.

June · the review

Horizon placed Strategy under “strategic review”. Its projects were paused, not cancelled. Nobody could say what Strategy would be for in 2028. The corner offices emptied one by one.

July · the wheel

Dorian stayed. He presented a 40-slide “Strategy 2028 Vision” to a meeting room with three people in it, one of whom was there to collect the projector.

— the AnnotatorShips rarely sink in an afternoon. Count the lifeboats while the band is still playing.

IIOrigin

flashbackHow Strategy became a ghost ship

  1. 2020 · the launch

    Benedict builds the Strategy department to give MetroVolt “a long-term view”. It's fashionable, well-funded and close to the CEO.

  2. 2021 to 2025 · drifting

    Strategy produces slides. The business produces electricity. The two meet less and less. Benedict is always travelling (see Act III, the Absent Lord).

  3. 2026 · the reorg

    Act I's reorg moves Grid Analytics into Strategy to give it substance. It helps, briefly.

  4. 2027 · Horizon

    Vexmoor's benchmark shows MetroVolt's Strategy is three times the size of peers'. Ops has numbers; Strategy has slides (see Reading the Omens). The ship takes on water. The officers take the lifeboats first.

The turning point. A department built around a leader and a fashion, not around work the business couldn't do without.

What might have saved them. A clear answer, every year, to “what would break if we disappeared?”. Strategy never had one.

— the AnnotatorEvery department should be able to answer one question: what breaks if we're gone? If yours can't, start counting lifeboats.

IIIKnow thy enemy

Sometimes a whole department, or company, is going down. The signs are usually visible for months: leaders leaving, work paused, purpose unclear. The question isn't whether to be loyal; it's when to leave, and in what order, while you still have choices.

What keeps people aboard

Loyalty, hope, sunk cost, and leaders who keep saying everything's fine (often while updating their own LinkedIn).

Why the officers leave first

Because they see the waterline earliest, and they have the best networks. That's not a betrayal to copy; it's a signal to read.

What helps you

  • An honest waterline check.
  • A lifeboat plan with an order.
  • A straight question to the most senior person left.
  • Your go-bag, packed.

Three kinds of ship

The Listing Ship

In trouble but salvageable. Help fix it, with a plan B.

The Ghost Ship

Leaders gone, purpose gone. Lifeboats now.

The Wreck

Already sinking. Get the best terms you can and go.

— the AnnotatorLoyalty is owed to people, not to org charts. The org chart won't notice you've gone.

IVSigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • Senior leaders leave one by one “to pursue new opportunities”.
  • Your department is under “strategic review”.
  • Projects are paused, not cancelled, and never restarted.
  • Leaders update their LinkedIn before the announcement.
  • Nobody can say what your department will be for next year.

VCounter-spells

  1. 1The Waterline Check

    Look honestly at the signs: leavers, paused work, budget, purpose. Write down what you see.

  2. 2The Lifeboat Plan

    Decide your order of exits: an internal move first, an external one second, staying for redundancy terms third. Prepare each.

  3. 3The Honest Captain Question

    Ask the most senior person still on board what the future of the department is. Their answer, or non-answer, tells you a lot.

    incantation“Benedict, honestly: what will Strategy look like in a year?”

  4. 4The Crew Care

    If you lead people, tell them what you know and help them find lifeboats too.

VIForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • Playing On

    Pretending nothing's wrong to keep morale up. Your team will find out you knew.

  • The First Lifeboat

    Leaving without a word while your team keeps working. You can leave first. Just don't leave silently.

  • Going Down With the Ship

    Staying out of loyalty to a department that's already gone. Loyalty is owed to people, not org charts.

VIIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

The Listing Ship: trouble, but salvageable.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Leaders are still committed.
  • Purpose is clear, if challenged.
  • There's a recovery plan.

Do

  1. Help fix it; it can be a great experience.
  2. Keep your go-bag packed.
  3. Watch the waterline monthly.

Don’t: Going down with the ship out of stubbornness.

If it gets worse: If leaders start leaving and work is paused, you're at Nightmare.

Nightmare

The Ghost Ship: leaders gone, purpose fading.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Senior leaders have left.
  • Projects are paused indefinitely.
  • Nobody can say what the department is for next year.

Do

  1. Do the Waterline Check, in writing.
  2. Make the Lifeboat Plan: internal move, external, stay for terms.
  3. Ask the Honest Captain Question.
  4. If you lead people, tell them what you know.

Don’t: Playing on: pretending to your team that all is well.

If it gets worse: If the department is being closed, you're at Hell.

Hell

The Wreck: closure is coming.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Closure or merger is confirmed or obvious.
  • Redundancies are certain.
  • People are leaving weekly.

Do

  1. Find out the redundancy terms; they may be worth staying for.
  2. Apply for internal roles early.
  3. Ask for references now, while managers are still there.
  4. Leave well: handovers, thanks, no burned bridges.

Don’t: The first lifeboat in silence: leaving your team to find out you've gone.

If it gets worse: When you land, write down what you'd look for next time. Then pack a new go-bag.

VIIIWhat would break if you were gone?

Ask it of your team, and of your department. Tick honestly.

  • You can't say what would break if your department disappeared.
  • Your best people have left in the last six months.
  • Your leaders' LinkedIn profiles say “open to work”.
  • Your work has been “paused” for more than a quarter.
  • You're staying mainly out of loyalty.

The cure, if you ticked two: Answer the question in one sentence: “If we were gone, ___ would break.” If you can't fill the blank, start your Lifeboat Plan this week.

IXThe rehearsal

The Waterline Check and the Lifeboat Plan, from the outside looking in.

Scene: Monday 5 July. Dorian, alone in Strategy, asks Pip for advice.

DorianBenedict's gone, Victor's gone. They say nothing's changing. Should I be worried?

PipHonestly? What would break if Strategy disappeared tomorrow?

Dorian…The 2028 Vision.

PipWho's waiting for it?

Dorian…Nobody, I suppose.

PipThen make a lifeboat plan. Ops is hiring a planning lead. You present brilliantly. Use it for someone else's work, with their name on it this time.

Dorian applies. In September, he presents Customer Ops' quarterly review. He names Asha and Pip on slide 1. It's the first time he's ever done it.

— the AnnotatorEven the Credit Vampire deserved a lifeboat. Pip offered one, with a condition. That's how famines end: with people helping each other ashore.

XThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

Two directors have left in a month and your department is under review. What do you do?

The Waterline Check, then the Lifeboat Plan. Ask the most senior person left an honest question.

Should you stay for redundancy pay or move now?

It depends on the terms, your savings and your options. Find out the terms; move if a good door opens.

You lead a team on a sinking ship. What do you owe them?

Honesty about what you know, and help finding their own lifeboats.

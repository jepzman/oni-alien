The Curse of “More with Less”

Fewer people. Same targets. A slide that says “smarter”.

Threat
Habitat
post-cut kick-offs, unchanged OKRs, the slide with the lightbulb
Weakness
an honest re-plan: less people means less work, chosen deliberately

IA sighting

Monday 6 September · the new year

Horizon was done. Customer Operations had lost 12% of its people. The annual targets, published the same morning, were identical to last year's, except one, which had gone up.

09:30 · the kick-off

Rupert Thorne opened the kick-off with a slide: a lightbulb, and the words Do More With Less. Below it, in smaller type: Work smarter, together.

September · the evenings

By the third week, Asha was answering emails at 22:00. Tomasz, who had been redeployed to Field Ops, was still being asked questions about the weekly report he no longer owned. Pip was doing the regulator dashboard on Thursday nights again.

Friday 1 October

Pip added up the team's hours. They were doing the work of four people with three, using roughly the hours of five. He looked at the lightbulb slide, which someone had printed and pinned up. Someone else had drawn a small skull on it.

— the AnnotatorLess is less. Anyone who says otherwise is selling a slide.

IIOrigin

flashbackHow a slide became a curse

  1. 2019 · Rotterdam

    In his famous turnaround, Rupert Thorne cuts a bloated subsidiary by 20% and finds real waste: duplicate teams, unread reports. It works, because the waste was real.

  2. 2019 · the slide

    His closing presentation has a lightbulb and the words “Do More With Less”. The group loves it.

  3. 2020 to 2026 · the slide travels

    The slide appears in every turnaround Rupert leads. The waste isn't always there any more. The slide is.

  4. 2027 · MetroVolt

    After years of freezes, MetroVolt has little real waste left. Horizon cuts people and keeps targets. The slide does the rest.

The turning point. A true lesson from one place (there was waste) turned into a slogan for every place (there's always waste).

What might have saved them. Asking, every time, “where is the waste, specifically?” before cutting. Sometimes the answer is “nowhere”, and the right reply is to cut work, not just people.

— the AnnotatorMost slogans were true somewhere once. The curse is applying them where they aren't.

IIIKnow thy enemy

After cuts, organisations often keep the same targets and hope efficiency fills the gap. Sometimes it does, a little: there's always some waste. Mostly, the gap is filled by unpaid overtime, until people break. The cure is an honest re-plan: fewer people means less work, chosen deliberately.

What leaders want

To keep promises they've made upwards, with the smaller team they now have. They hope the gap will close itself.

Why the curse holds

Because teams cover the gap with evenings, and from above that looks like efficiency. Nobody adds up the hours.

What breaks it

  • A written re-plan: deliver, slow, stop.
  • Real waste found and cut first.
  • Hours made visible.
  • Leaders agreeing what stops.

Three kinds of “more with less”

The Genuine

Real waste exists; cutting it really helps. Find it first.

The Hopeful

Leaders hope it'll work out. Re-plan honestly and they'll usually agree.

The Denial

Leaders insist targets stay and hours don't count. Escalate the risk in writing.

— the AnnotatorIf the only way to hit the plan is evenings, the plan is wrong. Say so while you still have the energy to.

IVSigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • Headcount drops; targets don't.
  • A slide with a lightbulb says “work smarter”.
  • Nobody says what will stop.
  • Overtime becomes the plan.

VCounter-spells

  1. 1The Honest Re-plan

    Re-plan the work for the team you actually have. List what stops, slows or gets worse, and get it agreed.

    incantation“With three people instead of four, here's what we'll deliver, here's what slows, and here's what stops. Can we agree it?”

  2. 2The Efficiency Hunt

    Find genuine waste (reports nobody reads, meetings nobody needs) and cut it first. There's always some.

    incantation“The weekly 30-page pack: who reads it? If nobody, we stop it and save a day a week.”

  3. 3The Line in the Hours

    Protect working hours. A plan that only works with permanent overtime is not a plan.

VIForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Heroic Fortnight

    Working evenings to hit the old targets with fewer people. It proves the cut was fine, and sets the new normal.

  • The Quiet Slide

    Letting quality drop without telling anyone. When something breaks, nobody knew it was at risk.

  • The Smarter Slide

    Repeating “work smarter” to your own team. You'll lose their trust in one sentence.

VIIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

The Genuine: there's real waste to cut.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Reports nobody reads exist.
  • Some meetings can go.
  • The gap is small.

Do

  1. Run the Efficiency Hunt.
  2. Cut waste first, visibly.
  3. Re-plan the rest.

Don’t: The heroic fortnight: covering the gap with overtime “just for now”.

If it gets worse: If the waste is gone and targets haven't moved, you're at Nightmare.

Nightmare

The Hopeful: same targets, fewer people, no plan.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Targets unchanged after cuts.
  • Overtime is creeping in.
  • Nobody says what stops.

Do

  1. Write the Honest Re-plan: deliver, slow, stop.
  2. Get it agreed in writing.
  3. Protect working hours visibly.
  4. Report risks before they become failures.

Don’t: The quiet slide: letting quality drop without telling anyone.

If it gets worse: If leaders refuse to re-plan, you're at Hell.

Hell

The Denial: targets fixed, hours ignored.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Re-plans are rejected.
  • Overtime is expected.
  • People are burning out.

Do

  1. Escalate the risk in writing, with numbers.
  2. Protect your team's wellbeing.
  3. Raise workload formally if people's health is at risk.
  4. Decide how long you can sustain it (Act III, the Escape Portal).

Don’t: The smarter slide: repeating “work smarter” to your own team.

If it gets worse: If people are burning out, it's a health issue. Treat it as one.

VIIIAre you doing more with less?

Check whether you've quietly accepted the curse. Tick honestly.

  • Your team works more hours than before the cuts.
  • Nothing has been formally stopped since the cuts.
  • You've said “work smarter” to your team.
  • You cover gaps yourself in the evenings.
  • Your plan assumes the old capacity.

The cure, if you ticked two: List what your team delivers. Mark one thing to stop. Take it to your manager this week. One agreed stop is worth ten evenings.

IXThe rehearsal

The Honest Re-plan, with numbers.

Scene: Friday 1 October. Pip takes the hours to Priya.

PipPriya, I added up our hours. We're delivering four people's plan with three people, using five people's hours. It won't last.

PriyaThe targets came from the group.

PipUnderstood. Here's a re-plan: we deliver these four, slow these two, and stop the monthly appendix nobody opens. And I've found one real saving: the weekly pack, 30 pages, read by one person.

PriyaWho reads it?

PipRupert's assistant, to file it. I asked.

Priya…Stop the pack. I'll take the re-plan to the group with the hours on it.

The group agrees two of the three changes. Asha's 22:00 emails stop by November.

— the AnnotatorPip did both halves: found real waste (the pack) and was honest about the rest (the re-plan). That's how you break the curse without breaking people.

XThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

Your team is cut by a quarter; targets are unchanged. What do you send your manager?

An Honest Re-plan: what you'll deliver, what slows, what stops. Ask for agreement in writing.

Is there any truth in “work smarter”?

Some. Most teams carry waste. Find it and cut it first; then be honest about the rest.

Your team starts working late to keep up. What do you do?

Stop it, visibly. Re-plan instead. Overtime hides the problem until people break.

The Annotator's last wordLess is less. Anyone who says otherwise is selling a slide.

The Credit Vampire

Feeds on your work by night. Presents it by day.

Threat
☠☠☠☠☠
Habitat
steering committees, all-hands slides, any email that starts “great work team”
Weakness
daylight: other people knowing what you did, before the meeting

IA sighting

On the 3rd of March, Pip spent eleven evenings building the Q3 load forecast, a model that predicted the heatwave peak to within 2% and saved MetroVolt an estimated €1.4 million in emergency power purchases. On the 4th, at the all-hands in the atrium, Dorian from Strategy presented it. Slide 7 read “Dorian's model.” Pip's name appeared nowhere. Not in the title, not in the footer, not even in the font.

Lord Varnish called it “exactly the kind of initiative we need.” Dorian looked humble. Pip clapped, because everyone was clapping, and then went to the bathroom on floor 9 and stared at the hand dryer for some time.

IIKnow thy enemy

The Vampire is rarely evil. It is hungry. It lives where work is invisible and only the presenter is seen. In most companies, the person who stands in front of leadership gets the credit by default. Nobody checks the git history of a slide deck.

So the Vampire does not need to lie. It only needs your work to stay in the dark until it can carry it into a room you are not in. Feed it darkness and it grows fat. Sunlight kills it.

— the Annotator“Sunlight” means other people knowing what you did, before the meeting. Not literal sun. HR has asked me to stop saying this.

The ones to watch are not the dramatic thieves. They are the gentle ones who say “we” about the work and “I” about the idea, and who always, always offer to “take it from here.”

IIISigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • “Send me the file, I'll take it from here.”
  • You are not invited to the meeting where your work is presented.
  • They say “I” about successes and “we” about problems.
  • Your manager first hears about your work from them.
  • Your name has quietly vanished from version 3 of the deck.

IVCounter-spells

  1. 1The Dawn Ward

    Announce the work before they can present it. A short note to your manager and the people who will use it, the moment it is done.

    incantation“Quick heads-up before Thursday: the Q3 load forecast is finished. Happy to walk anyone through how I built it.”

  2. 2The Name-Carving Rite

    Put your name in the artefact itself: the cover slide, the file name, the footer, the model's first tab. Vampires hate file names.

    incantationQ3-load-forecast_Pip_v4.xlsx · “Prepared by Pip, Grid Planning”

  3. 3The Invitation Hex

    Ask to present the part you built. Frame it as saving them effort, which it is.

    incantation“Could I take the five minutes on the model? It'll save you questions on the details.”

  4. 4The Grateful Reflection

    When credit is taken anyway, thank them publicly and generously for sharing your work. Polite. Accurate. Lethal.

    incantation“Thanks Dorian for presenting the forecast I built. Glad it helped. Shout if anyone wants the method.”

VForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Public Staking

    Calling them a thief at the all-hands. You look bitter; they look calm. The room remembers the scene, not the facts.

  • The Poisoned File

    Sabotaging the next model you hand over. The heatwave comes, the forecast is wrong, the city browns out. And so does your career.

  • The Whisper Campaign

    Complaining to everyone at the coffee machine. The coffee machine reports to Lord Varnish.

VIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

A careless peer who forgot to mention you. A friendly word fixes it: “Mind adding my name on slide 7?” Most people are mortified and do.

Nightmare

A habit. They do it every quarter. Cast the Dawn Ward every single time, keep the Weekly Deeds Scroll, and mention the pattern calmly to your manager with dates.

Hell

It's your boss, and the culture rewards it. Your paper trail is now your shield: keep the scroll, build relationships with your skip-level, and read Act III, entry 12 (the Escape Portal).

⚿The Weekly Deeds Scroll

A three-line Friday note to your manager. Five minutes a week. Your work is in daylight before anyone can carry it into a meeting.

Subject: Week 10 · Pip · Grid Planning

SHIPPED     Q3 load forecast v4 (heatwave peak within 2%). Shared with Ops + Trading.
IN PROGRESS Substation 14 capacity check, due 21 Mar.
NEED HELP   Access to the 2025 smart-meter data (waiting on IT, ticket #4471).

IXThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

Dorian asks for your file “to tidy the formatting” the day before the steering committee. What do you do?

Send it, with your name already in the file name and on the cover, and copy your manager: “Here's the forecast for tomorrow. Happy to join for the method questions.” You helped, and you're in daylight.

Your manager praises Dorian for your model in a group chat. What's the best reply?

The Grateful Reflection, in the same chat: “Thanks! Glad the forecast I built is useful. Dorian did a great job presenting it.” No accusation, just the record.

Credit is stolen once, by someone new to the team. Which difficulty is this, and which spell?

Normal. Assume carelessness, not malice. A private, friendly word, then the Dawn Ward from now on.

The Annotator's last wordThe best time to claim credit is before the work is finished. The second best time is in writing.

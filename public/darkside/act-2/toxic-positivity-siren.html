The Toxic Positivity Siren

Sings of opportunity as the ship goes down.

Threat
Habitat
town halls, wellbeing weeks, announcements with too many emojis
Weakness
calm, specific questions, asked kindly

IA sighting

The week after MetroVolt announced a hiring freeze, Kaylee, Head of Employee Joy, launched Gratitude Fortnight. There was a gong. When Pip asked at the Q&A how teams would cope with six unfilled roles in Grid Planning, Kaylee smiled. “I love that energy! Let's reframe that as a growth opportunity. 🚀”

The gong was struck. Nobody asked another question.

IIKnow thy enemy

The Siren is not lying. Often it's been told to keep morale up, and has no power to fix the problem underneath. Positivity is the only tool it has. So it reaches for it, every time, even when the ship has a hole in it.

The danger is to you: in a Siren culture, people who raise problems get labelled “negative”. The counter is not to be negative louder. It's to raise problems in a form the song can't drown: calm, specific, and with a suggestion.

— the AnnotatorYou can be positive about the people and honest about the problem in the same sentence. That's not negativity, it's management.

IIISigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • “No problems, only opportunities!”
  • Concerns are met with “let's stay positive”.
  • Bad news arrives with a celebration attached.
  • People share problems only in private chats.

IVCounter-spells

  1. 1The Both-And Chant

    Agree with the positive, then add the problem. You can't be dismissed as negative if you started with a yes.

    incantation“I'm genuinely glad we're investing in the team. And I'm worried about six open roles in Grid Planning. What can we drop?”

  2. 2The Specific Question

    Ask for a decision or a fact, not a feeling. Siren songs are about feelings.

    incantation“With the freeze, which of our Q4 projects should slow down first?”

  3. 3The Right Ear

    Take the real problem to the person who can fix it, not the one who runs the gong.

    incantation“Could I have 15 minutes with you on the Grid Planning workload? I have a proposal.”

VForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Doom Counter-Song

    Matching the Siren's cheer with gloom. You become the official Negative Person, and everything you say is discounted.

  • The Mocking Echo

    “Growth opportunity 🚀” in the team chat, with a skull. It will be screenshotted. Kaylee has feelings and a direct line to the CEO.

  • The Swallowed Concern

    Saying nothing. The problem grows, and when it bursts, someone will ask why nobody raised it.

VIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

An upbeat colleague. The Both-And Chant gets you heard and keeps the relationship.

Nightmare

A culture where concerns are career-limiting. Raise problems with data, with solutions, and to the right ear. Keep a short written record that you raised them.

Hell

Leadership punishes bad news. Raise serious issues in writing to the person accountable, keep a copy, find allies who see the same thing, and remember Act VIII, the Dark Pact, if it's ever about safety or ethics.

VIIILoot drop

⚿The Problem-With-a-Path

How to raise a problem in a positive culture: good news, the problem, a proposal, an ask. Four lines.

GOOD      We hit the Q3 forecast within 2%. Team is strong.
PROBLEM   Six roles frozen: two Q4 projects will slip ~8 weeks.
PROPOSAL  Pause the substation dashboard; keep Lumen on track.
ASK       Can you confirm by Friday which one you'd rather keep?

IXThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

At a town hall, you want to ask about workload after the freeze. How do you phrase it?

The Both-And Chant plus a Specific Question: “Glad we're keeping the team together. With six frozen roles, which projects should slow down?”

You get labelled “a bit negative” in your review. What do you change?

Keep raising problems, but use the Problem-With-a-Path format, and to the person who can act. Ask your manager for an example of where the tone missed.

Is it ever right to just be positive?

Yes: when the problem is small, already known, or nobody can do anything. Pick the problems worth raising.

The Annotator's last wordOptimism is a strategy. Ignoring the iceberg is not.

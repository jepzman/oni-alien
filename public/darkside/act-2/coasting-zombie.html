The Coasting Zombie

Does nothing. Survives everything.

Threat
☠☠☠☠☠
Habitat
shared projects, group tasks, any work with more than one owner
Weakness
named owners and visible deadlines

IA sighting

Neville has been in Asset Records for fourteen years and has survived six reorgs. In March he was given half of the Lumen asset clean-up: 40,000 meter records to check. In June, Pip noticed that his own half was done and Neville's half was “in progress”, as it had been since March.

Pip quietly did 8,000 of Neville's records over two weekends. At the July review, the clean-up was praised as “a real team effort”. Neville nodded wisely. He had, in a sense, been on the team.

IIKnow thy enemy

Some Zombies are lazy. Many are not: they are burnt out, bored, disengaged or quietly dealing with something at home. A few have learned that in a big company, work given to “the team” always gets done by someone. Usually by someone like Pip.

The Zombie survives because nobody can see what it does or doesn't do. Shared ownership is its natural habitat. Your job is not to judge or fix it. Your job is to stop absorbing its work silently.

— the AnnotatorEvery weekend you spend doing a Zombie's work is a weekend that proves to the company the Zombie isn't a problem.

IIISigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • Their status is always “in progress”, never a number.
  • Work given to “the team” lands on you.
  • They are always busy, and nobody knows with what.
  • You start doing their part “to be safe”.

IVCounter-spells

  1. 1The Naming of Owners

    Every task gets one named owner and a date, in a place everyone can see. “The team” cannot own anything.

    incantation“Shall we split it on the board? Neville: records 20,001 to 40,000, done by 30 April. Me: 1 to 20,000.”

  2. 2The Friendly Check-in

    Ask early, kindly and specifically. Sometimes the Zombie is stuck and ashamed to say so.

    incantation“How's your half going? If something's blocking you, I'm happy to look at it together for half an hour.”

  3. 3The Honest Status

    In status updates, report facts by owner. No covering, no blaming.

    incantation“My half is done. Neville's half is at 15%. At this pace we'll finish mid-August, not June.”

  4. 4The Escalation by Numbers

    If the date is at risk, tell your manager with numbers and a choice, not a complaint.

    incantation“The clean-up will miss June by about six weeks. Options: move the date, add someone, or cut scope. Which do you prefer?”

VForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Silent Martyr

    Doing their work in secret. You burn out, they get praised, and next time you'll get both halves officially.

  • The Public Shaming

    “Some of us actually work” in the team chat. Now you're the problem.

  • The Zombie's Path

    Deciding that if they can coast, so can you. Zombies who survive have fourteen years of relationships. You have eight months.

VIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

A colleague who's overloaded or stuck. The Friendly Check-in solves it, and you may gain an ally.

Nightmare

A known coaster the team works around. Named owners and Honest Status make it visible. Visibility is your whole job here; the rest is your manager's.

Hell

The Zombie is protected (friend of the director, untouchable). Protect yourself: named owners, dated records, and never quietly take on their work. If the project fails, the record shows why.

IXThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

You're asked to “work with Neville on it”. What's your first move?

Split it into named halves with dates, on a board both of you and your manager can see. Today, not when it's late.

It's two weeks before the deadline and their half is at 15%. You could finish it over the weekend. Should you?

No, not silently. Report the facts and options to your manager first. If you then agree to help, it's visible help, not invisible rescue.

How do you tell a Zombie from a colleague who's struggling?

You usually can't from outside. That's why the Friendly Check-in comes first. Treat them as stuck until proven otherwise.

The Annotator's last wordHelp openly or not at all. Secret rescue is just unpaid overtime with extra resentment.

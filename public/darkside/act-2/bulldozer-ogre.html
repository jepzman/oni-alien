The Bulldozer Ogre

Speaks first, loudest and longest. Hears no one.

Threat
Habitat
brainstorms, workshops, any meeting with a whiteboard
Weakness
structure: rounds, timers and the written word

IA sighting

Wednesday 14 May · 10:00 · the Lumen pricing workshop

Eight people, one whiteboard, a question: how should off-peak pricing work for smart-meter customers? Pip had spent two days on the off-peak data. He had a chart. He was ready.

10:04 to 10:15

Brett from Commercial interrupted Pip four times in eleven minutes. Each time Pip got as far as “I think the off-peak data shows—” before Brett said “Right, so what I'm hearing is,” and then described an idea that was not what he was hearing at all. On the fifth attempt Pip stopped trying.

10:15 to 11:30

Brett drew his plan on the whiteboard in red, then in blue, then in red again for emphasis. Two engineers stopped taking notes. A data scientist who had joined from Customer Ops turned her camera off. Someone said “good energy”.

11:31

The workshop produced Brett's plan. Six people left with ideas they never said out loud. One of them (Pip's off-peak chart) would have saved Lumen £300,000 a year. It stayed in his laptop until October.

— the AnnotatorInterrupting is a habit, not a character. Habits respond to friction. Add friction.

IIOrigin

flashbackHow a keen salesman became the Bulldozer Ogre

  1. 1990s · the youngest of five

    At Brett's family dinners, you talked loud and fast or you didn't get heard. He got very good at it.

  2. 2012 · a sales floor

    Brett joins a sales team where the loudest pitch wins the client. He wins a lot. Nobody ever asks what the quiet people would have said.

  3. 2022 · the promotion review

    Brett is promoted to lead Commercial at MetroVolt. His review praises his “executive presence” and “gravitas in meetings”. It means he talks over people. Nobody says so.

  4. 2025 · the workshops

    Brett runs workshops the way he ran dinners: whoever is loudest wins. He genuinely believes he's facilitating. Nobody has ever told him otherwise.

The turning point. The review that called interrupting “presence”, and turned a habit into a strength he was paid for.

What might have saved them. One honest piece of feedback (“you cut people off, and we lose their ideas”), from someone he respected, at any point in twenty years.

— the AnnotatorMost Ogres have never been told. They think the silence around them means agreement. It means surrender.

IIIKnow thy enemy

The Ogre is often not trying to dominate. It thinks out loud, fast, and confuses volume with contribution. It was probably rewarded for it: in many offices the confident talker gets promoted, and the quiet expert gets “needs to speak up more” in their review.

What it wants

To be heard, to move fast, and to win. Most Ogres also want good ideas; they just don't notice they're flattening them.

Why it thrives here

Because open discussion rewards the loudest voice, and most meetings at MetroVolt are open discussion. Confidence looks like competence from the top table. And quiet people rarely complain; they simply stop coming.

What it fears

  • Structure: rounds, timers, silent writing.
  • Being interrupted, calmly, by name.
  • Ideas written down before the meeting.
  • A facilitator who isn't them.

The three breeds

The Enthusiast

Thinks out loud and gets carried away. Mortified when it's pointed out kindly.

The Dominator

Wants to win the room. Needs structure and a firm chair.

The Anxious Talker

Fills every silence because silence frightens them. Needs a calmer room.

— the AnnotatorYou don't have to be louder. You have to be harder to interrupt, and to run meetings where volume doesn't count.

IVSigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • “Sorry, just to jump in—” (they are not sorry, and they are not jumping, they live there).
  • Your ideas come back to you in their voice, ten minutes later.
  • Quiet people stop attending.
  • Every meeting ends with their plan.

VCounter-spells

  1. 1The Held Ground

    When interrupted, keep going calmly, slightly louder, with their name. Don't stop, don't apologise.

    incantation“Brett, let me finish this point, then I want your view.”

  2. 2The Circle of Turns

    If you run the meeting, use rounds: everyone gets two minutes, in order, no replies until the round ends.

    incantation“Let's go round the table first, two minutes each, no comments until everyone's spoken.”

  3. 3The Silent Scrolls

    Start with five minutes of silent writing. Ideas go on sticky notes or a shared doc before anyone speaks. The Ogre can't talk over a Post-it.

    incantation“Before we discuss, five minutes: write your top idea on a note.”

  4. 4The Pre-Meeting Ally

    Agree with an ally beforehand to hand the floor back to each other.

    incantation“I think Pip was in the middle of something about off-peak data?”

VIForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Ogre Duel

    Out-shouting the Ogre. Now there are two Ogres and the meeting is a nature documentary.

  • The Sulk of Silence

    Giving up and saying nothing for the rest of the quarter. Your review will say “needs to contribute more in meetings”.

  • The Mockery Rune

    An eye-roll to the room. Ogres are bad at listening but excellent at noticing contempt.

VIIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

The Enthusiast. A keen talker who gets carried away.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • They interrupt everyone, not just you.
  • They're friendly and well-meaning.
  • When told, they apologise.

Do

  1. Hold your ground by name: “Brett, let me finish, then I want your view.”
  2. Have a quiet, friendly word afterwards.
  3. Put your key point in writing before the next meeting.

Don’t: Out-shouting them. Two Ogres make a nature documentary, not a meeting.

If it gets worse: If they run the room and keep steamrolling, you're at Nightmare.

Nightmare

The Dominator. A senior Ogre who runs the room.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Their plan always wins.
  • Quiet people have stopped coming.
  • You can't change the meeting format.

Do

  1. Send your idea in writing before the meeting, with your name on it.
  2. Agree with an ally to hand the floor back to each other.
  3. Suggest the Meeting Charm (silent writing, rounds) as a “new format to try”.
  4. Ask the chair for two uninterrupted minutes on your item.

Don’t: Sulking into silence. Your review will say “needs to contribute more”.

If it gets worse: If the Ogre is your manager, you're at Hell.

Hell

The Ogre is your manager, and volume is the culture.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Your manager talks over you in every meeting.
  • Promotions go to the loudest.
  • Ideas only count if they're shouted.

Do

  1. Put every important idea in writing first.
  2. Use 1:1s, where volume matters less.
  3. Find a sponsor who will hand you the floor in big meetings.
  4. Give your manager the feedback once, privately, with one example.

Don’t: Mockery. Eye-rolls are noticed even by people who notice nothing else.

If it gets worse: If your ideas never make it out, consider a team where they do (Act III, the Escape Portal).

VIIIAre you the creature?

Before you hunt an Ogre, listen to yourself. Tick honestly.

  • You often finish other people's sentences.
  • Your meetings usually end with your plan.
  • You think quiet people simply don't have ideas.
  • You've said “what I'm hearing is” and then said your own idea.
  • You can't remember the last time you asked the quietest person first.

The cure, if you ticked two: In your next three meetings, speak last. Ask the quietest person first. You'll be astonished by what you've been missing.

IXThe rehearsal

The Held Ground, then the Meeting Charm. Calm, a little louder, by name.

Scene: the next workshop, 10:06. Brett interrupts for the second time.

PipI think the off-peak data shows that—

BrettRight, so what I'm hearing is—

PipBrett, let me finish this point, then I really want your view.

A pause. Brett blinks. Nobody has done this before.

PipThe off-peak data shows a 14% shift is possible with a two-tier price. That's the chart on page 3 of what I sent yesterday.

Brett…OK. Interesting. What's the catch?

PipGood question. Could we go round the table on that? Two minutes each, then you sum up?

Six people speak. Brett sums up. The plan on the whiteboard, for the first time, has other people's handwriting on it.

— the AnnotatorNotice the last move: Pip gives the Ogre a role (summing up). Ogres don't need silencing. They need a different job in the room.

XThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

The Ogre interrupts you for the third time. What exactly do you say?

Use their name and keep going: “Brett, let me finish, then I want your view.” Calm, a bit louder, no apology.

You're running a workshop with two Ogres and four quiet engineers. What's your format?

The Meeting Charm: silent writing first, then rounds. The quiet engineers' ideas are on the wall before anyone can talk over them.

The Ogre repeats your idea as their own. Spell?

Link it back, warmly: “Yes, building on what I said earlier about off-peak data…” Then see the Credit Vampire, entry 1.

The Annotator's last wordYou don't have to be louder. You have to be harder to interrupt.

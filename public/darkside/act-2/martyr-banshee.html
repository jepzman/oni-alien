The Martyr Banshee

Wails of overwork. Refuses all help.

Threat
Habitat
Network Operations, Slack status “drowning 🫠”, the late-night email
Weakness
concrete offers of help, and saying yes to “no”

IA sighting

Every night · 23:58

Morag in Network Operations sent her emails just before midnight. They weren't urgent. They were evidence. Her Slack status had said drowning 🫠 since February. In every meeting she mentioned that she hadn't had a lunch break since 2023.

Monday 7 April · 11:15

Pip offered to take the weekly outage report off her plate. Morag looked at him the way people look at someone offering to hold their baby. “No, no,” she said. “It's quicker if I do it. Nobody else knows the system.”

Wednesday 9 April · the team meeting

Morag said, with real pain, that nobody ever offered to help. Three people looked at Pip. Pip looked at the table.

June · the contagion

Pip caught himself sending an email at 23:40 that could have waited until morning. He noticed he'd added “sorry for the late one, crazy week!” He had become, for one evening, a small Banshee.

— the AnnotatorThe Banshee's wail is contagious. Spend a month next to one and you'll start sending emails at midnight to prove you care too. Don't.

IIOrigin

flashbackHow the hero of the 2009 blackout became the Martyr Banshee

  1. October 2009 · the blackout

    Half the city goes dark. Morag, then a junior engineer, stays in the control room for 40 hours straight and helps bring the grid back. It was genuinely heroic.

  2. November 2009 · the email

    The CEO sends Morag a personal thank-you email. She prints it. It is still framed on her desk. It is the only time anyone senior has ever thanked her by name.

  3. 2010 to 2024 · the pattern

    Every recognition since has been for overwork: covering, staying late, never taking leave. Nobody has ever been thanked for training a backup. So she never trains one.

  4. 2025 · the identity

    Morag is the one who never goes home. If someone else could do the outage report, who would she be? Help feels like a threat. Overwork feels like love.

The turning point. The framed email: proof that what MetroVolt values is exhaustion, not systems that don't need heroes.

What might have saved them. Recognition, just once, for building a team that could run without her. And a manager who sent her home.

— the AnnotatorMartyrs are made by organisations that only notice suffering. If you lead people, thank the ones who made the crisis unnecessary.

IIIKnow thy enemy

The Banshee's identity is built on being indispensable. Being overworked is proof she matters. Letting go of a task would be a relief and a threat at the same time. So she wails, and refuses.

Be careful: the Banshee may also be genuinely burning out. Treat the person kindly and the pattern firmly.

What it wants

To be needed, to be seen as working hard, and, underneath, to be told it's OK to rest.

Why it thrives here

Because MetroVolt celebrates heroics (the 2009 story is told at every induction) and never celebrates the quiet work that prevents them. Late emails look like commitment. Single points of failure look like expertise.

What it fears

  • Being replaceable.
  • Specific offers of help it can't easily refuse.
  • Being asked to teach someone.
  • Going home on time and nothing falling over.

The three breeds

The Hero

Was praised for overwork once and never stopped. Needs recognition for something else.

The Gatekeeper

Keeps knowledge to stay needed. Needs to be the teacher, not the replaced.

The Genuinely Drowning

Really overloaded, really burning out. Needs support, not spells.

— the AnnotatorBusy is not a personality. Neither is tired. Go home.

IVSigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • Late-night emails that are not urgent.
  • “Nobody else can do it” about tasks that are written down somewhere.
  • Offers of help refused, then lack of help complained about.
  • The team starts competing over who is busiest.

VCounter-spells

  1. 1The Specific Offer

    Vague help is easy to refuse. Offer one specific task with a time.

    incantation“I can do the outage report this Friday. You review it Monday, ten minutes. Want to try once?”

  2. 2The Teach-Me Spell

    Ask to learn the task, not take it. Being the teacher keeps her important.

    incantation“Could you show me how the outage report works? Then you've got backup when you're on leave.”

  3. 3The Kind Boundary

    Sympathise without joining the chorus or taking on everything.

    incantation“That sounds like a lot. I can't take on more this week, but I'm free Thursday afternoon if something specific would help.”

  4. 4The Wellbeing Flag

    If it looks like real burnout, say so kindly, in private, and point to support.

    incantation“You seem really stretched lately. Is it worth raising the workload with Hugo? I'd back you up.”

VIForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Busy Duel

    Out-martyring the Banshee. “You think you're busy? I haven't seen daylight since March.” Now the whole team is competing in suffering.

  • The Eye-Roll Chorus

    Mocking her behind her back. She might be one bad week from collapse. You'll feel terrible, and you'll deserve to.

  • The Rescue Fantasy

    Taking over all her work to save her. She'll resent it, and you'll become the next Banshee.

VIIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

A busy colleague letting off steam.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • It's a bad week, not a lifestyle.
  • They accept help when it's specific.
  • They go home most days.

Do

  1. Listen for a minute.
  2. Offer one specific thing with a time.
  3. Move on without joining the chorus.

Don’t: The busy duel: “you think you're busy?” Nobody wins the suffering Olympics.

If it gets worse: If help is always refused and the wailing continues, you're at Nightmare.

Nightmare

The Hero or the Gatekeeper. Overwork is part of who they are.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Every offer of help is refused.
  • Their knowledge is a single point of failure.
  • Their late emails are setting a team norm.

Do

  1. Make the Specific Offer: one task, one date, a small review.
  2. Ask to learn the task, not take it.
  3. Raise the single-point-of-failure risk to your manager as a business risk.
  4. Model the opposite: leave on time, visibly.

Don’t: Mocking them behind their back. They might be one bad week from collapse.

If it gets worse: If it's your manager and the wail becomes everyone's expectation, you're at Hell.

Hell

The Banshee is your manager, or it's real burnout.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • You're expected to match their hours.
  • They make mistakes, withdraw, or get ill.
  • The team is burning out with them.

Do

  1. Protect your hours with clear availability.
  2. Deliver well inside your hours.
  3. If you see burnout signs, use the Wellbeing Flag kindly and privately.
  4. Read Act VIII, the Slow Hex of Burnout.

Don’t: The rescue fantasy: taking over all their work. You'll become the next Banshee.

If it gets worse: If you're burning out too, talk to your manager's manager or HR. It's a health issue, not a weakness.

VIIIAre you the creature?

Before you hunt a Banshee, check your own sent items. Tick honestly.

  • You send emails after 22:00 that could wait.
  • You mention how busy you are in most meetings.
  • You've refused help because “it's quicker if I do it”.
  • Nobody else could do your job next week.
  • You feel slightly proud of being exhausted.

The cure, if you ticked two: Teach one person one thing you do this month, and take a real lunch break on Friday. Being replaceable is what lets you take holidays.

IXThe rehearsal

The Specific Offer and the Teach-Me Spell: easy to say yes to, and no threat to who she is.

Scene: Monday 7 April, 11:15. Pip tries again, with a specific offer.

PipMorag, could you show me how the outage report works? Then you've got backup when you're on leave.

MoragOh, it's really complicated, honestly, it's quicker if—

PipI'm sure. That's why I'd want you to show me. Thursday, 30 minutes? Then I draft this week's and you check it Monday. Ten minutes.

Morag…I suppose I am on leave in August.

PipExactly. And you'd be able to actually switch off.

Thursday: Morag explains the report for 55 minutes. She is visibly proud of it. In August, she takes two weeks off. The report goes out on time. Nobody frames an email about it, but Morag tells three people that Pip did a good job.

— the AnnotatorBeing the teacher keeps the Banshee important. That's what makes saying yes possible.

XThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

The Banshee says “nobody ever helps” right after refusing your help. What do you say?

Calmly, without scorekeeping: “I'd still love to take the outage report one week. Want to try this Friday?” Repeat the specific offer, not the grievance.

How do you tell a Banshee from real burnout?

Often you can't, and it can be both. Look for changes: more mistakes, withdrawal, illness, emotion. If you see them, use the Wellbeing Flag instead of a spell.

Your team has started competing over who works latest. What do you do?

Don't join. Leave on time, visibly, and say so: “Logging off, back at 8.” Someone has to go first. Others are often waiting for permission.

The Boundary Goblin

“Quick question, no rush 🙂” (23:47, there is rush)

Threat
☠☠☠☠☠
Habitat
Slack, WhatsApp, your phone at dinner, your holiday
Weakness
clear, kind, repeated boundaries, and a reply that comes in the morning

IA sighting

Hugo runs Project Lumen and has never been seen offline. His first message to Pip arrived on a Sunday at 23:47: “Quick q, no rush 🙂 where's the meter data?”. At 23:52: “?”. At 07:10: “Morning! Any update on last night?”

Pip replied at 23:53, because he was awake and it was only a quick question. From then on, Hugo knew Pip was always available. The Goblin had been fed after midnight. Everyone knows what happens then.

IIKnow thy enemy

The Goblin is rarely a tyrant. It's usually anxious and badly organised: it thinks of things at night and sends them before it forgets. It often genuinely means “no rush”. But every fast reply teaches it that you're on call. You trained it. You can untrain it.

Boundaries are not about rules. They're about what you reliably do. If you always reply at 08:30, the Goblin learns 08:30. Consistency beats speeches.

— the AnnotatorA boundary you explain but don't keep is just a speech. A boundary you keep without explaining is a habit, and habits are what people learn.

IIISigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • Messages after 21:00, before 07:00 or on holiday.
  • “No rush” followed by “?” five minutes later.
  • You feel anxious when you hear a notification at dinner.
  • You've started replying fast to avoid the follow-up.

IVCounter-spells

  1. 1The Ward of Hours

    Set your hours visibly (Slack status, calendar working hours, email signature) and keep them. Reply at the start of your next working day.

    incantationSlack status: “🕯 Online 08:30–17:30 CET. Urgent outage? Call the Control Room.”

  2. 2The Morning Reply

    Answer late messages in the morning, warmly and fully. No apology for not replying at midnight.

    incantation“Morning Hugo! The meter data is in the Lumen folder, tab 3. Shout if anything's missing.”

  3. 3The Emergency Rune

    Agree with the Goblin what truly urgent means and how to reach you for it. Real emergencies get a call, not a message.

    incantation“If it's genuinely urgent outside hours, call me. Otherwise I'll pick up messages first thing.”

  4. 4Scheduled Send

    If you work late, schedule your own messages for the morning. Don't spawn Goblins of your own.

VForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Midnight Feed

    Replying at 23:53 “just this once”. Goblins don't remember exceptions. They remember precedents.

  • The Furious Lecture

    A long message at 07:00 about respecting boundaries. True, but it reads as an attack, and the Goblin will feel wronged.

  • The Total Blackout

    Turning everything off and refusing all contact, even for real emergencies. In a power company, outages happen at 3 a.m. Know which ones are yours.

VIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

A night owl who means “no rush”. The Morning Reply for two weeks, and it learns.

Nightmare

Expects fast replies and complains when it doesn't get them. Agree the Emergency Rune with them openly, and with your manager if needed.

Hell

It's your manager, and the culture rewards being always-on. Agree core hours in writing, deliver excellently inside them, and read Act VIII: the Salt Circle and the Slow Hex of Burnout.

IXThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

Sunday, 23:47: “Quick q, no rush 🙂”. What do you do?

Nothing until Monday at 08:30. Then the Morning Reply: warm, complete, no apology.

The Goblin says “I noticed you didn't reply last night”. Your answer?

Calmly: “I'm offline after 18:00. If it's urgent, call me and I'll always pick up.” Then keep doing it.

You're working late yourself and want to send the team a message. How?

Schedule it for 08:30. Leaders who message at midnight create Goblins, even when they say “no rush”.

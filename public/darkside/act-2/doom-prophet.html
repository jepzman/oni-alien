The Doom Prophet

“We tried that in 2014. It failed.”

Threat
Habitat
the Control Room, kick-offs, the back row of every town hall
Weakness
turning prophecy into a risk register

IA sighting

Terrence has worked in the Control Room since the 2009 blackout. When Pip proposed automated alerts for substation overloads, Terrence sighed the sigh of a man who has seen empires fall. “We tried automation in 2014. Alarms went off for three weeks straight. Nobody slept. It'll fail.”

Then he told the story of 2014 for nineteen minutes. It was, to be fair, a very good story.

IIKnow thy enemy

The Prophet is not a pessimist by nature. It is a survivor. It has watched good ideas fail for reasons nobody wrote down, and it remembers every one. Its doom is pattern-matching, done out loud, with feeling.

That makes it the most underused risk expert in the building. The problem isn't the warning; it's that the warning comes as a verdict (“it'll fail”) instead of a risk (“it failed because of X”). Convert the verdict, and the Prophet becomes useful.

— the AnnotatorThe Doom Prophet is wrong about the conclusion and often right about the reasons. Steal the reasons.

IIISigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • “We tried that.” “It never works here.” “Give it six months.”
  • History lectures with no question at the end.
  • New joiners stop suggesting things after week three.
  • They are sometimes, annoyingly, right.

IVCounter-spells

  1. 1The Transmutation

    Turn the prophecy into specific risks. Ask what went wrong last time, and write each answer down as a risk.

    incantation“That's really useful. What exactly went wrong in 2014? I want to make sure we don't repeat it.”

  2. 2The Prophet's Seat

    Give them a role: official risk reviewer. Their doom now has a job.

    incantation“Terrence, would you be our devil's advocate at the design review? Nobody knows the failure modes like you.”

  3. 3The Pre-Mortem

    Run a session where the team imagines the project has failed and lists why. The Prophet will shine, and it's all on the whiteboard.

    incantation“Imagine it's a year from now and the alerts failed. What happened?”

VForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Dismissal

    “That was 2014, things are different now.” Maybe. But you've just told the person who knows where the bodies are buried that you don't want the map.

  • The Hype Shield

    Answering doom with forced optimism. Now you're the Toxic Positivity Siren (entry 11), and the Prophet has a new story.

  • The Exclusion

    Leaving them out of planning to avoid the negativity. They'll find out, and the prophecy becomes a mission.

VIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

A cautious colleague. One Transmutation question and you have a list of useful risks.

Nightmare

Kills momentum in every meeting. Give them the Prophet's Seat, a fixed time to raise risks, and the rest of the meeting moves.

Hell

The Prophet is senior and their doom shapes decisions. Address each risk in writing, run a small pilot to beat the 2014 failure, and let the result update the story.

⚿The Prophecy Register

Turn every “it'll fail” into a row. Show the Prophet the register: their warnings are in it, with owners.

WARNING (from Terrence)           RISK                         WHAT WE'LL DO
“Alarms ran 3 weeks straight”     alert flood, operators mute   thresholds tuned on 2 yrs data,
                                                                max 5 alerts/hr per desk
“Nobody owned the rules”          stale thresholds             monthly review, owner: Control Room
“Vendor vanished after go-live”   no support                   support clause in contract

IXThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

In the kick-off, the Prophet says “this will fail like 2014”. What's your reply?

The Transmutation: “What went wrong in 2014? I want to avoid it.” Write the answers down where everyone can see them.

The Prophet's story runs 19 minutes. How do you keep the meeting on track?

Thank them, capture the key risks in one line each, and offer a separate session: “This is gold. Can we go deeper on Thursday so we can finish the agenda now?”

Six months later the project succeeds. What do you do?

Thank the Prophet publicly for the risks they caught. Next time they'll warn you earlier and grumble less.

The Annotator's last wordEvery prophet of doom is a risk register that hasn't been asked nicely.

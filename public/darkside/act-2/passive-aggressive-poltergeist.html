The Passive-Aggressive Poltergeist

Rattles the inbox. Never appears in person.

Threat
☠☠☠☠☠
Habitat
email, the kitchen noticeboard, shared calendars
Weakness
a friendly, direct conversation in person

IA sighting

On the 2nd of June, Pip booked meeting room 14B without the online form. At 09:04 he received an email from Felicity in Facilities, copied to his manager, the floor warden and something called FAC-ALL:

“Hi Pip! 😊 Just a gentle reminder, as per the policy (attached again for convenience), that rooms are booked via the form. I'm sure it was an oversight! Kind regards, Felicity.”

The smiley was the worst part. Pip felt it look at him.

IIKnow thy enemy

The Poltergeist is angry and has decided it is not allowed to be. So the anger leaks out sideways: through politeness that bites, CCs that sting, and notes in the kitchen about whose mug this is.

It usually comes from feeling unheard or powerless. Facilities is blamed for every cold room and broken lift; email is the one place Felicity can be in charge. Understanding that won't make the smiley less sharp. It will tell you what works: the Poltergeist needs to be heard, and it can't stand being treated like a person.

— the Annotator“Per my last email” means “read my emails, you fool”. “Kind regards” is 20% kind. “Thanks in advance” means you no longer have a choice.

IIISigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • “Per my last email…” “As previously mentioned…” “Just circling back…”
  • Your manager is copied on something small.
  • A smiley that does not reach the eyes.
  • A laminated sign in the kitchen, in bold, about you.

IVCounter-spells

  1. 1The Summoning to the Flesh

    Move it off email. Walk over or call. Poltergeists are bold in text and gentle in person.

    incantation“Hi Felicity, I got your email, sorry about 14B. Have you got two minutes so I understand the booking problem?”

  2. 2The Literal Reply

    Reply only to the content, cheerfully, as if the tone weren't there. You can't fight with someone who doesn't take the bait.

    incantation“Thanks Felicity, my mistake, I'll use the form from now on. Have a good day!”

  3. 3The Uncopying

    When your manager was copied, reply to the sender only, with the friendly literal reply. Never reply-all to a poltergeist.

  4. 4The Name for It

    When it's a pattern with a peer, name it once, kindly, in private.

    incantation“I've noticed some of our emails get a bit tense. Can we just talk when something bugs you? I'd much rather know.”

VForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Counter-Haunting

    Replying in the same style. “Per YOUR last email.” It's satisfying for one second and screenshotted forever.

  • The Escalation Spiral

    Adding your manager because they added yours. Now there are four managers reading about a meeting room.

  • The Anonymous Kitchen Note

    Fighting the sign with a sign. You are now a Poltergeist too. The kitchen is lost.

VIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

A bad day in text form. The Literal Reply ends it.

Nightmare

A pattern: every slip gets a CC'd email. The Summoning to the Flesh, then the Name for It. Most of the time, the emails soften within weeks.

Hell

The Poltergeist is building a paper case against you, CC'ing HR on trivia. Keep your own calm replies, never match the tone, and let your manager know early, so the CCs land on someone who's already heard your side.

VIIILoot drop

⚿The Cool Water Reply

Reply to the content, never the tone. Sender only. Done in thirty seconds.

Hi Felicity,

Thanks for flagging, that's on me. I'll use the booking form
from now on.

Have a good day,
Pip

IXThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

A colleague emails you with “as I mentioned twice before” and copies your manager. First move?

Reply to them only, with the facts and no tone. Then, if it matters, walk over or call. Never reply-all.

Your manager asks why Facilities is emailing about you. What do you say?

One line, calm: “I booked a room without the form, my mistake, I've apologised and it's sorted.” Owning a small slip makes you look bigger than the email.

Is a passive-aggressive kitchen sign ever worth answering?

Only by fixing the problem (wash the mug). Never by another sign.

The Annotator's last wordThe tone is their problem. The facts are yours. Answer the facts.

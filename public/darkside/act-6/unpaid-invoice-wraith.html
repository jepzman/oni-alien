The Unpaid Invoice Wraith

The work is done, the client is happy, and the money is a ghost.

Threat
Habitat
accounts payable, missing PO numbers, “next payment run”, 90-day terms
Weakness
the right paperwork, early, and a polite, steady, written cadence

IA sighting

Friday 1 September 2028 · the invoice

GridSight finished a water-demand study for Brackenfield Water. Hamish Grant, their operations director, loved it. Felix sent invoice 0047, £84,000, 30-day terms, to Hamish's email address, with a cheerful note.

October and November · the drift

Nothing. Hamish said it was “with finance”. Finance, it turned out, had never seen it: invoices without a PO number went into a shared mailbox nobody read. Felix assumed delivery was chasing. Delivery assumed Felix was. Phase two was due to start in December.

Wednesday 6 December · day 97

Asha noticed the invoice in the ledger, ninety-seven days old, and asked whose it was. Nobody's, it seemed. Pip rang Hamish, kindly, and asked who in finance could help. Hamish gave him a name: Moira Venn, accounts payable.

Thursday 7 December · Moira

Moira was not the villain. She needed a PO number, the invoice addressed to “Accounts Payable, Brackenfield Water”, and a PDF, not a link. Hamish raised the PO that afternoon. Pip resent the invoice. Moira confirmed receipt. It was paid on 19 December, in the next payment run, exactly as she'd said.

— the AnnotatorMost unpaid invoices aren't unpaid. They're lost, because nobody asked how this client pays.

IIOrigin

flashbackHow invoice 0047 became a wraith

  1. 2015 · Brackenfield's controls

    After a fraud, Brackenfield Water rules that no invoice is paid without a purchase order number. Very sensible.

  2. 2020 · the shared mailbox

    Invoices without POs are routed to a mailbox for “manual review”. The person who reviewed it leaves in 2023. Nobody replaces them.

  3. July 2028 · GridSight starts work

    Hamish is keen; the work starts on a handshake and an email. Nobody asks for a PO. Nobody knows to.

  4. September 2028 · the invoice is sent

    To Hamish, who forwards it to the mailbox, where it lies for ninety-seven days, unread and unpaid.

The turning point. Starting work without asking the dullest question in business: “How do you pay suppliers?”

What might have saved them. The Paper Ward, at kick-off: PO number, invoice address, terms. Two minutes. £84,000.

— the AnnotatorAccounts payable aren't trying not to pay you. They're trying not to pay fraudsters. Help them tell the difference.

IIIKnow thy enemy

Unpaid invoices are rarely about the client refusing to pay. Usually the paperwork is wrong: no PO, the wrong address, the wrong format, the wrong person. Sometimes it's cash flow, and sometimes it's tactics. Your job is to find out which, quickly and politely.

What it wants

The right paperwork. Finance teams pay what fits their process. Anything else drifts.

Why it haunts

Because chasing feels awkward, nobody on your side owns it, and the client contact who loved your work has no idea how their own finance team works.

What lays it to rest

  • A PO number before work starts.
  • One owner for chasing, on your side.
  • A named contact in their finance team.
  • A pause clause in the contract.

Three kinds of wraith

The Lost Wraith

Paperwork problem. Fix the paperwork; it's paid.

The Slow Wraith

Cash-flow trouble. Agree a schedule, in writing.

The Tactical Wraith

They're using you as a free loan. Pause clause, then escalation.

— the AnnotatorNever let “who's chasing?” go unanswered. Write the owner's name next to every invoice.

IVSigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • The client loves your work and hasn't paid for it.
  • “It's with finance” has been the answer for six weeks.
  • Nobody on your side knows who to chase.
  • You're about to start phase two of an unpaid phase one.

VCounter-spells

  1. 1The Paper Ward

    Before you start, get the PO number, the invoice address and the payment terms in writing.

    incantation“Before we kick off, could you send the PO number and the address invoices should go to? It helps it get paid first time.”

  2. 2The Polite Cadence

    Chase on a schedule: a reminder at due date, a call at +14 days, an escalation at +30. Always friendly, always written.

  3. 3The Pause Clause

    Agree in the contract that work pauses if invoices are more than 60 days late. Then you can use it without a fight.

    incantation“As per clause 9, we'll need to pause phase two until invoice 0047 is settled. I'd much rather not; can we sort it this week?”

VIForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Awkward Silence

    Not chasing because it feels rude. It isn't rude; it's your money.

  • The Angry Letter

    Sending a furious email to your client contact. They're usually not the problem, and now they're not your friend.

  • Starting Phase Two Anyway

    Doing more unpaid work to be nice. Now they owe you double.

VIIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

The Lost Wraith.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • The client is happy.
  • Nobody refuses to pay.
  • Something's wrong with the paperwork.

Do

  1. Ask your contact who in finance can help.
  2. Ask finance exactly what they need.
  3. Resend it, and confirm receipt.

Don’t: The angry letter.

If it gets worse: If it's still unpaid at 60 days, you're at Nightmare.

Nightmare

The Slow Wraith.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • The paperwork is right.
  • Payment keeps slipping.
  • Phase two is about to start.

Do

  1. Talk to your contact's boss, kindly.
  2. Agree a payment schedule in writing.
  3. Don't start new work until it's settled.
  4. Tell your finance team.

Don’t: Starting phase two anyway.

If it gets worse: If they're doing it on purpose, you're at Hell.

Hell

The Tactical Wraith.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • They pay everyone late, on purpose.
  • Excuses change every time.
  • They want more work anyway.

Do

  1. Use the pause clause.
  2. Escalate formally, with dates.
  3. Consider late-payment interest (in the UK, the law allows it).
  4. Read Banishing a Client.

Don’t: The awkward silence.

If it gets worse: If they still don't pay, take advice on debt recovery.

VIIIAre you the Wraith?

Have you been the client who didn't pay, without meaning to? Tick honestly.

  • You've let a supplier start work before raising a PO.
  • You don't know how your own finance team pays suppliers.
  • You've forwarded an invoice and never checked it was paid.
  • A supplier has chased you and you felt annoyed, not sorry.
  • You've asked for more work from a supplier you haven't paid.

The cure, if you ticked two: Next time you bring in a supplier, send them the PO number and your finance contact on day one. It's the kindest thing a client can do.

IXThe rehearsal

The Paper Ward, ninety-seven days late.

Scene: Thursday 7 December 2028, Pip calls Moira Venn.

PipHi Moira, I'm Pip from GridSight. Hamish suggested you. We've an invoice from September I think got lost. I'd love to know what you need.

MoiraHave you got a PO number?

PipNo, and I think that's the problem. What else do you need?

MoiraAddressed to Accounts Payable, as a PDF. With a PO, it'll be in the next run, on the nineteenth.

PipBrilliant. I'll get the PO from Hamish today. Can I check it's arrived before I hang up next time?

MoiraYou'd be the first. Yes, please.

— the AnnotatorPaid on 19 December. GridSight's kick-off checklist now starts with: “PO number. Invoice address. Finance contact.”

XThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

Your invoice is 30 days late. Who do you contact?

Your client contact first, kindly, to ask who in finance can help. Then that person directly.

Finance says they never got it. What now?

Ask exactly what they need (PO, address, format), resend it, and confirm they've received it.

Is it ever right to stop work over an invoice?

Yes, if it's agreed in the contract and you've chased properly. Say it calmly and early.

The Annotator's last wordGetting paid is part of the job. Do it as carefully as the work.

The Labyrinth of Procurement

You've won. Now walk through fourteen forms, a security review, two lawyers and a Minotaur called Insurance.

Threat
Habitat
supplier onboarding, security questionnaires, legal redlines, insurance certificates
Weakness
a map, a guide, starting early, and answers you can reuse

IA sighting

Thursday 30 November 2028 · the win

GridSight won Northshire. Felix popped a bottle of fizzy wine. Pip said they could start in two weeks. Bernard Quill, from Northshire procurement, sent a polite email the next morning, with attachments.

December · the forms

Fourteen forms. A supplier registration. A modern slavery statement. Two insurance certificates (one of which GridSight, being six months old, didn't have). A data-protection impact assessment. And a security questionnaire of 412 questions, including “Describe your physical controls for your data centre.” GridSight used MetroVolt's.

Monday 11 December · the map

Pip rang Bernard and asked for the full list of steps, owners and timings. Bernard was delighted: nobody ever asked. He sent a spreadsheet. Pip ran legal, security and insurance in parallel instead of one after another, and borrowed MetroVolt's security team's answers for 300 of the 412 questions.

Wednesday 21 February 2029 · the centre

Signed, twelve weeks after the win. Bernard said the average was twenty. Asha saved every answer in a folder called “The Answer Vault”. The next labyrinth, from Ardmore in April, took five weeks.

— the AnnotatorProcurement isn't the enemy. It's a maze with a guide. The guide is usually lonely. Ask for the map.

IIOrigin

flashbackHow Northshire's labyrinth was built

  1. 2011 · the first wall

    A public body elsewhere hires a vendor that turns out to have no insurance. Questions about insurance enter every form in the land.

  2. 2017 · the next walls

    A data breach, a modern slavery scandal, a supplier collapse. Each produces a new form. None is removed.

  3. 2023 · the security questionnaire

    A cyber attack on a council. Northshire's questionnaire grows from 40 questions to 412, mostly copied from a national template.

  4. 2028 · the labyrinth

    Every wall was built for a good reason. Together they make a maze that takes twenty weeks to walk through.

The turning point. Each scandal adding a new form, and nobody ever taking one away.

What might have saved them. Nothing in Northshire's power; it's national rules. What saves vendors is a map, a guide and a vault.

— the AnnotatorEvery form in a procurement process exists because something once went badly wrong. Respect that, and fill it in quickly.

IIIKnow thy enemy

Procurement is the process a buyer uses to bring a supplier on board: registration, legal terms, security, insurance, data protection. For public bodies and large firms it can take months. It exists to protect buyers from bad suppliers, so it treats every supplier as a possible bad one, including you.

What it wants

Evidence that you're safe: insured, secure, legal and solvent. Not opinions. Evidence.

Why it's slow

Because steps run one after another, nobody owns the whole path, and every answer is written from scratch.

What speeds it

  • The full map, up front.
  • Parallel paths.
  • A vault of reusable answers.
  • A friendly guide.

Three kinds of labyrinth

The Simple Maze

A few forms, a quick sign-off. Fill them in fast.

The Public Labyrinth

National rules, many walls. Map, parallel, vault.

The Endless Labyrinth

The buyer keeps adding steps. Ask your sponsor in the buyer to push.

— the AnnotatorYour client's project sponsor wants you to start too. Tell them where you're stuck; they can often unlock it.

IVSigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • You won the contract three months ago and haven't started.
  • A new form arrives every time you finish one.
  • Legal on both sides are emailing each other, not you.
  • Nobody can tell you what's left.

VCounter-spells

  1. 1The Map Request

    Ask the procurement officer for the full list of steps, owners and timings, up front.

    incantation“Bernard, could you send me every step between now and signature, and who owns each one? I'll keep a tracker.”

  2. 2The Answer Vault

    Keep every security answer, policy and certificate in one place. The next labyrinth asks the same questions.

  3. 3The Parallel Path

    Run legal, security and insurance at the same time, not one after another.

VIForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Sequential Shuffle

    Waiting for one form to clear before starting the next. Four months becomes eight.

  • Cursing the Guide

    Being rude to procurement. They're the only people who know the way out.

  • Starting Without Signature

    Beginning work before the contract is signed, to be helpful. If it falls through, nobody pays.

VIIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

The Simple Maze.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • A short list of forms.
  • A clear owner.
  • A reasonable timeline.

Do

  1. Fill everything in within a week.
  2. Confirm receipt of each.
  3. Start the Answer Vault.

Don’t: The sequential shuffle.

If it gets worse: If it's dozens of steps over months, you're at Nightmare.

Nightmare

The Public Labyrinth.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Many forms, several teams.
  • Security, legal and insurance all involved.
  • No visible end.

Do

  1. Ask for the full map.
  2. Run paths in parallel.
  3. Track weekly with the procurement officer.
  4. Borrow answers from your wider company.

Don’t: Cursing the guide.

If it gets worse: If new steps keep appearing, you're at Hell.

Hell

The Endless Labyrinth.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Steps keep being added.
  • Nobody can say what's left.
  • The project is months late before it starts.

Do

  1. Ask your client sponsor to intervene.
  2. Get a letter of intent if they want you to start.
  3. Agree a date for signature, in writing.
  4. Know your walk-away point.

Don’t: Starting without signature.

If it gets worse: If it never ends, it may be a no in disguise. Ask directly.

VIIIAre you building a labyrinth?

If you've ever made other people fill in forms. Tick honestly.

  • You've added a step to a process and never removed one.
  • You can't say how long your own process takes, end to end.
  • You ask suppliers questions you never read the answers to.
  • You run steps one after another by habit.
  • You've never asked a supplier how your process felt.

The cure, if you ticked two: Pick one form you own. Ask what would happen if it disappeared. If the answer is “nothing”, remove it.

IXThe rehearsal

The Map Request.

Scene: Monday 11 December 2028, Pip rings Bernard Quill.

PipBernard, could you send me every step between now and signature, with who owns each one? I'll keep a tracker.

Bernard…Nobody's ever asked me that. Yes. Give me an hour.

PipAnd can legal, security and insurance run at the same time?

BernardThey can. Vendors usually do them one by one, then complain it's slow.

PipWe'd like to be the other kind. Weekly check-in, fifteen minutes?

BernardThursdays. I'll bring biscuits.

— the AnnotatorBernard and Sven, it later emerged, buy biscuits from the same shop. Pip did not pursue this.

XThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

You've won, but the contract is stuck in procurement. What do you do?

The Map Request, then run everything in parallel and track it weekly.

The client wants you to start before signing. Should you?

Only with a written letter of intent that covers payment. Otherwise, no.

Is procurement just bureaucracy?

Partly. But it also protects the client from bad vendors. Help them do it fast.

The Annotator's last wordEvery labyrinth has a map. The procurement officer has it. Ask nicely.

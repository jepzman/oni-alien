The Overpromise Golem

Your own sales team made it out of slides and optimism. It walks, it talks, it doesn't exist.

Threat
Habitat
sales calls, signed contracts, “it's on the roadmap”, demos of things that are mockups
Weakness
the truth, told early, with options and a plan

IA sighting

Tuesday 26 September 2028 · the sale

Felix, at lunch with Hamish Grant of Brackenfield Water, sold phase two: a real-time AI forecasting module, live in six weeks, £120,000. He'd seen a mockup Asha had made for an internal demo. He believed, sincerely, it was “basically done”. The contract was signed on the Friday.

Monday 2 October · the discovery

Pip read the contract on Monday. Real-time didn't exist. AI didn't exist, unless you counted a regression model Asha had built in an afternoon. Six weeks meant 7 November. Asha said, very calmly, that the honest estimate was sixteen weeks for hourly forecasts, and that real-time needed sensor data Brackenfield didn't collect.

Tuesday 3 October · the Golem Audit

Pip wrote one page: what was promised, what existed, the gap, and three options. He took it to Ruth first. Then to Felix, who went pale, then defensive, then quiet. Then, with Ruth's backing, to Hamish.

Wednesday 4 October · the Early Truth

Hamish was angry for about four minutes. Then he asked what he'd get. Daily forecasts by 7 November. Hourly by January. Real-time when Brackenfield installed the sensors. Price unchanged. Hamish, who had been oversold by vendors before, said: “Nobody's ever told me this in week one. Usually it's week twenty.” He chose all three phases.

— the AnnotatorA golem made of promises falls apart on delivery day. Unmake it in week one, and you might keep the client.

IIOrigin

flashbackHow Felix came to sell a golem

  1. 2014 · the commission

    Felix starts in sales at a software company. He's paid on contracts signed, not delivered. He's very good at it.

  2. 2020 · the habit

    He learns that clients rarely cancel when delivery slips; they just complain. Overpromising has no cost to him.

  3. 2028 · GridSight

    MetroVolt hires Felix to grow GridSight fast. His bonus is on bookings. Nobody asks delivery to sign off proposals.

  4. 26 September · lunch

    He sees Asha's mockup, believes it's nearly real, and sells it. He isn't lying. He just never asked.

The turning point. Paying salespeople for promises, and never asking delivery whether the promise was possible.

What might have saved them. A rule: nothing goes in a proposal without delivery's sign-off. And a bonus that depends on delivery, not just signature.

— the AnnotatorMost overpromising isn't dishonesty. It's an incentive that rewards the promise and never meets the delivery.

IIIKnow thy enemy

The overpromise golem is a commitment your own side made that you can't keep: a feature that doesn't exist, a date that can't be met, a result nobody can guarantee. Usually sales made it, sincerely, under pressure. Delivery finds out when the contract lands.

What it wants

Nothing. It's a golem: it does what its maker's incentives told it to. Fixing it means changing the incentives.

Why it walks

Because sales and delivery don't talk before contracts are signed, and because salespeople are rewarded for signing.

What unmakes it

  • The truth, early.
  • Real options, not excuses.
  • One voice to the client.
  • Delivery sign-off on proposals.

Three kinds of golem

The Optimistic Golem

Sold in good faith, slightly too big. A replan fixes it.

The Mockup Golem

Sold from a demo of something unbuilt. Needs the Early Truth.

The Fantasy Golem

Sold knowing it was impossible. Needs the Early Truth and your boss's boss.

— the AnnotatorClients forgive a broken promise told early far more readily than one discovered late. Early is everything.

IVSigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • A contract lands with features you've never heard of.
  • The salesperson says “it's basically done”.
  • The demo the client saw was a mockup.
  • The delivery date was chosen before anyone asked delivery.

VCounter-spells

  1. 1The Golem Audit

    List exactly what was promised, what exists, and the gap, in writing, within a week.

  2. 2The Early Truth

    Tell the client soon, plainly, with what you can deliver and when. Bad news ages badly.

    incantation“I need to be straight with you. Real-time forecasting isn't ready. Here's what we can give you in six weeks, and what follows in sixteen.”

  3. 3The Sales–Delivery Seal

    Agree with sales that nothing new is sold without delivery's sign-off. Make it a rule, not a favour.

    incantation“Felix, from now on, can delivery sign off any feature before it goes in a proposal? It protects you too.”

VIForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Silent March

    Working nights to build the impossible thing and hoping. You'll miss the date and lose the client's trust as well.

  • Blaming Sales to the Client

    Telling the client your salesperson lied. It's true, maybe. It makes your whole company look broken.

  • Faking It

    Delivering a mockup and calling it the product. That's not a delay any more; it's a scandal.

VIIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

The Optimistic Golem.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • The promise is a bit too big.
  • The date is tight but possible with changes.
  • Sales will listen.

Do

  1. Do the Golem Audit.
  2. Replan with sales.
  3. Tell the client about any change early.

Don’t: The silent march.

If it gets worse: If what was sold doesn't exist, you're at Nightmare.

Nightmare

The Mockup Golem.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • The feature doesn't exist.
  • The client saw a demo.
  • The date is weeks away.

Do

  1. Golem Audit within days.
  2. Tell your boss first.
  3. Go to the client with sales, in one voice, with options.
  4. Agree the Sales–Delivery Seal.

Don’t: Blaming sales to the client.

If it gets worse: If sales refuses to admit it, you're at Hell.

Hell

The Fantasy Golem, and its maker denies it.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Sales insists it's possible.
  • Your boss wants to keep the deal.
  • The client is excited.

Do

  1. Write it all down, with dates.
  2. Escalate to your boss's boss.
  3. Refuse to fake it.
  4. Offer the best real alternative.

Don’t: Faking it.

If it gets worse: If you're forced to deliver a lie, think about whether this is where you want to work.

VIIIAre you the Golem-Maker?

Whether you sell to clients or to your own leadership. Tick honestly.

  • You've promised something before checking it could be done.
  • You've shown a mockup without saying it was a mockup.
  • You've given a date to please someone.
  • You've said “basically done” about something not done.
  • You've never asked the person who'll deliver it.

The cure, if you ticked two: Before your next promise, ask the person who'll deliver it: “Can we do this, by then?” Then say what they say.

IXThe rehearsal

The Early Truth, with one voice.

Scene: Wednesday 4 October 2028, Pip, Felix and Ruth meet Hamish Grant.

FelixHamish, I need to correct something I told you. I was wrong about what's ready.

PipReal-time forecasting isn't built. I'm sorry. Here's what we can give you, and when.

Hamish…You're telling me this now? Week one?

PipDaily forecasts by 7 November. Hourly by January. Real-time once you've installed sensors. Same price.

RuthAnd from now on, nothing goes into a GridSight proposal without delivery's sign-off. That's my commitment.

HamishNobody's ever told me this in week one. Usually it's week twenty. Fine. All three phases.

— the AnnotatorFelix asked Pip afterwards to review every proposal. His bonus scheme was changed in January to include delivery. He hated it, then didn't.

XThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

You discover sales promised a feature that doesn't exist. What first?

The Golem Audit: write down exactly what was promised and what's real.

When do you tell the client?

Within days, not weeks. With options. Bad news gets worse with age.

How do you stop it happening again?

The Sales–Delivery Seal, agreed with sales, backed by your boss.

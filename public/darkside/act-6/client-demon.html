The Client Demon

It pays your invoices, so it believes it owns your evenings, your weekends and your sense of self.

Threat
Habitat
late-night calls, weekend emails, “urgent” goals that change every Monday
Weakness
clear agreements, agreed rhythms, and a calm, written “here's what that changes”

IA sighting

Saturday 1 July 2028 · the first client

GridSight signed its first contract: a heat-pump and EV-charging demand map for Wexley Borough Council, due 31 August. The client lead was Gavin Pike, Head of Sustainability. He was charming at the kick-off. He gave Pip his mobile number “for anything at all” and asked for Pip's.

Tuesday 18 July · 22:07

Pip's phone lit up on the kitchen table. Gavin. A “quick thought” about adding social housing. Then again at 22:31, about bus depots. Then at 22:58: “Sorry, last one!” It wasn't. The goals document was on version 11. The deadline was still 31 August.

Thursday 20 July · the Rhythm Rite

Pip, who had not slept well, read this entry. He proposed a Tuesday call, a Friday written update, and same-working-day replies to email. Gavin said “absolutely, great idea”, and rang the following Monday at 21:40. Pip didn't answer. He replied at 08:30, warmly, with a Change Ledger. Gavin rang at 21:40 once more, the next week. Then stopped.

Tuesday 1 August · the fear

On the Tuesday call Pip asked what would happen if the map slipped. A long pause. Gavin had to present it to the full council on 12 September, and Nadia Sesay, his director, had told him his budget depended on it. The 22:00 calls weren't about the map. They were about Gavin's job.

— the AnnotatorDifficult clients are usually frightened clients. Find what they're frightened of and you'll find what they actually need.

IIOrigin

flashbackHow Gavin became the Client Demon

  1. 2019 · the first consultants

    Wexley hires a big consultancy for its climate plan. £400k, 200 slides, nothing usable. Gavin, newly appointed, is blamed for the bill he didn't sign.

  2. 2022 · the lesson

    Gavin decides vendors only deliver when watched. He starts checking in daily on every contract. It works, a bit. He becomes known as thorough.

  3. 2027 · the squeeze

    Council budgets are cut. Nadia tells Gavin his team's survival depends on showing results by autumn 2028.

  4. July 2028 · GridSight

    A new, small vendor, eager to please, gives him a mobile number. Every fear Gavin has finds a phone that answers.

The turning point. One bad vendor in 2019 taught Gavin that watching is the only safety. Budget cuts turned watching into haunting.

What might have saved them. A clear rhythm on day one, and someone asking, early, “What happens to you if this slips?”

— the AnnotatorThe demon isn't the client. It's the client's fear, wearing the client's phone number.

IIIKnow thy enemy

A difficult client is usually a client under pressure, from their boss, their budget or their last bad vendor, who's found that pushing you is the one thing they can control. They change goals because their own goals keep changing. They call late because they're worrying late.

What it wants

Certainty. Proof the project won't fail them. Usually, protection from someone above them.

Why it haunts

Because nobody set a rhythm, so every worry becomes a call. And because you answered the first one.

What it fears

  • Being blamed for another failed vendor.
  • Surprises.
  • Looking foolish in front of their boss.
  • Losing control of the timeline.

Three kinds of client demon

The Worrier

Frightened, not cruel. A rhythm and regular written updates calm it.

The Shape-Shifter

Goals change weekly. The Change Ledger makes the cost visible.

The Tyrant

Abusive, contemptuous, never satisfied. If it won't change, see Banishing a Client.

— the AnnotatorMost clients are Worriers. Treat a Worrier like a Tyrant and you'll create one.

IVSigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • The client calls your mobile at night and you answer, every time.
  • The goals change each week but the deadline never does.
  • Nothing is written down; every call starts from scratch.
  • You dread their name in your inbox more than your own manager's.

VCounter-spells

  1. 1The Rhythm Rite

    Agree a weekly call, a weekly written update and a response time. Then keep to them, both ways.

    incantation“Let's do a call every Tuesday at ten and a written update every Friday. Anything urgent in between, email me and I'll reply the same working day.”

  2. 2The Change Ledger

    Write down every change in goals, with what it does to time and cost, and send it after each call.

    incantation“Just confirming today's change: adding EV chargers to the map. That moves delivery from 30 September to 14 October. Shall I go ahead?”

  3. 3The Fear Finder

    Ask what's driving the pressure. Difficult clients are usually frightened clients with a boss of their own.

    incantation“What happens on your side if this slips? I'd like to help you with that, not just the map.”

VIForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Always-On Offering

    Answering every call at any hour. You've trained them, and now it's the contract.

  • The Silent Absorb

    Saying yes to every change without saying what it costs. The cost arrives anyway, as your weekend.

  • The Moan-Back

    Complaining about the client to someone who knows the client. It always gets back.

VIIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

The Worrier.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • They call often but they're polite.
  • Their worry has a clear cause.
  • They respond well to updates.

Do

  1. Set the Rhythm Rite in week one.
  2. Send a written update every Friday, even if nothing changed.
  3. Ask what they're worried about.

Don’t: Answering every late call.

If it gets worse: If the goals keep changing, you're at Nightmare.

Nightmare

The Shape-Shifter.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • The goals change every week.
  • The deadline never moves.
  • Nothing is written down.

Do

  1. Start the Change Ledger today.
  2. Send it after every call.
  3. Ask them to choose: date, scope or cost.
  4. Tell your boss early.

Don’t: The silent absorb.

If it gets worse: If they become abusive, you're at Hell.

Hell

The Tyrant.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • They're rude, especially to junior staff.
  • Nothing is ever good enough.
  • They threaten to leave, or to escalate, often.

Do

  1. Document every incident.
  2. Involve your boss and theirs.
  3. Protect your team: take their calls yourself.
  4. Read Banishing a Client.

Don’t: Sacrificing your team to keep them.

If it gets worse: If it doesn't change after a clear warning, banish.

VIIIAre you the Client Demon?

You probably are a client to someone: a vendor, an agency, another team. Tick honestly.

  • You've emailed or called a supplier after 21:00 about something non-urgent.
  • Your requirements have changed since you signed, and you haven't asked what that costs.
  • You've never told a vendor what's really driving your deadline.
  • You've threatened to “take this higher” before talking to their manager.
  • You think vendors only work when watched.

The cure, if you ticked two: Tell your vendor what you're worried about. One honest sentence about your real deadline saves weeks of late-night calls.

IXThe rehearsal

The Fear Finder, then the Change Ledger.

Scene: Tuesday 1 August 2028, the weekly call with Gavin.

PipBefore the updates, can I ask: what happens on your side if this slips?

Gavin…I present to full council on the twelfth of September. Nadia's made it clear my budget rests on it.

PipThank you. That helps. Then let's make sure you have something great on the twelfth, even if not everything.

GavinBut I need the bus depots and social housing too.

PipThen let's choose. Core map by 31 August for council. Depots and housing in phase two. I'll send it in writing today.

Gavin…Yes. That actually works. Sorry about the calls.

— the AnnotatorGavin presented on 12 September. The council voted to fund phase two. He rang at 22:00 once more, to say thank you, and apologised for the time.

XThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

Your client rings at 22:00 about a non-urgent change. What do you do?

Don't answer. Reply the next morning, warmly, and propose the Rhythm Rite.

Their goals changed again. The deadline didn't. What now?

The Change Ledger: write down what changed and what it does to the date. Ask them to choose.

Is the difficult client just a bad person?

Rarely. Usually they're scared of someone above them. Find the fear and help with it.

The Annotator's last wordClients don't respect boundaries you never set. Set them early, kindly, and in writing.

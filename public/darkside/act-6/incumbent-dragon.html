Act VI · The Outer Realms · entry 6 of 12

The Incumbent Dragon

It's been there twelve years, sleeps on your data, and breathes the word “risk” at anyone who comes near.

Threat
Habitat
long contracts, auto-renewals, systems nobody else understands
Weakness
an exit plan, knowledge you own, and a fair, real competition

IA sighting

January 2029 · the renewal

MetroVolt's billing contract with Halvorsen Systems was due to renew in April. It had renewed automatically in 2020 and 2024. Nobody remembered why Halvorsen had been chosen in 2016. Ruth asked Pip, as someone who now knew what vendors were like from the other side, to sit on the evaluation.

The hoard

Pip did the Hoard Count. Halvorsen held: twelve years of billing data in a format only they could export, 34 custom integrations nobody at MetroVolt had documented, and the only two people who understood the tariff engine. Every internal conversation about switching ended with someone saying “far too risky”. Usually after a call with Sven.

February · the real contest

MetroVolt went to market, properly. Pip told Sven, plainly, that Halvorsen was welcome to bid and would be judged on the same criteria as everyone. Sven, who had brought biscuits to every review meeting for twelve years, looked genuinely startled. Then he went back to Halvorsen and, for the first time in years, they sharpened.

March 2029 · the outcome

Halvorsen won. Their bid was 18% cheaper than their last renewal, included a full data-export clause and documentation of all 34 integrations, and offered three new features. Pip's verdict: “The dragon is still here. It's just not asleep any more.”

— the AnnotatorYou don't have to slay the dragon. You just have to make it fly.

IIOrigin

flashbackHow Halvorsen became a dragon

  1. 2016 · the good choice

    MetroVolt picks Halvorsen after a proper tender. They're genuinely the best. The project goes well.

  2. 2020 · the first renewal

    The contract renews automatically. Nobody reviews it; things work. Halvorsen quietly builds custom integrations.

  3. 2024 · the hoard

    Only Halvorsen understands the tariff engine. Switching would mean rebuilding twelve years of knowledge. The contract renews again.

  4. 2029 · the dragon

    Halvorsen isn't evil. It's comfortable. And comfort, over twelve years, becomes lock-in.

The turning point. The 2020 renewal, unexamined because things were working.

What might have saved them. An exit plan and a documentation clause in the 2016 contract, and an honest review at every renewal.

— the AnnotatorMost incumbent dragons were once the best choice. They became dragons because nobody asked again.

IIIKnow thy enemy

An incumbent vendor is one that's been there for years. They know your systems, hold your data and understand your quirks. That makes them valuable and hard to replace. Over time it can make them complacent, expensive and the only people who understand how your business works.

What it wants

Renewal. Stability. Not to be put through the effort of a competition it might lose.

Why it grows

Because every year without review adds more knowledge to its hoard, and makes switching seem scarier.

What weakens it

  • Your own documentation.
  • A data-export clause.
  • A real competition.
  • A credible alternative.

Three kinds of dragon

The Sleeping Dragon

Comfortable, not malicious. A real contest wakes it up.

The Hoarding Dragon

Keeps knowledge to itself on purpose. Demand documentation in the contract.

The Good Dragon

Still the best. Renew, with an exit plan anyway.

— the AnnotatorIf you're the incumbent: the day your client stops asking for new ideas is the day they start planning to leave.

IVSigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • Nobody remembers why this vendor was chosen.
  • The contract renews automatically and nobody checks.
  • Only the vendor understands how your system works.
  • Every alternative is described, by the vendor, as “very risky”.

VCounter-spells

  1. 1The Hoard Count

    Find out what the dragon holds: data, knowledge, integrations. Start bringing it back in-house, gently.

  2. 2The Real Contest

    Run a fair competition, and let the incumbent compete. Sometimes they're still the best. Sometimes they just need to sharpen.

    incantation“Sven, we're going to market for the renewal. You're very welcome to bid. We'll judge on the same criteria as everyone.”

  3. 3The Dragon's Own Counter (if you are the dragon)

    Never coast. Bring a new idea every year, and never treat renewal as a right.

    incantation“Before you ask: here's what we'd change in the next contract, and what it'd save you.”

VIForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Silent Renewal

    Letting the contract roll over because reviewing it is hard. Twelve years happen that way.

  • The Slay-at-Any-Cost

    Switching vendors to make a point, without an exit plan. You'll wake a worse dragon.

  • The Smug Hoard

    If you're the incumbent: assuming they can't leave. They're just waiting for a reason.

VIIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

The Good Dragon.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • The vendor still performs well.
  • Pricing is fair.
  • You could leave if you had to.

Do

  1. Review at every renewal.
  2. Keep an exit plan current.
  3. Ask them for one new idea a year.

Don’t: The silent renewal.

If it gets worse: If you couldn't leave if you wanted to, you're at Nightmare.

Nightmare

The Sleeping Dragon, sitting on your hoard.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Only they understand key systems.
  • Prices rise every renewal.
  • Everyone says switching is too risky.

Do

  1. Do the Hoard Count.
  2. Start documenting in-house.
  3. Run a real competition.
  4. Add an export clause to the next contract.

Don’t: The slay-at-any-cost.

If it gets worse: If they're blocking you deliberately, you're at Hell.

Hell

The Hoarding Dragon.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • They refuse to share documentation.
  • They lobby your executives directly.
  • Data exports are “not possible”.

Do

  1. Get legal to review data ownership.
  2. Brief your executives first, with facts.
  3. Plan a staged exit.
  4. Talk to other clients of theirs.

Don’t: Any switch without an exit plan.

If it gets worse: If you must leave, leave slowly, piece by piece.

VIIIAre you the Dragon?

As a vendor, a team, or a person who's been in a role for years. Tick honestly.

  • You're the only person who understands something important, and you like it that way.
  • You haven't brought your client a new idea this year.
  • You assume your contract or role will renew.
  • You describe every alternative to you as “risky”.
  • Your documentation lives mostly in your head.

The cure, if you ticked two: Write down one thing only you know, and share it. Dragons that share their hoard get renewed because they're good, not because they're stuck.

IXThe rehearsal

The Real Contest, said plainly.

Scene: Monday 5 February 2029, Pip calls Sven Aldous.

PipSven, I wanted to tell you directly: we're going to market for the billing renewal.

SvenAh. After twelve years. Switching would be very risky, Pip.

PipIt might be. That's why we'd love you to bid. You'll be judged on the same criteria as everyone.

Sven…What would make our bid stand out?

PipHonestly? A data-export clause, documentation for the integrations, and something new. We haven't had something new since 2019.

SvenThat's… fair. I'll take it back.

— the AnnotatorSven took it back. Halvorsen's bid was their best in a decade. He still brings biscuits.

XThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

Your team says switching from a twelve-year vendor is too risky. Is it?

Maybe. Find out with the Hoard Count, then decide on evidence, not fear.

You run a fair contest and the incumbent wins. Did it fail?

No. You now have a better contract, a sharper vendor, and an exit plan.

You're the incumbent and a rival is circling. What do you do?

Bring the client something new before they ask. Never wait for the tender.

The Annotator's last wordA dragon is only invincible if you never count its hoard.

The Darkside of the Corporation · Act III

The Throne Room

You cannot slay your lord. But you can manage them

People don't leave companies. They leave managers. Pip did not leave MetroVolt in the winter of 2026. But he looked at the door quite often.

After the reorg in Act I, Grid Analytics moved into Strategy, and Pip moved with it. His new lord was Victor Kraal, recently arrived from a consultancy, who brought with him a 47-slide operating model and a habit of asking for updates on the updates. Above Victor sat Benedict Crane, Director of Strategy, last seen in person in October. Above them both, for one day each month, descended Rupert Thorne from the parent group.

— the AnnotatorA bad manager is the single biggest risk to your sanity at work. The good news: most bad managers are not evil. They're anxious, untrained, overloaded or badly managed themselves. That gives you a lot to work with.

This Act is about the person who writes your review, approves your leave and decides which work you get. You can't choose them. You can't fire them. But you can manage them: understand what drives them, give them what they need, protect yourself where you must, and recognise when it's time to leave the throne room.

How to read an entry

  1. A sighting: the creature in the wild, at MetroVolt Electric Corporation, scene by scene.
  2. Origin: how it came to be. Most monsters were bitten once, and the company rewarded what happened next.
  3. Know thy enemy: what it wants, why it thrives, what it fears, and its three breeds.
  4. Signs and omens, counter-spells (with the exact words: the incantation) and the forbidden spells you must not cast.
  5. Difficulty (Normal, Nightmare, Hell): how to tell which you're facing, and what to do at each.
  6. Are you the creature? A mirror. Then the rehearsal (the counter-spells, said out loud) and the trial.

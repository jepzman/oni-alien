The Absent Lord

Never there when you need them. Always there when it goes wrong.

Threat
Habitat
airports, board away-days, a calendar of solid purple
Weakness
short written choices with a default and a deadline

IA sighting

October to February · the calendar

Benedict Crane, Director of Strategy, had a calendar that looked like a bruise: solid purple, marked “travelling”. In four months he was in The Grid for nine days.

Monday 6 January · the question

Pip needed one decision for the Q1 forecast: the regulator's heatwave scenario, or MetroVolt's own? He emailed. He messaged. He booked a call, which moved three times, then disappeared.

Monday 20 January

After two weeks of silence, Victor decided (the regulator's) so the work could continue. Pip noted it in the project log.

Thursday 6 February · the board

Benedict, back for one day, asked at the board with real anger: “Why was I not consulted on the scenario?” Victor looked at Pip. Pip looked at the email thread, which contained six follow-ups, a meeting request and a message that began “Sorry to chase again”.

— the AnnotatorAn absent boss is a boss who'll read the first two lines on their phone in a taxi. Write the first two lines for the taxi.

IIOrigin

flashbackHow a sharp analyst became the Absent Lord

  1. 2015 · the best analyst

    Benedict is the best analyst in MetroVolt's strategy team. He's promoted, then promoted again, each time for being “strategic”.

  2. 2020 · the committees

    At MetroVolt, “strategic” means being in rooms. Benedict joins one committee, then five, then fourteen. Each one wants him in person.

  3. 2023 · the inbox

    Benedict now gets 400 emails a day. He reads the subject lines, answers what's on fire, and plans to come back to the rest. He never does.

  4. 2026 · the ghost of himself

    His team sees him nine days in four months. He cares about them. He just has nothing left for them by the time he gets back. Then, at the board, his own boss asks a question, and suddenly everything matters at once.

The turning point. Being rewarded for being in every room, until there was no time left for his own.

What might have saved them. A boss who measured him on his team's output, not his committee count. Or a chief of staff to do for him what Ottoline does for the CEO.

— the AnnotatorMost absent bosses aren't uncaring. They're drowning in a job designed to make them absent. Short, decidable messages are a kindness to them, too.

IIIKnow thy enemy

Absent managers are usually overloaded and badly organised, not uncaring. Long questions get postponed. Open questions get ignored. Then, when it matters, they suddenly care, because someone above them is asking. The fix is to make deciding effortless: one short message, two options, your recommendation, a default and a deadline.

What it wants

To stay informed without effort, and not to be embarrassed in front of its own boss.

Why it thrives here

Because MetroVolt rewards senior people for being in meetings, not for being reachable. And their teams, too polite to decide without them, wait.

What it fears

  • Being surprised by its own boss.
  • Decisions made without it, silently.
  • Long emails it can't answer from a taxi.
  • Being shown, in writing, how long it took to reply.

The three breeds

The Overloaded

Too many rooms. Grateful for short, decidable messages.

The Strategist

Thinks detail is beneath them. Needs to know what's above the detail.

The Ghost

Has checked out. Decide with your direct manager and keep a record.

— the AnnotatorIf they won't decide, make not deciding a decision too. In writing. With a date.

IVSigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • Meetings with them move three times.
  • Your questions get “let's discuss” and never get discussed.
  • Decisions stall, then they're angry about the delay.
  • They seem surprised by work they assigned.

VCounter-spells

  1. 1The Default Rune

    Send one short message: the decision, two options, your recommendation, and what you'll do by when if you hear nothing.

    incantation“Decision needed: Q1 scenario. A) regulator's, B) ours. I recommend A. If I don't hear by Thursday 12:00, I'll go with A.”

  2. 2The Taxi Summary

    Put the ask in the subject line and the first line. Assume they read nothing else.

    incantationSubject: “DECIDE BY THU: Q1 heatwave scenario (A or B, I recommend A)”

  3. 3The Standing Slot

    Ask for a regular 15 minutes, even fortnightly. Short, fixed meetings survive busy calendars; long ad-hoc ones don't.

    incantation“Could we have 15 minutes every other Monday? I'll bring a list of decisions and nothing else.”

VIForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Waiting Game

    Doing nothing until they reply. The work stalls, and the delay becomes yours.

  • The Silent Override

    Deciding without telling them. When they find out, it's insubordination, however sensible it was.

  • The Passive Reminder

    “Just bumping this up 🙂” for the sixth time. It's a seventh unread message. Change the format, not the frequency.

VIIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

The Overloaded. Busy, but reachable.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • They reply to short messages.
  • They're apologetic about delays.
  • They do read subject lines.

Do

  1. Use the Default Rune: two options, your recommendation, a default, a deadline.
  2. Put the ask in the subject line.
  3. Ask for a 15-minute standing slot.

Don’t: The waiting game: doing nothing until they reply.

If it gets worse: If they're unreachable for weeks, you're at Nightmare.

Nightmare

The Strategist or the Ghost. Unreachable for weeks.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Decisions stall for weeks.
  • Your manager can't reach them either.
  • They're angry about delays they caused.

Do

  1. Agree in advance which decisions your manager can make in their absence.
  2. Default decisions, in writing, copied to your manager.
  3. Keep a decision log they can read in five minutes.
  4. Book their assistant, not them.

Don’t: The silent override: deciding without telling them.

If it gets worse: If they're absent and then blame you, you're at Hell.

Hell

Absent, then blaming.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • You're blamed for decisions you couldn't get.
  • Your follow-ups are ignored, then disputed.
  • Your manager is caught in the middle.

Do

  1. Every decision in writing, with defaults and dates.
  2. Copy your direct manager on all of it.
  3. Keep calm records; never “as per my six emails”.
  4. Ask, once things calm down, how they'd like decisions flagged.

Don’t: The passive reminder: “just bumping this 🙂” for the sixth time. Change the format, not the frequency.

If it gets worse: If blame lands on you regardless, raise the pattern with your manager and skip-level, with the record.

VIIIAre you absent?

Busy people become absent without noticing. Tick honestly.

  • Someone in your team is waiting for a decision from you right now.
  • You've rescheduled the same 1:1 three times.
  • You have more than 50 unread emails from your own team.
  • You've been surprised by work you assigned.
  • You've been angry about a delay you caused.

The cure, if you ticked two: Give your team a written list of what they can decide without you, and a default rule: if you haven't replied in three days, they proceed and tell you.

IXThe rehearsal

The Default Rune and the Taxi Summary: one decision, decidable from a phone.

Scene: Monday 6 January, 09:12. Pip writes the email again.

Subject: DECIDE BY THU 12:00: Q1 heatwave scenario (I recommend A)

PipBenedict, one decision for the Q1 forecast. A) regulator's heatwave scenario (I recommend; the board expects it). B) our own (2% lower peak). If I don't hear by Thursday 12:00, I'll use A. Victor is copied and agrees.

Thursday, 11:48. A reply arrives from an airport.

BenedictA. Thanks for making it easy.

February's board: nobody asks why Benedict wasn't consulted. He was. In four words, from Gate 22.

— the AnnotatorSame question as before. The only change was the format: subject line, two options, a default, a deadline. The busiest man in MetroVolt answered in under three days.

XThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

Your director hasn't answered a key question in two weeks. What do you send?

The Default Rune: two options, your recommendation, a default and a deadline. In the subject line.

They're angry they weren't consulted, but you have six emails. What do you say?

Calmly, without “as per my emails”: “I'm sorry it didn't reach you in time. I'll forward the decision note from the 3rd. How would you like me to flag these in future?”

Is it OK to decide without your boss?

Only with an agreed default or a clear mandate, and always in writing. Never silently.

The Annotator's last wordIf they won't decide, make not deciding a decision too. In writing. With a date.

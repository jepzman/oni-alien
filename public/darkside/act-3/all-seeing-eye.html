The All-Seeing Eye

Watches every keystroke. Trusts none of them.

Threat
Habitat
your Teams status, shared drafts, “quick check-ins” at 9, 11, 2 and 4
Weakness
predictable, proactive updates, before it asks

IA sighting

In his first week, Victor asked Pip for a status update four times a day. He edited Pip's emails before they were sent. He asked Pip to share his screen “just to see where you are”. He moved a decimal point in the load model himself, at 23:10, and left a comment: “Fixed. Pls check before sending things.”

On Tuesday there was a sticky note on Pip's monitor: “UPDATE PLS (3rd time today) — V.” Victor sat four metres away. He could have just asked. He had, in fact, asked. Twice.

IIKnow thy enemy

Micromanagers are usually driven by anxiety, not contempt. They're new, or under pressure from above, or they've been burned by someone who missed a deadline. Watching closely feels like control. Not knowing feels like danger. Every unanswered question becomes a check-in.

So the cure is to remove the not-knowing. If they get regular, predictable information before they ask, the anxiety drops, and the hovering usually follows. It's not fair that you have to do this. It works anyway.

— the AnnotatorMicromanagement is a trust problem wearing a productivity costume. Feed the trust and the costume falls off.

IIISigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • Several “quick check-ins” a day.
  • They rewrite your work rather than comment on it.
  • Requests to share your screen or copy them on everything.
  • Decisions you used to make now need their approval.
  • Sticky notes. On your monitor. From four metres away.

IVCounter-spells

  1. 1The Rhythm Ward

    Offer updates at fixed times before they ask: a short note at 10:00 and 16:00. Predictability kills anxiety.

    incantation“To keep you in the loop without interrupting, I'll send a three-line update at 10 and 4 every day. Does that work?”

  2. 2The Pre-emptive Strike

    Flag risks and decisions early. A micromanager hates surprises more than problems.

    incantation“Heads-up: the substation data is late, so the forecast moves to Thursday. Here's my plan B.”

  3. 3The Trust Ladder

    After two weeks of reliable updates, ask to step down to fewer: daily, then twice a week.

    incantation“The updates seem to be working. Could we try one at the end of each day, and see if that's enough?”

  4. 4The Working Agreement

    Agree, explicitly, which decisions you make alone and which need them.

    incantation“Can we agree: I decide model choices and formatting; you approve anything going to Benedict or above?”

VForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Blackout

    Going quiet to show you don't need watching. To an anxious manager, silence means disaster. The hovering doubles.

  • The Malicious Compliance

    Asking permission for every tiny thing to make a point. They'll accept it, and you'll have trained them to need it.

  • The Public Rebuke

    “Victor, I can do my job, you know” in a team meeting. True. Unforgettable. Not in a good way.

VIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

A new or nervous manager. Two weeks of Rhythm Ward and it calms down.

Nightmare

They're micromanaging because they're being micromanaged. Help them answer upwards: give them what their boss asks for, before they ask.

Hell

Control is their personality and nothing reassures them. Keep the updates, protect your evenings, document decisions, and start reading the Escape Portal (entry 12).

IXThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

Your new manager checks in four times a day. What do you propose?

The Rhythm Ward: two fixed updates a day, before they ask. Then ask to step down after two weeks.

They rewrite your emails before you send them. What do you say?

Ask what they're trying to get right: “What would you like these emails to do differently?” Then agree a standard, and stop needing the edits.

When is micromanagement a sign to leave?

When months of reliable updates change nothing, and it's affecting your health or growth. Then it's not anxiety; it's the job.

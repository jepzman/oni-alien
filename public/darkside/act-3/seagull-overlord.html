The Seagull Overlord

Flies in, makes a lot of noise, craps on everything, flies out.

Threat
Habitat
monthly reviews, quarterly “deep dives”, the 14:05 train
Weakness
context, delivered before he lands

IA sighting

Rupert Thorne, Interim Transformation Director, arrived at The Grid once a month. In January he looked at the Lumen plan for eleven minutes and announced: “This is too slow. Do the city centre first. And use the new vendor. I've seen this work in Rotterdam.” Then he caught the 14:05 to London.

The city centre had been last on purpose (underground cabling, permits). The new vendor had failed a security review. It took the team three weeks to un-change the plan. In February, Rupert came back, and asked why Lumen was three weeks late.

IIKnow thy enemy

The Seagull is a senior person with little context and a lot of authority. They visit briefly, feel they must add value, and so they change things. Big, visible changes feel like leadership. They rarely stay long enough to see the cost.

You can't stop the visits. But you can shape what they see: brief them before they land, show them the reasons behind the plan, and give them something useful to say yes to. A Seagull with context is just a bird.

— the AnnotatorThe Seagull isn't stupid. They're under-informed and over-powered. Fix the first one and the second matters less.

IIISigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • A senior visitor who's “seen this before” somewhere else.
  • Plans change most in the week after a visit.
  • Reviews where nobody explains why the plan is as it is.
  • The team quietly undoes visit decisions for weeks.

IVCounter-spells

  1. 1The Pre-Landing Brief

    Send a one-page briefing two days before the visit: status, the three big decisions already made, and why.

    incantation“Ahead of Thursday: Lumen is on track. Three decisions you'll see, and why we made them: city centre last (permits), current vendor (security), phase 2 in May.”

  2. 2The Reason Reveal

    When they suggest a change, show the constraint calmly. Ask what problem they're trying to solve.

    incantation“Good thought. We put the city centre last because of the cabling permits. What's the worry behind going faster: the regulator date?”

  3. 3The Bone to Carry Home

    Give them a real, small improvement they can own. Seagulls need to leave with something.

    incantation“One thing you could help with: the security review for the new vendor is stuck at group. Could you unblock it?”

  4. 4The Written Echo

    After the visit, write down what was actually agreed, and send it to them and your director.

VForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Instant Yes

    Agreeing to every change in the room to keep him happy. Three weeks of un-doing, and the delay is yours.

  • The Hide-and-Seek

    Avoiding the visit and hoping he talks to someone else. He will. Someone with less context.

  • The Eye-Roll Debrief

    Mocking him to the team after he leaves. It feels great. It travels to London faster than he does.

VIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

An occasional visitor with good intentions. A pre-landing brief turns him into an ally.

Nightmare

Monthly disruption. Agree with your director who speaks for the plan in the visit, and always do the Written Echo.

Hell

His changes override your director's. Escalate the pattern with facts (cost of each change), and get your director to agree a change process he must use.

IXThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

A senior visitor wants to change your plan in a meeting. What do you say?

The Reason Reveal: explain the constraint calmly, then ask what problem they're trying to solve.

How do you stop the monthly chaos?

Brief them before they land, give them a useful job, and write down what was really agreed afterwards.

He overrules your director. Whose instruction do you follow?

Raise it with your director straight away, in writing. Don't pick a side alone.

The Escape Portal

Sometimes the best counter-spell is the door.

Threat
Habitat
Sunday evenings, LinkedIn notifications, a recruiter's “quick chat”
Weakness
a plan, made calmly, before you need it

IA sighting

By February Pip had tried most of this Act. The Rhythm Ward had calmed Victor's eye a little. The 1:1 doc helped. But Victor still took the credit, still changed his mind every Tuesday, and still sent messages at 23:00. Pip's Sunday evenings had developed a particular taste.

Then Priya Nair stopped him in the lift. “I've got an analytics lead role in Customer Ops,” she said. “You'd be good at it. No pressure.” That evening, a recruiter also messaged. Pip sat on floor 9 after everyone had gone and stared at the exit sign for a long time. It was, he thought, the only light in the building that told the truth.

IIKnow thy enemy

Not every bad manager can be managed. Sometimes the job is costing more than it gives: your health, your growth, your evenings. Signs that it's time: you've genuinely tried the counter-spells for months, nothing has changed, and you're shrinking.

The portal has several doors. An internal move keeps your network, history and pension, and often changes everything that matters (your manager). An external move resets more. Staying with a clear end date is also a choice. What matters is that you choose, calmly and with a plan, rather than flee on a bad Tuesday.

— the AnnotatorNever resign on a bad day. Decide on a good day. If you still want to go on a good day, go.

IIISigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • Sunday evenings feel like dread.
  • You've tried for months and nothing has changed.
  • You're no longer learning, only coping.
  • Your health, sleep or relationships are suffering.
  • Someone good offers you a door, and you feel relief.

IVCounter-spells

  1. 1The Honest Ledger

    Write down what the job gives you and what it costs. Re-read it on a good day.

  2. 2The Inside Door

    Explore an internal move first: different manager, same company. Talk to the hiring manager before telling yours.

    incantation“Priya, I'm interested. Could we talk about what the role really involves before I mention it to Victor?”

  3. 3The Quiet Preparation

    Update your CV, talk to your network, and save some money before you need to. Options make you calmer, even if you stay.

  4. 4The Good Exit

    If you go, leave well: notice served properly, handover written, no burning of bridges. The company is smaller than you think.

    incantation“I've accepted a role in Customer Ops. I'll write a full handover for the heatwave model and train whoever takes it on.”

VForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Rage Quit

    Resigning in the middle of a bad meeting. It feels magnificent for an hour. Then there's rent.

  • The Scorched Exit

    A farewell email listing everyone's failings. It will be forwarded. It will follow you to your next job.

  • The Haunting

    Staying, miserable, and telling everyone how miserable you are, for years. You become the Doom Prophet (Act II).

VIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

A bad patch with a decent manager. Try the counter-spells for three months before deciding.

Nightmare

A manager who won't change and a job that's stalling your growth. Explore an internal move seriously.

Hell

Your health is suffering, or you're being harassed or bullied. Protect yourself first: talk to a doctor, HR or a lawyer, and leave as soon as you safely can.

IXThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

You've had a terrible meeting and want to resign today. What should you do?

Nothing today. Write the Honest Ledger. Decide on a good day.

You're offered an internal role. Who do you tell first?

Talk to the hiring manager first about the role. Tell your manager once it's real, before it's public.

How do you leave well?

Proper notice, a thorough handover, thanks where it's due, and no farewell essay. The industry is small.

The Annotator's last wordEvery throne room has a door. The trick is walking through it on a good day, with a plan.

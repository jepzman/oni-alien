IA sighting

December to February · the counter-spells

Pip tried most of this Act. The Rhythm Ward calmed Victor's eye a little. The 1:1 doc helped. Core hours were agreed. But Victor still took the credit, still changed his mind every Tuesday, and still sent messages at 23:00 that started with “sorry, one more thing”.

Sunday evenings

Pip's Sunday evenings had developed a particular taste. He'd start feeling it around four. By nine, he'd be checking email “just to see”.

Thursday 20 February · the lift

Priya Nair stopped him in the lift. “I've got an analytics lead role in Customer Ops,” she said. “You'd be good at it. No pressure.” That evening, a recruiter also messaged him on LinkedIn about a role at a rival utility, “with a very competitive package”.

19:40 · floor 9

Pip sat on floor 9 after everyone had gone and stared at the exit sign for a long time. It was, he thought, the only light in the building that told the truth.

— the AnnotatorNever resign on a bad day. Decide on a good day. If you still want to go on a good day, go.

IIOrigin

flashbackHow the Annotator found the portal

  1. 2020 · the Annotator joins MetroVolt

    Before she was the Annotator, she was a data lead in Network Assets: talented, loud, happy.

  2. 2021 to 2023 · the storm years

    She works for a Weather Tyrant (not Gareth; another one, long gone). Three years of ⛈️ days. She tries every counter-spell in this book, some of which she invented.

  3. March 2023 · a bad Tuesday

    After a storm in a meeting, she resigns on the spot. No plan, no savings, no job. She's right to leave. She leaves the wrong way.

  4. 2023 to now · the margins

    It takes six months to find work. She builds a freelance career, and writes in the margins of this book so others can find the portal on a good day, with a plan.

The turning point. Leaving was right. Leaving on a bad Tuesday, with no plan, was the mistake she's spent three years warning people about.

What might have saved them. A Stay-or-Go Ledger, written on a calm Sunday. And six months of savings. She'd still have left; she'd just have landed softer.

— the AnnotatorI'd leave again. I'd just do it on a Thursday, with a plan, a reference and a pension transfer form. Learn from my Tuesday.

IIIKnow thy enemy

Not every bad manager can be managed. Sometimes the job is costing more than it gives: your health, your growth, your evenings. The portal has several doors: an internal move keeps your network and history; an external move resets more; staying with an end date is also a choice. What matters is that you choose calmly, with a plan.

What keeps people in the throne room

Fear of the unknown, loyalty, sunk cost (“I've put so much in”), and the hope that next quarter will be different.

Why the portal matters here

Because some managers won't change, and MetroVolt doesn't always move them. Your career can't wait for the organisation to notice.

What makes leaving go well

  • Deciding on a good day.
  • Options prepared before you need them.
  • Talking to the new team before deciding.
  • A proper handover and no burnt bridges.

Three kinds of door

The Internal Move

New manager, same company, same pension. Often the best first door.

The Leap

A new company. More reset, more risk, sometimes more reward.

The Stay With an End Date

Stay, deliberately, until a set date or goal. A choice, not a trap.

— the AnnotatorEvery throne room has a door. The trick is walking through it on a good day, with a plan.

IVSigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • Sunday evenings feel like dread.
  • You've tried for months and nothing has changed.
  • You're no longer learning, only coping.
  • Your health, sleep or relationships are suffering.
  • Someone good offers you a door, and you feel relief.

VCounter-spells

  1. 1The Honest Ledger

    Write down what the job gives you and what it costs. Re-read it on a good day.

  2. 2The Inside Door

    Explore an internal move first: different manager, same company. Talk to the hiring manager before telling yours.

    incantation“Priya, I'm interested. Could we talk about what the role really involves before I mention it to Victor?”

  3. 3The Quiet Preparation

    Update your CV, talk to your network, and save some money before you need to. Options make you calmer, even if you stay.

  4. 4The Good Exit

    If you go, leave well: notice served properly, handover written, no burning of bridges. The company is smaller than you think.

    incantation“I've accepted a role in Customer Ops. I'll write a full handover for the heatwave model and train whoever takes it on.”

VIForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Rage Quit

    Resigning in the middle of a bad meeting. It feels magnificent for an hour. Then there's rent.

  • The Scorched Exit

    A farewell email listing everyone's failings. It will be forwarded. It will follow you to your next job.

  • The Haunting

    Staying, miserable, and telling everyone how miserable you are, for years. You become the Doom Prophet (Act II).

VIIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

A bad patch with a decent manager.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • It's been weeks, not months.
  • Counter-spells are working a bit.
  • You still learn things.

Do

  1. Try the counter-spells for three months.
  2. Write the Honest Ledger on a good day.
  3. Keep your network warm anyway.

Don’t: The rage quit: resigning in the middle of a bad meeting.

If it gets worse: If months of trying have changed nothing, you're at Nightmare.

Nightmare

A manager who won't change, and growth that's stalled.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • You've tried for months.
  • You're coping, not learning.
  • Sunday evenings feel like dread.

Do

  1. Explore an internal move seriously.
  2. Talk to the hiring manager before telling yours.
  3. Talk to people in the new team.
  4. Update your CV and save some money.

Don’t: The haunting: staying miserable and telling everyone, for years. You'll become the Doom Prophet.

If it gets worse: If your health is suffering or you're being harassed, you're at Hell.

Hell

Your health is suffering, or you're being harassed or bullied.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • You're not sleeping.
  • You're anxious or ill.
  • You're scared of your manager.

Do

  1. Protect yourself first: talk to a doctor.
  2. Talk to HR or get legal advice if there's harassment.
  3. Leave as soon as you safely can.
  4. Lean on people outside work.

Don’t: The scorched exit: a farewell email listing everyone's failings. It will follow you.

If it gets worse: Your health comes first. No job is worth more than that.

VIIIAre you stuck in the throne room?

Check honestly how you're doing. Tick what's true.

  • Sunday evenings feel like dread.
  • You've tried for months and nothing has changed.
  • You're no longer learning, only coping.
  • Your health, sleep or relationships are suffering.
  • Someone has offered you a door, and you felt relief.

The cure, if you ticked two: If you ticked three or more, write the Stay-or-Go Ledger this weekend, on a calm day. Then talk to one person about one door. You don't have to walk through it. You just have to know it's there.

IXThe rehearsal

The Inside Door: explore first, decide later, and tell your manager before it's public.

Scene: Monday 24 February, 12:30. Coffee with Priya, before telling anyone.

PipPriya, I'm interested. Could we talk about what the role really involves before I mention it to Victor?

PriyaOf course. Honestly? Messy data, angry customers, a great team, and a manager who goes home at six. That's me.

PipCould I talk to someone on the team?

PriyaTalk to Sam. Tell him I said to be brutally honest.

PipThank you. I'll decide by the end of March, and I'll tell Victor myself before anything's announced.

Pip writes the Stay-or-Go Ledger on Sunday afternoon, on a calm day. He hasn't decided. But for the first time in months, Sunday evening tastes of nothing in particular.

— the AnnotatorHe hasn't walked through the door. He's just checked it opens. That alone changes how the throne room feels.

XThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

You've had a terrible meeting and want to resign today. What should you do?

Nothing today. Write the Honest Ledger. Decide on a good day.

You're offered an internal role. Who do you tell first?

Talk to the hiring manager first about the role. Tell your manager once it's real, before it's public.

How do you leave well?

Proper notice, a thorough handover, thanks where it's due, and no farewell essay. The industry is small.

The Annotator's last wordEvery throne room has a door. The trick is walking through it on a good day, with a plan.

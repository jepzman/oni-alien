The Ritual Circle of the 1:1

Thirty minutes a week that most people waste on status updates.

Threat
Habitat
Thursday afternoons, cancelled more often than held
Weakness
an agenda you write, with decisions on it

IA sighting

Every Thursday · 15:00

Pip's 1:1s with Victor went like this. Victor asked for status. Pip gave status. Victor gave four new tasks. The meeting ended at 22 minutes.

Twice a month

It was cancelled for “something urgent”. The cancellation arrived at 14:58.

Three months

In thirteen weeks, Pip had used the 1:1 for exactly zero things he wanted. He had never mentioned level 5, his workload or the fact that he hated the Spine.

Friday · the pub

The Annotator, over a drink, was appalled. “It's your meeting,” she said. “Not his. Write the agenda. Put the status in an email. Use the time for things only he can do: decisions, blockers, your career. And never, ever let it be cancelled without asking for another slot.”

— the AnnotatorIf your 1:1 is all status, send the status the day before and walk in with a different list. Watch your manager relax.

IIOrigin

flashbackHow MetroVolt's 1:1s died

  1. 2022 · the policy

    An engagement survey shows that staff feel unsupported. HR introduces mandatory weekly 1:1s for every manager. There's a policy, a calendar template and no training.

  2. 2022 · the default

    Nobody tells managers what a 1:1 is for. Most fill it with the only thing they know: status. “Where are we on X?”

  3. 2023 to 2025 · the decline

    Status 1:1s feel pointless, so they're cancelled whenever anything comes up. Staff learn to expect nothing from them. Managers learn they're a box to tick.

  4. 2026 · the ritual

    The 1:1 survives as a ritual with no magic left in it: a weekly calendar entry, mostly cancelled, mostly status, almost never about the person in it.

The turning point. A good idea introduced as a policy, without anyone explaining what it was for.

What might have saved them. One page from HR in 2022: “The 1:1 is for the employee's agenda: decisions, blockers, feedback and growth. Status goes in writing.”

— the AnnotatorMost dead 1:1s were never alive. Nobody explained the ritual. You can explain it to your manager by running it well.

IIIKnow thy enemy

The 1:1 is the only regular meeting where you set the agenda, or could. Most people let it become a status report, which wastes it: status belongs in writing. The 1:1 is for things that need a conversation: decisions, problems, feedback, and your growth. Many of this Act's creatures are tamed here.

What your manager wants from it

To know what's going on without effort, and to spot problems early. A good 1:1 does both in half the time.

Why it dies here

Because it was introduced as a policy, not a practice. Nobody knows what it's for, so it becomes status, then a cancellation, then nothing.

What brings it to life

  • A shared agenda you fill in beforehand.
  • Status in writing, not in the meeting.
  • A monthly slot for your career.
  • Rescheduling, never cancelling.

Three kinds of 1:1

The Status Report

The default. Send the status; repurpose the time.

The Cancelled

Always “something urgent”. Ask for shorter, fixed slots.

The Real 1:1

Decisions, blockers, feedback, growth. Rare, and powerful.

— the AnnotatorThe 1:1 is the only throne room where you set the agenda. Sit in the chair.

IVSigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • Your 1:1 is a status report.
  • It's cancelled more often than not.
  • You've never discussed your career in it.
  • You leave with more tasks than you came with, and no decisions.

VCounter-spells

  1. 1The Shared Agenda

    Keep a running doc you both can edit. You fill it in before each 1:1. Status goes in writing; the meeting is for the rest.

    incantation“I've started a shared 1:1 doc. Status is at the top so we don't need to talk through it. The rest is decisions and questions.”

  2. 2The Monthly Career Slot

    Once a month, use the 1:1 for your development only.

    incantation“Could we use the last 1:1 each month for my development? This month: what level 5 would look like for me.”

  3. 3The Rescheduled, Not Cancelled

    When it's cancelled, ask for a new slot that week, even 10 minutes.

    incantation“No problem. Could we grab 10 minutes on Friday instead? I have one decision I need.”

VIForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Grievance Hour

    Using every 1:1 to complain. Your manager will start cancelling, and you'll have earned it.

  • The Quiet Acceptance

    Letting it die because they keep cancelling. No 1:1s means no relationship, and a surprise review.

  • The Status Monologue

    Reading out your task list for 25 minutes. Send it. Nobody needs to hear a spreadsheet.

VIIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

A busy manager who's never thought about 1:1s.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • The 1:1 happens but it's all status.
  • Your manager is open to suggestions.
  • Nobody's against it; nobody's tried.

Do

  1. Start a Shared Agenda doc.
  2. Put status at the top, in writing.
  3. Bring decisions and questions to the meeting.

Don’t: The status monologue: reading out your task list.

If it gets worse: If it's cancelled more often than held, you're at Nightmare.

Nightmare

The Cancelled. Constant cancellations.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Half your 1:1s are cancelled.
  • Cancellations come at the last minute.
  • You haven't discussed your career in months.

Do

  1. Ask for a shorter, fixed slot that's easier to keep.
  2. When cancelled, ask for 10 minutes later that week.
  3. Send the agenda anyway, so decisions can happen by email.
  4. Book a monthly career slot separately.

Don’t: The quiet acceptance: letting it die. No 1:1s means a surprise review.

If it gets worse: If your manager refuses 1:1s altogether, you're at Hell.

Hell

No 1:1s at all.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Your manager says they're a waste of time.
  • You only see them in group meetings.
  • Your review is written without a conversation.

Do

  1. Ask for at least a monthly one.
  2. Find a mentor and a skip-level relationship to fill the gap.
  3. Keep your own written record of your work.
  4. Raise it with HR if reviews happen without conversations.

Don’t: The grievance hour: if you do get one, don't spend it all complaining.

If it gets worse: A manager who won't talk to you is a reason to look at the Escape Portal.

VIIIAre your 1:1s alive?

If you run 1:1s for others, check them. Tick honestly.

  • Your 1:1s are mostly status.
  • You've cancelled more than you've held this quarter.
  • You set the agenda, not them.
  • You haven't talked about anyone's career this month.
  • Your 1:1s end with more tasks for them, not decisions from you.

The cure, if you ticked two: Ask each person to bring the agenda next time, and make status written. Then ask one question, every time: what's getting in your way?

IXThe rehearsal

The Shared Agenda: status in writing, the meeting for the rest.

Scene: Thursday 23 January, 15:00. The first 1:1 with a shared agenda.

PipI've started a shared 1:1 doc. Status is at the top so we don't need to talk through it. The rest is two decisions and one question.

Victor…OK. Decisions?

PipHeatwave scenario A or B. I recommend A. And can you nudge IT on the substation data? It's my only blocker.

VictorA. And yes, I'll email the IT director today. The question?

PipFrom next month, could we use the last 1:1 of each month for my development? I'd like to talk about level 5.

Victor…Nobody's asked me that before. Yes.

The meeting ends at 14 minutes. Victor says it's the most useful 1:1 he's had at MetroVolt. It's also the shortest.

— the AnnotatorSame 30 minutes. Status moved to writing, and the time went to decisions, a blocker and Pip's career.

XThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

Your 1:1s are just status. What do you change first?

Send status in writing beforehand and bring a short list of decisions and questions.

Your manager cancels half your 1:1s. What do you ask for?

A shorter fixed slot, and a quick reschedule when it's cancelled.

How often should you talk about your career?

At least once a month, in a 1:1 set aside for it.

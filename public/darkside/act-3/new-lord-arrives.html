The New Lord Arrives

A new manager is a new kingdom. The first 90 days decide your place in it.

Threat
Habitat
the first all-team meeting, the first 1:1, the first “operating model” deck
Weakness
early, honest usefulness

IA sighting

Monday 2 December · 09:00 · day one

Victor arrived with a 47-slide operating model, three new acronyms (GAOM, DART, and something called “the Spine”) and a plan to “reset ways of working”.

Day two · three camps

The team split into three camps: the eager (volunteering for everything, nodding at every acronym), the sceptical (folded arms, eyebrows, “we tried that”) and the invisible (camera off, hoping to be forgotten).

Day three · Pip

Pip, unusually, did something sensible. He booked 30 minutes, brought a one-page “what you should know” about the team's work, and asked Victor what he was being measured on.

Day three · 30 minutes later

Victor, who was terrified, nearly hugged him. He'd been at MetroVolt for three days, knew nobody, and had a director who'd told him the team was “too comfortable” and then left for a conference in Lisbon.

— the AnnotatorNew managers are more scared than you are. They've just bet their reputation on a team they don't know. Be the person who makes that bet look smart.

IIOrigin

flashbackHow Victor came to arrive with 47 slides

  1. October 2026 · the hiring

    Benedict hires Victor to “shake things up” in Grid Analytics, which he calls “too comfortable”. He's never actually watched the team work.

  2. November 2026 · the brief

    Victor is given a target (faster, more strategic analytics), no history and a 90-day deadline to show change.

  3. November · the slides

    With no knowledge of the team, Victor builds what consultants build: an operating model. 47 slides. Three acronyms. It's how he proves he has a plan.

  4. 2 December · arrival

    Victor walks in convinced the team needs fixing, because that's what he was told, and terrified it won't work, because he doesn't know them.

The turning point. Being told a team needed fixing by someone who'd never watched it work.

What might have saved them. A director who introduced him properly, explained the history and said “understand before you change”.

— the AnnotatorMost new managers arrive with a brief written by someone else. Help them see the real team, and they'll often rewrite the brief themselves.

IIIKnow thy enemy

A new manager is under pressure to prove themselves fast. They don't know who's good, what's broken, or where the bodies are buried. They form impressions quickly, and those impressions stick for a year. Your best move: be early, honest and useful.

What it wants

Early wins, a reliable picture of the team, and allies who'll make its first 90 days work.

Why the first weeks matter

Because impressions formed in the first month shape who gets the good work, who gets trusted and who gets “managed”, often for years.

What it fears

  • Failing publicly in its first 90 days.
  • A team that resists everything.
  • Not knowing what it doesn't know.
  • Its own boss's verdict.

The three breeds

The Listener

Learns first, changes later. Give it the landscape.

The New Broom

Mandated to change things. Help it pick the right changes.

The Clearer

Brings its own people. Be useful fast; keep your options warm.

— the AnnotatorA new lord remembers who helped them find the map. Be on the map.

IVSigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • A new manager is announced.
  • An operating-model deck appears in week one.
  • Colleagues split into the eager, the sceptical and the invisible.
  • Old decisions are being quietly re-opened.

VCounter-spells

  1. 1The Landscape Gift

    In week one, offer a one-page view of your work: what you do, what's working, what's broken, and the history they'd never guess.

    incantation“I've written one page on what Grid Analytics does and the three things I'd fix. Happy to walk you through it in 30 minutes.”

  2. 2The Measure Question

    Ask what they're measured on and what would make their first six months a success. Then help with that.

    incantation“What does a great first six months look like for you? I'd like to help with it.”

  3. 3The Honest Context

    When they propose changing something that was tried before, explain the history without defending it.

    incantation“We tried a shared queue in 2024. It failed because of X. If we try again, here's what I'd do differently.”

VIForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Old Guard

    “That's not how we do things here.” It positions you as the obstacle on day one.

  • The Invisible

    Keeping your head down until they settle. By the time they settle, they've decided who matters, without you.

  • The Courtier's Rush

    Agreeing with every new idea to get in early. The team notices, and so will the manager, eventually.

VIIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

The Listener. A thoughtful new manager.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • They ask questions before changing things.
  • They meet everyone 1:1.
  • They admit what they don't know.

Do

  1. Give them the Landscape Gift in week one.
  2. Ask the Measure Question.
  3. Explain history honestly when they propose something old.

Don’t: The invisible: keeping your head down until they settle.

If it gets worse: If they're mandated to change everything, you're at Nightmare.

Nightmare

The New Broom. A mandate to change everything.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • An operating model appears in week one.
  • Old decisions are reopened.
  • The team splits into camps.

Do

  1. Help them choose which changes matter most.
  2. Argue only about changes that will really hurt.
  3. Volunteer for the first change that's actually good.
  4. Stay out of the camps.

Don’t: The old guard: “that's not how we do things here.”

If it gets worse: If they're bringing in their own people and clearing out the team, you're at Hell.

Hell

The Clearer. Bringing their own people.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Former colleagues of theirs arrive.
  • Long-serving people are sidelined.
  • Reorg rumours start.

Do

  1. Be useful fast and visibly.
  2. Keep your network warm (Act I, Blood Pacts).
  3. Know your options inside and outside.
  4. Don't take it personally; it rarely is.

Don’t: The courtier's rush: agreeing with everything to survive. They'll notice, eventually.

If it gets worse: If you're on the list anyway, negotiate well (and read Act V, The Famine).

VIIIWhen you're the new lord

One day you'll arrive somewhere new. Check your first-week instincts. Tick honestly.

  • You'd present a plan before meeting the team.
  • You'd assume the team is the problem because you were told so.
  • You'd change things in your first month to show impact.
  • You'd judge people on your first impression.
  • You'd skip the history because it slows you down.

The cure, if you ticked two: In your first 30 days, ask everyone the same three questions: what's working, what's broken, what would you change? Then decide. Your plan will be better, and so will your welcome.

IXThe rehearsal

The Landscape Gift and the Measure Question.

Scene: Wednesday 4 December, 11:00. Pip's first 1:1 with Victor.

PipWelcome! I've written one page on what Grid Analytics does: what works, what's broken, and history you'd never guess. Want me to walk you through it?

Victor…Yes. Please. Nobody's given me anything like this.

PipAnd what does a great first six months look like for you? I'd like my work to help with it.

VictorFaster answers for the board. Benedict says we're slow.

PipThat's fair. The main reason is the board asks reach us the night before. Could we get them a week earlier? That would fix half of it.

Victor…That's not in my operating model.

PipIt could be slide 48.

— the AnnotatorVictor's first useful change came from Pip's one page, not his 47 slides. He remembered that for a long time.

XThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

A new manager arrives with a 47-slide operating model. What's your first move?

Book 30 minutes. Bring the Landscape Gift and ask the Measure Question.

They want to reintroduce something that failed before. What do you say?

Explain the history honestly, then help them try it better. Don't say “we tried that” and stop.

Your old manager was your champion. Does that carry over?

No. You start again with the new one. Earn it in the first 90 days.

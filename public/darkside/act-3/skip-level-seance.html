The Skip-Level Séance

Summoning your boss's boss. Powerful. Dangerous. Read the instructions.

Threat
Habitat
skip-level 1:1s, open-door policies, the lift with the director
Weakness
transparency: your manager knows you're summoning

IA sighting

After three months of Victor, Pip booked a meeting with Benedict Crane (who, miraculously, was in the building). He prepared a list of eleven problems with Victor. Halfway through, Benedict held up a hand. “Have you raised these with Victor?” Pip had not. “Then do that first,” said Benedict, “and come back if it doesn't work.”

That evening, Victor knew. He didn't say how. He didn't need to. Their 1:1 the next day lasted four minutes.

IIKnow thy enemy

Your boss's boss has power over your manager and, often, over your career: they're in your calibration and promotion discussions. A good relationship with them is valuable. A complaint about your manager delivered to them is radioactive: they must back their manager first, and the manager will almost always find out.

So: build the skip-level relationship openly and regularly, about work and ideas. Escalate problems only after you've raised them with your manager, or when it's serious enough that you must (harm, ethics, safety).

— the AnnotatorA skip-level is a relationship, not an emergency exit. Visit when nothing's on fire, and they'll listen when something is.

IIISigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • You've never spoken to your boss's boss.
  • You're tempted to “just mention” your manager's flaws to them.
  • An “open door policy” that nobody seems to walk through.
  • Calibration happens and you don't know who spoke for you.

IVCounter-spells

  1. 1The Open Summoning

    Ask your manager if you can have occasional skip-levels, and share what you'll discuss.

    incantation“Would you mind if I had a quarterly coffee with Benedict? I'd like to understand Strategy's priorities. I'll share what I learn.”

  2. 2The Idea Offering

    Bring the skip-level ideas and questions about the business, not complaints.

    incantation“What's the one thing you wish Grid Analytics did that we don't?”

  3. 3The Proper Escalation

    If you must escalate about your manager: raise it with them first, then tell them you're taking it further, then go with facts.

    incantation“Victor, I raised the workload twice and nothing changed. I'm going to talk to Benedict about it. I wanted you to hear it from me.”

VForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Secret Séance

    Complaining about your manager to their boss behind their back. Your manager will find out. Every manager finds out.

  • The Eleven Grievances

    Bringing a long list. One problem, with facts and a request, is heard. Eleven is a rant.

  • The Name-Drop

    “As I was saying to Benedict…” to your manager. You've just told them you're going over their head, as a threat.

VIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

A healthy skip-level culture. Quarterly coffees, open and useful.

Nightmare

Your manager is defensive about skip-levels. Keep it about the business, share notes, and build it slowly.

Hell

Serious issues (harassment, safety, fraud) and your manager is part of it. Go to the skip-level, HR or the formal channel directly. Here, telling your manager first is not required.

VIIILoot drop

⚿The Skip-Level Coffee Plan

Four quarterly questions that build the relationship without poisoning your manager's.

Q1  What are Strategy's top three priorities this year?
Q2  Where is Grid Analytics most / least useful to you?
Q3  What do you look for in people you promote?
Q4  What's one thing you'd change about how we work?

Share a 3-line summary with Victor after each one.

IXThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

You have serious frustrations with your manager. Do you go to their boss?

Not first. Raise them with your manager, clearly. If nothing changes, tell your manager you're escalating, then go with facts.

Is a regular skip-level coffee disloyal?

Not if it's open, agreed and about the business. It's how directors get to know the people they'll judge in calibration.

When can you go straight to the skip-level?

When it's serious (harm, harassment, safety, fraud), or when your manager is the problem in that sense.

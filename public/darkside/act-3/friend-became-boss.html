The Friend Who Became Your Boss

Yesterday you shared complaints about the boss. Today they are the boss.

Threat
Habitat
the team you both used to be in, Friday drinks, the first awkward 1:1
Weakness
an honest conversation, early, about how it'll work now

IA sighting

Friday 10 January · 08:02

An email: Jules had been made team lead of Grid Analytics. Pip found out from the email, like everyone else.

08:05

Jules messaged: “Coffee? I want to talk before it gets weird.” It was already a bit weird.

January and February · small things

Jules stopped joining the Friday moan about Victor. Jules asked about deadlines in a new tone. Jules was, quite rightly, now in meetings about Pip. One Friday, Pip told a joke about a decision Jules had made, at the pub, and Jules laughed a fraction too late.

The unspoken thing

Pip had applied for the same role. Both of them knew. Neither mentioned it. It sat at every coffee like a third person.

— the AnnotatorIf you also wanted the job, say so, once, kindly. “I wanted it, I'm a bit gutted, and I'm glad it's you.” Then it's said, and you can both move on.

IIOrigin

flashbackHow two friends became a boss and a report

  1. September 2022 · the graduate intake

    Pip and Jules join MetroVolt on the same day. At the induction drinks they discover a shared hatred of pie charts. Friends by midnight.

  2. 2023 to 2025 · two paths

    Pip builds brilliant models in silence. Jules builds good models and helps everyone (Act I, Blood Pacts). By 2025, three directors know Jules's name.

  3. December 2026 · the opening

    Victor needs a team lead. Both apply. Jules gets it, mostly because more people know what Jules does. Pip's work is as good; it's just less visible.

  4. January 2027 · the new rules

    Nobody gives either of them guidance on what happens to a friendship when one becomes the other's boss. They work it out alone, awkwardly, over coffee.

The turning point. One promotion between two equals, decided by visibility, with no guidance on what came after.

What might have saved them. Nothing broken, yet. What helps is an honest conversation early, before the unspoken thing grows.

— the AnnotatorFriendships survive this when they're named and given new rules. Unspoken, they rot. Both of you lose something; say so.

IIIKnow thy enemy

When a friend becomes your boss, both of you lose something: you lose an equal; they lose a confidant. They now have duties that may conflict with the friendship: reviewing you, deciding your workload, keeping secrets about reorgs. It's often more awkward for them than for you. Friendships survive when both sides name it early and agree new rules.

What the new boss wants

To be fair, to be taken seriously by the team, and to keep the friend. All three at once is hard.

Why it's hard here

Because MetroVolt promotes from within teams and offers no guidance on this. New leads are expected to switch from peer to boss overnight.

What protects the friendship

  • An early, honest conversation.
  • New rules, agreed together.
  • Visible support in their first months.
  • Saying the unspoken thing once.

Three kinds of new boss

The Graceful

Names it, sets rules, stays a friend. Support them.

The Overcorrector

Becomes strict to prove fairness. Give them time; tell them kindly.

The Pal

Avoids being the boss. Help them lead; don't take advantage.

— the AnnotatorSay the awkward thing once, out loud, early. Then you get to keep the friend.

IVSigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • A friend or peer is promoted over you.
  • Conversations stop when they walk in.
  • You're tempted to test them (“you've changed”).
  • You both avoid the subject of the job you both wanted.

VCounter-spells

  1. 1The Naming Rite

    Have the conversation early: congratulate, acknowledge the awkwardness, agree how you'll work.

    incantation“Congratulations, really. It's a bit strange for me, I'll admit. How do you want to handle the friend-and-boss thing?”

  2. 2The New Boundaries

    Agree what changes: no moaning about the team to them, no expecting favours, feedback at work, friendship outside it.

    incantation“Let's keep work at work. If I'm annoyed with a decision, I'll tell you in a 1:1, not at the pub.”

  3. 3The Visible Support

    Back them publicly in their first months. It helps them, and the team sees you're not a rival.

VIForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Insider Favour

    Expecting easier treatment or inside information. They can't give it, and asking puts them in an impossible spot.

  • The Resentful Ally

    Joining the people who resent their promotion. They'll know. Friendships rarely survive that.

  • The Old Moan

    Still complaining about the team to them as if they were a peer. Now you're complaining to management.

VIIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

The Graceful. A good friend, promoted fairly.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • They raised the awkwardness first.
  • They're fair to everyone.
  • Nothing's been said that can't be fixed.

Do

  1. Have the Naming Rite: congratulate, acknowledge, agree how to work.
  2. Write the new rules together.
  3. Back them visibly.

Don’t: The insider favour: expecting easier treatment.

If it gets worse: If you both wanted the job and it's sitting between you, you're at Nightmare.

Nightmare

You both wanted it, and it's unspoken.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • The job sits at every coffee like a third person.
  • Conversations stop when they arrive.
  • You're tempted to test them.

Do

  1. Acknowledge it once: “I wanted it, I'm gutted, I'm glad it's you.”
  2. Ask them to help you towards the next opportunity.
  3. Support them visibly in their first months.
  4. Keep work moans away from them.

Don’t: The resentful ally: joining people who resent the promotion.

If it gets worse: If they've changed for the worse, or use the friendship to pressure you, you're at Hell.

Hell

Power has changed them, or they use the friendship against you.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • They expect favours because you're friends.
  • They share your confidences with others.
  • They're harder on you than on others.

Do

  1. Treat them as a manager first.
  2. Keep important things in writing.
  3. Use normal channels for problems.
  4. Grieve the friendship if you must, and protect your career.

Don’t: The old moan: still complaining to them as if they were a peer.

If it gets worse: If it's unworkable, an internal move protects both the career and what's left of the friendship.

VIIIWhen you're the friend who got promoted

One day it might be you. Check your instincts. Tick honestly.

  • You'd avoid the awkward conversation and hope it fades.
  • You'd be extra strict with your friend to prove you're fair.
  • You'd share team secrets with them “because they're a friend”.
  • You'd keep moaning about leadership with them, now that you're leadership.
  • You'd let them get away with things you wouldn't let others.

The cure, if you ticked two: In the first week, have the conversation: name it, agree rules, and promise to tell them hard things straight. Friends can take honesty. They can't take silence.

IXThe rehearsal

The Naming Rite and the New Boundaries.

Scene: Friday 10 January, 08:30. Coffee, as Jules asked.

PipCongratulations. Really. I'll be honest: I wanted it, I'm a bit gutted, and I'm glad it's you.

JulesThank you for saying that. I was dreading this coffee.

PipHow do you want to handle the friend-and-boss thing?

JulesWork at work. If you disagree with something, tell me in a 1:1, not at the pub. And no work moaning at the pub, in either direction.

PipDeal. And will you help me towards the next lead role?

JulesThat's the first thing I want to do as a lead.

They write the rules on a napkin. Jules keeps it. In March, at their three-month check-in, it's still in Jules's wallet.

— the AnnotatorOne honest sentence (“I wanted it”) took the third person out of the room.

XThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

Your friend is promoted to be your boss. What's your first move?

The Naming Rite: congratulate them, acknowledge it's strange, and agree how you'll work.

You both applied for the job. Do you mention it?

Once, kindly: “I wanted it, I'm a bit gutted, and I'm glad it's you.” Then let it go.

At Friday drinks, colleagues start moaning about your friend's decisions. What do you do?

Don't join in, and don't report it. Change the subject, or say: “I'd rather talk to Jules about it directly.”

The Puppet King

Wears the crown. Can't sign anything.

Threat
Habitat
approval chains, “leave it with me”, decisions that never come back down
Weakness
the strings: find out who really pulls them, and work with both

IA sighting

Victor approved Pip's plan for a second heatwave model. Then he “took it upstairs”. It came back three weeks later, changed beyond recognition, approved by Ottoline. Victor had approved nothing; he had carried it. He couldn't hire, couldn't spend more than £5,000, and couldn't move a deadline without asking Benedict, who asked Ottoline.

Pip realised he had spent three months trying to persuade someone who could not say yes.

IIKnow thy enemy

Some managers have titles without authority: new to the role, constrained by a central team, or simply not trusted by their own boss. They can't make decisions, so they postpone them, carry them upwards or quietly change them to please the real decider.

Don't humiliate them by going around them. Work with them to reach the real decider: help them build the case, offer to join them upstairs, and make them look competent while you do it. A puppet who trusts you will show you the strings.

— the AnnotatorEvery puppet king knows exactly who holds the strings. Ask them gently and they'll usually tell you. They're relieved someone noticed.

IIISigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • “Leave it with me”, and it disappears.
  • Approvals come back changed, from someone else.
  • Your manager can't approve spend, hiring or deadlines.
  • They're visibly nervous before meetings with their boss.

IVCounter-spells

  1. 1The Gentle Question

    Ask, kindly, who makes the final call and what they need to see.

    incantation“Who'll make the final decision on this? What do they need to see to say yes? I'd like to make it easy for you.”

  2. 2The Ammunition

    Prepare what your manager needs to win upstairs: a one-page case in the decider's language.

    incantation“I've written a one-page case for Ottoline, focused on billing complaints, since that's what she tracks.”

  3. 3The Joint Ascent

    Offer to go upstairs together, with them leading.

    incantation“Would it help if I came along when you present it to Benedict? You lead, I'll answer the data questions.”

VForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Puppeteer's Bypass

    Going straight to the real decider and cutting your manager out. They'll know, and they'll have nothing left to protect you with.

  • The Pity Glance

    Treating them as powerless in front of the team. They'll feel it, and they'll resent you more than the strings.

  • The Eternal Wait

    Waiting for decisions they can't make. Learn the strings or nothing moves.

VIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

A new manager still earning authority. Help them build cases and it grows.

Nightmare

A manager kept on a short leash. Use the Joint Ascent and give them the credit.

Hell

A manager with no authority and no influence, who blocks you to look important. Build the skip-level relationship openly, and consider moving.

VIIILoot drop

⚿The Case in Their Language

One page for the person who really decides, written for what they care about.

FOR      Ottoline Grey (decides) · via Victor (owner)
ASK      Second heatwave model, £18k, 6 weeks
WHY SHE  Billing complaints in last heatwave: 2,400.
CARES    A better forecast cuts estimated bills by ~40%.
RISK     Without it: same complaints next summer, regulator review.
ASKED OF Decision by 14 Feb, so it's ready before May.

IXThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

Your manager keeps saying “leave it with me” and nothing happens. What do you ask?

Gently: who makes the final call, and what do they need to see? Then help your manager prepare it.

You've found out who really decides. Should you go straight to them?

No. Go with your manager, or prepare their case. Cutting them out burns the relationship you need.

How do you help a powerless manager gain power?

Make them look competent: give them strong cases, credit them, and help them win small decisions upstairs.

The Annotator's last wordDon't cut the strings. Learn where they go, and help the king walk.

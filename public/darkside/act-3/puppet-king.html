The Puppet King

Wears the crown. Can't sign anything.

Threat
Habitat
approval chains, “leave it with me”, decisions that never come back down
Weakness
the strings: find out who really pulls them, and work with both

IA sighting

Monday 13 January · approved

Victor approved Pip's plan for a second heatwave model: £18,000, six weeks. “Great,” he said. “I'll take it upstairs.”

January and most of February · upstairs

It went upstairs. It stayed there. Victor said “leave it with me” on four occasions.

Tuesday 4 February · down again

The plan came back down three weeks later, changed beyond recognition: £9,000, four weeks, no new data. It had been approved by Ottoline. Victor had approved nothing; he had carried it.

That evening · the discovery

Pip looked up the delegation rules. Victor couldn't hire, couldn't spend more than £5,000 and couldn't move a deadline without asking Benedict, who asked Ottoline. Pip realised he had spent three months trying to persuade someone who could not say yes.

— the AnnotatorEvery puppet king knows exactly who holds the strings. Ask them gently and they'll usually tell you. They're relieved someone noticed.

IIOrigin

flashbackHow the crown came off its strings

  1. 2023 · the scandal

    A manager in Strategy signs a £400,000 consultancy contract without proper approval. The work is useless. The story reaches the parent group.

  2. 2023 · the clampdown

    Ottoline centralises approvals. Managers in Strategy keep their titles and lose their spending power. Anything over £5,000 goes through Benedict, then through her.

  3. 2024 to 2025 · the hollow crowns

    Strategy managers learn to “take things upstairs” and wait. They can't say yes, and they're embarrassed to admit it.

  4. December 2026 · Victor

    Victor arrives expecting a manager's authority. He discovers, quietly, that he has a manager's title and an analyst's budget. He hides it, and says “leave it with me”.

The turning point. One bad contract in 2023 that turned every manager in Strategy into a messenger.

What might have saved them. Proportionate controls: tighter limits for contracts, not for £18,000 of analysis. And honesty with new managers about what they can approve.

— the AnnotatorMost puppet kings are embarrassed, not malicious. Make the strings visible, kindly, and help them pull together.

IIIKnow thy enemy

Some managers have titles without authority: new, constrained by a central team, or simply not trusted by their own boss. They can't decide, so they postpone, carry decisions upward or quietly change them to please the real decider. Don't humiliate them by going around them. Work with them to reach the real decider.

What it wants

To look like a real manager, and not to have its lack of power exposed.

Why it thrives here

Because after 2023, MetroVolt centralised power without changing titles. Managers have the crown and none of the strings.

What helps it

  • A one-page case written in the real decider's language.
  • Someone to go upstairs with.
  • Small wins that build its authority.
  • Gentle questions about who decides.

The three breeds

The New King

Still earning authority. Help them build cases and it grows.

The Leashed

Kept on a short leash from above. Use the Joint Ascent.

The Hollow Crown

No authority and blocks you to feel important. Build your own routes, openly.

— the AnnotatorDon't cut the strings. Learn where they go, and help the king walk.

IVSigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • “Leave it with me”, and it disappears.
  • Approvals come back changed, from someone else.
  • Your manager can't approve spend, hiring or deadlines.
  • They're visibly nervous before meetings with their boss.

VCounter-spells

  1. 1The Gentle Question

    Ask, kindly, who makes the final call and what they need to see.

    incantation“Who'll make the final decision on this? What do they need to see to say yes? I'd like to make it easy for you.”

  2. 2The Ammunition

    Prepare what your manager needs to win upstairs: a one-page case in the decider's language.

    incantation“I've written a one-page case for Ottoline, focused on billing complaints, since that's what she tracks.”

  3. 3The Joint Ascent

    Offer to go upstairs together, with them leading.

    incantation“Would it help if I came along when you present it to Benedict? You lead, I'll answer the data questions.”

VIForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Puppeteer's Bypass

    Going straight to the real decider and cutting your manager out. They'll know, and they'll have nothing left to protect you with.

  • The Pity Glance

    Treating them as powerless in front of the team. They'll feel it, and they'll resent you more than the strings.

  • The Eternal Wait

    Waiting for decisions they can't make. Learn the strings or nothing moves.

VIIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

The New King. Still earning authority.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Decisions are slow but they come.
  • Your manager is open about the limits.
  • Strong cases get approved.

Do

  1. Ask gently who makes the final call.
  2. Prepare cases in the decider's language.
  3. Credit your manager when it works.

Don’t: The eternal wait: waiting for decisions they can't make.

If it gets worse: If they're kept on a short leash and decisions come back changed, you're at Nightmare.

Nightmare

The Leashed. On a short leash from above.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • “Leave it with me”, and it disappears.
  • Approvals come back changed, from someone else.
  • They're nervous before meetings with their boss.

Do

  1. Write the Case in Their Language for the real decider.
  2. Offer the Joint Ascent: go upstairs together, them leading.
  3. Help them win small decisions to build authority.
  4. Keep it all visible to them.

Don’t: The puppeteer's bypass: going straight to the real decider and cutting them out.

If it gets worse: If they have no authority and block you to look important, you're at Hell.

Hell

The Hollow Crown. Powerless, and blocking.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • They block your access upwards.
  • They take credit for carrying decisions.
  • Nothing you need ever gets decided.

Do

  1. Build your skip-level relationship openly.
  2. Document what's stalled and its cost.
  3. Ask your manager directly how decisions can move faster.
  4. Consider moving to where decisions happen.

Don’t: The pity glance: treating them as powerless in front of the team.

If it gets worse: If nothing moves, take your work where it can.

VIIIIs your crown hollow?

If you manage anyone, check your strings. Tick honestly.

  • You've said “leave it with me” about something you can't approve.
  • Your team doesn't know what you can and can't decide.
  • You've changed a team decision to please your boss without telling them why.
  • You hide your lack of authority from your team.
  • You've blocked someone's direct contact upwards.

The cure, if you ticked two: Tell your team plainly what you can approve and what needs your boss. Then help them build cases that go upstairs well. Honesty about limits earns more respect than pretending.

IXThe rehearsal

The Gentle Question, the Ammunition and the Joint Ascent.

Scene: Wednesday 5 February. Pip's 1:1 with Victor.

PipVictor, who'll make the final decision on the heatwave model? What do they need to see to say yes? I'd like to make it easy for you.

Victor…Ottoline. Honestly, I can't approve anything over five thousand.

PipThat's useful to know, thank you. What does Ottoline care about most?

VictorComplaints reaching the CEO.

PipThen let me write a one-page case for her about billing complaints. 2,400 in the last heatwave. Would it help if I came with you when you take it up? You lead, I'll answer the data questions.

Victor…Yes. Thursday.

Thursday: Victor presents, Pip answers two questions, Ottoline approves £18,000 in six minutes. Victor tells the team he “got it through”. Pip lets him.

— the AnnotatorPip let Victor have the win. That's the point: a puppet king who wins with you becomes a king who fights for you.

XThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

Your manager keeps saying “leave it with me” and nothing happens. What do you ask?

Gently: who makes the final call, and what do they need to see? Then help your manager prepare it.

You've found out who really decides. Should you go straight to them?

No. Go with your manager, or prepare their case. Cutting them out burns the relationship you need.

How do you help a powerless manager gain power?

Make them look competent: give them strong cases, credit them, and help them win small decisions upstairs.

The Annotator's last wordDon't cut the strings. Learn where they go, and help the king walk.

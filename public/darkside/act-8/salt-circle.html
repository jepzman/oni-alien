The Salt Circle

A boundary drawn in plain sight. Demons can see it. Most of them respect it, if you do.

Threat
Habitat
evenings, weekends, holidays, the space between “quick question” and your life
Weakness
lines drawn clearly, kept consistently, and explained kindly once

IA sighting

March 2030 · the warning

Watching Hugo, Pip noticed his own habits: email in bed, replies at 21:31 to messages sent at 21:30, a laptop on holiday “just in case”. Nothing was wrong yet. That, the Annotator wrote in the margin, is exactly when to draw the circle.

Monday 7 April · the Drawn Line

Pip told Ruth and his team: offline after 19:00 and all weekend; if something's genuinely urgent, ring. He agreed with Ruth what counted as urgent (the control room, a regulator, a board paper due next morning). Everything else would wait until 08:00. Ruth said: “Good. Will you nag me to do the same?”

Thursday 22 May · 21:34 · the test

Rupert messaged: “Quick one: can you send the churn numbers tonight?” It wasn't on the urgent list. Pip's phone was face down on the hall shelf. At 08:00 he sent the numbers with a warm note. Rupert replied: “Thanks, perfect.” He hadn't looked at them until 10:00 anyway.

Summer · the Unbroken Ring

The messages after 19:00 dropped from a dozen a week to one or two. Nobody said anything about it. They'd just learned. Pip slept better, read four books, and was noticeably less irritable on Tuesday mornings, according to Rafi.

— the AnnotatorA boundary is a circle drawn where everyone can see it. Draw it once. Keep it always.

IIOrigin

flashbackHow Pip's circle was broken in the first place

  1. 2025 · the eager start

    New and keen, Pip replies to everything instantly. People notice and appreciate it.

  2. 2027 · the Famine

    During Horizon, being always available feels like safety. He never turns his phone off.

  3. 2029 · the habit

    Back at HQ with a long commute, he works on the train both ways, then again at night. It's just how it is.

  4. March 2030 · the mirror

    He sees Hugo in the car park, and sees where his own road leads.

The turning point. Fear during the Famine turning availability from a courtesy into armour.

What might have saved them. Drawing the line in his first year, when it would have seemed normal rather than a change.

— the AnnotatorBoundaries are easiest to set at the start of a job. The next easiest time is now.

IIIKnow thy enemy

A boundary is a limit on when, how and how much you're available for work. Good boundaries are specific, known to others and consistently kept. Without them, work expands to fill every hour you allow, not out of malice but because everyone else's urgency reaches you directly.

What tests it

“Quick” messages, late requests from senior people, deadlines, guilt.

Why it breaks

Because nobody knew it was there, or because it was kept only sometimes, which teaches people to keep trying.

What makes it hold

  • Specific times.
  • An agreed emergency route.
  • Telling people once, kindly.
  • Keeping it every time.

Three kinds of circle

The Time Circle

Hours you're offline. The most common, and the most tested.

The Scope Circle

Work you won't take on. See the Word of Power: No.

The Self Circle

Things you won't do or accept, however asked. See the Dark Pact.

— the AnnotatorIf you manage people, your boundaries are permission for theirs. Send late emails, and your team will reply to them.

IVSigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • You check email in bed.
  • Messages at 21:30 get replies at 21:31.
  • Your holiday has a “just in case” laptop.
  • People are surprised when you don't answer.

VCounter-spells

  1. 1The Drawn Line

    Decide your boundary specifically (“no work messages after 19:00”) and tell people once.

    incantation“I'm offline after seven. If something's genuinely urgent, ring me; otherwise I'll pick it up at eight.”

  2. 2The Emergency Gate

    Agree what counts as an emergency and how it reaches you. Then everything else can wait.

  3. 3The Unbroken Ring

    Keep the line consistently. A boundary you break every third time teaches people to try three times.

VIForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Invisible Line

    Having a boundary nobody knows about, then resenting them for crossing it.

  • The Angry Ring

    Enforcing it with irritation. People feel judged and push harder.

  • The Just-This-Once

    Breaking it for anything that feels important. Everything will start feeling important.

VIIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

A reasonable workplace, an undrawn circle.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Nobody expects instant replies.
  • You do it anyway.
  • Your manager would support a boundary.

Do

  1. Draw the line specifically.
  2. Tell people once.
  3. Turn off notifications after hours.

Don’t: The invisible line.

If it gets worse: If people keep testing it, you're at Nightmare.

Nightmare

The tested circle.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Late messages keep coming.
  • Some are from senior people.
  • You feel guilty not replying.

Do

  1. Agree the Emergency Gate with your manager.
  2. Reply warmly in the morning.
  3. Keep it every time.
  4. Restate it once if needed.

Don’t: The just-this-once.

If it gets worse: If out-of-hours replies are demanded, you're at Hell.

Hell

The always-on culture.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Instant replies are expected at all hours.
  • Boundaries are seen as lack of commitment.
  • Others are burning out.

Do

  1. Talk to your manager about expectations and pay.
  2. Know your contract and working-time rights.
  3. Find allies.
  4. Read the Slow Hex.

Don’t: The angry ring.

If it gets worse: If it never changes, read Life After the Dungeon.

VIIIDo you break other people's circles?

Tick honestly.

  • You send work messages late at night.
  • You expect quick replies outside hours.
  • You've said “sorry to message late” and messaged anyway.
  • You've been irritated when someone didn't reply on a weekend.
  • Your team replies to you at 22:00.

The cure, if you ticked two: Use scheduled send. Write your late-night message, schedule it for 08:00. You keep your thought; they keep their evening.

IXThe rehearsal

The Drawn Line, said once.

Scene: Monday 7 April 2030, Pip's team meeting.

PipA small thing. From today I'm offline after seven and at weekends.

RafiWhat if something breaks?

PipIf it's the control room, a regulator or a board paper due next morning, ring me. Anything else, I'll pick up at eight.

RuthGood. And you should all do the same.

PipWill you?

Ruth…Remind me in a month.

— the AnnotatorPip reminded her in May. Ruth's last late email was sent on 2 May 2030.

XThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

Your boss messages at 21:30. Do you reply?

Not unless it's an agreed emergency. Reply at 08:00, warmly and fully.

Won't boundaries hurt your career?

Clear, consistent ones rarely do. Burning out definitely does.

Someone keeps crossing your line. What now?

Restate it once, kindly and clearly. Then keep it. If it continues, talk to your manager.

Life After the Dungeon

Every adventurer leaves the dungeon one day. The ones who leave well are the ones who remember there's a world outside.

Threat
Habitat
resignations, retirements, redundancies, the last day, the first Monday after
Weakness
leaving well, keeping the people, and knowing who you are without the job

IA sighting

March 2030 · the decision

The Chronicler booked a meeting with HR called “Retirement: options”. Twenty-two years at MetroVolt, eight reorgs, four transformations, two return-to-office decrees and one notebook, begun in 2008, of everything they'd learned about surviving it. They chose a date: 31 January 2031. They told Ruth in April, nine months ahead, “so the handover can be boring”.

Autumn 2030 · the Good Ending

They documented everything, trained two people, and said thank you, specifically, to forty-one colleagues. They left every Slack channel but three. They unpacked the go-bag from Act V: the CV went in the recycling, the five names became five coffees, the six months' savings in the pink pig became a train ticket to somewhere warm.

Thursday 30 January 2031 · the boomerang

At the leaving drinks was Marcus, made redundant in Act V in 2027, back since September 2030 as a contractor on Ascend's data work. Better paid, three days a week, and visibly happier. “Best thing that ever happened to me,” he said, “though I wouldn't have said so in room 4B.” Boomerangs, the Chronicler observed, need a runway, and Marcus had left one.

Friday 31 January · the last day

At the turnstile, the Chronicler handed in their badge. Then they handed Pip a battered notebook. On the first page, in 2008 handwriting: “Rule 1: the org chart lies.” On the last page, in fresh ink: “Rule 96: the dungeon was never the whole world.” And beneath it: “Your turn. Keep it honest. Keep it kind. Keep it funny.”

Monday 3 February 2031 · the first Monday after

The Chronicler was on an allotment in the rain, very happy. Pip, at his desk on floor 14, opened the notebook to a fresh page, and began a new line.

— the AnnotatorThe dungeon was never the whole world. Leave well, keep your people, and walk out into the daylight.

IIOrigin

flashbackHow the Chronicler came to keep the grimoire

  1. 2008 · the first page

    In their first month at MetroVolt, the Chronicler watches someone brilliant get sidelined by someone political. They write it down: Rule 1.

  2. 2008–2026 · the notes

    Every reorg, every creature, every lord: written down, with what worked. A survival guide for an audience of one.

  3. 2026–2030 · the apprentice

    Pip arrives. The Chronicler sees themselves in him, and starts sharing the notes. The Annotator adds the margins.

  4. 2031 · the handing over

    The notes become this grimoire. The badge goes back. The book goes on.

The turning point. One person deciding, in 2008, to write down what they learned, for no reason except that someone might need it.

What might have saved them. Nothing needed saving. This is the ending: someone leaves well, and leaves something behind.

— the AnnotatorEveryone leaves eventually. The question is what you leave: a crater, a door, or a book.

IIIKnow thy enemy

Everyone leaves a job eventually: by choice, by retirement, by redundancy. How you leave shapes your reputation, your relationships, and sometimes whether you can come back. And for people whose job has become their whole identity, leaving can feel like losing themselves. The answer is to build a self outside work long before you need it.

Leaving well

Good notice, a clean handover, specific thanks, and no burnt bridges.

The boomerang

Many people return to former employers, often in better roles. It only works if you left a runway.

The unbadged self

  • People who know you outside work.
  • Interests that aren't your job.
  • A sense of purpose that doesn't need a title.
  • Rest, without guilt.

Three kinds of leaving

The Chosen Leaving

Resignation or retirement. Do the Good Ending.

The Forced Leaving

Redundancy or dismissal. Read Act V; leave with dignity anyway.

The Returning

The boomerang. Go back only if something real has changed.

— the AnnotatorYou are not your job title. You are the person who did the job, and will do the next thing, and has a life beyond both.

IVSigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • You describe yourself only by your job title.
  • You can't imagine a week without work.
  • You're leaving and feel strangely lost.
  • Old colleagues have come back, and seem happier.

VCounter-spells

  1. 1The Good Ending

    Leave well: notice, handover, thank-yous, no burnt bridges. Your last month is what people remember.

  2. 2The Kept Fellowship

    Keep the people. Colleagues become friends, references, clients and future bosses.

    incantation“I'm leaving MetroVolt but not you. Coffee in March?”

  3. 3The Unbadged Self

    Before you leave (or long before), build a life and identity outside work: people, interests, purpose.

VIForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Burnt Bridge

    Leaving with a bang. The world is small, and boomerangs need a runway.

  • The Ghost Haunting

    Leaving but staying in every channel, commenting on everything. Let go.

  • The Badge Identity

    Being your job, entirely. When the badge goes, so do you.

VIIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

The Chosen Leaving.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • You're leaving on your terms.
  • Relationships are good.
  • You have something next.

Do

  1. Give generous notice.
  2. Hand over properly.
  3. Say thank you, specifically.

Don’t: The ghost haunting.

If it gets worse: If leaving makes you feel lost, you're at Nightmare.

Nightmare

The lost leaving.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Your job was your identity.
  • You don't know who you are without it.
  • The first Monday after feels empty.

Do

  1. Build routines outside work.
  2. Keep the fellowship.
  3. Try things you never had time for.
  4. Be patient with yourself.

Don’t: The badge identity.

If it gets worse: If you were forced out, you're at Hell.

Hell

The Forced Leaving.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Redundancy or dismissal.
  • It wasn't your choice.
  • You're angry or ashamed.

Do

  1. Read Act V's Go-Bag Scroll.
  2. Leave with dignity; it protects you.
  3. Get advice on your rights.
  4. Remember Marcus.

Don’t: The burnt bridge.

If it gets worse: Many people say, later, it was the best thing that happened. You don't have to believe that yet.

VIIIWho are you without the badge?

The last mirror in the grimoire. Tick honestly.

  • You describe yourself mainly by your job.
  • You can't imagine a month without work.
  • Most of your friends are colleagues.
  • You've no interests that aren't connected to work.
  • You've been planning to leave for years but can't picture after.

The cure, if you ticked two: This month, do one thing that has nothing to do with your job, with someone who has nothing to do with your job. It's the first page of your next chapter.

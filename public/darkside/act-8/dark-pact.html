The Dark Pact

Someone powerful offers you a small favour: change a number, skip a check, look away. The price is your name.

Threat
Habitat
board deadlines, targets, “just this once”, senior people who say “trust me”
Weakness
saying no clearly, offering an honest alternative, writing it down, and not standing alone

IA sighting

Tuesday 9 September 2030 · 16:30 · floor 27

Rupert called Pip to his office. The parent group's board met on 30 September. Ascend's savings, based on Pip's churn model, came to £9m. The target in Rupert's 2029 memo had implied £14m. Rupert slid a page across the table: four assumptions, changed. “Just use these. Everyone adjusts assumptions. The board expects £14m.”

The pact, examined

Pip read the page. Churn reduction doubled with no evidence. A cost saving counted twice. Two programmes' benefits claimed by both. It wasn't an assumption; it was a story. He felt sick. Then he remembered: there's always an honest alternative.

16:45 · the refusal

Pip said, calmly: he couldn't change the assumptions; the data didn't support it. He could show the £9m base case and a clearly labelled upside scenario, with what would have to be true for it. Rupert said “Let's not put this in writing” and “I'd hoped you were a team player”. Pip went back to his desk, wrote the Confirming Email (see the Phylactery), and rang Ruth.

30 September · the board

Ruth backed him, took it to the CFO, and the board paper showed £9m with a labelled upside of £11m. The board asked sensible questions and approved Ascend's next phase anyway. The pact was never signed. It did, however, have a price, which arrived on 2 October (see HR's True Allegiance).

— the AnnotatorEvery dark pact is offered as a small favour. Read the price: it's always your name.

IIOrigin

flashbackHow Rupert came to offer the pact

  1. 2028 · the target

    The parent group gives Rupert a cost target. His bonus and next role depend on it.

  2. June 2029 · the memo (Act VII)

    He announces “30% more with 20% fewer”. The number becomes a promise he has to keep.

  3. 2030 · the gap

    The real savings come in at £9m. The promise was £14m. He's seen others “adjust” before. Nobody was caught.

  4. 9 September · the pact

    He asks the person whose name is on the model. Not out of evil. Out of fear, and habit, and a deadline.

The turning point. A public number, promised before the work was done, that became impossible to walk back.

What might have saved them. Rupert saying to his own boss in June: “The real number is £9m. Here's why.” It would have cost him less than the pact did.

— the AnnotatorMost people who offer dark pacts aren't villains. They're frightened people with a deadline. That doesn't make the pact less dark.

IIIKnow thy enemy

A dark pact is a request to do something unethical or dishonest at work: change numbers, hide a problem, mislead a customer or regulator, skip a safety step. It's usually framed as small, normal or necessary, and comes from someone with power over you. Refusing is frightening. Agreeing is usually worse, because the work has your name on it.

How it's offered

Verbally, under time pressure, with “everyone does it”, “it's just an assumption”, “be a team player”.

Why people sign

Fear of the boss, wanting to be liked, believing it's small, and not seeing an alternative.

How to refuse

  • Say no clearly and calmly.
  • Offer an honest alternative.
  • Write it down the same day.
  • Tell your manager.

Three kinds of pact

The Grey Pact

Spin, emphasis, optimism. Argue for honesty; label scenarios clearly.

The Dark Pact

Falsifying, misleading, hiding. Refuse, document, escalate.

The Black Pact

Illegal or dangerous. Refuse, take advice, and see the Whistle of Last Resort.

— the AnnotatorIf you're asked to do something that might be illegal, take independent advice before you do anything else, including refusing in a way that could be used against you.

IVSigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • You're asked to change a number to fit a story.
  • The request is verbal, and late.
  • “Everyone does it” is part of the argument.
  • You feel sick when you think about it.

VCounter-spells

  1. 1The Honest Alternative

    Refuse the change, and offer something true that helps: a range, an upside scenario, a clearer story.

    incantation“I can't change the assumptions to reach £14m; the data doesn't support it. I can show £9m with a clearly labelled upside case.”

  2. 2The Written Record

    After the conversation, send the Confirming Email (see the Phylactery). Stick to facts.

  3. 3The Ally

    Tell your manager, the same day. Don't hold a dark pact alone.

    incantation“Ruth, I've been asked to change the churn model's numbers for the board. I said no. I want you to know before anyone else tells you.”

VIForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Small Signature

    Changing it “just a little”. There is no small version. Your name is on it.

  • The Silent Refusal

    Just not doing it, and saying nothing. They'll get someone else to, and you'll be blamed later.

  • The Public Accusation

    Denouncing them in a meeting. Facts first, privately and in writing.

VIIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

The Grey Pact.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Pressure to present things optimistically.
  • No falsification yet.
  • Your manager is reasonable.

Do

  1. Offer clearly labelled scenarios.
  2. Show what would have to be true.
  3. Discuss it openly.

Don’t: The small signature.

If it gets worse: If you're asked to change facts, you're at Nightmare.

Nightmare

The Dark Pact, from someone senior.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • You're asked to change numbers or hide facts.
  • It's verbal and urgent.
  • You're told to keep it quiet.

Do

  1. Refuse calmly.
  2. Offer the Honest Alternative.
  3. Send the Confirming Email.
  4. Tell your manager the same day.

Don’t: The silent refusal.

If it gets worse: If there's retaliation or it's illegal, you're at Hell.

Hell

The Black Pact, or retaliation for refusing.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • It may be illegal or unsafe.
  • You're being punished for refusing.
  • Your manager can't or won't help.

Do

  1. Get independent legal advice.
  2. Go to HR with your file.
  3. Use the speak-up route.
  4. Read the Whistle of Last Resort.

Don’t: The public accusation.

If it gets worse: Read HR's True Allegiance.

VIIIHave you offered a pact?

Especially if you manage people. Tick honestly.

  • You've asked someone to make numbers “look better”.
  • You've said “let's not put this in writing”.
  • You've promised a number before the work was done.
  • You've told someone refusing wasn't being a team player.
  • You've adjusted something you'd be embarrassed to explain.

The cure, if you ticked two: If you've promised a number you can't hit, tell your boss now. The truth costs less today than it will on board day.

The Honest Alternative.

Scene: Tuesday 9 September 2030, 16:40, Rupert's office.

RupertJust use these assumptions. Everyone adjusts. The board expects fourteen.

PipI can't change them. The data doesn't support doubling the churn reduction, and one saving's counted twice.

RupertIt's an assumption, Pip. Not a crime.

PipWhat I can do: the £9m base case, and a clearly labelled upside, with exactly what would have to be true to reach it.

— the AnnotatorPip's hands were shaking when he left. He wrote the email anyway. Then he rang Ruth from the stairwell.

XThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

A senior leader asks you to make the numbers look better than they are. What do you do?

Refuse, offer the Honest Alternative, write it down, tell your manager.

They say everyone does it. Is that true?

Sometimes. It doesn't matter. Your name is the one on the work.

What if refusing costs you your job?

It might cost you something. Signing can cost you far more: your reputation, your career, sometimes your freedom. Get advice.

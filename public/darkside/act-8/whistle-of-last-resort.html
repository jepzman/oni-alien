The Whistle of Last Resort

When something seriously wrong is happening, and the normal routes have failed, there is one more spell. Use it carefully.

Threat
Habitat
safety risks, fraud, serious legal breaches, cover-ups, the silence after you've raised it
Weakness
the right route, in the right order, with evidence, advice, and legal protection

IA sighting

Monday 3 November 2030 · the discovery

Tomasz, in Field Ops since Act V, was matching substation inspection records to van location data for a route-planning project. Forty inspections by Ironvale Services, MetroVolt's maintenance contractor, couldn't have happened: two signed at the same hour, sixty miles apart; vans that never left the depot. Substation inspections exist so that faults are found before they hurt someone.

Tuesday 4 November · “not your problem”

Tomasz showed his manager. His manager said inspections were “the contractor's responsibility, not ours”, and that Tomasz should “stick to the routing project”. Tomasz went home and didn't sleep.

Wednesday 5 November · the Advisor

He rang Pip, who'd once sat next to him, and Pip listened and said two things: write down exactly what you found, and get independent advice before you do anything else. Tomasz called an independent whistleblowing advice line. They explained his protections, and that the right first step was MetroVolt's own speak-up process, in writing.

6 to 27 November · the Proper Route, then the Higher Gate

He raised it formally through the Speak Up line, with a short factual summary and the evidence he'd legitimately used for his project. He got an acknowledgement, then nothing for three weeks. On 27 November, following the policy, he wrote again, to the Speak Up team and the Chair of the Audit Committee.

December · the inspections

The Audit Committee chair commissioned an independent check within days. All forty substations were inspected by 12 December. Two had faults; one was a serious oil leak. Ironvale's contract was suspended, then ended. MetroVolt reported it to the regulator itself, so Tomasz never had to. His manager was spoken to. Tomasz was thanked, in writing, by Dame Helena. He still hasn't fully slept well since November, but he says he'd do it again.

— the AnnotatorThe whistle is a last resort, not a first. But when it's needed, it's needed. Blow it carefully, with advice, in the right order.

IIOrigin

flashbackHow the inspections came to be invented

  1. 2027 · Horizon (Act V)

    The Famine cuts MetroVolt's own inspection team. The work goes to Ironvale, a contractor, on a fixed price per inspection.

  2. 2028 · the squeeze

    Ironvale's margins shrink. Its engineers are told to hit their numbers. Nobody at MetroVolt checks the records against anything.

  3. 2030 · the shortcut

    Some Ironvale crews start signing inspections they didn't do. The paperwork is perfect. The substations aren't inspected.

  4. 3 November · the match

    Tomasz joins two datasets for an unrelated project. The lie becomes visible, because two facts can't both be true.

The turning point. Outsourcing safety work without anyone checking it actually happened.

What might have saved them. One routine check, from 2027: match a sample of inspection records against independent data.

— the AnnotatorSerious wrongdoing is usually found by accident, by someone ordinary, doing something else. That someone might be you.

IIIKnow thy enemy

Whistleblowing means reporting serious wrongdoing (danger to health and safety, crime, fraud, environmental damage, cover-ups) usually after normal routes have failed or aren't safe. In many countries whistleblowers have legal protection from retaliation, but often only if they report in the right way, to the right people, in the right order. Getting independent advice early is the single most protective thing you can do.

The usual order

Write down what you know. Get advice. Raise it internally through the proper route, in writing. Escalate internally if ignored. Then, if serious and still ignored, a regulator. Going public is a last resort, and can remove your protection.

What protects you

Following the process, acting in good faith and in the public interest, raising it in writing, and taking advice.

What to keep

  • Your own notes and timeline.
  • Copies of what you raised and when.
  • Only the evidence you need, and advice on what you may keep.
  • Every reply, or the lack of one.

Three gates of the whistle

The Inner Gate

Your company's speak-up process. Often enough.

The Higher Gate

Audit committee, board, or group compliance. When the inner gate fails.

The Outer Gate

The regulator. When serious and ignored. With advice.

— the AnnotatorLaws differ by country. Many have independent charities or advice lines for whistleblowers; call one before you act.

IVSigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • You've found something that could hurt people or break the law.
  • You raised it and nothing happened.
  • You were told it's “not your problem”.
  • Records seem to have been changed or invented.

VCounter-spells

  1. 1The Proper Route

    Use the company's speak-up or whistleblowing process first, in writing. It often works, and it usually protects you.

    incantation“I'm raising a concern under the speak-up policy: 40 substation inspection records appear to describe inspections that couldn't have happened.”

  2. 2The Advisor

    Get independent advice early, from a whistleblowing charity, union or lawyer. Protections depend on how and to whom you raise it.

  3. 3The Higher Gate

    If internal routes fail and the risk is serious, escalate: audit committee, then the regulator. In that order, usually, and with advice.

VIForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Social Media Blast

    Going public first. It can remove your legal protection and end your career, and the problem may not be fixed.

  • The Evidence Raid

    Copying large amounts of confidential data. Keep what you need, take advice on what you may keep.

  • Looking Away

    Deciding it's not your problem. If it's safety, it might become someone's funeral.

VIIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

The Inner Gate works.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Something serious is wrong.
  • Your company has a speak-up process.
  • It's taken seriously.

Do

  1. Write down what you found.
  2. Get advice.
  3. Raise it in writing through the process.

Don’t: Looking away.

If it gets worse: If nothing happens, you're at Nightmare.

Nightmare

The silence after raising it.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • You raised it properly.
  • No meaningful response.
  • Your manager told you to drop it.

Do

  1. Follow up in writing.
  2. Escalate to the Higher Gate per policy.
  3. Keep every document.
  4. Stay in touch with your advisor.

Don’t: The social media blast.

If it gets worse: If you face retaliation or it's still ignored, you're at Hell.

Hell

Ignored, or punished.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • The risk is serious and ongoing.
  • Internal routes have failed.
  • You're being treated differently.

Do

  1. Take legal advice now.
  2. Consider the regulator, with advice.
  3. Document any retaliation.
  4. Look after yourself; this is hard.

Don’t: The evidence raid.

If it gets worse: Protections exist for this. Use them, carefully.

VIIIWould you look away?

Tick honestly.

  • You've noticed something seriously wrong and said nothing.
  • You don't know your company's speak-up process.
  • You've told someone it's “not our problem” when it might be.
  • As a manager, you've discouraged someone from raising a concern.
  • You think whistleblowers are troublemakers.

The cure, if you ticked two: Find your company's speak-up policy today and read it. If you ever need it, you'll be glad you know the way.

IXThe rehearsal

The Advisor, before anything else.

Scene: Wednesday 5 November 2030, Pip and Tomasz, on the phone.

TomaszForty inspections that couldn't have happened. My manager says it's not our problem.

PipIt might be someone's life. First: write down exactly what you found, today. Facts, dates.

TomaszThen go to the press?

PipNo. Get independent advice first. There are advice lines for exactly this. Then the speak-up process, in writing.

TomaszAnd if they ignore it?

PipThen there's a next gate, and your advisor will tell you which. You're not alone in this.

XThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

You find evidence of serious wrongdoing. What first?

Write down what you found. Get advice. Then raise it through the proper internal route, in writing.

Nothing happens after you raise it. What now?

Follow up in writing. Escalate to a higher internal body. If it's serious and still ignored, the regulator, with advice.

Are you protected?

In many countries, yes, if you follow the right process and act in the public interest. Take advice; it varies.

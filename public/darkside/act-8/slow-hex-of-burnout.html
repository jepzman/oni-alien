The Slow Hex of Burnout

A curse cast so slowly you don't notice it, until one morning you can't get out of the car.

Threat
Habitat
long seasons of pressure, roles with no end, people who care too much and rest too little
Weakness
noticing early, naming it out loud, real rest, and changing the conditions that cast it

IA sighting

January and February 2030 · the signs

After the kitchen conversation in Act VII, Hugo cancelled twenty meetings. It helped for a fortnight. Then Ascend's second phase began and the meetings grew back, like weeds. He stopped going to the Tuesday lunch. He snapped at Rafi over a typo, then apologised for twenty minutes. He told Pip he couldn't remember the last book he'd finished.

Thursday 6 March · 08:40

Hugo drove to The Grid, parked, turned off the engine, and could not get out of the car. He sat there for an hour and ten minutes with his badge in his hand, while it rained. Then he rang Pip, the one person who'd once asked how he was.

That morning · the Naming

Pip came down to the car park with two coffees and got in the passenger seat. Hugo said: “I think something's wrong with me.” Pip said: “I think you might be burning out. You're not the first. Will you see a doctor today?” Hugo's GP signed him off for four weeks. Ruth told Hugo's manager, kindly and quickly, and the programme was reassigned for a month.

April to June · the Condition Change

Occupational health recommended a phased return: three days a week for a month, one programme instead of three, no meetings after 16:00. Hugo agreed, and asked for it in writing (he'd read the Phylactery). By June he was back full time, with one programme, a Friday Outcome Ledger and a Salt Circle. He still says “crazy week”. Now he means it as a joke.

— the AnnotatorBurnout isn't weakness. It's what happens to strong people who carried too much for too long. Name it, get help, and change the load.

IIOrigin

flashbackHow the hex was cast on Hugo, over three years

  1. 2026 · Lumen (Act II)

    Hugo lives on Slack and never sleeps. It works. He's praised for commitment. The first thread of the hex.

  2. 2027 · Horizon (Act V)

    He survives the Famine by being indispensable. Fear joins exhaustion. He stops taking holidays.

  3. 2029 · Ascend (Act VII)

    Fifty-four meetings a week, a mouse jiggler, three programmes. Cynicism arrives. He calls it “realism”.

  4. March 2030 · the car park

    The hex completes. His body refuses what his mind kept agreeing to.

The turning point. Every year, being rewarded for the very habits that were wearing him down.

What might have saved them. A manager in 2027 asking “When did you last take a full week off?”, and acting on the answer.

— the AnnotatorBurnout is rarely one bad month. It's years of small overloads, each one praised.

IIIKnow thy enemy

Burnout is a state of chronic work stress that hasn't been managed: exhaustion, cynicism or detachment about work, and a sense of being ineffective. It builds slowly. It overlaps with depression and anxiety, and can affect sleep, health and relationships. It's caused mostly by conditions (load, control, fairness, recognition), not by personal weakness.

What it feeds on

Unending demands, little control, no recovery time, and caring deeply about work that never finishes.

Why it's missed

Because it arrives slowly, and because its early signs (long hours, always available) are often praised.

The three marks of the hex

  • Exhaustion that rest doesn't fix.
  • Cynicism and distance from work you used to care about.
  • Feeling you achieve nothing.
  • Often: poor sleep, irritability, getting ill more.

Three stages of the hex

The Smouldering

Tired and stretched, still functioning. The best time to act.

The Burning

Cynical, exhausted, slipping. Needs real changes and probably a doctor.

The Ash

Can't function. Needs time off, medical support and a different return.

— the AnnotatorIf you ever feel hopeless or have thoughts of harming yourself, talk to a doctor or a crisis line today. That's not a work problem; it's a health emergency.

IVSigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • You're tired after a weekend off.
  • Work you used to enjoy feels pointless.
  • You've become cynical about everything and everyone.
  • Small tasks feel impossible; you can't concentrate.

VCounter-spells

  1. 1The Naming

    Say it out loud, to yourself and then to someone: “I think I'm burning out.” The hex weakens when named.

    incantation“I think I'm burning out. I'm not sure what to do, but I needed to say it.”

  2. 2The Healer's Door

    See a doctor. Burnout overlaps with depression and anxiety, and it's not a weakness to get help.

  3. 3The Condition Change

    Rest helps, but if you return to the same conditions, the hex recasts. Change something real: hours, scope, role.

    incantation“When I come back, I need fewer meetings and one programme, not three. Can we agree that now?”

VIForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • Pushing Through

    Ignoring it until after the launch. There is always another launch.

  • The Holiday Cure

    Assuming two weeks off will fix it. It helps; it doesn't change what caused it.

  • The Shame Hide

    Hiding it because it feels like failure. It's an injury, not a character flaw.

VIIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

The Smouldering.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • You're tired most of the time.
  • Work feels heavier than it used to.
  • You still function well.

Do

  1. Do the Naming, to yourself and one person.
  2. Take real time off.
  3. Cut one thing from your load now.

Don’t: Pushing through.

If it gets worse: If cynicism and exhaustion stay after a break, you're at Nightmare.

Nightmare

The Burning.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Rest doesn't help.
  • You're cynical about everything.
  • Your work is slipping.

Do

  1. See a doctor.
  2. Tell your manager what you need changed.
  3. Ask about occupational health.
  4. Protect sleep above everything.

Don’t: The holiday cure.

If it gets worse: If you can't function, you're at Hell.

Hell

The Ash.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • You can't face work.
  • Your health is affected.
  • Small tasks are impossible.

Do

  1. Stop. See a doctor today.
  2. Take the time off you're signed off for.
  3. Plan a phased return, in writing.
  4. Change the conditions before you go back.

Don’t: The shame hide.

If it gets worse: If the job can't change, read Life After the Dungeon.

VIIIIs the hex on you?

Tick honestly. This one matters.

  • You're exhausted after a weekend or holiday.
  • You feel cynical about work you used to care about.
  • You feel you achieve nothing, however hard you work.
  • You can't remember your last full week off.
  • People close to you have said you seem different.

The cure, if you ticked two: If you ticked three or more, tell one person this week: “I think I might be burning out.” Then book a doctor's appointment. Naming it is the first counter-spell.

IXThe rehearsal

The Naming.

Scene: Thursday 6 March 2030, 09:55, Hugo's car.

PipCoffee. Can I sit in?

HugoI've been here an hour. I can't make myself go in. I think something's wrong with me.

PipI think you might be burning out. You're not the first, and it's not a weakness.

HugoThere's the programme board at eleven…

PipRuth can cover it. Will you call your doctor this morning? I'll sit here while you do.

Hugo…Yes. Okay. Thank you.

— the AnnotatorHugo says now that the most important thing anyone did was sit in the car. Not fix it. Just sit there.

XThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

You're exhausted, cynical and can't concentrate. What first?

The Naming: say it to someone you trust. Then the Healer's Door.

Is burnout just being tired?

No. Tired is fixed by rest. Burnout is exhaustion plus cynicism plus feeling useless, and needs more than sleep.

A colleague seems to be burning out. What can you do?

Notice out loud, kindly, privately. Ask how they are, twice. Then help them reach someone who can help.

The Annotator's last wordThe hex is slow, so notice early. Name it, get help, and change what cast it.

The Phylactery of Paper

Keep your soul somewhere they can't destroy it: in writing, dated, in a place you control.

Threat
Habitat
verbal agreements, disputed decisions, reviews, the day someone says “I never said that”
Weakness
nothing; it is the weakness of everything else

IA sighting

Tuesday 9 September 2030 · 16:30 · floor 27

Rupert asked Pip, in a meeting with no one else present, to change the churn model's assumptions so that Ascend's savings showed £14m rather than £9m for the parent board (see the Dark Pact). Pip said no and offered an alternative. Rupert said: “Let's not put any of this in writing.”

17:12 · the Confirming Email

Pip wrote anyway. Short, factual, friendly: “Thanks for today. To confirm what we discussed: you asked for the savings to show £14m; I explained the model supports £9m. I'll send both the base case and a clearly labelled upside scenario, with assumptions, by Friday.” He copied Ruth. He saved it in his own folder, alongside his Book of Deeds.

Thursday 2 October · the test

Rupert removed Pip from the Ascend steering group and told Ruth Pip “wasn't a team player on the board paper”. When Ruth asked what that meant, Rupert said Pip had “misunderstood the request”. Pip's email, sent the same day and never disputed, said otherwise.

October · the folder speaks

When Pip went to Karen in HR (see HR's True Allegiance), he brought a timeline, the email, his Book of Deeds (three years of Fridays) and his last three reviews, all excellent. Karen said, after reading it: “This is the clearest file I've been given all year.” The phylactery held.

— the AnnotatorWords fade, memories change, people leave. Paper stays. Keep your soul in writing.

  2. 2028 · the Change Ledger (Act VI)

  3. 2029 · the Decision Rule (Act VII)

The turning point. Good habits adopted for ordinary reasons, which turned out to be armour.

What might have saved them. Nothing needed saving. This is how it should go: write things down long before you need to.

— the AnnotatorDocumentation isn't paranoia when it's a habit. It only looks paranoid when you start the day you're in trouble.

IIIKnow thy enemy

Documentation protects you because memories differ and change, especially under pressure. A short written record of decisions, requests and your achievements, made at the time and kept somewhere you control, is often the deciding factor in disputes, reviews and investigations. Done as a habit, it also just makes you better at your job.

What to keep

Decisions and requests (especially unusual ones), your achievements, your reviews, your contract and pay letters, and any concerns you've raised.

Where to keep it

Your own notes and copies of your own documents, somewhere you control, following company policy. Never confidential company data.

How to write it

  • Same day.
  • Facts, not feelings.
  • Short and friendly.
  • Copy the right people.

Three kinds of phylactery

The Deed Book

Your achievements. For reviews, promotions and CVs.

The Decision Scroll

What was agreed. For disputes and changing memories.

The Concern Record

What you raised and when. For the rare day it really matters.

— the AnnotatorSecretly recording conversations is illegal or against policy in many places. A friendly confirming email is legal everywhere and far more useful.

IVSigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • Important decisions are only ever verbal.
  • Someone senior says “let's not put this in writing”.
  • Your achievements live only in your memory.
  • A manager's version of events keeps changing.

VCounter-spells

  1. 1The Confirming Email

    After any important conversation, send a short, friendly summary. It's a record, and it's polite.

    incantation“Thanks for today. To confirm what we discussed: you asked for the savings to show £14m; I explained the model supports £9m. I'll send both scenarios with assumptions.”

  2. 2The Book of Deeds

    Keep a weekly record of what you did (see Act IV). It's your review, your CV, and your defence.

  3. 3The Personal Copy

    Keep your own copies of your reviews, contracts and key feedback, somewhere you control, within your company's rules.

VIForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Paranoid Record

    Recording conversations secretly or documenting everything about everyone. It's often against policy, and it poisons trust.

  • Stealing the Vault

    Taking confidential company data “for protection”. Keep your own records, not theirs.

  • The Memory Gamble

    Trusting you'll remember what was agreed. You won't. They won't either.

VIIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

The ordinary habit.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Nothing is wrong.
  • Decisions are sometimes verbal.
  • Reviews rely on memory.

Do

  1. Start the Book of Deeds.
  2. Confirm big decisions by email.
  3. Keep copies of your reviews.

Don’t: The memory gamble.

If it gets worse: If someone senior avoids writing things down, you're at Nightmare.

Nightmare

The unwritten request.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • You're asked for something unusual, verbally.
  • “Let's not put this in writing.”
  • You feel uneasy.

Do

  1. Send the Confirming Email the same day.
  2. Copy your manager.
  3. Save it in your own folder.
  4. Stick to facts.

Don’t: The paranoid record.

If it gets worse: If the record is disputed or used against you, you're at Hell.

Hell

The disputed record.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Someone denies what was said.
  • Your role or review is affected.
  • HR or an investigation is involved.

Do

  1. Assemble a timeline with documents.
  2. Go to HR with the folder.
  3. Get independent advice if it's serious.
  4. Stay factual and calm.

Don’t: Stealing the vault.

If it gets worse: Read HR's True Allegiance.

VIIIIs your soul unprotected?

Tick honestly.

  • You don't have copies of your last three reviews.
  • Your achievements live only in your memory.
  • Important decisions with your manager are never written down.
  • You couldn't build a timeline of last year if you needed to.
  • You've been told “I never said that” and had nothing to show.

The cure, if you ticked two: Tonight, start a document. One line: what you did this week. Then one line every Friday. In three years, it'll be your best friend.

XThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

Your manager agreed something important verbally. What do you do?

The Confirming Email, the same day, friendly and short.

Isn't documenting everything a bit paranoid?

Writing down decisions is just good practice. Writing down everything about people is paranoia. Know the difference.

What should you keep personal copies of?

Your contract, reviews, pay letters and your Book of Deeds. Not company data.

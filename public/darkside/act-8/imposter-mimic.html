The Mimic of Imposter Syndrome

It looks like a treasure chest of your inadequacy. Look closer. It's furniture.

Threat
Habitat
new roles, promotions, rooms full of senior people, the first week of anything
Weakness
evidence, written down; saying the fear out loud; and noticing who else feels it

IA sighting

Monday 2 June 2030 · the appointment

Asha was appointed Head of GridSight: twelve people, eight clients, a budget of £2.4 million. Ruth announced it. Everyone was delighted. Asha went home and told her partner she'd fooled them all, and it was only a matter of time.

June · the mimic

She worked until midnight preparing for meetings she'd have aced unprepared. She explained away every compliment (“the team did it”, “I got lucky with Northshire”). In her first leadership meeting she said nothing for an hour because everyone else seemed to know things she didn't.

Friday 20 June · the Spoken Fear

She told Pip, over lunch, quietly: “I think I'm a fraud.” Pip laughed, then saw she meant it, and stopped. He said he'd felt exactly the same as acting head in 2027. Then she asked Ruth, who said: “Every single week of my first year at Northgate.” Then she asked the Chronicler, who said: “Twenty-two years. Still sometimes.”

The Evidence Chest

Pip suggested she open her own files. Asha had kept Friday notes since the Green Dot episode, three years of them: the Northshire win, the Brackenfield rescue, 312 data questions, four junior analysts trained. She read them on a Sunday evening. The chest wasn't full of inadequacy. It was full of her.

— the AnnotatorThe mimic looks like proof you don't belong. Open it. It's full of proof you do.

IIOrigin

flashbackHow the mimic came to live in Asha's office

  1. Childhood · the high bar

    Asha grows up being told she has to be twice as good to be considered equal. She becomes very, very good.

  2. 2025 · the glue years

    Her work at MetroVolt is invisible (see Act IV's Invisible Work Curse). Nobody says she's excellent, so she doesn't believe it.

  3. 2028 · GridSight

    She does the hardest work and gets the least credit (see Act VI). Success feels like luck because it was never named.

  4. 2030 · the promotion

    Finally named, she can't believe it. Years of unrecognised work became years of doubting herself.

The turning point. Years of excellent work that nobody named, so she never learned to name it herself.

What might have saved them. Managers saying, specifically and often: “That was excellent, and here's why.”

— the AnnotatorImposter feelings are common in people whose work has been undervalued. They're a symptom of the room, not a verdict on you.

IIIKnow thy enemy

Imposter syndrome is the persistent feeling that you aren't as capable as others think, and that you'll be found out. It's very common, especially in new roles and among people from groups who've been underestimated. It's not a diagnosis; it's a pattern of thinking, and it often coexists with genuine competence.

What it whispers

“You got lucky.” “They'll find out.” “Everyone else knows what they're doing.”

Why it lies

Because you compare your inside (doubts, effort) with everyone else's outside (confidence, results).

What quietens it

  • Written evidence.
  • Saying the fear out loud.
  • Hearing others admit it.
  • Calling it learning, not failing.

Three kinds of mimic

The New-Role Mimic

Appears in the first months of anything. Fades with evidence.

The Room Mimic

Appears around senior people. Remember they're often feeling it too.

The Lifelong Mimic

Never quite leaves. Learn to hear it and do the work anyway.

— the AnnotatorIf you manage someone, praise specifically. “Great job” bounces off the mimic. “Your Northshire plan won the contract because it was credible” sticks.

IVSigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • You think your success was luck.
  • You over-prepare for everything, out of fear.
  • Praise makes you uncomfortable.
  • You're waiting to be found out.

VCounter-spells

  1. 1The Evidence Chest

    Keep a file of what you've done, the feedback you got and the problems you solved. Read it when the mimic speaks.

  2. 2The Spoken Fear

    Tell someone you trust. Almost everyone senior will say: me too.

    incantation“Honestly, I feel like a fraud in this job. Did you ever feel like that?”

  3. 3The Learning Frame

    Replace “I should already know this” with “I'm learning this”. New roles are meant to stretch.

VIForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Over-Preparation Spiral

    Working twice as hard to avoid being found out. That's how imposters burn out.

  • Refusing the Stretch

    Turning down roles because you don't feel ready. Nobody does.

  • Dismissing Praise

    Explaining away every compliment. Say thank you. Write it in the chest.

VIIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

The New-Role Mimic.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • You've just started something new.
  • You feel out of your depth.
  • Your results are fine.

Do

  1. Open your Evidence Chest.
  2. Use the Learning Frame.
  3. Ask questions; it's allowed.

Don’t: Dismissing praise.

If it gets worse: If the feeling makes you overwork, you're at Nightmare.

Nightmare

The overworking imposter.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • You over-prepare everything.
  • You work late from fear.
  • You never feel ready.

Do

  1. Do the Spoken Fear with someone senior.
  2. Set a limit on preparation.
  3. Read your evidence weekly.
  4. Draw a Salt Circle.

Don’t: The over-preparation spiral.

If it gets worse: If it stops you taking opportunities, you're at Hell.

Hell

The paralysing mimic.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • You turn down roles.
  • You stay silent in meetings.
  • You've stopped trying new things.

Do

  1. Talk to a mentor or coach.
  2. Take one small stretch on purpose.
  3. Consider talking to a professional if anxiety is constant.
  4. Keep the chest open.

Don’t: Refusing the stretch.

If it gets worse: Remember: nobody feels ready. The ready-feeling comes after.

VIIIAre you feeding someone else's mimic?

Especially if you manage people. Tick honestly.

  • You rarely praise people specifically.
  • You assume confident people are competent and quiet ones aren't.
  • You've never told your team you felt out of your depth once.
  • You correct in public and praise in private, or not at all.
  • You've attributed someone's success to luck.

The cure, if you ticked two: This week, tell one person exactly what they did well and why it mattered. You may be quietening a mimic you didn't know was there.

XThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

You've been promoted and feel like a fraud. What do you do?

Open the Evidence Chest. Then tell someone you trust how you feel.

Is imposter syndrome a sign you're not good enough?

Usually the opposite. It's common in capable people who take their work seriously.

When is the feeling worth listening to?

When there's a specific skill gap. Then it's a learning plan, not a verdict.

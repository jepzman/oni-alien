Reputation Is Your Hit Points

Your reputation is your health bar. One hit can drain it. Only small, steady acts heal it.

Threat
Habitat
every meeting, every email, every room you're not in
Weakness
consistency over time, owning mistakes fast, and being the same person in every room

IA sighting

2026 · the drain

In Act II, Dorian was the Credit Vampire: he presented Pip's outage model as his own, twice. When it came out, his reputation dropped like a health bar hit by a boss. For a year, people checked his work twice, left him out of meetings, and said “Dorian?” with a particular tone.

2027 to 2029 · the Steady Heal

Dorian did the small things, every time. He named his builders in every presentation (“Asha's data, Tomasz's model”). He delivered what he said, when he said. When he got something wrong in 2028, he owned it in the same meeting: “That was my error; here's the fix.” Nobody noticed any single one of these. Everybody noticed the pattern, eventually.

Monday 14 April 2030 · the test

A new director, who'd never met Dorian, asked around about him before giving him a big planning role. The answers came back: “reliable”, “gives credit”, “owns his mistakes”. One person said: “He used to be a bit of a credit-taker, years ago. Not now.” The director gave him the role.

Later that week

Dorian told Pip about it over coffee, and then said something he'd never said before: “I'm sorry about the outage model. In 2026. I should have said it then.” Pip said: “You've been saying it every week for four years.”

IIOrigin

flashbackHow Dorian lost his hit points, and got them back

  1. 2024 · the old school

    Dorian's first manager taught him that whoever presents the work owns it. He believed it.

  2. 2026 · the big hit

    Caught presenting Pip's model as his own. In one meeting, years of reputation drain away.

  3. 2027 · the choice

    He could leave and start again elsewhere, or stay and rebuild. He stays. It's harder.

  4. 2027–2030 · the ticks

    Hundreds of small, reliable, credit-giving acts. By 2030, the bar is full again, and stronger for being rebuilt.

The turning point. Choosing to stay and rebuild, slowly, rather than run from a damaged reputation.

What might have saved them. Nothing saved him from the first hit. What saved his career was consistency afterwards.

— the AnnotatorA reputation can be rebuilt. Not with an apology tour. With years of being reliably the person you want to be known as.

IIIKnow thy enemy

Your reputation at work is what people say about you in rooms you're not in. It's built from many small experiences: did you deliver, were you fair, did you give credit, were you the same with juniors as with seniors? It's lost much faster than it's built, usually through a single visible act of unreliability or unfairness, and it's healed only by consistent behaviour over time.

What builds it

Reliability, fairness, credit given, mistakes owned. Boring, repeated, unglamorous.

What drains it

Broken promises, credit taken, mistakes hidden, two faces. One big hit or many small cuts.

What heals it

  • Consistency over months and years.
  • Owning mistakes fast.
  • Giving credit visibly.
  • Patience.

Three kinds of reputation damage

The Scratch

A small mistake. Owned fast, healed in weeks.

The Wound

A visible failure or unfairness. Healed in a year or two of consistency.

The Curse

A betrayal of trust. Heals slowly, if ever, and only with years of change.

— the AnnotatorJuniors become seniors. The way you treat someone in their first year is the reputation they'll carry about you for thirty.

IVSigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • People describe you with one word, and it's not the one you'd choose.
  • An old mistake still comes up in conversation.
  • You're not invited into rooms you used to be in.
  • People check your work more than others'.

VCounter-spells

  1. 1The Steady Heal

    Do the small reliable things every time: deliver what you said, when you said, and give credit.

  2. 2The Fast Own

    When you make a mistake, own it quickly and plainly. It costs fewer hit points than a slow cover-up.

    incantation“That was my error. Here's what happened and what I've already fixed.”

  3. 3The Same Face

    Be the same person in every room: with seniors, juniors, clients. People compare notes.

VIForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Big Gesture Heal

    Trying to fix a damaged reputation with one grand act. It looks like performance.

  • The Slow Cover-Up

    Hiding a mistake until it's found. The finding costs ten times the mistake.

  • The Two Faces

    Charming upwards, dismissive downwards. Juniors become seniors, and they remember.

VIIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

The Scratch.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • A small, visible mistake.
  • People noticed.
  • Your record is otherwise good.

Do

  1. Do the Fast Own.
  2. Fix it visibly.
  3. Carry on being reliable.

Don’t: The slow cover-up.

If it gets worse: If people now doubt you, you're at Nightmare.

Nightmare

The Wound.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • People check your work more.
  • You're left out of rooms.
  • An old mistake keeps coming up.

Do

  1. Do the Steady Heal, every day.
  2. Give credit visibly.
  3. Ask a trusted colleague what people say.
  4. Be patient: a year, maybe two.

Don’t: The big gesture heal.

If it gets worse: If it was a betrayal of trust, you're at Hell.

Hell

The Curse.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • You did something genuinely unfair.
  • People don't trust you.
  • It's your reputation's defining story.

Do

  1. Acknowledge it, once, sincerely, to the person affected.
  2. Change the behaviour permanently.
  3. Accept it'll take years.
  4. Consider whether a fresh start is wiser.

Don’t: The two faces.

If it gets worse: Ask Dorian. It can be done.

VIIIHow full is your bar?

Tick honestly.

  • You've missed a deadline without warning someone first.
  • You've taken credit that wasn't fully yours.
  • You're nicer to seniors than juniors.
  • You've hidden a mistake and hoped nobody noticed.
  • You don't know what people say about you.

The cure, if you ticked two: Ask one trusted colleague: “What's my reputation here, honestly?” Then listen without defending.

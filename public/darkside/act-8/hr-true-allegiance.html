HR's True Allegiance

HR serves the company. Sometimes that means protecting the company from you. Sometimes it means protecting the company from your manager.

Threat
Habitat
grievances, investigations, policies, the quiet room on floor 6
Weakness
understanding what HR is for, bringing facts, and knowing when your interests and the company's align

IA sighting

Thursday 2 October 2030 · the price

Three weeks after Pip refused the Dark Pact, Rupert removed him from the Ascend steering group and told Ruth he “wasn't a team player on the board paper”. Pip's September review, due that month, was suddenly “under discussion”.

Friday 3 October · the doubt

Pip didn't want to go to HR. Everyone said HR protected managers. The Annotator, in the margin: “HR protects the company. A senior manager punishing someone for refusing to falsify a board paper is a very large risk to the company. Go.”

Tuesday 7 October · Karen

Pip brought the Facts Folder: a one-page timeline, the 9 September email, the board paper, his Book of Deeds, his last three reviews. He asked for two things: his review to be completed on its merits, and the steering-group decision to be looked at. Karen read everything twice, asked careful questions, and explained what would happen next and what she could and couldn't keep confidential.

October to December · the allegiance

Karen opened a formal review. The CFO confirmed the board-paper history. Pip's review was completed by Ruth: “exceeds expectations”. He was reinstated to the steering group in November. Rupert's contract with MetroVolt ended in December; the parent group said only that Ascend was “moving to a new phase”. Karen told Pip, carefully: “I work for the company. This time, that meant working for you.”

— the AnnotatorHR serves the company. Bring them a problem that's also the company's risk, with the facts, and you'll find an ally.

IIOrigin

flashbackHow HR came to be mistrusted

  1. 1990s · Personnel

    HR is “personnel”: payroll, hiring, policies. It's seen as admin.

  2. 2000s · the business partner

    HR becomes a partner to managers, helping them deliver. Employees start to see it as management's side.

  3. 2010s · the stories

    Everyone hears of someone who went to HR and was managed out. The stories spread faster than the good outcomes.

  4. Karen · 2027 onwards

    Karen (Act IV's pay-band owner) has seen it all. She knows exactly whose side she's on, and when that's you.

The turning point. A role that serves the company being mistaken, by everyone, for one that serves either managers or employees.

What might have saved them. Understanding from the start: HR's job is to protect the company, including from bad managers.

— the AnnotatorHR isn't your friend or your enemy. It's the company's immune system. Bring it a genuine infection, with evidence.

IIIKnow thy enemy

HR exists to protect and serve the organisation: to hire, pay, develop and manage people, and to keep the company out of legal and reputational trouble. That means HR is not your advocate. But it also means HR has strong reasons to act when a manager's behaviour (retaliation, discrimination, harassment, unsafe or unlawful requests) exposes the company to risk.

What HR is for

The company's people processes, and its legal and reputational safety.

When your interests align

When your problem is also a risk: discrimination, harassment, retaliation, safety, legal breaches, serious misconduct.

How to go well

  • A one-page timeline.
  • Documents, not just feelings.
  • A clear ask.
  • Independent advice if serious.

Three kinds of HR visit

The Advice Visit

Questions about policy or options. Usually low risk.

The Concern Visit

A problem with a manager or colleague. Bring facts; ask what's confidential.

The Formal Complaint

A grievance or whistleblowing. Get advice; follow the process exactly.

— the AnnotatorAsk HR, before you share details: “What can you keep confidential, and what would you have to act on?”

IVSigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • You're unsure whether to go to HR.
  • Your problem involves a senior manager.
  • You've heard HR “always sides with management”.
  • Your problem could become a legal risk for the company.

VCounter-spells

  1. 1The Aligned Interest

    Go to HR when your problem is also a risk to the company: discrimination, retaliation, safety, legal breaches. Then you're on the same side.

  2. 2The Facts Folder

    Bring facts, dates and documents, not feelings alone. HR acts on evidence.

    incantation“On 9 September I was asked to change the model's assumptions. I refused, in writing. On 2 October I was removed from the steering group. Here are the emails.”

  3. 3The Outside Counsel

    If it's serious, get independent advice (a union, a lawyer, an advice service) before or alongside HR.

VIForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Confession Booth

    Telling HR everything you feel, assuming confidentiality. HR may have to act on what you say.

  • The Total Distrust

    Never going to HR because “they side with management”. On serious issues, they often can't afford to.

  • The Emotional Complaint

    Arriving with anger and no facts. It's hard for anyone to act on.

VIIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

The Advice Visit.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • A question about policy.
  • No conflict yet.
  • HR is approachable.

Do

  1. Ask clearly.
  2. Ask what's confidential.
  3. Keep notes of the answer.

Don’t: The confession booth.

If it gets worse: If your manager is the problem, you're at Nightmare.

Nightmare

The Concern Visit.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Your manager or a senior person is the problem.
  • It's affecting your work or review.
  • You're unsure HR will help.

Do

  1. Bring the Facts Folder.
  2. Make a clear ask.
  3. Frame it as a risk: retaliation, fairness.
  4. Follow up in writing.

Don’t: The emotional complaint.

If it gets worse: If it's retaliation, discrimination or illegal, you're at Hell.

Hell

The Formal Complaint.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Serious wrongdoing or retaliation.
  • Your job may be at risk.
  • Lawyers may get involved.

Do

  1. Get independent advice first.
  2. Follow the formal process exactly.
  3. Keep every document.
  4. Don't discuss it widely.

Don’t: The total distrust.

If it gets worse: If internal routes fail on something serious, read the Whistle of Last Resort.

VIIIAre you giving HR reasons to doubt you?

Tick honestly.

  • You've gone to HR angry with no facts.
  • You've expected HR to take your side automatically.
  • You've told HR things you assumed were confidential, without asking.
  • You've never read your company's grievance policy.
  • You've avoided HR on something serious because of rumours.

The cure, if you ticked two: Read your company's grievance and speak-up policies once, on a calm day. You'll know the map before you need it.

XThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

Is HR on your side?

HR is on the company's side. When the company's interest is fairness and legality, that's your side too.

When should you go to HR?

When a problem involves discrimination, harassment, retaliation, safety or law, or when your manager is the problem.

What should you bring?

A clear timeline, documents, and what you're asking for.

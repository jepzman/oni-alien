Quiet Quitting vs Quiet Thriving

Two people leave at 17:30. One has given up. One has decided what's enough. They look identical from the corridor.

Threat
Habitat
the years after disillusionment, roles with no next step, people who've stopped giving extra
Weakness
choosing deliberately what you give, doing it well, and finding meaning that isn't only the job

IA sighting

2015 · the Chronicler's decision

After their third reorg, the Chronicler made a decision they never announced. They would do excellent work, in their hours, with care. They'd mentor anyone who asked. They'd leave at 17:30 with the work done. They'd stop volunteering for things that fed other people's ambitions. They called it, privately, enough.

2015 to 2030 · the quiet thriving

For fifteen years, the Chronicler's work was reliable and good. They trained Pip, Asha and a dozen others. They gardened, sang in a choir and read everything. Some people thought they'd given up. The ones who worked with them knew otherwise.

Wednesday 15 October 2030 · Neil

Neil, in Billing, had been passed over three times. In 2024 he'd stopped caring: minimum effort, camera off, resentful silence. He asked the Chronicler, over coffee, how they'd done the same thing for so long without hating it. The Chronicler said gently: “I didn't do the same thing. You stopped caring. I chose what to care about. They look alike from the corridor.”

That winter · the Honest Conversation

Neil thought about it for a month. Then he asked Field Ops if they needed someone who understood billing data. They did. In January he moved. He still leaves at 17:30. Now he leaves having done something he's proud of.

— the AnnotatorEnough is not a failure. Enough, chosen and done well, is a kind of freedom.

IIOrigin

flashbackHow two people came to leave at 17:30

  1. The Chronicler · 2015

    After a reorg that undid two years of their work, they decide work will be part of life, not all of it. They choose what to give.

  2. Neil · 2019–2024

    Passed over three times, without explanation. Each time, he gives a little less. Nobody notices until he gives nothing extra at all.

  3. 2024–2030 · the two paths

    The Chronicler thrives quietly: good work, mentoring, a full life. Neil coasts resentfully: minimum work, a small grey life at his desk.

  4. 2030 · the conversation

    Neil notices they leave at the same time, and asks why one of them seems happy.

The turning point. Neil drifting into giving less, from hurt, rather than choosing it, from clarity.

What might have saved them. Neil, in 2019, being told honestly why he'd been passed over, and what would change it.

— the AnnotatorQuiet quitting is usually a wound. Quiet thriving is usually a decision. Both look like leaving on time.

IIIKnow thy enemy

“Quiet quitting” describes doing only what your job requires and no more. Sometimes it's a healthy correction to overwork: doing your job, not donating your life. Sometimes it's a sign of deep disengagement: someone who's given up but hasn't left. The behaviour can look identical. The difference is inside: resentment and emptiness, or clarity and peace.

Quiet thriving

Good work, in your hours, with care, by choice, and a life outside that fills you.

Quiet quitting (the wounded kind)

Minimum work, with resentment, because you've stopped believing anything will change.

How to tell which you are

  • Are you proud of your work?
  • Do you leave feeling calm or bitter?
  • Is your life outside full?
  • Did you choose this, or drift into it?

Three kinds of quiet

The Thriving Quiet

Chosen, peaceful, good work. Keep it.

The Resting Quiet

Recovering from overwork or burnout. Healthy, for a while.

The Wounded Quiet

Resentful, empty. Needs the Honest Conversation.

— the AnnotatorIf you manage someone who's gone quiet, don't judge it. Ask, kindly, whether they chose it or were wounded into it.

IVSigns and omens

Tick two or more and you are in its lair.

  • You've stopped volunteering for anything.
  • You do exactly what's asked, no more.
  • You feel either resentful or peaceful about it.
  • Work used to be your identity. It isn't now.

VCounter-spells

  1. 1The Deliberate Enough

    Decide what you'll give: good work, in your hours, with care. Then give it fully, without guilt.

  2. 2The Chosen Extra

    Pick one thing beyond the job you genuinely enjoy (mentoring, a community, a craft) and give that freely.

  3. 3The Honest Conversation

    If you've quietly stopped caring, say so to yourself. Then decide: rekindle, rebalance, or leave.

    incantation“I've stopped caring about this job. Do I want to fix that, or find a job I can care about?”

VIForbidden spells

Tempting. Effective for about a day. Each one has a soul cost = one ember of your soul.

  • The Resentful Coast

    Doing the minimum while hating it, for years. It hollows you out.

  • The Martyr's Return

    Swinging back to total devotion out of guilt. See the Slow Hex.

  • Judging the Quiet Ones

    Assuming everyone who leaves at 17:30 has given up. Many have just grown up.

VIIDifficulty

The same creature at three strengths. Pick the one you're facing.

Normal

The Thriving Quiet.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • You do your job well.
  • You leave on time.
  • You're content.

Do

  1. Keep the Deliberate Enough.
  2. Find a Chosen Extra.
  3. Ignore anyone who calls it quitting.

Don’t: The martyr's return.

If it gets worse: If you feel resentful, you're at Nightmare.

Nightmare

The Wounded Quiet.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • You do the minimum.
  • You feel bitter.
  • You've stopped hoping.

Do

  1. Have the Honest Conversation with yourself.
  2. Ask why you were passed over, or what hurt.
  3. Look for a role you could care about.
  4. Talk to someone.

Don’t: The resentful coast.

If it gets worse: If it's become your whole life, you're at Hell.

Hell

The Hollow Years.

You’ll know it’s this level when

  • Years of resentful minimum.
  • Work feels meaningless.
  • It's affecting the rest of your life.

Do

  1. Consider whether it's burnout or depression; see a doctor.
  2. Decide: rekindle, move, or leave.
  3. Read Life After the Dungeon.
  4. Find meaning outside work.

Don’t: Staying out of inertia.

If it gets worse: You can leave. People do, and are glad.

VIIIWhich quiet are you?

Tick honestly.

  • You leave on time feeling bitter rather than calm.
  • You've stopped caring about work you used to enjoy.
  • You haven't had a conversation about your future here in years.
  • Your life outside work feels thin.
  • You judge colleagues who still care.

The cure, if you ticked two: Ask yourself the Chronicler's question: “Did I choose this, or did I drift into it?” If you drifted, choose now.

IXThe rehearsal

The Honest Conversation, prompted kindly.

Scene: Wednesday 15 October 2030, the Chronicler and Neil.

NeilHow have you done the same thing as me for fifteen years without hating it?

The ChroniclerI don't think we do the same thing.

NeilWe both leave at half five. We both stopped volunteering.

The ChroniclerYou stopped caring. I chose what to care about. They look alike from the corridor.

Neil…What would you care about, if you were me?

The ChroniclerSomething you'd be proud of at half five. Is there anything here like that?

— the AnnotatorNeil found it in Field Ops. The Chronicler sent him a card on his first day: “Enough, chosen.”

XThe trial

What would you do? Tap a question for the answer.

You do your job well and leave on time. Are you quiet quitting?

No. That's quiet thriving. It's a job, done well, with a life.

You've stopped caring entirely. Is that okay?

For a while, maybe. Long term, it hurts you. Have the Honest Conversation.

How do you find meaning without giving work everything?

The Chosen Extra, and a life outside the building.

The Annotator's last wordEnough is not a failure. Enough, chosen and done well, is a kind of freedom.

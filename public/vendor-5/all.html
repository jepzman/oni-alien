Other People's Politics

Reading the room politics from outside, a new sponsor, the budget holder and an executive's slide

  1. 01How Do We Read the Client's Politics from Outside?Read the signals (meetings, budgets, seating, announcements), update the power map, and use it to avoid surprises, never to play.p. 01
  2. 02What If Our Sponsor Moves On?Thank the old sponsor and keep them as an ally; brief the new one in a page within two weeks, learn what they measure, and re-confirm the decisions.p. 02
  3. 03What About the Stakeholder Who Holds the Budget?When an opponent gains budget power, find what they need, give them credit and a shared win, and keep everything in writing.p. 03
  4. 04What If an Executive Wants a Slide?Give senior people the slide they need, built from facts, with credit to the people who did the work, and let them claim the rest.p. 04

Holding your ground saying no, trust, gifts and a rival at the next desk

  1. 05How Do We Say No to a Client?Say no to the request, yes to the need: explain why, offer what you can do, and put both in writing.p. 05
  2. 06Do They Like Us, or Trust Us?Being liked opens doors; being trusted keeps them open. Earn trust with accurate numbers, kept promises and early bad news.p. 06
  3. 07Can We Take the Client to the Football?Know the client's gifts and hospitality rules, keep a register, and say no to anything during a procurement, however friendly.p. 07
  4. 08How Do We Share a Floor with the Rival?Be polite, careful and unremarkable: clean desks, private screens, no corridor talk, no poaching, and a kitchen kettle you share.p. 08

People, theirs and ours frightened staff, your own boss, your team, and a client who shouts

  1. 09What If Client Staff Fear We'll Take Their Jobs?Don't promise what you can't, say what you know, involve them in what comes next, and send job questions to the client.p. 09
  2. 10How Do We Manage Up in Our Own Firm?Give your own leadership the same honesty as the client: a weekly note, the numbers, options, and the decisions only they can make.p. 10
  3. 11How Do We Protect Our Team from the Client?Set boundaries for how your team is treated, route demands through one door, and step in at once when someone crosses the line.p. 11
  4. 12What Do We Do When the Client Shouts?Stay calm, acknowledge the worry, answer with facts, follow up in writing, and talk about the shouting privately, later.p. 12

Integrity under pressure what you overhear, the numbers, and a champion

  1. 13What Do We Do with What We Overhear?Treat what you overhear as confidential: don't repeat it, don't trade on it, and if it matters legally, get advice.p. 13
  2. 14What If the Client Asks Us to Change the Numbers?Refuse to misstate facts, offer an accurate way to say what they want to say, and put your refusal and your offer in writing.p. 14
  3. 15How Do We Build a Champion?Champions are made by evidence they can repeat, credit they can share and promises you keep; never ask them to lobby improperly.p. 15

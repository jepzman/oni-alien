Do They Like Us, or Trust Us?

TL;DR

Being liked and being trusted are different things, and vendors often confuse them. Liked opens doors: people answer your calls. Trusted keeps them open when things go wrong: people believe your numbers. Trust comes from credibility (right numbers), reliability (kept promises), intimacy (they can tell you bad news), and low self-interest. Build trust deliberately; liking usually follows.

1The situation The Grid, October 2028

Customer Operations loves the Wrenfield team. Callum brings cake on Fridays. Lottie knows the exception desk's children's names. Bert has asked Lottie to his retirement party in March.

  • Rupert's office, Lodestar's new home, re-checks every Wrenfield figure. Vexmoor's assurance notes have taught it to.
  • On 13 October Rupert's analyst asks for the source of every number in the weekly status, “for assurance”.
  • Callum is hurt: “Everyone here likes us.” Not everyone who decides phase 2.

2Both sides of the table the same moment, seen twice

MetroVolt Rupert's analyst

Rupert's office has heard two years of Lodestar history through Vexmoor. It doesn't dislike Wrenfield; it doesn't know Wrenfield. Its job is to check, and it can't check “the team is lovely”.

“Liking you isn't my job. Checking you is.”

Wrenfield Ines and Callum

Callum built goodwill on the floor, and it's real and valuable. But phase 2 will be decided by people who weren't there for the cake. Ines needs trust that survives a hostile reading: numbers with sources, promises anyone can check.

“Cake for the floor. Sources for the top.”

Liking is about how you make people feel. Trust is about whether they can rely on what you say when you're not in the room.

3Real-life analogy the friendly mechanic and the honest one

You might like the mechanic who chats and gives you coffee. You trust the one who shows you the worn part, tells you what can wait, and whose estimate is right every time. The best mechanics are both, and when you have to choose, you take your car to the one you trust.

4How it works credibility, reliability, intimacy, low self-interest

Ines uses the trust equation from The Trusted Advisor as a checklist, not a slogan.

  • Credibility: from 20 October every number in the weekly status carries its source (report, query, date). Rupert's analyst can check any of them in a minute.
  • Reliability: a promises checklist, shared with Rupert's office: what Wrenfield said it would do in hypercare, by when, and whether it did. 31 of 31 kept by mid-November.
  • Intimacy: Rupert's analyst gets Ines's direct number and an invitation to ask anything. The first time she reports a data error, Ines thanks her publicly.
  • Low self-interest: the readiness-pack decision (the previous note) is the clearest proof: Wrenfield chose a fair tender over an advantage.

Callum keeps bringing cake. By January, Rupert's analyst has stopped re-checking the weekly figures. She tells Elaine: “Their numbers are the only ones I don't have to.”

  1. Ask who needs to trust you

    Not only who likes you.

  2. Source every number

    Checkable in a minute.

  3. Keep a visible promises list

    Done or not, by date.

  4. Make it easy to bring you bad news

    And thank them when they do.

  5. Show low self-interest

    Choose fairness over advantage, visibly.

  6. Keep the warmth

    Liking still matters.

Vocabulary

Trust equation
Trust = (credibility + reliability + intimacy) ÷ self-orientation.
Credibility
Whether what you say is accurate.
Reliability
Whether you do what you say.
Intimacy
Whether people feel safe telling you difficult things.
Self-orientation
How much you seem to act for yourself rather than the client.
Rapport
A friendly connection; helpful, but not the same as trust.

5What the contract says the contract can require accuracy, not trust

Lodestar SOW · §14.3 and Schedule 7signed 30 June 2027
checkable

14.3 Each Party shall ensure information it provides for the other's governance is accurate.

the minimum. trust is what you build on top.

Sch. 7, 5.4 Figures in the weekly status shall state their source.

added on 20 Oct, at our suggestion.

In plain words: a contract can require accurate information; it can't make anyone trust you. Volunteering clauses that make your work checkable is one of the fastest ways to build trust.

9Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Confusing liked with trusted

    Surprised when the top doubts you. Fix → ask who needs to trust you.

  • Unsourced numbers

    Every figure re-checked. Fix → a source on each.

  • Hidden promises

    Nobody can see you keep them. Fix → a visible list.

  • Defensiveness about errors

    People stop telling you. Fix → thank them publicly.

  • Visible self-interest

    Every number looks like a sales pitch. Fix → choose fairness over advantage.

11Self-check quiz

What's the difference between liked and trusted?

Liked: people want to work with you. Trusted: they rely on what you say.

What is the trust equation?

(Credibility + reliability + intimacy) ÷ self-orientation.

What did Wrenfield add to every weekly figure?

Its source.

What was on the promises checklist?

Each hypercare promise, its date, whether kept, and evidence.

How did the readiness pack help trust?

It showed low self-interest: fairness over advantage.

what would you do?Rupert's analyst finds a wrong figure in the weekly status. What does Ines do?

Thank her publicly, correct it the same day with the cause, and add a check so it can't recur. That turns one error into evidence of reliability.

try it

Score yourself on credibility, reliability, intimacy and self-interest with one stakeholder. Which is weakest? Pick one action that would raise it this month.

remember!!

Liked opens doors; trusted keeps them open. Source every number, keep every promise where people can see.

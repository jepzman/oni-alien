How Do We Read the Client's Politics from Outside?

TL;DR

From the vendor's desk you see the client's politics in small signals: who's invited, who moves floors, what budget papers say, who waits for whom. Collect them as facts, update your power map monthly, and ask only one question: what does this change for our project? Then act on that, by briefing the right people and writing things down. Never repeat what you see, never take sides, never trade on it.

1The situation The Grid, October 2028

Three weeks after wave 1, hypercare is going well: the exception queue is down to 140 accounts. But floor 19 has started to feel different.

  • A new weekly meeting, “Ascend readiness”, chaired by Rupert. Priya isn't on the invitation. Elaine is.
  • Gareth's Network Assets team moves to floor 20, next to Rupert's office.
  • A Horizon budget paper Elaine shares for another reason mentions “customer data services (Network Assets)”.
  • None of it is announced. All of it could matter to phase 2.

2Both sides of the table the same moment, seen twice

MetroVolt MetroVolt

Inside MetroVolt, people know far more than they say. Rupert is preparing the next phase of transformation; Gareth sees an opportunity; Priya is defending her area. Nobody will tell a supplier, and nobody should have to.

“Not your business. Yet.”

Wrenfield Ines

Wrenfield isn't part of MetroVolt's politics, but its next contract depends on them. Ines needs to notice changes early enough to brief the right people and protect what's been agreed, without ever looking like she's playing a game she has no right to play.

“I can't change it. I mustn't be surprised by it.”

Reading politics is about not being surprised. Playing politics is about changing outcomes. A vendor should only ever do the first.

3Real-life analogy the sailor reading the weather

A sailor can't change the weather. She watches the clouds, the wind and the barometer, writes them in the log every few hours, and asks one question: what does this mean for my boat? Then she reefs the sail or changes course. She doesn't argue with the sky, and she doesn't tell other boats what to do.

4How it works collect facts, map monthly, ask what changes, act narrowly

Collect facts, not interpretations. Ines keeps a “signals” page: “6 Oct: Ascend readiness series, chair Rupert; invitees include Elaine, Gareth, Oliver; not Priya.” No adjectives, no guesses.

Update the power map monthly (the one from August 2027). October's changes: Rupert's influence on Lodestar up; Gareth's up; Priya's down; Vexmoor's assurance role close to Rupert.

Ask one question: what does this change for Lodestar? Two things. Phase 2 decisions may move from Priya to Rupert, so Rupert's office needs to hear about wave 1 from Wrenfield directly, not only through Vexmoor. And the meter data could change owner, so every decision about it should be in writing now.

Act only on that. Ines asks Elaine (not Rupert) whether a one-page wave 1 summary for Rupert's office would help; Elaine says yes and sends it herself. She doesn't speculate with anyone about Ascend, and when Callum asks “is Priya on the way out?”, she says: “I don't know, and it isn't ours to guess.”

  1. Keep a signals page

    Facts with dates; no interpretations.

  2. Update the power map monthly

    Influence, stance, needs.

  3. Ask what it changes for you

    Usually one or two things.

  4. Act narrowly

    Brief the right person, put things in writing.

  5. Go through your counterpart

    Not around them.

  6. Never speculate aloud

    Not even inside your own team.

Vocabulary

Political awareness
Understanding how influence works in an organisation, without exploiting it.
Signal
A small observable fact that may indicate a change.
Power map
A drawing of influence, stance and needs around a decision.
Neutrality
Not taking sides in the client's internal disputes.
Proxy briefing
Briefing a senior person through your counterpart.
Speculation
Guessing aloud about others' motives or futures; harmful when repeated.

5What the contract says who the contract still says decides

Lodestar SOW · §8.1 and §8.5signed 30 June 2027
unchanged (for now)

8.1 MetroVolt's Sponsor is the Head of Customer Operations.

a reorg can change the person before it changes the clause.

8.5 Instructions to the Supplier are valid only if given by MetroVolt's Programme Director or the Steering Committee.

whoever gains influence, instructions still come through Elaine. say so, politely, if needed.

In plain words: politics changes who has influence faster than contracts change who has authority. Keep taking instructions from the people the contract names until the contract changes.

9Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Ignoring the signals

    Surprised by a new decider. Fix → a signals page, monthly map.

  • Speculating aloud

    Rumours traced back to you. Fix → facts only, never repeated.

  • Taking sides

    The losing side remembers. Fix → strict neutrality.

  • Going around your counterpart

    Trust broken. Fix → brief through them.

  • Over-reacting

    Changing plans on rumours. Fix → act only on what changes for you.

10When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Any long engagement in a large organisation.
  • A reorganisation or transformation is underway.
  • The next contract depends on who decides.

Skip it when…

  • A small client where the owner decides everything.

try it

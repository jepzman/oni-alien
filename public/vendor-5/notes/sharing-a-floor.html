How Do We Share a Floor with the Rival?

TL;DR

Sharing a client's floor with a rival is normal in big organisations, and it needs discipline, not hostility. Clean desks and whiteboards, private screens, calls in booked rooms, nothing confidential on the shared printer, and bid work off site. Never poach, fish or gossip. And be warm and ordinary: share the kettle. The rival's people are colleagues today and possibly yours tomorrow.

1The situation The Grid, floor 19, December 2028

On Monday 11 December, Vexmoor's Ascend team, twelve people, moves into the desks next to Wrenfield's on floor 19. Same kitchen, same printer, same meeting rooms.

  • Wrenfield's whiteboard shows the wave 2 estimates from the readiness sprint.
  • Phase 2's tender opens in March. Both firms will bid.
  • Lottie, chatting at the kettle on day one, mentions Wrenfield's new test tool. Bea writes it down.

2Both sides of the table the same moment, seen twice

MetroVolt MetroVolt facilities

MetroVolt has more suppliers than desks on floor 19. Putting two of them side by side is a seating decision, not a strategy. MetroVolt expects both to behave professionally.

“You're both suppliers. Please share nicely.”

Wrenfield Ines and the team

For Wrenfield, a competitor at the next desk means every whiteboard, screen and kettle chat is visible. Hostility would look petty and could be reported to Rupert. Carelessness could hand Vexmoor the phase 2 bid. The answer is boring, consistent discipline.

“Friendly at the kettle, careful at the desk.”

Most information leaks to competitors aren't espionage. They're whiteboards, printers and kitchens.

3Real-life analogy the shared office kitchen in an open-plan building

Two law firms share a floor in a serviced office. Neither hates the other. Both keep files locked, discuss cases in their own rooms, never leave papers on the shared printer, and are perfectly pleasant in the kitchen. Nobody thinks the discipline is unfriendly. It's simply professional.

4How it works floor rules: clean, private, off-site, never, always

On the first evening Ines writes floor rules on one page, discusses them with the team the next morning, and pins them inside a cupboard (not on the wall):

  • Clean: desks and whiteboards wiped every evening. The wave 2 estimates go back to Leeds.
  • Screens: privacy filters on every laptop, from Wednesday.
  • Calls: anything about the bid, only from a booked room or from Leeds.
  • Printing: nothing about the bid on floor 19's printer.
  • Never: poach Vexmoor's people, fish for their plans, or gossip about them.
  • Always: share the kettle, say good morning, help if they ask where things are.

Lottie is mortified about the test tool. Ines tells her it was a good lesson cheaply learned: the tool is public anyway. In January, Bea asks Lottie where the good coffee is. Lottie tells her. That's the right answer too.

  1. Write simple floor rules

    One page, discussed with the team.

  2. Clean every evening

    Desks, whiteboards, printer trays.

  3. Move sensitive work off site

    Bids in your own office.

  4. Use privacy filters and booked rooms

    For screens and calls.

  5. Ban poaching, fishing and gossip

    In both directions.

  6. Stay warm

    Professional, not hostile.

5What the contract says what protects both firms

Lodestar SOW · §16 (confidentiality) and §17 (non-solicitation)signed 30 June 2027
both ways

16.2 The Supplier shall protect MetroVolt's Confidential Information, including information about other suppliers obtained in the course of the Services.

what we see of Vexmoor's work is MetroVolt's confidential information. we don't use it.

17.1 Neither Party shall solicit the other's staff involved in the Services for 12 months.

and we don't poach from Vexmoor either. as a rule, not a clause.

In plain words: your contract usually treats what you learn about other suppliers as the client's confidential information. Using it would breach the contract as well as good manners.

9Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Whiteboards left up

    Estimates visible to the rival. Fix → wipe every evening.

  • Bid calls in the kitchen

    Overheard. Fix → booked rooms or off site.

  • Hostility

    Looks petty to the client. Fix → share the kettle.

  • Fishing

    Breaches confidentiality and trust. Fix → never ask about their bid.

  • Blaming juniors for slips

    They hide the next one. Fix → teach kindly.

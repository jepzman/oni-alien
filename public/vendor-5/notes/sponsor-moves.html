What If Our Sponsor Moves On?

TL;DR

When your sponsor moves on, the project's support can quietly go with them. Thank the old sponsor in person and keep them as an ally. Within a week, give the new one a one-page “what you've inherited” in their language. Within two, learn what they measure and fear. Then re-confirm in writing the decisions that matter. A new sponsor isn't obliged to keep their predecessor's promises unless they've made them their own.

1The situation The Grid, 7 November 2028

On Tuesday 7 November, an all-staff email: as part of Rupert Thorne's “Ascend” programme, Lodestar moves under the transformation office, with Rupert as sponsor. Priya Nair will “focus on customer service excellence”.

  • Priya sponsored Lodestar for eighteen months: through Horizon, CR-007, the ambush, the go / no-go.
  • Rupert has heard about Lodestar mostly from Vexmoor, his advisers.
  • Phase 2's tender is planned for March. The person who'll approve it has never had a meeting with Wrenfield.

2Both sides of the table the same moment, seen twice

MetroVolt Rupert

Rupert inherits a programme he didn't choose, run by a supplier he didn't pick, with a history he knows second-hand. He'll judge it by Ascend's measures: cost out, targets met, no surprises at the board. He has no obligation to honour understandings he wasn't part of.

“Show me why this is a good use of Ascend's money.”

Wrenfield Ines

For Wrenfield, a new sponsor is a fresh start or a quiet end. Ines needs Rupert to see wave 1 with his own eyes, in his own measures, before Vexmoor's version sets. And she owes Priya a proper thank-you; she's still a director, and still a friend of the programme.

“Earn the new sponsor without forgetting the old one.”

A sponsor change resets trust to zero. The only thing that carries over automatically is what was written down.

3Real-life analogy the new head teacher

A school gets a new head teacher. The good teachers don't complain that the old head promised them a new lab. They thank the old head, give the new one a short note on what each class is doing and why, find out what the new head cares about (exam results, say), and ask her to confirm the lab, in writing, in her own words.

4How it works thank, brief in their language, learn, re-confirm

Day one: thank Priya. Ines asks for fifteen minutes. She thanks Priya for specific things (the telling order in December, the go / no-go decision) and asks if she'd be willing to stay in touch. Priya: “Of course. And tell Rupert about the 31 %. He thinks it was your mistake.”

Week one: one page, his language. “What you've inherited” for Rupert, through Elaine: cost (£5.7m of £5.7m committed, milestones paid to M4), what it saves (wave 1 retired 16 % of MAVIS's batch load), what's left (warranty to January, phase 2 tender in March), risks, and the 31 % story told in two lines, with the contract clause that priced it.

Week two: what does he measure? At their first meeting, Ines asks more than she tells: “What would make Lodestar a success for Ascend? What would worry you?” Answer: cost out, a credible phase 2 price, no surprises at the board.

Then re-confirm in writing. Elaine puts three items on the next steering for Rupert to confirm as his own: baseline v2.0 to the end of warranty, the change-pool rules, and the phase 2 timetable. All three confirmed (DL-071).

  1. Thank the old sponsor, specifically

    And ask to stay in touch.

  2. Write a one-page handover

    In the new sponsor's measures.

  3. Send it through your counterpart

    Not directly.

  4. Ask before you tell

    What does success look like for them?

  5. Re-confirm key decisions

    As the new sponsor's own, in writing.

  6. Correct second-hand stories gently

    With facts, once.

Vocabulary

Sponsor change

Promises made by a predecessor; not binding on the successor unless re-made.
Re-confirmation
Asking a new decider to adopt earlier decisions explicitly.
Second-hand story
What someone has heard about you from others.

5What the contract says what changes when the sponsor changes

Lodestar SOW · §8.1 (as amended 14 Nov 2028)variation V-04
sponsor changed

8.1 MetroVolt's Sponsor is the Head of Customer Operations. MetroVolt's Sponsor is the Director of Transformation.

a variation, signed by both sides. the clause caught up with the email.

DL-071 The Sponsor confirms baseline v2.0 to the end of warranty, the change-pool rules in clause 7.4, and the phase 2 timetable.

Rupert's decisions now. not Priya's.

In plain words: a sponsor change should be a formal variation to the contract, and the new sponsor should re-confirm the key decisions. Otherwise you're relying on a predecessor's word.

9Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Assuming continuity

    The new sponsor doesn't feel bound. Fix → re-confirm in writing.

  • Forgetting the old sponsor

    You lose an ally and look ungrateful. Fix → thank them specifically.

  • Your language, not theirs

    The handover isn't read. Fix → their measures.

  • Going around your counterpart

    Elaine looks bypassed. Fix → send it through her.

  • Arguing with second-hand stories

    You sound defensive. Fix → facts, once, briefly.

11Self-check quiz

What does Ines do on day one?

Thanks Priya in person, specifically, and asks to stay in touch.

What's in the one-page handover?

Cost, savings, results, the 31 % story, what's next, risks, and what to confirm.

Why ask before telling at the first meeting?

To learn what the new sponsor measures and fears.

What did Rupert re-confirm?

Baseline v2.0 to the end of warranty, the change-pool rules and the phase 2 timetable.

Why make the sponsor change a contract variation?

So the contract names the person who now decides.

what would you do?Rupert says in the first meeting: “Vexmoor tells me your 31 % was a bidding mistake.” What do you say?

Calmly, once: the bid assumed 5 % in writing (A1-07), MetroVolt's own sample showed 7.8 %, the full data showed 31 %, and the contract priced the difference. Offer the documents. Then ask what he'd like Lodestar to achieve.

try it

Imagine your sponsor leaves tomorrow. Write the one page you'd give their successor, in the successor's likely measures. Which three decisions would you ask them to re-confirm?

remember!!

A new sponsor starts at zero. Thank the old one, brief the new one in their language, and re-confirm in writing.

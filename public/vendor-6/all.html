The Cost and the Ending

The cost a tired team, your own nights, pace and rotation

  1. 01How Do We Notice Stress on a Vendor Team?Look for the quiet signs (hours, tone, silence, weekends), ask directly in private, and change the work, not just the mood.p. 01
  2. 02What About My Own 23:00 Notes?The leader's stress spreads. Notice your own signs, say them to someone, and set limits you'll actually keep.p. 02
  3. 03Can a Fixed-Price Team Keep a Sustainable Pace?Measure hours worked against hours booked, cap overtime, plan recovery after peaks, and make pace part of the plan, not a favour.p. 03
  4. 04How Do We Rotate Someone Off an Account?Rotate people before they break: agree it with them and the client, hand over properly, and keep the door open for their return.p. 04

Handing it over warranty, knowledge, lessons and the architecture they'll keep

  1. 05What Do We Owe After Go-Live?Know exactly what the warranty covers, triage every report against it, fix fast, and close the warranty in writing.p. 05
  2. 06How Do We Hand It Over So It Sticks?Transfer knowledge by doing, not presenting: shadow, reverse-shadow, runbooks, and a sign-off by the people who'll be called at night.p. 06
  3. 07How Do We Run a Lessons Session Both Sides Attend?A blameless session with client, vendors and the people who did the work, ending in a few lessons with owners, not a list nobody reads.p. 07
  4. 08What Should the Handover Architecture Say?Hand over the why as well as the what: a one-page picture, the decisions and their reasons, the known debts, and how to change it safely.p. 08

The next deal the phase 2 bid, the wait, the story and when to walk away

  1. 09How Do We Bid Against the Rival for the Next Phase?Bid from evidence: real velocity, real data, real lessons and a named team, and price the next phase on what you now know.p. 09
  2. 10How Do We Wait for the Decision?Prepare for both outcomes: a plan for winning, a plan for handing over gracefully, and a team that knows both.p. 10
  3. 11Can We Tell Lodestar's Story?Tell the story with the client's permission, the hard parts included, and nothing published during a procurement.p. 11
  4. 12When Should We Walk Away?Set walk-away conditions before you need them, test the deal against them calmly, and leave politely if it fails.p. 12

Endings leaving well, what it taught, and the last day

  1. 13How Do We Leave Well?Thank people by name, finish what you promised, leave the documents tidy and the relationships intact.p. 13
  2. 14What Did the Vendor Side Teach Me?Write down what you learned while it's fresh: the rules you'd give your younger self.p. 14
  3. 15The Last Day on Floor 19The last day: hand back the badge, say goodbye properly, and walk into whatever room you're asked to.p. 15

The Last Day on Floor 19

TL;DR

The last day is short and matters more than it should. Return what you borrowed, say goodbye properly, desk by desk, leave something useful behind, and hand back the badge yourself. You won't control what happens next. Walk into whatever room you're asked to, calmly, with your notebook, ready for either answer.

  • Family: endings
  • Lives in: room 19C (empty) · visitor pass V-0417 · room 27A
  • Pairs with: leaving well, waiting for the decision

1The situation Floor 19, Friday 15 June 2029

It is Friday 15 June 2029, Ines's last day on site. The phase 2 decision is due today.

  • By 16:00 the tender portal shows nothing. Callum has refreshed it eleven times from Leeds.
  • Room 19C, the goldfish bowl, is empty except for one box, a kettle and a pot plant.
  • Her visitor pass, V-0417, has been renewed 26 times. It goes back to reception tonight.
  • Nobody on floor 19 knows the decision. Or, if they do, nobody's saying.

2Both sides of the table the same moment, seen twice

MetroVolt MetroVolt

Floor 19 is half empty for the summer and full of Ascend rumours. Kemi's team is running Lodestar on its own. Elaine has been in meetings on floor 27 since lunch. Rupert's door has been closed all day.

“(Nobody says anything.)”

Wrenfield Ines

Ines has done everything the notes in this book say to do: the promises closed, the paper tidy, the letters sent, both plans written, the walk-away letter signed. What happens next isn't hers to decide. She notices she's calm, and is surprised by it.

“Whatever it is, we did it properly.”

At the end of a long engagement, the work you control is finished. What's left is how you walk out, and into what.

3Real-life analogy the last day of term

On the last day of term, the classroom walls are bare and the desks are empty. Teachers hand back the last books, say goodbye to each child, and give the room one more look. Results come later, in an envelope. Nobody can change them now. The last day is for leaving well, and for being ready to open the envelope, whatever's in it.

4How it works return, goodbye, leave something, the badge, the room

Return what you borrowed. The kettle goes back to Yusuf, who lent it on day 2 and pretends to have forgotten. Bert's book, bound, goes back to Bert, who turns the pages slowly and says, “Somebody will finally read it.” The pot plant goes to Rosa.

Say goodbye properly. Desk by desk, not an email. Kemi, Marta and Asif. Rosa. Kofi on floor 20. Hollis by the lifts, who shakes her hand and says “the template's working”. Lottie walks with her to the stairs and says she'll decide on Monday.

Leave something useful. A card on the door of 19C: the handbook link, the runbooks, the run team's phone number, and “ask Kemi”.

The badge. At 17:00 Ines is at the lift with her box and pass V-0417 in her hand, on the way to reception.

At 17:05 her phone goes. Elaine.

“Ines. Before you go. Have you got a minute? A quick word, in room 27A.”

Elaine doesn't say what about. Her voice gives nothing away. Room 27A is on floor 27, where Rupert's office is, and where Desmond has been all afternoon.

Ines puts the box down by the lift, keeps the pass, picks up the notebook, and presses 27.

  1. Return what you borrowed

    Personally.

  2. Say goodbye desk by desk

    Not by email.

  3. Leave something useful

    A link, a name, a number.

  4. Hand back the badge yourself

    When you're ready.

  5. Go to the room you're asked to

    Calmly.

  6. Take the notebook

    Either way, you'll want to write it down.

The formal message telling bidders the procurement result.
Debrief
A meeting explaining a decision, often to an unsuccessful bidder.
Standstill
The pause after an award, before the contract is signed.
Open ending
A story that stops before its final question is answered.

5What the contract says the last clause that matters today

Phase 2 invitation to tender · §8 (award)issued 5 Mar 2029
pending

8.1 MetroVolt intends to notify all bidders of its decision by 15 June 2029.

“intends”. it's 17:05.

8.3 Unsuccessful bidders will be offered a debrief.

a debrief, or something else. Elaine didn't say.

In plain words: award notices come in writing, through the portal, to every bidder at once. A phone call first means something. It doesn't say what.

9Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Slipping away early

    Nobody gets to say goodbye. Fix → desk by desk.

  • Keeping what you borrowed

    A small thing, remembered. Fix → return it, personally.

  • Leaving nothing behind

    The next question has nowhere to go. Fix → a card on the door.

  • Guessing what the call means

    It changes nothing. Fix → go and find out.

  • Going in without the notebook

    You'll want to write it down. Fix → take it.

How Do We Bid Against the Rival for the Next Phase?

TL;DR

The incumbent's advantage in a follow-on bid isn't the relationship: it's evidence. Bid from real velocity, real data, real lessons and a named team, and price the next phase on what you now know, not on what would beat the rival. If the rival is cheaper because it knows less, say clearly what you know, and let the client decide.

1The situation Leeds, 4 April 2029

MetroVolt's phase 2 tender was issued on 5 March: waves 2 to 4, the remaining 1.9 million customers, a budget of about £9m. Bids are due Wednesday 4 April.

  • Two bidders: Wrenfield, wave 1's supplier, and Vexmoor, already advising Rupert on Project Horizon.
  • Everyone expects Vexmoor to be cheaper.
  • Vivian's suggestion: “we promise all three waves in eighteen months, and sort it out later.” Two years ago she promised twelve months over dinner.

2Both sides of the table the same moment, seen twice

MetroVolt Desmond and Elaine

Desmond's evaluation is 60 % quality, 40 % price. Elaine knows what wave 1 really took and will be on the panel; Rupert's office will be too, and it likes Vexmoor's ambition. Lesson 1 from the joint session is in the tender: bidders get the full data profile.

“Show us the plan you'd actually deliver.”

Wrenfield Ines and Callum

Wrenfield knows the work better than anyone: velocity, data, the queue on cutover night, Halvorsen's quirks. It also knows that the bid it could win cheaply is one it would lose money and people delivering. Graham needs phase 2; the pace policy has added 6 %.

“We're the only bidder who's done this. Bid like it.”

A follow-on bid is the incumbent's chance to replace promises with evidence. Throwing that away to match a rival's price wastes the only real advantage.

3Real-life analogy the second ascent

A climber who has been up a mountain once doesn't plan her second ascent from the brochure. She knows where the ice is, how long the ridge really takes, and which camp floods. Another guide might promise clients the summit in two days. She says four, and here's why: the ridge, the ice, the camp. Clients who've heard both pitches know which guide has been there.

4How it works evidence, a named team, an honest price

Real velocity. The plan uses wave 1's 38 points a sprint, not a hoped-for 45, at 85 % of capacity.

Real data. Bilal profiles all 1.9 million remaining accounts from the tender's data pack (lesson 1): business accounts and prepay meters are messier than wave 1's. The bid shows the numbers.

Real lessons. The five joint lessons are in the plan: interface tests on Halvorsen's test system from week 2; the performance environment booked from day 1; queue capacity planned for each cutover night.

A named team. Ines, Callum, Tariq, Bilal, Oskar, Lottie, plus four engineers. For Hana, the bid says honestly: “Hana Sato, wave 1 tech lead, may rejoin for wave 3; this is her decision.”

An honest price. £9.4m over 28 months, wave 2 live in June 2030, margin 15 % (the floor), including the pace policy. Vivian's eighteen months is not in it. The bid's executive summary has one sentence Ines insisted on: “This is the plan we would deliver, not the plan we think you'd like to hear.”

Submitted through the portal at 11:40, twenty minutes before the deadline.

  1. Use your real velocity

    And plan below full capacity.

  2. Profile the real data

    All of it, if you can.

  3. Build in the lessons

    Visibly, in the plan.

  4. Name the team

    Honestly, including who's uncertain.

  5. Price on what you know

    At or above your floor.

  6. Say why you're not the cheapest

    If you aren't.

Vocabulary

Follow-on bid
A bid for the next phase of work you've delivered.
Incumbent
The supplier currently doing the work.
Evidence-based estimate
An estimate from measured past performance.
Quality / price split
How the evaluation weights each (here 60 / 40).
Margin floor
The lowest margin the firm will accept.
Winner's curse
Winning a bid by underpricing it, then losing money.

5What the contract says what the tender scores

Phase 2 invitation to tender · §6 (evaluation)issued 5 Mar 2029
submitted

6.1 Bids are evaluated 60 % quality and 40 % price. Quality includes the credibility of the plan and the evidence for it.

credibility is 60 %. we have the evidence; use it.

6.4 MetroVolt may reject a bid it considers abnormally low.

a reminder that cheaper isn't automatically safer, for them either.

In plain words: read how a tender scores quality. Evidence for the plan often counts more than the price difference, especially for an incumbent with real numbers.

9Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Bidding the brochure

    The winner's curse. Fix → the plan you'd deliver.

  • Relying on the relationship

    Procurement scores evidence. Fix → show the numbers.

  • Dropping below the floor

    You win and lose money. Fix → 15 %.

  • Overstating the team

    Key-personnel promises you can't keep. Fix → say who's uncertain.

  • Attacking the rival

    It looks weak. Fix → say what you know.

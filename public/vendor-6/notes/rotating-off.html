How Do We Rotate Someone Off an Account?

TL;DR

Long accounts wear people out, and the most important people wear out first. Rotate them before they break: agree it with them first, with the client (especially if they're named in the contract), plan a real handover with overlap, tell the story honestly (“to rest and grow”, not “leaving”), and keep the door open for their return. A planned rotation is a sign of a healthy supplier, not a weak one.

1The situation The Grid, 5 February 2029

After two weeks' leave and a week of reduced hours, Hana rotates off Lodestar on Monday 5 February to a smaller client in Leeds.

  • She's named key personnel in the contract (§6.1).
  • She holds most of the knowledge of the back-feed and the MetroBus adapters.
  • Vivian worries: “MetroVolt will think we're losing our best people before phase 2.”

2Both sides of the table the same moment, seen twice

MetroVolt Elaine and Hollis

MetroVolt's worry is continuity: who knows the back-feed now? Elaine also cares about Hana, and is relieved to see a supplier rotate someone before a crisis rather than after. What would worry her is a silent disappearance.

“Who knows it now, and is she all right?”

Wrenfield Ines

Ines has to protect three things: Hana's recovery, Lodestar's knowledge and MetroVolt's confidence before phase 2. The rotation has to be visible, planned and honest, with the knowledge moved, not just the person.

“Move the person, keep the knowledge, tell the truth.”

Clients worry about rotations they hear about late. A rotation explained early, with a handover they can see, increases confidence.

3Real-life analogy the relay runner's handover

In a relay, the incoming runner doesn't stop and hand the baton to someone standing still. Both run together through the exchange zone, matching pace, until the new runner has it firmly. Then the first runner slows down and steps off the track, and nobody watching thinks she's quitting.

4How it works with the person, with the client, a handover, honesty, an open door

With Hana, first. The rotation was her choice in the 10 January conversation; she chose the Leeds client and the timing.

With the client. Elaine consented under §6.3 on 10 January. Ines briefs Hollis and Rupert's office in person, before the team hears it from anyone else.

A real handover. Tariq Rahman, a senior Wrenfield engineer from Leeds, overlaps for a week (29 January to 2 February). Tariq shadows Hana for two days; Hana shadows Tariq for three. Hana writes 14 handover pages in the style of Bert's book: the back-feed, the MetroBus adapters, the queue limit from wave 1 night, and “things I'd do differently”. Bilal becomes Tariq's backup.

An honest story. To MetroVolt and the team: “Hana is rotating to a smaller client to rest and to lead something of her own. Tariq is taking over, with a week's overlap, and Bilal behind him.” No euphemisms, no medical details.

An open door. Hana's role in phase 2 is her decision, later. The phase 2 bid will say so honestly (next notes). Elaine sends Hana a card. Bert sends her his last copybook printout, framed.

  1. Let the person lead the decision

    Timing, destination.

  2. Get the client's consent early

    Especially for key personnel.

  3. Overlap, both directions

    Shadow, then reverse-shadow.

  4. Write it down

    Pages in the team's handover style.

  5. Tell an honest story, early

    Client first, then the team.

  6. Keep the door open

    Their return is their choice.

Vocabulary

Rotation
Moving someone off an account on purpose, with a plan.
Key personnel
People named in the contract who can't be swapped without consent.
Shadowing
The new person watches the old one work.
Reverse shadowing
The old person watches the new one work.
Handover pages
Written knowledge for the successor.
Continuity
Keeping the work running across people changes.

5What the contract says key personnel, changed properly

Lodestar SOW · §6.3–6.4 · variation V-055 Feb 2029
replaced

6.3 The Supplier shall not remove or replace Key Personnel without MetroVolt's prior written consent, not to be unreasonably withheld.

consent given before anyone else knew.

V-05 Schedule 6 is amended: Technical Lead, Tariq Rahman, from 5 February 2029.

Hana's name leaves the schedule. Not the story.

In plain words: replacing a named key person is a contract change. Ask early, give the client a credible successor and a handover they can see, and record it as a variation.

9Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Rotating too late

    After the breakdown. Fix → before they break.

  • Deciding for them

    It feels like punishment. Fix → they lead.

  • No overlap

    Knowledge leaves with the person. Fix → shadow both ways.

  • Euphemisms

    Rumours fill the gap. Fix → an honest story, without private details.

  • Closing the door

    You lose them for good. Fix → their return is their choice.

10When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Long engagements.
  • Key people showing signs of strain.
  • Before a new phase, if someone needs a change.

Skip it when…

  • Short engagements where nobody's overloaded.

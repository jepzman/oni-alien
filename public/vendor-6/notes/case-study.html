Can We Tell Lodestar's Story?

TL;DR

Your best project is your best marketing, but it's the client's story too. Ask permission (the contract almost certainly requires it), tell it honestly, hard parts included, write it with the client, and publish nothing during a live procurement. A case study with its problems in it is more convincing than one that says “flawless”.

  • Family: the next deal
  • Lives in: marketing/lodestar-case-study-v3.md · approved, held
  • Pairs with: references (vol. 1), the joint lessons

1The situation Leeds, 16 May 2029

Graham wants a case study on Wrenfield's website, with MetroVolt's logo and a headline: “380,000 customers migrated flawlessly.” He wants it now, to help win two other utility bids.

  • The phase 2 procurement is live: publishing anything about MetroVolt now could look like lobbying.
  • The SOW's §18 forbids publicity without MetroVolt's written consent.
  • And it wasn't flawless: 379,997 customers, a 22-minute queue, 1,206 exceptions, three accounts excluded.

2Both sides of the table the same moment, seen twice

MetroVolt Elaine and Anjali

Elaine is proud of wave 1 and happy to be a reference. She doesn't want MetroVolt's logo on a marketing claim during a procurement she's evaluating, and Anjali needs every word checked. After Orion, MetroVolt is wary of any story that sounds too good.

“Yes, later, and true.”

Wrenfield Graham and Ines

Graham needs the story to win other work; Wrenfield's pipeline matters to everyone on the team. Ines agrees it's Wrenfield's best project, and thinks the hard parts are what make it worth reading.

“Tell it right, or don't tell it yet.”

Clients read case studies too. The ones they trust admit something went wrong.

3Real-life analogy the wedding speech

A good best-man speech is true, a little embarrassing, and approved by nobody's mother-in-law in advance, which is why best men check the dodgier stories with the couple first. The speech that says “a perfect couple who never argue” is forgotten by dessert. The one with the story about the tent in the rain is remembered for years, and the couple are glad it was told.

4How it works permission, honesty, written together, held

Ask permission. Ines asks Elaine, who brings in Anjali. Agreed in principle, under §18, with one condition: nothing published until after the phase 2 award, whichever way it goes. Graham isn't happy, then agrees.

Tell it honestly. The draft includes the hard parts: the 7.8 % data-quality surprise in discovery and CR-007; the first red status; M3 rejected, then accepted ten days later; the 22 minutes at 04:10 on wave 1 night; the three excluded accounts. And what fixed each.

Write it with the client. Elaine edits two paragraphs and adds her own quote: “They told us bad news early, every time. That's why we got there.” Kemi adds one about the handover. Anjali changes six words.

The headline. Not “flawlessly”. “Lodestar wave 1: 379,997 customers, one long night, and what we learned.”

Hold it. Version 3, approved in writing on Wednesday 6 June, sits in a folder marked “after the award”. Elaine also agrees to take reference calls, after the award. For the two other bids, Graham uses an anonymised summary that Anjali has also approved.

  1. Check the contract

    Publicity clauses, confidentiality.

  2. Ask the client

    And their legal team.

  3. Tell the hard parts

    And what fixed them.

  4. Write it together

    Their edits, their quotes.

  5. Wait if a procurement is live

    Approve now, publish later.

  6. Use an anonymised version meanwhile

    Also approved.

5What the contract says who owns the story

Lodestar SOW · §18 (publicity) · ITT §4signed 30 June 2027
held

18.1 Neither Party shall make any public announcement concerning this Agreement without the prior written consent of the other.

written consent: Elaine and Anjali, 6 June.

ITT 4.3 Bidders shall not seek to influence the evaluation by publicity or by contact with MetroVolt staff.

so: approved now, published after the award.

In plain words: publicity clauses are common and strict. Ask early, agree the words in writing, and never publish anything about the client during a procurement you're in.

9Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Publishing without consent

    A contract breach. Fix → ask, in writing.

  • “Flawlessly”

    Nobody believes it; the client minds. Fix → the hard parts.

  • Publishing during a procurement

    Looks like lobbying. Fix → hold it.

  • Writing it alone

    The client's corrections arrive in public. Fix → together.

  • No anonymised version

    Other bids go without. Fix → approve one meanwhile.

How Do We Leave Well?

TL;DR

However the next decision goes, this engagement is ending, and endings are remembered. Finish what you promised, leave the documents tidy, thank people by name and in writing (copied to their managers), say nothing bad about anyone, and support your people's choices, even when they choose the client. Leave the floor as if you'll be back.

  • Family: endings
  • Lives in: the promises register · 31 letters · room 19C, empty

1The situation Floor 19, 11 June 2029

Wave 1's contract ends on 30 June. The phase 2 decision is due on Friday. Whatever it is, this part of the engagement is over.

  • The promises register (47 lines since the orals of May 2027) still has 3 open.
  • Room 19C, the goldfish bowl, holds two years of paper.
  • Bert retires in the autumn.
  • And this morning Lottie tells Ines, nervously, that MetroVolt has offered her a job on Kemi's run team.

2Both sides of the table the same moment, seen twice

MetroVolt MetroVolt

MetroVolt will remember the last month more clearly than the middle year. A supplier that leaves tidily, keeps its promises to the end and thanks people properly is one it will want back, or recommend. One that disappears after the last invoice is remembered too.

“How they leave is how we'll describe them.”

Wrenfield Ines

Ines is tired, proud and uncertain. The team is half packed. Lottie's news stings for an hour, then it doesn't: Lottie applied to a public advert, she's good, and she'd be good at MetroVolt. Wrenfield might lose phase 2 and Lottie in the same week.

“Finish it properly. Whatever happens on Friday.”

The end of an engagement is the part both sides remember. Make it the tidiest part.

3Real-life analogy leaving a holiday cottage

Good guests leave a holiday cottage cleaner than they found it: beds stripped, bins emptied, a note in the visitors' book thanking the owner by name and mentioning the thing they loved. They didn't have to. It's why the owner gives them the good week next year, and tells friends about them.

4How it works promises, paper, thanks, relationships, people

Finish what you promised. The last three open lines: P-31 (a training session for Customer Ops team leaders, run Tuesday 12 June), P-40 (the data dictionary, delivered to Bilal's MetroVolt counterpart), P-44 (Bert's book reprinted and bound, three copies). 47 of 47 closed by Thursday 14 June.

Leave the documents tidy. Room 19C is cleared: two years of paper either archived to MetroVolt's records team or shredded under §16 (confidentiality). The handbook and runbooks are checked for the last time. The whiteboard wall is photographed and wiped.

Thank people by name, in writing. Ines writes 31 letters: Elaine, Hollis, Desmond, Anjali, Nkem, Kofi, Rosa, the run team, Yusuf from the service desk, Bert. Each names one specific thing they did. Each is copied to their manager. Gareth gets one too, for IA-02.

Keep the relationships. No bad words about anyone, on the floor or in the pub, Vexmoor included. Ines offers Oliver's team a walkthrough of the handbook, whoever wins.

Support your people's choices. Lottie applied to a public advert, so §17's non-solicitation doesn't apply. Ines tells her: “You'd be brilliant there. Take a week, decide for yourself, and whatever you choose, I'll write you the reference.” Lottie hasn't decided.

5What the contract says what the contract says about leaving

Lodestar SOW · §16.4 and §17.2signed 30 June 2027
left tidy

16.4 On expiry, the Supplier shall return or securely destroy MetroVolt's Confidential Information.

11 sacks. the shredding van came at 07:00.

17.2 Clause 17.1 does not prevent either Party employing a person who responds to a general advertisement.

Lottie answered an advert. her choice, not a breach.

In plain words: exit clauses cover the paper; non-solicitation clauses usually allow staff to answer public adverts. Neither covers the thank-you letters, which matter most.

9Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Disappearing after the invoice

    Remembered. Fix → finish properly.

  • Open promises

    Someone was relying on one. Fix → close or explain.

  • Generic thanks

    Forgotten by Friday. Fix → by name, one thing, cc their manager.

  • Bitterness about a rival

    It reaches the client. Fix → say nothing bad.

  • Resenting a team member's choice

    You lose them twice. Fix → support it.

How Do We Wait for the Decision?

TL;DR

Between the bid and the decision, nothing you do changes the result, and everything you do shows who you are. Write two plans: one for winning, one for losing well (a proper handover, warm cooperation, next roles for the team). Tell the team both. Don't chase procurement or read the lift. And keep running today's work as if nothing were being decided.

1The situation Floor 19, 30 April 2029

The orals were on Thursday 26 April: an hour each, Wrenfield at 10:00, Vexmoor at 14:00. The decision is promised by 15 June.

  • Procurement is silent, as it must be.
  • Rumours in the lift: “Rupert's office loved Vexmoor's demo.” “Elaine scored us top on quality.” Nobody knows.
  • Lottie asks at the stand-up: “What happens to us if we lose?”
  • Halvorsen's billing re-tender, which wave 1's interface depends on, is also being decided.

2Both sides of the table the same moment, seen twice

MetroVolt Desmond

Desmond's job is a fair, defensible decision. Any contact from a bidder about the evaluation (a call, a lunch, a hint) is a risk he'll record. MetroVolt also still needs wave 1 run well, whoever wins phase 2.

“Don't call me. I'll call you.”

Wrenfield Ines

The team is anxious and so is Ines. Graham checks the portal daily. The temptation is to read signals into everything, and to let wave 1 slide while everyone waits. Losing is a real possibility, and the team deserves to know what it would mean.

“We can't change the decision. We can be ready for it.”

How a supplier behaves while waiting, and after losing, is remembered longer than the bid.

3Real-life analogy the expectant parents' bag

Expectant parents pack a hospital bag weeks before the due date, and plan for both a quick birth and a long one. Then they carry on with ordinary life: go to work, cook dinner. Checking the bag every hour doesn't change when the baby comes. Having it packed means that whatever happens, at 03:00, nobody panics.

4How it works two plans, both told, no chasing, today's work

The win plan. Mobilise wave 2 from Monday 2 July: the named team, four new engineers from Leeds, design authority dates booked, Hana asked (not told) about wave 3.

The lose plan. Schedule 10's three months of exit assistance: a full handover to Vexmoor using the handbook, Wrenfield's engineers available to answer questions, the run team supported. Warm cooperation: Ines drafts a note offering Oliver's team a walkthrough. And for the people: Graham confirms nobody is made redundant; each person has a next role in Leeds, named.

Tell the team both. At the stand-up on Monday 30 April, Ines answers Lottie's question with both plans on one page. The room relaxes. Oskar says it's the first time a bid hasn't felt like a cliff.

No chasing. No calls to Desmond. No questions to Elaine about the scores. If anyone at MetroVolt raises phase 2, the answer is: “we're not allowed to discuss it, and we're busy with wave 1.”

Today's work. Wave 1's April billing runs clean. The run team's first month alone has one severity 2, fixed by Kemi. And in May, news that matters: Halvorsen wins the billing re-tender, so IA-01 stays as it is.

5What the contract says what happens if we lose

Lodestar SOW · Schedule 10 (exit) · §2signed 30 June 2027
ready either way

Sch. 10, 2.1 On expiry or termination, the Supplier shall provide exit assistance for up to three months, including cooperation with any Replacement Supplier.

“cooperation with any Replacement Supplier”: Vexmoor, if so.

ITT 4.6 Bidders shall not contact MetroVolt staff about the evaluation before the award is announced.

so: no calls. Graham has been told twice.

In plain words: your current contract usually says what you owe if a rival takes over. Read it while you're waiting; it's half of your lose plan already written.

9Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Only a win plan

    Losing becomes chaos. Fix → write both.

  • Not telling the team

    Rumour and anxiety. Fix → both plans, early.

  • Chasing procurement

    A recorded risk. Fix → silence.

  • Reading the lift

    False hope or false despair. Fix → ignore it.

  • Letting today's work slip

    It's still being watched. Fix → run it well.

What Do We Owe After Go-Live?

TL;DR

After go-live, the client will report everything: real defects, new wishes and problems that aren't yours. Read what the warranty actually covers, triage every report against it with the client in the room, fix real defects fast and free, price changes honestly, and close the warranty in writing. A generous gesture is fine when it's recorded as one.

  • Family: handing it over
  • Lives in: warranty log · Schedule 9 · certificate WC-01
  • Pairs with: acceptance (vol. 2), UAT triage (vol. 4)

1The situation Floor 19, 12 February 2029

Wave 1's warranty runs for 90 days after hypercare: from M5's acceptance on 22 November 2028 to 20 February 2029. In that time, 37 reports have arrived.

  • Some are real defects: a rounding error on a refund letter.
  • Some are new wishes: “can the screen show the last three meter reads?”
  • Rupert's office says the slow monthly arrears report is a defect and should be fixed free.
  • The warranty ends in eight days and nobody has agreed which reports are closed.

2Both sides of the table the same moment, seen twice

MetroVolt MetroVolt

To MetroVolt, anything that annoys a user after go-live feels like the supplier's problem. After Lodestar's history (and Orion's) nobody wants to pay for something that might have been the supplier's mistake.

“If it's wrong after go-live, it's yours.”

Wrenfield Ines and Callum

Wrenfield owes real defects, fixed fast. It doesn't owe new features for free, and a warranty that never closes is an open-ended cost. But arguing every report as a lawyer would damage the relationship weeks before the phase 2 bid.

“Fix what's ours, fast. Be clear about what isn't.”

A warranty is a promise about the agreed specification, not about everything users wish for. The specification is the referee.

3Real-life analogy the builder's snagging list

When a builder finishes a house, the owner walks round with a snagging list: a door that sticks, a crack in the plaster. The builder fixes snags free. A new window the owner has decided she'd like is not a snag; it's an extra, and it's priced. Good builders fix the snags quickly and say “happy to quote” for the extras, and everyone signs the list off at the end.

4How it works read it, triage against it, fix fast, close in writing

Read what's covered. Schedule 9: a defect is a failure to meet the agreed specification; severity 1 responded to within an hour, fixed within a day; severity 3 within ten working days. Changes in function, and problems caused by MetroVolt's operation or other suppliers, are excluded.

Triage with the client in the room. Twice a week, Rosa (MetroVolt) and Oskar go through new reports against the spec, as they did in UAT. Each one gets a label and a reason, agreed by both: 14 defects, 15 changes, 8 not ours (5 not reproducible, 2 operational, 1 Halvorsen's).

Fix defects fast and free. All 14 are fixed inside the target times; the refund rounding error in two days.

The slow report. The spec says the arrears report runs in under 30 seconds; it runs in 22. It's not a defect: users want it in five. Ines offers to tune it free, as a recorded goodwill gesture (about £6k of effort), rather than argue. Rupert's office accepts; the log says “change, done as goodwill”, not “defect”.

Close it in writing. Warranty certificate WC-01, signed by Elaine on Friday 23 February: 37 reports, each closed or moved, with the remaining changes in the wave 2 backlog.

  1. Read what the warranty covers

    Definition, severities, exclusions.

  2. Triage every report against it

    With the client, twice a week.

  3. Fix real defects fast

    Inside the target times.

  4. Price changes honestly

    Or give them as recorded goodwill.

  5. Move the rest

    To the change pool, wave 2 or the right owner.

  6. Close the warranty in writing

    A certificate, signed.

Vocabulary

Warranty
A period after acceptance when the supplier fixes defects free.
Defect
A failure to meet the agreed specification.
Change
Something new or different from the specification.
Goodwill gesture
Free work given as a favour, recorded as such.
Warranty certificate
A signed record that the warranty is closed.
Long tail
Small issues that keep arriving long after go-live.

5What the contract says what the warranty promises

Lodestar SOW · Schedule 9 (warranty)signed 30 June 2027
closed

Sch. 9, 2.1 For 90 days after acceptance of Milestone 5, the Supplier shall correct Defects at no charge. A Defect is a failure of the Deliverables to meet the Specification.

the specification is the referee. not the mood of the meeting.

Sch. 9, 2.4 The warranty excludes changes to function, and faults caused by MetroVolt's operation or by Other Suppliers.

8 of 37 were outside it. we helped anyway, and said so.

In plain words: a warranty covers failures against what was agreed. Use the specification to decide each report, with the client, and close the period in writing.

9Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Fixing everything free

    The warranty never ends. Fix → triage against the spec.

  • Arguing every report

    The relationship pays. Fix → recorded goodwill for small ones.

  • Triage without the client

    Every label is disputed. Fix → Rosa in the room.

  • Unrecorded favours

    They become obligations. Fix → write “goodwill”.

  • No closing document

    It drifts on. Fix → a signed certificate.

10When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Every go-live with a warranty.
  • When “defect” is being stretched.
  • Before a follow-on bid.

Skip it when…

  • Time-and-materials work with no warranty (still triage).

what would you do?A week after WC-01, Rosa reports a new rounding error in a rare letter. Is it in warranty?

No, the warranty is closed. But if it's a genuine failure against the spec, fix it quickly and say so: it costs little and the relationship matters. Record it as goodwill, or under the support terms if there are any.

try it

Take your last go-live's issue list. Label each item: defect (against which line of the spec?), change, or not yours. How many would you have argued about? Which would you give as goodwill?

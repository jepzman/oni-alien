Can a Fixed-Price Team Keep a Sustainable Pace?

TL;DR

On a fixed price, unpaid overtime is invisible: it doesn't appear on invoices, only in people. Measure it (worked versus booked, per person, weekly), cap it, repay it with time off, plan recovery after peaks, and plan the work at less than full capacity, so the plan doesn't need heroes. Then put the pace in the next bid, where the client can see it.

1The situation Wrenfield, Leeds, 2 February 2029

Callum adds up eighteen months of Lodestar timesheets, the strip at the top of the early notes. Booked: 7.5 hours a day. Worked: an average of 9.1, with peaks of 12–13 in bid week, rehearsal weekends and cutover.

  • About 2,900 hours of unpaid overtime across the team: roughly 390 working days, nearly two people for a year.
  • That's the real reason the margin held at 10 %: the team paid for it.
  • Phase 2 is three times bigger.

2Both sides of the table the same moment, seen twice

MetroVolt MetroVolt

MetroVolt got a fixed price and a team that always delivered. It never saw the overtime and never asked for it. It also doesn't want phase 2's team to burn out halfway through wave 3.

“We pay for the result. We'd rather the team lasted.”

Wrenfield Ines and Callum

Wrenfield's margin was partly built on people's evenings. For phase 2 (three waves, about 30 months) that can't continue: Hana has already rotated off, and others would follow. Pace has to be measured and planned, not hoped for.

“We priced the work. We didn't price the people.”

Overtime on a fixed price is a hidden discount the team gives the client, and the vendor gives itself credit for.

3Real-life analogy the marathon and the sprint

Nobody runs a marathon at sprint pace. Runners plan a pace they can hold, check their watch every mile, and save effort for the hills. A runner who sprints the first ten miles because she feels good is the one walking at mile twenty. A long project is a marathon with a few hills in it.

4How it works measure, cap, repay, recover, plan at 85 %

Ines, Callum and Graham agree a pace policy in February, for all Wrenfield accounts:

  • Measure: worked versus booked, per person, weekly, on the internal status beside the margin. A ratio above 1.15 for three weeks triggers a conversation.
  • Cap: no more than 45 hours a week except planned peaks agreed in advance.
  • Repay: time off in lieu, taken within a month.
  • Recover: a light week after every bid, rehearsal weekend or cutover, in the plan.
  • Plan at 85 %: phase 2's estimates assume the team delivers 85 % of its theoretical capacity, using wave 1's real velocity (38 points).

Graham's worry: it makes the phase 2 price higher. By about 6 %. Ines's answer: the alternative is hiding 6 % in people's evenings, and losing them by wave 3. The phase 2 bid says so in §5.4, in plain words. Desmond later tells Elaine it was the only bid that mentioned the team's wellbeing.

  1. Measure worked vs booked

    Per person, weekly, beside the margin.

  2. Cap weekly hours

    With planned exceptions only.

  3. Repay overtime

    Time off within a month.

  4. Plan recovery after peaks

    In the schedule, not as a favour.

  5. Plan at less than full capacity

    So the plan doesn't need heroes.

  6. Price it honestly

    And say so in the bid.

Vocabulary

Sustainable pace
A pace a team can keep indefinitely without harm.
Time off in lieu (TOIL)
Paid time off given back for extra hours worked.
Capacity
How much work a team can do in a period.
Planned peak
An agreed period of extra effort, with recovery after.
Hidden discount
Unpaid overtime that lowers the real cost of a fixed price.
Heroics
Relying on individuals' extraordinary effort to hit a plan.

5What the contract says what the next bid says about pace

Phase 2 bid · §5.4 (team and pace)submitted 4 Apr 2029
priced in

5.4 Our plan assumes the team works at a sustainable pace: estimates use 85 % of capacity, with recovery after each wave's cutover. We have priced this in rather than relying on unplanned overtime.

the first time we've written this in a bid.

Lodestar SOW, 6.1 The Supplier shall provide the Key Personnel … for the duration of Wave 1.

a team that burns out can't keep this promise. pace protects it.

In plain words: saying in a bid that your plan assumes a sustainable pace is unusual, and honest. It explains part of your price and protects the key-personnel promises you're making.

9Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Not measuring

    Overtime is invisible on a fixed price. Fix → worked vs booked, weekly.

  • Celebrating long hours

    Everyone copies them. Fix → a cap, and recovery.

  • Planning at 100 %

    Every surprise becomes an evening. Fix → plan at 85 %.

  • Hiding the cost

    The price looks better than it is. Fix → price it, say so.

  • Unrepaid overtime

    Resentment and exits. Fix → time off within a month.

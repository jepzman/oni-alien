How Do We Run a Lessons Session Both Sides Attend?

TL;DR

A lessons session run by one side becomes a list of the other side's failures. Run it jointly: client, vendors (even rivals) and the people who did the work. Make it blameless: what happened and why it made sense at the time, never who. Agree the rules with procurement if a tender is open. End with a few lessons, each with an owner and a date, not a document nobody reopens.

1The situation Room 27B, 15 March 2029

Elaine wants a lessons session on wave 1 before phase 2 begins. The difficulties:

  • The phase 2 tender opened on 5 March. Vexmoor and Wrenfield are rival bidders.
  • Rupert's office asks for “a list of supplier failures”.
  • Bert says the last lessons document he saw, from 2019, was never opened again.
  • Everyone in the room has something to protect: a bid, a budget, a reputation.

2Both sides of the table the same moment, seen twice

MetroVolt Elaine and Desmond

Elaine wants real lessons for phase 2, whoever wins it. Desmond wants the tender kept fair: anything said in the session mustn't give one bidder an advantage. Rupert's office wants something it can show the board.

“Learn from it, without breaking the procurement.”

Wrenfield Ines

Wrenfield could use the session to look good before the bid, or keep quiet to avoid giving Vexmoor anything. Either would waste it. Ines suggests Lottie facilitates: junior, neutral, and the one who's asked the obvious questions for two years.

“If it's honest, everyone learns, including us.”

Lessons sessions fail when they're about blame or when nothing happens afterwards. Make them blameless, and make them small.

3Real-life analogy the air accident investigation

Air accident investigators don't ask “whose fault was it?”. They ask what happened, and why it made sense to the crew at the time. Pilots, engineers and controllers talk freely because they won't be punished for honesty. The report ends in a short list of recommendations, each sent to someone who must reply. That's why flying keeps getting safer.

4How it works agreed rules, blameless, the doers, a few lessons with owners

Rules from procurement. Desmond agrees the session if: no phase 2 discussion, both bidders invited, and the same notes published to every bidder through the tender portal. Rupert's office gets the same notes, not a list of failures.

The doers in the room. Twenty-four people: Elaine, Hollis, Rosa, Kofi, Bert, the run team, Wrenfield's team (Hana comes back for the day), Sven from Halvorsen, and from Vexmoor Bea, who worked on the acceleration review. Oliver declines; Bea comes anyway, with his blessing.

Blameless. Lottie opens with one rule: “We talk about what happened and why it made sense at the time. Never who.” When someone starts “Wrenfield should have…”, Lottie rewrites it on the board as “the plan assumed…”.

From many to a few. 63 sticky notes, grouped on the wall, voted on. Five lessons survive, each with an owner and a date:

  1. Profile real data before pricing (owner: Desmond, in the phase 2 tender rules).
  2. Test interfaces against the partner's real test system, early (Sven).
  3. Book a performance environment at the start (Hollis).
  4. Plan queue capacity for the cutover night, not the average day (Kemi).
  5. Measure team hours on fixed-price work (Elaine, as a phase 2 reporting line).

Bea's sticky note, on the MetroBus queue, is the one that becomes lesson 4. Nobody says “Vexmoor” or “Wrenfield” in the notes.

  1. Agree the rules with procurement

    If a tender is open.

  2. Invite the doers, all sides

    Including rivals and partners.

  3. Pick a neutral facilitator

    Junior is fine.

  4. Make it blameless

    What happened and why; never who.

  5. Converge to a few lessons

    Vote; keep five.

  6. Give each an owner and a date

    And check them later.

5What the contract says what the tender rules allow

Phase 2 invitation to tender · §4 (conduct)issued 5 Mar 2029
fair to all

4.2 Bidders shall not seek to discuss this procurement with MetroVolt staff other than through the tender portal.

so: no phase 2 talk in the session. Lottie had a bell.

4.5 Any information MetroVolt gives to one bidder about this procurement will be given to all bidders.

hence the notes on the portal, to everyone.

In plain words: during a live tender, every joint meeting is a procurement risk. Ask procurement for rules first; publishing the same notes to all bidders keeps it fair.

9Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • One side runs it

    It becomes a list of the other's failures. Fix → jointly.

  • Names in the notes

    People stop talking. Fix → what, not who.

  • Fifty lessons

    Nobody acts on any. Fix → five, with owners.

  • No owners or dates

    The 2019 document. Fix → owner, date, review.

  • Ignoring the tender

    Procurement challenge. Fix → agree rules, publish to all.

10When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • After a major milestone or go-live.
  • Before the next phase.
  • When several suppliers did the work.

Skip it when…

  • During an incident (do it after).

What Should the Handover Architecture Say?

TL;DR

When the supplier leaves, the reasons leave with it. Hand over a short architecture handbook: one picture (one version), the decisions that matter and why, with what would change them, the known debts with costs and fixes, how to change it safely, and what you'd do differently. Twenty pages someone reads beat four hundred nobody does.

  • Family: handing it over
  • Lives in: architecture/lodestar-handbook-v1.md · 22 pages
  • Pairs with: the decision log (vol. 2), knowledge transfer

1The situation Floor 19, 23 March 2029

Lodestar's architecture is spread across:

  • 74 decision log entries (DL-001 to DL-074), some superseded.
  • 11 interface agreements, IA-01 to IA-11.
  • A wiki of 410 pages, many out of date.
  • Three versions of the context diagram, each slightly different.
  • And the reasons, mostly in Hana's head and Ines's notebook. If Wrenfield loses phase 2, nobody from Wrenfield will be there to ask.

2Both sides of the table the same moment, seen twice

MetroVolt Hollis

Hollis's design authority will own Lodestar's architecture after wave 1, possibly with Vexmoor building phase 2 on it. He needs to know what can change, what mustn't, and why each odd-looking decision was made, so that nobody “fixes” something that was deliberate.

“Tell me why, so we don't undo it by accident.”

Wrenfield Ines

Ines could hand over everything: all 410 pages, all 74 decisions. That would satisfy the contract and help nobody. Writing it short means choosing what matters, and admitting the debts and regrets in writing, possibly for a rival to read.

“The short version is harder. And it's the only one they'll read.”

The most valuable part of an architecture handover is the reasons: without them, the next team will undo good decisions and keep bad ones.

3Real-life analogy the house handbook

When you sell a house you've renovated, the best thing to leave is a short house handbook: where the stopcock is, why the kitchen wall can't come down (it's holding up the bathroom), which bit of the roof needs doing in five years, and the builder's number. Not every receipt. The new owners need the few things that would hurt if they didn't know them.

4How it works one picture, the decisions and why, the debts, changing safely, regrets

Ines, Tariq and Bilal write Lodestar architecture handbook v1.0: 22 pages, reviewed by Hollis and the run team.

  • One picture. A single context diagram, version-controlled; the other two are archived with a note saying so.
  • Twelve decisions that matter out of 74, each with its reason and what would change it: e.g. DL-012, the anti-corruption layer around MAVIS (“remove only when MAVIS is switched off”); DL-047, velocity planned at 38; the back-feed's queue limit after wave 1 night.
  • Nine known debts, each with an estimated cost and a suggested fix: the manual workaround for the 3 excluded accounts; ten years of meter history only readable in MAVIS (CR-019); the arrears report index.
  • How to change it safely: run the contract tests, rehearse any migration twice, ask the design authority, and check the runbooks.
  • What we'd do differently: book the performance environment on day 1; profile all the data, not a sample; plan for the queue, not the average.

The handbook is written so that whoever builds phase 2 can use it, Vexmoor included. Hollis adopts its structure as the template for every supplier handover from April.

  1. Draw one picture

    Archive the rest, with a note.

  2. Pick the decisions that matter

    With the reason and what would change it.

  3. List the known debts

    Cost and suggested fix.

  4. Explain how to change it safely

    Tests, rehearsals, who to ask.

  5. Write what you'd do differently

    Honestly.

  6. Review it with the new owners

    Then let them own it.

Vocabulary

Architecture handbook
A short document explaining a system's design and its reasons.
Architecture decision record
A note of one decision, its context and consequences.
Technical debt
Shortcuts that cost more to change later.
Context diagram
A picture of a system and what it connects to.
Design authority
The client group that approves architectural changes.
Fitness function
An automated check that an architectural property still holds.

5What the contract says what the exit clause asks for

Lodestar SOW · Schedule 10 (exit) · §4signed 30 June 2027
delivered

Sch. 10, 4.1 On exit, the Supplier shall deliver documentation sufficient for MetroVolt or a Replacement Supplier to operate, maintain and develop the solution.

“or a Replacement Supplier”. we wrote it for Vexmoor too.

Sch. 10, 4.2 Documentation includes the design, the key decisions and their rationale, and known defects and limitations.

rationale and limitations: the parts suppliers usually leave out.

In plain words: exit schedules often require rationale and known limitations, not just design. Those are the parts most worth writing, and the hardest to write honestly.

9Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Handing over everything

    Nobody reads it. Fix → the 22 pages that matter.

  • Design without reasons

    Good decisions get undone. Fix → why, and what would change it.

  • Hiding the debts

    The next team finds them painfully. Fix → list them, with costs.

  • Several pictures

    Nobody knows which is true. Fix → one, versioned.

  • Writing it alone

    The owners don't trust it. Fix → review it with them.

10When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • At the end of any supplier engagement.
  • When the build team changes.
  • Before a phase handed to another supplier.

Skip it when…

  • Never; shorten it, but keep the reasons.

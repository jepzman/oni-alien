How Do We Hand It Over So It Sticks?

TL;DR

Knowledge transfer done with slides transfers slides. Transfer it by doing: the client's people shadow you, then you reverse-shadow them, hands off the keyboard; they write the runbooks; drills with real failures; and the sign-off comes from the people who'll be called at night, not from a manager who attended the presentations.

1The situation Floor 19, March 2029

From April, MetroVolt's run team (Asif, Marta and Kemi) will run Lodestar's wave 1 platform on their own. §11.2 of the contract requires Wrenfield to transfer knowledge “so that MetroVolt can operate the solution”.

  • The first draft plan, written in a hurry in January, was 23 presentations and a sign-off box for a manager.
  • The run team has watched demos. None of them has run a release or fixed a stopped back-feed alone.
  • Hana, who knew the back-feed best, has rotated off.

2Both sides of the table the same moment, seen twice

MetroVolt Kemi, Marta and Asif

The run team will be the ones paged at 03:00. They've worked beside Wrenfield in mixed squads, but always with a Wrenfield engineer nearby. Kemi says it plainly: “I don't want a certificate. I want to know I can fix it.”

“Let us break it while you're still here.”

Wrenfield Ines and Tariq

Wrenfield wants a clean handover: the contract requires it, and a client who can't run the system calls the supplier forever, for free. Tariq is new and has his own learning to do. Presentations would be quicker. They wouldn't work.

“If they can't run it, we haven't handed it over.”

Knowledge transfer is finished when the client's people can do it without you, under pressure, not when you've finished talking.

3Real-life analogy learning to drive

Nobody learns to drive from a lecture. You sit beside the instructor, then you drive while she watches, with her hands in her lap and a foot near the dual brake. Then you practise the hard things: a hill start, a roundabout in the rain. The test is taken by the driver, not by the instructor's manager.

4How it works shadow, reverse-shadow, their runbooks, drills, their sign-off

Tariq and Ines rewrite the plan in January with Kemi, Marta and Asif. 23 presentations become 4, for the background. The rest is doing:

Shadow (12–23 February). The run team sits with Wrenfield during every operational task: the nightly checks, a release, the MetroBus queue, the back-feed.

Reverse shadow (26 February – 9 March). The run team does everything; Wrenfield watches with hands off the keyboard. Lottie keeps a tally of every time a Wrenfield engineer was tempted to grab the mouse (19, falling to 2).

Their runbooks. The run team writes the 11 runbooks themselves, starting from Hana's 14 pages, in their own words. Wrenfield reviews them. Bert reads the back-feed one and adds a page about the 02:10 file.

Drills with real failures. Four, in the pre-production copy: the back-feed stopped; the MetroBus queue full (wave 1 night's problem); a bad file from Halvorsen; a failed release to roll back. In drill 3, Kemi finds and fixes the bad file in 41 minutes without anyone from Wrenfield touching a key.

Their sign-off. On Friday 9 March, the knowledge transfer certificate is signed by Kemi, Marta and Asif, then countersigned by Hollis. Kemi's comment in the box: “I'd take the call at three.”

  1. Plan it with the people who'll run it

    Not for them.

  2. Shadow

    They watch you do the real work.

  3. Reverse shadow

    You watch them, hands off.

  4. Let them write the runbooks

    In their own words.

  5. Drill real failures

    The ones you've actually had.

  6. Sign-off by the night-call people

    Then their manager countersigns.

5What the contract says what the knowledge transfer clause asks

Lodestar SOW · §11.2 and Schedule 10signed 30 June 2027
transferred

11.2 The Supplier shall transfer knowledge to MetroVolt staff throughout the Services, so that MetroVolt can operate the solution at the end of Wave 1.

“can operate”. not “has attended”.

Sch. 10, 3.1 Knowledge transfer is complete when MetroVolt's nominated operational staff confirm in writing that they can operate the solution.

we nominated the people who'd be paged. Hollis agreed.

In plain words: read what “complete” means in your knowledge-transfer clause. If it says “can operate”, the evidence is the client's people operating it, not attendance lists.

9Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Slides as transfer

    Nobody can run it. Fix → do it, with them.

  • Hands on the keyboard

    They never practise. Fix → reverse shadow, hands off.

  • Writing runbooks for them

    They don't trust them. Fix → they write, you review.

  • Drilling the easy things

    The real failure is new. Fix → drill what actually broke.

  • Manager's sign-off

    It measures attendance. Fix → the 03:00 people sign.

10When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Every handover to a client run team.
  • Before a supplier leaves.
  • When key people rotate off.

Skip it when…

  • Never; shorten it, but keep the drills.

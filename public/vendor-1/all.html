Winning the Work

Reading a tender, bid or no bid, clarification questions, a solution on two pages, assumptions, estimating, pricing models, the incumbent, a big rival, a sales promise, the orals, references, the final offer and the handover to delivery.

Reading the tender what they asked for, what they meant, and whether to try

  1. 01How Do I Read a Tender?Read it three times: for how it's scored, for the work hidden in every “will”, and for the history nobody wrote down.p. 01
  2. 02Should We Even Bid?A bid costs real money. Decide with a scorecard, a reason to win and your conditions, before anyone falls in love with it.p. 02
  3. 03Who Wrote This Tender?Requirements copied from someone else's design tell you who the client has been talking to. Ask openly, then decide if you can still win.p. 03
  4. 04What Do We Ask Before We Answer?Every bidder sees every answer: ask what shrinks your risk, word it so it gives nothing away, and read the other bidders' questions.p. 04

Shaping the answer a design, its assumptions, an estimate and a price

  1. 05How Do I Design on Two Pages of Facts?Compare three honest options, pick one you can draw on a page, and say what you must learn before you commit.p. 05
  2. 06What Are We Assuming?Write down the world your price assumes: assumptions, dependencies and exclusions, each with a number, an owner, a test and a consequence.p. 06
  3. 07How Long Will It Really Take?Estimate in ranges, check them against what similar projects really took (including the client's own failure), and price the range, not the hope.p. 07
  4. 08Fixed Price, or Time and Materials?Match the model to what you know: paid discovery while it's foggy, a fixed price once you can see, a rate card for the rest.p. 08

Up against the others the incumbent, the big rival, our own sales team, and the room

  1. 09What About the Vendor Already Here?The incumbent knows the systems and the people, and you'll need their interfaces. Treat them as a future partner, not an enemy.p. 09
  2. 10How Do We Compete with a Big Consultancy?Don't fight a famous name on size. Win on named people, specific knowledge and a plan the client can check, and never attack their past.p. 10
  3. 11What If Sales Already Promised?When someone has already promised a date, re-anchor it in public with a smaller first delivery, before the promise becomes the plan.p. 11
  4. 12How Do We Win the Room?Bring the people who'll do the work, show your thinking on the client's own data, and answer the hardest question before it's asked.p. 12

Closing the deal references, the final offer, and handing the promises to delivery

  1. 13Who Will Vouch for Us?References win on specifics. Brief your referees on the client's real worries, and let them be honest.p. 13
  2. 14What Do We Give Away in the Final Offer?Trade, don't cut: every pound off the price buys back scope, risk or time, and the contingency goes last.p. 14
  3. 15Who Remembers What We Promised?Hand the bid to the delivery team with a promises register, named people and the assumptions, so nobody discovers the deal in month six.p. 15

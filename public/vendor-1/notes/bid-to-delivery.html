Who Remembers What We Promised?

TL;DR

A bid makes dozens of promises, in the document, in clarifications, at the orals, in reference calls and in the final offer, and the people who made them often step back once it's won. Before the champagne is finished, build a promises register: every promise, its source, an owner and a date. Hand it over in one session, with the assumptions and the named people, so nobody discovers the deal in month six.

  • Lives in: delivery/lodestar/promises-register.md

1The situation MetroVolt and Wrenfield, 6–7 May 2027

On Thursday 6 May at 09:00, MetroVolt's all-hands announces Project Horizon: a 15 % cost cut, about 600 roles, led by Rupert Thorne, with Vexmoor Consulting as his advisers. At 11:40 Wrenfield gets Desmond's email: preferred bidder for Lodestar, “subject to Horizon governance”.

  • Champagne in Leeds at noon. Graham makes a speech. Vivian is thanked twice.
  • Ines counts what Wrenfield has promised: in the bid, in 63 clarifications, at the orals, on the reference calls, in the final offer. 47 promises.
  • Vivian moves back to account management on Monday. Half the promises were made in rooms the delivery team never sat in.
  • And “subject to Horizon governance” means nobody knows yet what happens next.

2Both sides of the table the same moment, seen twice

MetroVolt Elaine, holding the bid

For MetroVolt, the bid is the promise: every sentence, every answer at the orals. Elaine will hold Wrenfield to what Bilal said about the sample and what Vivian said about twelve months. She doesn't care who in Wrenfield said it.

“You said it. I wrote it down.”

Wrenfield Ines and Callum

Wrenfield's bid team (Vivian, a bid writer, Graham) made promises the delivery team has to keep. Some were never written in the bid at all: a sentence at the orals, a reassurance to a referee. Callum wants them all in one list before people forget what they said.

“If it's not on the list, we'll find out about it at a steering committee.”

Clients remember promises by what was said. Vendors remember them by who said it. The register makes both the same list.

3Real-life analogy the house sale's fixtures list

When you buy a house, the sellers made promises at every viewing: “the boiler was serviced last year”, “we'll leave the curtains”. A good solicitor makes them write all of it on the fixtures and fittings form, signed, so on moving day nobody argues about the curtains. The estate agent who said it all has moved on to the next sale.

4How it works every promise, its source, an owner and a date

On Friday 7 May Callum books three hours with everyone who touched the bid. They go source by source: the bid document (31 promises), clarification answers (4), the orals (7, from Bilal's notes and the panel's questions), the reference calls and site visit (3) and the BAFO (2). Each promise gets a line: ID, the exact words, the source, an owner in the delivery team and a due date.

Some are big (P-01, first 20,000 live in month 9). Some are small and dangerous because they're easy to forget (P-12, a status report every Friday; P-31, sharing the sample profile with MetroVolt's data team). Two are promises Wrenfield should never have made, and are flagged for the contract talks (an orals answer that implied “no change requests for data” and a reference-call comment that Hana would be on site “every day”).

Vivian walks the team through what she said at dinner, at the orals and to Priya's assistant. Then she steps back, with one standing job: tell Callum within a day if she ever promises anything again. The register goes into the delivery folder next to the assumptions. It'll be walked through with MetroVolt at the kick-off.

  1. Book the handover before the celebration ends

    Within a week, while memories are fresh.

  2. Go source by source

    Bid, clarifications, orals, references, final offer, any emails.

  3. Write each promise in the exact words

    With its source.

  4. Give each an owner and a date

    In the delivery team, not the bid team.

  5. Flag the dangerous ones

    Take them into the contract talks to fix in writing.

  6. Keep a line open to sales

    New promises reach the register within a day.

5What the contract says what “preferred bidder” means, and doesn't

MetroVolt award letter · 6 May 202711:40
not yet a contract

1 MetroVolt intends to award the Lodestar contract to Wrenfield Digital Ltd, subject to contract, to the standstill period, and to approval under Horizon governance.

three “subject to”s. no contract yet.

3 The contract will incorporate the tender, the tender response, clarifications and the best and final offer.

so all 47 are in. including P-23.

In plain words: preferred bidder means you've won the right to negotiate, not the contract. Everything you said while bidding will be incorporated, so the promises register is really a list of contract terms, and the dangerous ones must be fixed before signing.

6Account health the same move, green, amber and red

Green

smooth award

  • Handover within a week.
  • Walk the register with the client at kick-off.
  • Close the flagged ones in the contract.

Amber Lodestar now

won, but “subject to” something

  • Handover now anyway: memories fade.
  • Fix dangerous promises in the contract talks.
  • Keep sales close but quiet.

Red

won, and the client remembers different promises

  • Compare registers line by line.
  • Agree one list in writing.
  • Fix the gaps before you sign, not after.

9Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Handing over the document only

    Promises from the orals and calls are lost. Fix → go source by source.

  • Paraphrasing

    “About nine months” becomes “nine months” becomes “by March”. Fix → exact words, with the source.

  • Owners in the bid team

    They've moved on. Fix → owners in the delivery team.

  • Ignoring dangerous promises

    They're incorporated into the contract. Fix → flag them and fix them before signing.

  • Sales keeps promising

    New commitments after the handover. Fix → a standing rule: tell the owner within a day.

10When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Any won bid.
  • Sales and delivery are different people.
  • Promises were made outside the document.

Skip it when…

  • The same three people sold it and will deliver it, and wrote everything down: a short check is still worth it.

11Self-check quiz

What is a promises register?

Every commitment made while selling, with exact words, source, owner and due date.

Why go source by source?

Promises were made in the bid, clarifications, orals, reference calls and the final offer, not only the document.

Why write the exact words?

Paraphrases drift; the client will quote the exact words.

What does “preferred bidder” mean?

You've been chosen to negotiate the contract; it isn't signed yet.

Why fix dangerous promises before signing?

Everything said during the bid is incorporated into the contract.

what would you do?Three weeks after the handover, Vivian mentions she told Priya's assistant the pilot “might be ready by Christmas”. What do you do?

Add it to the register with the exact words and source, flag it, and agree with Vivian how to correct it with Priya directly, before it reaches a steering committee.

try it

Think of a deal, a project or a job you've just won. List every promise made while winning it, from every conversation, in the exact words. Who owns each one now?

remember!!

The bid team leaves. The promises stay. Write them all down, in the exact words, before the champagne is finished.

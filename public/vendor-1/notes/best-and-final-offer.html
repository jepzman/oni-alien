What Do We Give Away in the Final Offer?

TL;DR

When the client asks for a lower final price, trade, don't cut. Every pound off should buy something back: less scope, less risk carried or more time. Make a list of what you can give and what the client gets in return, and a list of what you won't give and why. The contingency goes last: without it, your fixed price is a bet. Explain the offer in one paragraph the client can repeat.

1The situation Wrenfield, 29 April 2027

On Tuesday 27 April Desmond writes to the two remaining bidders, Wrenfield and Vexmoor: “Please submit your best and final offer by Friday 30 April. MetroVolt invites you to consider a 10 % reduction.” Ten per cent of £4.8m is £480k.

  • Graham's first idea: take £480k out of the contingency. “We'll be careful.”
  • The contingency is exactly the gap between the P50 and P80 estimates. Without it, the fixed price is a coin toss.
  • A rumour reaches Vivian that Vexmoor will cut 12 %. Nobody can check it.

2Both sides of the table the same moment, seen twice

MetroVolt Desmond and Nkem

Asking for 10 % is procurement's job, not an insult. Every pound saved is a pound they can show the Horizon board. They also know a bidder who cuts 10 % with no explanation either padded the first price or is about to suffer.

“Show us it was a real price, and what you'd move.”

Wrenfield Ines and Graham

Wrenfield wants to win, and Graham wants to show willing. Ines wants the fixed price to stay fixed for a world Wrenfield can survive. The answer is a trade table: things that genuinely cost less if MetroVolt accepts something different.

“We can move the scope. We can't move the odds.”

A clean cut with no trade tells the client your first price had fat in it. A trade tells them your price was real.

3Real-life analogy the car salesman and the extras

A good car dealer asked for 10 % off doesn't cut the price of the engine. He says: “Without the extended warranty, it's £600 less; with steel wheels instead of alloys, £400 less. The engine price is the engine price.” You get a cheaper car and you know exactly what you gave up. If he just knocked 10 % off, you'd wonder what was wrong with it.

4How it works a trade table, and the contingency goes last

Callum and Ines list everything that could change the price. Each item gets three columns: what we give, what MetroVolt gets, what it costs them.

  • Train-the-trainer instead of training 40 super-users directly: −£120k. MetroVolt's own trainers learn the system, which helps waves 2–4. (X-04 changes.)
  • Warranty 90 → 60 days: −£60k. Faster handover to MetroVolt support.
  • Not offered, with reasons in the letter: the contingency (it's what makes the price fixed), day rates (they price every future change), discovery (already capped).

Total: −£180k (3.75 %), to £4.62m. Graham worries it isn't 10 %. Ines shows him the curve: at £4.32m the P80 margin would be about 10 %, below Wrenfield's 15 % floor. Graham agrees. The letter's last paragraph: “We have not reduced our contingency. That is what allows us to offer a fixed price we are confident we can honour.”

  1. Don't answer the percentage

    Answer with a list of trades.

  2. List everything that drives the price

    Scope, risk, time, service levels, payment terms.

  3. For each trade, say what they get

    A trade the client values is worth more than a cut.

  4. List what won't move, and why

    Contingency, rates, capped work.

  5. Check the margin at P80 after the trade

    Against your floor, not your hopes.

  6. Explain it in one paragraph

    The panel will repeat it to the board.

Vocabulary

BAFO
Best and final offer: the last price, after negotiation.
Trade
Giving something in return for a lower price.
Contingency
Money in the price for the risks you carry.
Margin floor
The lowest margin your firm will accept.
Train-the-trainer
Teaching the client's trainers, who then train users.
Winner's curse
Winning by pricing below what the work will really cost.

4 Changes to scope in a BAFO must be stated explicitly and will be reflected in the contract.

so X-04 changes from “beyond 40 super-users” to “train-the-trainer”. write it.

In plain words: a BAFO is final, and any scope you trade away is written into the contract. List every change explicitly, or you'll deliver the old scope at the new price.

9Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Cutting the contingency

    The fixed price stops being fixed for you. Fix → the contingency goes last, if ever.

  • A cut with no trade

    The client wonders what was padded. Fix → every pound buys something back.

  • Pricing against a rumour

    You race a number nobody can check. Fix → price your own work.

  • Trading scope silently

    You deliver the old scope at the new price. Fix → state every change explicitly.

  • Below your floor to win

    The winner's curse. Fix → check the margin at P80 against the floor.

10When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Any final price negotiation.
  • A client asks for a percentage off.
  • A rival is rumoured to be cheaper.

Skip it when…

  • The price really did have room in it: then say so, and cut honestly.

11Self-check quiz

What does “trade, don't cut” mean?

Every reduction buys back scope, risk or time, instead of simply lowering the price.

Why does the contingency go last?

It covers the gap between the P50 and P80 estimates; without it the fixed price is a coin toss.

What did Wrenfield trade?

Train-the-trainer instead of 40 super-users (−£120k) and a 60-day warranty instead of 90 (−£60k).

Why explain what didn't move?

It shows the first price was real and gives the panel a reason to repeat.

What is the winner's curse?

Winning by pricing below what the work will really cost.

what would you do?Desmond phones (through the proper channel) and says “Vexmoor is at £4.2m.” What do you do?

Thank him, and don't move below your floor. Restate what your price buys and offer another trade if one exists. If £4.2m is real, it may be the winner's curse for them.

try it

Take a price you've quoted. List three things you could change that would genuinely cost you less, and what the client would get in return for each. Which part of the price must never move, and how would you explain why in one sentence?

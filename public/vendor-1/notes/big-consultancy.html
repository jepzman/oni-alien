How Do We Compete with a Big Consultancy?

TL;DR

You won't beat a big consultancy on size, brand or relationships, so don't try. Write down their real strengths and your specific answer to each: named senior people who will do the work, specific experience, a plan the client can check. Describe the risks of approaches, not the failures of firms. Never attack their past work: the panel was part of it.

1The situation Wrenfield, 16 March 2027

From the clarification log, Wrenfield knows Vexmoor Consulting is bidding, plans to reuse Orion's data hub, and may deliver partly offshore. Vexmoor has about 40,000 people, has worked at MetroVolt for three years, and its partner, Oliver Thane, knows the board.

  • Vivian's draft of the “why us” section begins: “Unlike previous suppliers, who failed to deliver…”
  • Elaine ran Orion. Hollis approved its architecture. Priya sponsored its last year. The sentence insults three of the six people scoring the bid.
  • Graham asks the real question: “Why would anyone pick 80 people over 40,000?”

2Both sides of the table the same moment, seen twice

MetroVolt Priya weighs it

Choosing a famous firm is safe for the chooser: if it fails, nobody blames the person who picked the big name. Choosing a small one is a personal risk. Priya needs reasons she can repeat to the board that sound like evidence, not like a gamble.

“If I pick the small firm and it fails, it's my name.”

Wrenfield Ines and Callum

Wrenfield can't match Vexmoor's brand. It can match the work: senior people who do it themselves, a smaller and more honest plan, and recent experience moving a legacy customer system. It has to say that without ever saying “Orion failed”.

“Make choosing us feel like the careful option.”

The panel already knows what went wrong with Orion. It doesn't need the bidder to tell it; it needs a bidder who seems to have learned.

3Real-life analogy the local restaurant next to the chain

A small restaurant opens across from a famous chain. It doesn't put up a sign saying “the chain's food is bad”: half its customers ate there last week. It puts the chef's name on the menu, says where the fish came from this morning, and lets people taste. The chain's size isn't the weakness. Its sameness is.

4How it works their strengths, your specific answers, and no attacks

Callum and Ines write a competitor sheet that never leaves Wrenfield. Vexmoor's strengths: brand, scale, board relationships, knowledge of Orion. Weaknesses the panel will see without help: Orion's history, a model where partners sell and juniors deliver, offshore delivery.

Each strength gets a specific answer in the bid. Brand → named people with CVs, who present at the orals and are named as key personnel in the contract. Relationships → an honest plan with P50 and P80 that Hollis can check line by line. Orion knowledge → a paid discovery that learns MAVIS properly. Cheaper rates → fewer, more senior days.

Where a risk applies to Vexmoor's likely approach, the bid describes the approach, not the firm: “Reusing partly built components is often the fastest route, provided their data model is checked against current data first. Our discovery includes that check for any component MetroVolt wishes to keep.” That's fair, useful to MetroVolt, and true of anyone. Vivian's sentence is deleted.

  1. Write their strengths honestly

    If you can't name them, you'll lose to them.

  2. Answer each with something specific

    A name, a number, a document, a date.

  3. Choose ground you can win on

    Seniority, focus, honesty, a specific experience.

  4. Describe risks of approaches, not firms

    True of anyone; useful to the client.

  5. Make choosing you feel careful

    Evidence the sponsor can repeat to the board.

  6. Keep the sheet in-house

    It's for your thinking, not for the client.

Vocabulary

Win theme
The main reason to choose you, repeated through the bid.
Discriminator
Something you offer that rivals can't easily claim.
Ghosting
Raising the risks of a rival's likely approach without naming them; fair only if true of anyone.
Pyramid staffing
A few senior people sell, many junior people deliver.
Key personnel
People named in the contract who can't be swapped without consent.
Safe choice bias
Preferring the famous option because failure is easier to defend.

5What the contract says how “named people” becomes a promise

Lodestar SOW · §6 key personnel (draft)from the bid
named

6.1 The Supplier shall provide the Key Personnel named in Schedule 6 (including the Lead Architect and the Technical Lead) for the duration of Wave 1.

Ines and Hana. by name.

6.3 The Supplier shall not remove or replace Key Personnel without MetroVolt's prior written consent, not to be unreasonably withheld.

our discriminator is now our obligation. good.

In plain words: if your advantage is the people, offer to name them in the contract. It turns a sales claim into a promise the client can enforce, which is exactly why it's believed.

9Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Attacking the last supplier

    The panel worked on it. Fix → talk about approaches and what you'll do.

  • Competing on size

    You'll never be bigger. Fix → compete on seniority, focus and specifics.

  • Generic claims

    “Agile, experienced, passionate” is in every bid. Fix → a name, a number or a date for each claim.

  • Unfair ghosting

    Hinting at faults that aren't true. Fix → only risks that are true of any firm.

  • Forgetting the chooser's risk

    The sponsor needs a defensible choice. Fix → give them evidence they can repeat.

10When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • A bigger, better-known rival is bidding.
  • The rival has history with the client.
  • Your team wants to point at a past failure.

Skip it when…

  • You're the big firm: then read it backwards.

11Self-check quiz

Why not attack Orion in the bid?

The people scoring the bid ran, approved and sponsored it.

What is a discriminator?

Something you offer that rivals can't easily claim, e.g. named senior people.

When is ghosting fair?

When it describes the risk of an approach, true of anyone, rather than accusing a firm.

Why offer to name key people in the contract?

It turns a sales claim into an enforceable promise, which makes it credible.

What is safe choice bias?

Choosers prefer the famous option because a failure is easier to defend.

what would you do?At the orals, a panel member asks “Why should we trust a small firm after what happened last time?” How do you answer without mentioning Orion?

Point to the specifics: the named team in the room will do the work and is named in the contract; the plan has ranges they can check; discovery is paid and separate; and a reference can describe a similar move in 2025.

try it

Write down a rival's three real strengths. For each, write one specific answer you could prove: a name, a number, a document. Then delete any sentence in your pitch that mentions them.

remember!!

Don't fight a famous name on size. Put the chef's name on the menu, and never say a word against the kitchen next door.

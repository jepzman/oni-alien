Post-Launch Review

TL;DR

Most teams launch, celebrate and move on, so they never learn whether their estimates were any good. A post-launch review, a few weeks after launch, compares what you expected (the business case, the bets) with what happened: outcomes, effort, costs, side effects. Note the surprises and why, decide what's next for the feature, and what you'll do differently next time. Blameless, written, an hour.

1The situation Larder, 6 July 2026

Approvals launched in Amsterdam on 13 April. The team celebrated, then moved straight to Rotterdam and live updates. Twelve weeks later, nobody has compared what happened with what the business case and the bets expected. Were the estimates good? What surprised us? Nobody knows, so the next business case will be written with the same blind spots.

  • Launches are treated as the finish line.
  • Estimates are never checked, so they never improve.
  • Lessons stay in people's heads, and leave with them.

2Real-life analogy the debrief on the dock

After a race, a good sailing crew spends fifteen minutes on the dock: what we planned, what happened, why, and what we'll change next race. No blame; it goes in the logbook. Crews that skip it make the same mistake at the same mark every season.

3How it solves it expected vs actual, then next time

Grace ran the review with the trio and Sam on 6 July, in an hour. Outcomes matched the bets: swap refund tickets 3.4 % (expected about 3.5 %), answers 82 %, benefit about €28k a month. Effort didn't: 11 person-weeks, not 8, mostly the platform integrations. The surprises (notifications off, Android grouping) were written down; next time's business cases get a buffer for platform work.

  1. Schedule it at launch

    Six to twelve weeks after, when outcomes can be read.

  2. Bring the expectations

    The business case, the bets, the test cards.

  3. Compare, line by line

    Outcomes, effort, costs, guardrails: expected vs actual.

  4. Name the surprises and why

    Without blame; facts and causes.

  5. Decide what's next for the feature

    Invest more, keep as is, or stop.

  6. Change how you estimate

    One or two concrete changes for the next case.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • Estimates improve over time.
  • Benefits checked, not assumed.
  • Surprises recorded, not repeated.
  • Clear next step for the feature.
  • Trust with finance: cases get checked.

What we lose

  • An hour for several people.
  • Uncomfortable when estimates were off.
  • Easy to skip when busy.
  • Blame creeps in without care.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Never scheduled

    Always next month. Fix → book it on launch day.

  • Only outcomes

    Effort and cost never compared. Fix → compare everything the case promised.

  • Blame

    “Who estimated 8 weeks?” Fix → causes, not culprits.

  • No change

    Lessons noted, nothing different. Fix → one or two concrete changes for next time.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Every launch with a business case or bets.
  • Big migrations and city launches.
  • Before writing the next business case.
  • When a feature's value is being questioned.

Skip it when…

  • Tiny changes; a note in the release log is enough.

try it

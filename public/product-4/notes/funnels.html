Funnels

TL;DR

A single rate (“77 % answer”) tells you how much, not where people drop out. A funnel counts users at every step of a journey, in order, so the biggest drop shows itself. Split each step by segment (platform, city, new vs returning) to find the cause. Fix the biggest drop first, with the same funnel as your measure. Guesses about “why” come after you know “where”.

  • Family: instrumenting
  • Origin: conversion funnels in marketing and web analytics; AARRR (Dave McClure, 2007)
  • Lives in: the analytics tool, built on the tracking plan's events
  • Pairs with: event tracking plan, customer journey map, A/B tests

1The situation Larder, 18 May 2026

With consistent events, approvals' answer rate is 77 %. Grace wants more, and the team has three theories about the other 23 %: the countdown is too short, people don't trust the price, the screen is confusing. Each theory has a fan. Nobody knows where customers actually drop out, so any fix is a guess.

  • One rate hides which step loses people.
  • Teams fix the step they have a theory about, not the one that's broken.
  • Averages hide segments where it's much worse.

2Real-life analogy the ride queue

A theme park counts people at each step of a ride's queue: join, first turn, height check, on the ride. Most leave at the height check: so that's where to put a sign at the entrance (“120 cm minimum”), not a faster ride. The count shows where; then you ask why.

3How it solves it count every step, split by segment

Grace built the funnel from the four tracking-plan events: offered 100 → seen 88 → opened 82 → answered 77 → applied 76. The biggest drop was seen → opened, and split by platform it was almost all Android (71 % against iOS's 96 %). Dee found Android was grouping Larder's notifications, hiding the swap under an order update. Fixed on 1 June: answers rose to 82 %. None of the three theories had been the problem.

  1. Write the journey's steps

    In order, each matching an event from the tracking plan.

  2. Count users at each step

    For a fixed period and population.

  3. Find the biggest drop

    In absolute numbers, not just percentages.

  4. Split that step by segment

    Platform, city, new vs returning, time of day.

  5. Then ask why

    Interviews, recordings, logs, for that step and segment.

  6. Fix and re-measure

    Same funnel, same period length.

Vocabulary for the exam

Funnel
Users counted at each step of a journey, in order.
Conversion rate
The share moving from one step to the next.
Drop-off
Users lost between two steps.
Segment
A subgroup: platform, city, customer type.
Step
One event in the journey.
AARRR
Acquisition, Activation, Retention, Referral, Revenue: a growth funnel.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • Where, before why: no more guessing.
  • Segments reveal causes (Android).
  • The right fix first.
  • Measurable improvement, same funnel.
  • Theories tested cheaply against data.

What we lose

  • Needs good events first.
  • Funnels miss loops and non-linear journeys.
  • Segment fishing can find noise.
  • “Why” still needs research.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Percentages only

    A 50 % drop of 10 users. Fix → look at absolute numbers too.

  • Fixing the favourite theory

    A longer timer, without data. Fix → find the biggest drop first.

  • No segments

    The average hides Android. Fix → split the worst step by platform, city, type.

  • Segment fishing

    Ten splits until one looks interesting. Fix → split the worst step; confirm with a second period.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Any multi-step journey: checkout, onboarding, approvals.
  • When a rate is lower than expected.
  • Before choosing which step to improve.
  • After a launch.

Skip it when…

  • Single-step actions.
  • Journeys with no events yet: write the tracking plan first.

8Self-check quiz

What does a funnel show that a single rate doesn't?

Where people drop out, step by step.

What should you do with the biggest drop?

Split it by segment, then investigate why.

What was Larder's biggest drop?

Seen → opened on Android: notifications were grouped and the swap hidden.

What did the fix achieve?

Answers within two minutes rose from 77 % to 82 %.

try it

Pick a journey with a disappointing rate. List its steps, count users at each for last month, and split the biggest drop by platform. Before discussing any fix, write down which step and which segment lose the most people.

remember!!

Count at every turn of the queue. Fix the biggest drop first.

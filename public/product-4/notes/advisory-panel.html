Customer Advisory Panel

TL;DR

A customer advisory panel is a small group (around a dozen) of real customers, varied on purpose, who agree to give feedback regularly: a session each quarter, short questions in between. Because they know your product and each other's context, you get depth and early reactions fast. Use it for understanding, not voting; rotate members so it doesn't become a fan club; and never let it be the only evidence for a decision.

  • Family: listening
  • Origin: customer advisory boards in B2B; community research panels
  • Lives in: a roster, a quarterly calendar and a notes folder
  • Pairs with: customer interviews, proto-personas, prototype testing

1The situation Larder, 21 September 2026

The trio wants early reactions to ideas like slot reminders and the live order timeline. The Thursday interviews give depth, but always with someone new who doesn't know what came before; recruiting each one takes a week; surveys are too shallow to explain anything. Every conversation starts from zero.

  • New interviewees need context every time.
  • Surveys say what, rarely why.
  • Early ideas need quick, informed reactions.

2Real-life analogy the regulars and the new menu

Before a new menu launches, a good restaurant invites a dozen regulars to try it, chosen from different tables: families, couples, the Friday business lunch. They know the place, so their reactions are quick and specific. Their reactions shape the menu; their votes don't decide it, because twelve regulars aren't all the guests.

3How it solves it small, varied, regular, not a vote

Grace and Noor recruited twelve customers, varied on purpose: four dinner parents, three Sunday planners, two allergy households, two office managers and one lapsed customer. A 90-minute session each quarter, short questions by message monthly, a thank-you voucher. The first session (21 September) reshaped the live timeline (“show the courier's name, not a map”) and cooled slot reminders (“I'd switch them off”). Both went to a test, not straight to the backlog.

  1. Recruit for variety

    Personas, segments, at least one lapsed or unhappy customer.

  2. Keep it small

    Ten to fifteen people; enough variety, few enough to know.

  3. Set a rhythm

    A quarterly session, short monthly questions.

  4. Thank them properly

    A voucher or credit, and tell them what changed.

  5. Use for depth

    Reactions and reasons; test anything important with more customers.

  6. Rotate yearly

    A third out each year so it doesn't become a fan club.

Vocabulary for the exam

Advisory panel
A standing group of customers giving regular feedback.
Recruitment
Choosing who joins, deliberately varied.
Rotation
Replacing some members each year.
Fan club effect
A panel growing too loyal to be critical.
Depth vs breadth
Why and how (panel) vs how many (surveys, data).
Incentive
A thank-you for members' time.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • Depth fast: people who know the product.
  • Early reactions before building.
  • Continuity: they remember what came before.
  • Varied voices on purpose, including critics.
  • Customers feel heard.

What we lose

  • Small and not representative.
  • Members grow loyal over time.
  • Running costs: time and vouchers.
  • Tempting to treat as a vote.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • A vote

    “8 of 12 want it, build it.” Fix → depth, not votes; test with more customers.

  • A fan club

    Only happy, loyal customers. Fix → include lapsed and critical members; rotate yearly.

  • The only evidence

    Panel opinion beats data. Fix → combine with data, tests and interviews.

  • No thank-you

    Members drift away. Fix → a voucher and a “what changed” note.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Products with a steady base of repeat customers.
  • Early reactions to ideas before tests.
  • Following how needs change over a year.
  • B2B: a few key clients' office managers.

Skip it when…

  • Questions needing numbers: use data or experiments.
  • One-off research on a new segment: recruit fresh.

8Self-check quiz

What is a panel good for?

Depth and early reactions from people who know the product.

What's it not for?

Voting, or being the only evidence for a decision.

How do you avoid a fan club?

Recruit for variety, include critics and lapsed customers, rotate a third yearly.

Who's on Larder's panel?

Twelve customers across personas, allergy households, office managers and one lapsed customer.

try it

Sketch a panel of ten for your product: which segments, at least one critic, and a thank-you. Invite three this month for a 60-minute session on one early idea, and note what they told you that data couldn't.

remember!!

Invite the regulars early. Their reactions shape it; they don't vote on it.

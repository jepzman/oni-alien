OKRs

TL;DR

OKRs pair an objective (where we want to be, in words people remember) with two to four key results (how we'll know, in numbers) for a quarter. Key results are outcomes, not things to ship; the features are bets underneath, free to change. Set them with the team, review them monthly, and grade honestly at the end: a 0.7 on an ambitious key result is a good quarter.

  • Family: goals
  • Origin: Andy Grove at Intel (1970s), John Doerr, Measure What Matters (2018)
  • Lives in: one page per team per quarter, next to the health page
  • Pairs with: outcome-based roadmaps, dashboards that get used, bets not promises

1The situation Larder, 29 June 2026

For Q3, Priya asks each team for goals. Grace's first draft looks like everyone else's: ship the three-minute window, ship live updates, launch slot reminders, migrate the payment screen. A list of outputs. None says what should change for customers, so at the end of September the team could ship all four and still not know whether the quarter went well.

  • Goals as features measure activity, not progress.
  • If a feature turns out not to help, the goal still says “ship it”.
  • Nobody can tell a good quarter from a busy one.

2Real-life analogy the expedition

A mountain expedition's objective is in words: reach the summit hut by Friday, safely. Its checks are numbers: 1,200 metres climbed by Wednesday, everyone fit at the morning check, no more than one rest day. If a path is blocked, the route changes; the objective and the checks don't.

3How it solves it an objective, a few numbers, bets underneath

Grace rewrote it with the trio: one objective, “every customer trusts their order”, and three key results: swap refund tickets 3.4 % → 2.5 %, “where is my order?” tickets −30 %, perfect orders 84 % → 86 %. The features became bets underneath; slot reminders and the payment screen dropped off, serving neither. Graded on 30 September: 0.8, 1.0, 0.75, a good quarter, and an honest one.

  1. Start from the strategy

    Which bet or outcome matters most this quarter?

  2. Write one objective

    In words people remember; ambitious, qualitative.

  3. Add two to four key results

    Outcomes with a start, a target and a date; no features.

  4. Put bets underneath

    The features and tests you'll try; free to change.

  5. Review monthly

    On the health page: on track, at risk, off track.

  6. Grade honestly

    0 to 1 per key result; 0.7 on a stretch is good.

Vocabulary for the exam

OKR
Objectives and key results.
Objective
Where we want to be, in words.
Key result
A measurable outcome that shows progress toward it.
Initiative / bet
Work we think will move a key result.
Grading
Scoring each key result 0 to 1 at the end.
Stretch goal
Ambitious enough that 0.7 counts as success.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • Outcomes over outputs, every quarter.
  • Freedom to change bets without changing goals.
  • Focus: work that serves no KR drops off.
  • Honest grading shows real progress.
  • Alignment with leadership in a page.

What we lose

  • Can become bureaucracy at scale.
  • Bad KRs (features, vanity) undo the point.
  • Gaming if grades affect pay.
  • Quarterly rhythm may not fit every team.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Features as KRs

    “Ship live updates.” Fix → the outcome it should move.

  • Too many

    Five objectives, fifteen KRs. Fix → one objective, two to four KRs.

  • Set and forget

    Opened again on 30 September. Fix → review monthly.

  • Grades as pay

    Sandbagged targets. Fix → keep grading separate from performance reviews.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Quarterly planning for product teams.
  • Aligning a team's work with a strategy.
  • When goals keep turning into feature lists.
  • Alongside outcome-based roadmaps.

Skip it when…

  • Very small teams with one clear goal (just track it).
  • Operational targets that don't change quarterly (use SLAs).

8Self-check quiz

What's the difference between an objective and a key result?

The objective says where, in words; key results say how we'll know, in numbers.

Where do features go?

Underneath, as bets; not as key results.

What happened to slot reminders?

Dropped: they served no key result.

Is 0.75 a bad grade?

No: on an ambitious key result, around 0.7 is a good quarter.

try it

Take your team's goals for next quarter. Rewrite them as one objective and three key results with a start, target and date; move every feature underneath as a bet. Drop anything that serves no key result, and book a monthly fifteen-minute review.

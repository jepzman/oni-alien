Guardrail Metrics

TL;DR

Every test is designed to move one number; most changes can also hurt others. Guardrail metrics are the few numbers that must not get worse while you chase the primary one: conversion, crashes, opt-outs, operational load, things another feature depends on. Pick them before the test, give each a limit, and treat a tripped guardrail as a no, even when the primary metric wins.

1The situation Larder, 24 August 2026

Live order updates are bet 3. The first version sends a push at every step: picked, packed, courier assigned, ten minutes away. In the test, “where is my order?” tickets fall 34 %, better than the 30 % target. Then Dee notices push opt-outs are up 0.9 points: customers are switching off notifications altogether, including the ones that ask them to approve swaps.

  • Tests are judged on the number they were built to move.
  • Side effects show up in other teams' numbers, later.
  • A win on one metric can quietly undo another feature.

2Real-life analogy the diet

A diet works: the scale shows four kilos lost in a month. But the doctor also checks blood pressure and energy, and both are worse. Weight was the goal; the others are guardrails. A diet that breaks them isn't a good diet, however good the scale looks.

3How it solves it a primary metric and limits that mean no

The test card had three guardrails with limits: opt-outs ≤ +0.5 points, conversion not down, approvals answered not down. Round one tripped the first, so it wasn't shipped, despite the win. Noor redesigned it: two pushes only (a swap, and ten minutes away) plus an in-app timeline. Round two: tickets −31 %, opt-outs +0.2. Shipped on 21 September.

  1. List what could get worse

    Other features, operations, revenue, trust, performance.

  2. Pick two or three guardrails

    The ones that matter most and can be measured in the test.

  3. Set a limit for each

    “Opt-outs ≤ +0.5 points”: a number, before the test.

  4. Write the rule

    Any guardrail tripped → don't ship as is.

  5. Watch them during the test

    Alert if one crosses its limit early.

  6. Redesign, not override

    Find a version that wins without breaking the guardrail.

Vocabulary for the exam

Guardrail metric
A number that must not get worse during a change.
Primary metric
The number the change is meant to improve.
Limit
The worst acceptable value for a guardrail.
Opt-out rate
The share of users switching off notifications.
Side effect
An unintended change in another number.
OEC
Overall evaluation criterion: the combined measure of success.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • Side effects caught before shipping.
  • Other features protected (approvals).
  • Better designs: a win that doesn't break things.
  • Trust from other teams.
  • Clear “no” rules, set in advance.

What we lose

  • More metrics to watch.
  • Some wins are blocked.
  • Picking guardrails takes judgement.
  • Bigger samples to detect small guardrail moves.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • No guardrails

    A win on one number, damage elsewhere. Fix → two or three guardrails on every test card.

  • Limits after the fact

    “+0.9 isn't that bad.” Fix → write the limit before the test.

  • Overriding a trip

    Shipped anyway, it won. Fix → redesign until it wins without tripping.

  • Too many guardrails

    Fifteen numbers; something always moves. Fix → the two or three that matter most.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Every A/B test.
  • Changes to notifications, pricing, checkout, operations.
  • Features that other features depend on.
  • Rollouts (as in dark launches).

Skip it when…

  • Purely cosmetic changes with no plausible side effects (rare).

8Self-check quiz

What's a guardrail metric?

A number that must not get worse while you improve the primary one.

When should limits be set?

Before the test starts, on the test card.

What happened in round one?

Tickets fell 34 %, but opt-outs rose 0.9 points (limit 0.5), so it wasn't shipped.

How did round two pass?

Two pushes plus an in-app timeline: tickets −31 %, opt-outs +0.2.

try it

For your next test, list everything the change could make worse. Pick the two that matter most, write a limit for each, and add the rule “any guardrail tripped means we don't ship as is”.

remember!!

Weigh yourself, and check the blood pressure. A win that breaks a guardrail isn't a win.

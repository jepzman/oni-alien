Who Decides to Invoke the Plan?

TL;DR

A continuity plan is useless if nobody feels allowed to start it. Name one person who decides, and deputies in order for when they can't be reached. Write the triggers that force the question, and set a decision clock: asked at a fixed point in a major incident, decided by another, logged either way. Then practise the decision itself, not only the technology.

1The situation Larder, Thursday 13 April 2028

Game day 2 is a tabletop exercise: “The Frankfurt region is down. It's Friday 17:30. Nobody knows for how long.” Ana's ADR 0026 says a pilot-light copy in Paris can be running in about 4 hours, at a cost, if the region is out for more than an hour. The group spends 40 minutes discussing who is allowed to start it. In the scenario Femke is on a plane, and nobody else is sure they may spend the money or tell the stores to go to paper.

  • Plans describe how to switch, not who decides to.
  • Every minute of “who's allowed?” is a minute of the four hours.
  • The people who'd advise (legal, customers) aren't named.

2Real-life analogy the captain and the first officer

On a ship, the decision to abandon ship belongs to the captain. If the captain can't make it, the first officer decides, by rule. The signs that force the question are written down, and there's a time by which it must be decided. Nobody holds a meeting about who's in charge while the water rises.

3How it solves it one decider, deputies, triggers, and a decision clock

After the tabletop, Eli writes the crisis team page with Femke:

  • Decides: Femke. If she can't be reached within 15 minutes: Marco, then Joost. Each has agreed in writing.
  • Advises: Eli (IT), Joost (operations), Sam (customers), Lena (legal), Mo (business clients).
  • Triggers: a region outage beyond an hour or with no estimate; a city unable to operate for 2 hours; a suspected data breach; a key vendor out for a business day.
  • Decision clock: at 30 minutes of a major incident with a trigger, the incident lead asks “should we invoke?”, and the answer is decided by 60 minutes and written in the decision log, yes or no.
  • Invoking means: start the pilot light, declare paper mode in the affected stores, switch customer messages to the continuity templates, and tell Korenwolf through Mo.

The tabletop is run again on 11 May. The decision takes 12 minutes, with Marco deciding because “Femke” is again on a plane.

  1. Name one decider

    And agree it with them.

  2. Name deputies in order

    With a time to try the decider first.

  3. Write the triggers

    The signs that force the question.

  4. Set a decision clock

    Asked at a fixed point, decided by another.

  5. Say what invoking means

    Every action that follows, per team.

  6. Practise the decision

    A tabletop with the real people.

Vocabulary for the exam

Invocation
The decision to switch to a continuity plan.
Crisis team
The small group that decides and coordinates in a serious disruption.
Deputy
The person who decides if the decider can't.
Trigger
A condition that forces the invocation question.
Decision clock
Fixed times for asking and deciding.
Pilot light
A minimal copy of the system in another region, ready to scale up.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Only the technology practised

    The switch works; nobody starts it. Fix → practise the decision.

  • A single decider

    Unreachable on the day. Fix → deputies in order.

  • No triggers

    The question is never asked. Fix → written triggers and a clock.

  • Invoking means only IT

    Stores and clients aren't told. Fix → list every action that follows.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • You have a continuity plan (a pilot light, paper mode).
  • A tabletop stalled on “who decides?”.
  • Leaders travel or are often unreachable.
  • Before a peak or a contract with continuity terms.

Skip it when…

  • When there's nothing to invoke: build the plan first.

8Self-check quiz

Who decides to invoke at Larder?

Femke; if not reached in 15 minutes, Marco, then Joost.

What are the triggers?

Region outage beyond an hour or no estimate, a city stopped 2 hours, a suspected breach, a key vendor out a business day.

What is the decision clock?

At 30 minutes of a major incident with a trigger, the lead asks; decided by 60 minutes, logged.

What does invoking set in motion?

The Paris pilot light, paper mode in stores, continuity messages, Mo telling Korenwolf, Lena on notifications.

What changed between the two tabletops?

40 minutes on “who decides?” → a decision in 12 minutes.

what would you do?A region has been down 50 minutes with an estimate of “30 more minutes”. Femke says wait. Is that allowed?

Yes: deciding not to invoke is a decision, logged with the reason. The clock forces a decision, not a particular one; she can be asked again if the estimate slips.

try it

For your worst plausible outage, write who decides to switch to the fallback, who decides if they can't, what forces the question, and by when. Run a 30-minute tabletop with those people.

remember!!

A plan nobody may start is no plan. Name who decides, who deputises, and by when, and practise the decision.

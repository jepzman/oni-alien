When the Incident Is a Security Incident

TL;DR

A security incident uses the normal incident process plus four things: contain without destroying evidence (disable, don't delete; isolate, don't wipe), preserve the logs, bring in legal early because the law may set deadlines (personal data breaches: 72 hours to the regulator in the EU), and assess exactly what was touched before deciding who to tell. Then fix the weakness, without blaming the person who was tricked.

1The situation Larder, Thursday 9 March 2028

At 09:40 the impossible-travel alert fires for a support agent's account: signed in from Amsterdam at 08:55, and from abroad at 09:31. After a phishing email, the agent had approved a sign-in prompt on their phone by habit. Bram's first instinct, from every other incident, is to reset the password and restart things. That would destroy the evidence of what the attacker did, and Larder would need that evidence to decide what to tell the regulator and its customers.

  • The normal incident reflex (restore fast) can erase what you need to know.
  • Legal deadlines start when you become aware, not when you finish.
  • Blaming the agent would make the next person hide a mistake.

2Real-life analogy the shop after a burglary

After a burglary, a shop owner's instinct is to tidy up and get trading. The police say otherwise: secure the broken door so nobody else gets in, touch nothing, write down what's missing, and call the insurer today, because there's a deadline. Tidying up comes last.

3How it solves it contain, preserve, legal, assess, notify, fix

Eli takes the lead at 09:42 and follows the incident roles, with four security steps added:

  • Contain without destroying (09:44): disable the account and revoke its sessions. Don't delete it.
  • Preserve (09:50): export the account's sign-in, helpdesk and portal logs to a locked evidence folder.
  • Legal early (10:05): Lena joins and owns the legal assessment.
  • Assess exactly (11:30): in 18 minutes the attacker viewed 37 customer orders (names, addresses, phone numbers) and exported nothing. No other accounts were touched.

Lena decides it's a personal data breach to notify. The Dutch data protection authority is told on Friday 10 March, within 72 hours, and the 37 customers on 13 March, with Sam's team ready for calls. The fix is aimed at the weakness: number-matching sign-in prompts for everyone, and passkeys for support and admin accounts by May. The review is blameless. The weakness was a prompt that could be approved by habit, not the agent who approved it.

  1. Declare it as a security incident

    Normal roles, plus a security note.

  2. Contain without destroying

    Disable, revoke, isolate; don't delete or wipe.

  3. Preserve the evidence

    Export and lock the logs early.

  4. Bring in legal at once

    Deadlines start at awareness.

  5. Assess exactly what was touched

    Whose data, which fields, how long.

  6. Notify on time, then fix the weakness

    Blameless review; fix the design.

Vocabulary for the exam

Security incident
An event that threatens confidentiality, integrity or availability through misuse or attack.
Containment
Stopping the attacker's access without destroying evidence.
Evidence preservation
Keeping logs and systems as they were, for investigation.
Personal data breach
Personal data accessed, lost or changed without authorisation.
72-hour rule
Under the GDPR, notify the supervisory authority within 72 hours of becoming aware, where required.
Phishing
Tricking someone into giving access, often by email or prompts.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Wiping before looking

    No way to know what happened. Fix → contain, then preserve, then fix.

  • Legal last

    Deadlines missed. Fix → legal at once.

  • Guessing the scope

    Over- or under-notifying. Fix → assess exactly from the logs.

  • Blaming the person

    The next one hides it. Fix → fix the design; blameless review.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • An account, device or key may be in the wrong hands.
  • Customer data may have been seen or taken.
  • A security alert looks real.
  • Planning game days (add a security scenario).

Skip it when…

  • Ordinary outages with no sign of misuse: the normal process is enough.

8Self-check quiz

What's different about containment in a security incident?

Disable and revoke, but don't delete or wipe: evidence must be kept.

Why bring legal in at once?

Deadlines (like the GDPR's 72 hours) start when you become aware.

What did the attacker do on 9 March?

Viewed 37 customer orders in 18 minutes; exported nothing.

Who was notified, and when?

The data protection authority on 10 March (within 72 hours), the 37 customers on 13 March.

What was fixed?

Number-matching sign-in prompts for everyone and passkeys for support and admin accounts.

what would you do?A developer's laptop is stolen from a car. It had production database credentials in a file. What are the first steps?

Declare a security incident: rotate the credentials at once (contain), check the database logs for use since the theft (preserve and assess), bring in Lena, and decide on notification from what the logs show.

try it

Write the first six steps your team would take if a staff account were taken over this morning. Who decides about notifying the regulator, and how would they know the deadline? What would you be careful not to delete?

remember!!

When the Picker App Is Down

TL;DR

When a system the front line depends on is down longer than its offline mode lasts, people need a practised way to keep working without it. Prepare the information they'll need in advance (lists saved offline, refreshed often), say who declares the switch and when, keep the steps on paper in the store, plan the switch back, and drill it so it's a routine rather than an improvisation.

1The situation Larder, January 2028

At the store leads' meeting after the restore, Eli asks: if the picker app's back end were down for three hours on a Friday, what would the stores do? The picker app works offline for about two hours. After that, nobody knows. Tomás remembers a paper process from 2024 that nobody has used since. Tier 2 says the picker back end must be back within an hour. Reality says “we'd improvise”.

  • Systems have recovery plans; the people using them don't.
  • The information needed on paper (what to pick) is inside the broken system.
  • Nobody knows who decides to stop waiting and switch.

2Real-life analogy paper charts in the hospital

When a hospital's computers go down, every ward has paper charts in a drawer and a printed list of today's patients and their medicines, refreshed every few hours. A nurse who has practised it leads the switch. When the system comes back, someone types the paper in. It's slower, and it's safe.

3How it solves it information saved in advance, one person declares, paper steps, a switch back, drills

Eli, Tomás and Dee design the store fallback in February:

  • Information in advance: every 15 minutes the store laptop saves the next two hours of pick lists as printable files, kept offline, so the lists exist even if the cloud doesn't.
  • One person declares: if the picker app is down for more than 30 minutes, or offline mode is about to run out, the store lead declares “paper”.
  • Paper steps, in a printed binder at the packing bench: print the lists, pick and tick on paper, write substitutions by hand, run Dispatch by phone from a printed courier list. Sam's team tells affected customers.
  • Switch back: when the app returns, each paper order is scanned, and a reconciliation list shows what's left.

The first drill is at Zuid, Wednesday 16 February, 06:00, for 45 minutes. Picking runs at 60 % of normal speed with 3 errors per 100 orders. Two fixes come out of it: bigger print, and swaps written on a sticker, not in the margin. Every store now drills twice a year.

  1. Ask what people do when the system is down for longer than offline mode

    At the front line, per role.

  2. Save the information they'll need in advance

    Offline, refreshed often.

  3. Name who declares the switch, and when

    A person and a trigger.

  4. Put the steps on paper where the work is

    Not in the cloud.

  5. Plan the switch back

    Reconcile what was done on paper.

  6. Drill it

    Twice a year per site.

Vocabulary for the exam

Manual fallback
A way to keep working by hand when a system is down.
Business continuity
Keeping the business running through disruption, not just restoring IT.
Offline mode
An app working without its back end for a while.
Declaring the switch
One named person deciding to move to the fallback.
Reconciliation
Matching what was done by hand with the system afterwards.
Drill
A planned practice of the fallback.

  when    picker app down > 30 min, or offline mode about to run out (≈ 2 h)
  1       say "paper" in the store leads' group + call on-call
  2       laptop → folder "pick lists (offline)" → print the next 2 hours

  5       Sam's team tells customers (on-call tells Sam)
  back    when the app returns: scan each paper order (barcode at the top)

  drills  twice a year · Zuid 16 Feb 2028: 45 min, 60 % speed, 3 errors / 100

tools: a scheduled export to the store laptop, a printer, a binder, yellow stickers and a drill date.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Information trapped in the system

    No lists to print. Fix → save them offline in advance.

  • Nobody declares

    Everyone waits for the system. Fix → a named person and a trigger.

  • No way back

    Paper orders lost in the switch. Fix → scan and reconcile.

  • Never drilled

    The binder is wrong when it matters. Fix → twice a year, fix after each.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Front-line work stops when a system does.
  • Offline modes have limits.
  • Recovery targets can't cover every case.
  • New stores or sites open.

Skip it when…

  • Work that can simply wait a few hours.

8Self-check quiz

What does the store lead do when the picker app is down for more than 30 minutes?

Declares “paper”: prints the saved lists and switches to the paper steps.

Why save pick lists offline every 15 minutes?

So the information needed on paper exists even when the system that holds it is down.

How do paper orders get back into the system?

Each is scanned when the app returns; a reconciliation list shows what's left.

What did Zuid's drill show?

45 minutes at 60 % speed, 3 errors per 100; bigger print and swap stickers added.

How often do stores drill?

Twice a year.

what would you do?At 18:10 on a Friday the picker app has been down 25 minutes and IT expects a fix “soon”. Does Yara declare paper?

Not yet: the trigger is 30 minutes. At 18:15, if it's still down and “soon” has no time attached, she declares paper. Switching back early costs little; waiting too long costs orders.

try it

Pick a front-line role in your organisation. If their main system were down for three hours, what would they do? What information would they need on paper, and is it available now, offline?

remember!!

Systems get recovery plans; people need fallbacks. Save the lists in advance, name who declares, and drill it.

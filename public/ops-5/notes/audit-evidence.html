Prove It to the Auditor

TL;DR

Audits and client questionnaires don't ask what you do; they ask how you know you did it. If evidence is collected as a by-product of the work (the change feed, restore logs, access review files, patch reports, incident reviews), answering takes days and every answer has a link. If not, it takes weeks of reconstruction and some answers can't be proved. Design practices so the proof falls out of doing them.

1The situation Larder, February 2028

On Monday 14 February Korenwolf sends its supplier questionnaire for the renewed contract: 186 questions. How are changes authorised? How often are backups restored? Who reviews access, and how often? How fast are critical vulnerabilities fixed? How are incidents reviewed? “Describe” is easy. The hard part is “show evidence”. A year earlier this would have meant weeks of digging through chats and memory, with answers nobody could prove.

  • Evidence collected for an audit is slow, partial and doubtful.
  • Clients increasingly ask for proof, not policies.
  • The same questions come back every year, from every client.
meanwhile, in Sanne’s notebook
  • What Does It Leave Behind? (Sanne, vol. 6): Sanne's sustainability work answered the questionnaire's environmental part on 13 March 2028, with Ana's platform carbon figures.

2Real-life analogy the kitchen inspection

A good restaurant kitchen doesn't prepare for the food-safety inspector. The fridge temperatures are written on a sheet twice a day, the cleaning rota is signed, and the delivery notes are filed. When the inspector comes, the evidence is already on the wall, because it's part of how the kitchen works.

3How it solves it evidence that falls out of the work, linked from one folder

Most of Larder's practices since 2026 already leave a trail, so Eli builds ops/evidence/ as a set of links, not copies:

  • Changes: the change feed, and pull requests with risk scores and reviewers.
  • Restores: the monthly restore test log with times.
  • Access: the quarterly access review files.
  • Patching: nightly and monthly patch reports, and the 72-hour critical record.
  • Incidents: reviews and the action list. Vendors: scorecards. Security: the monitoring rules and the 9 March record. Continuity: plans with last-tested dates.

The 186 answers take 6 working days, each with a link to its evidence. Lena reviews them, Ana answers the carbon questions, and they're sent on Monday 13 March. Ruben comes back with only 4 follow-up questions. The questionnaire also does Larder a favour by finding two gaps: exit plans for vendors and a plan for losing office sign-in. Both are the next notes.

  1. List what auditors and clients ask every year

    Changes, backups, access, patching, incidents, vendors.

  2. For each, find where the evidence already falls out

    Logs, feeds, reviews.

  3. Fill the gaps by changing the practice

    So doing it leaves proof.

  4. Link it from one folder

    Links, not copies.

  5. Answer with links, reviewed by legal

    Not essays.

  6. Turn every unprovable answer into an action

    Gaps found are gifts.

Vocabulary for the exam

Evidence
A record showing a control or practice actually happened.
Control
A practice that reduces a risk (access review, change approval).
Supplier questionnaire
A client's list of questions about how you run services.
Audit trail
A chronological record of actions.
By-product evidence
Proof created by doing the work, not prepared for an audit.
Gap
A question you can't answer with evidence.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Preparing evidence for the audit

    Slow, and looks like it. Fix → change practices so proof falls out.

  • Copies instead of links

    Out of date the day after. Fix → link to the live records.

  • Answering with policy prose

    “We take security seriously.” Fix → a link to evidence.

  • Hiding gaps

    Found later, by the client. Fix → say so, and make them actions.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Clients or auditors send questionnaires.
  • Renewals depend on trust.
  • You answer the same questions every year.
  • After building new practices: check they leave a trail.

Skip it when…

  • Nobody ever asks: but someone will.

8Self-check quiz

What do audits really ask?

How you know you did it: evidence, not descriptions.

What is by-product evidence?

Proof created by doing the work (logs, feeds, review files), not prepared for an audit.

How long did the 186 answers take?

6 working days, each with a link, reviewed by Lena.

Why links, not copies?

Links stay current; copies are out of date the next day.

What gaps did the questionnaire find?

Vendor exit plans and a plan for losing office sign-in.

what would you do?A question asks “How do you ensure leavers lose access on their last day?” and you know agency workers were missed until February. How do you answer?

Honestly, with evidence: the process, the Q1 review finding (9 agency accounts), the fix (agency workers on HR's form from 1 February) and the Q2 result. Clients trust an honest gap with a fix more than a perfect claim.

try it

Take the last questionnaire or audit you answered. For each answer, could you link to evidence created by the work itself? Pick the weakest one and change the practice so it leaves proof.

remember!!

Don't prepare for the auditor. Make the proof fall out of the work, link it from one place, and turn every gap into an action.

When the Office Is the Outage

TL;DR

Sign-in, email, the office network and laptops feel like background, until they fail and the people who serve customers can't work. Treat them as services: put them in the catalogue with a tier, and plan for losing each one. That means break-glass accounts for the tools support can't be without, cached sign-in on laptops, a way to reach staff outside your normal tools, and a backup network, all tested.

1The situation Larder, Tuesday 16 May 2028

At 08:30 the sign-in provider has an outage that lasts until 10:40. The stores keep working: the handhelds use device certificates. But nobody in the office can sign in to anything: email, the helpdesk, the cloud console, the portal's admin. Sam's support team can't open the helpdesk for 2 hours 10 minutes while about 210 customer chats queue up. Korenwolf's questionnaire had asked about this in March, and it was one of the two gaps.

  • The office wasn't in the catalogue, so it had no tier and no plan.
  • The production break-glass account didn't help support.
  • There was no way to tell office staff what was happening, because every channel needed sign-in.

2Real-life analogy the hospital's dark admin office

A hospital puts its operating theatres on backup generators and leaves the admin office on the mains. When the power fails, surgery continues, but nobody can admit patients, phone families or find the rota. The quiet back office turns out to be on the critical path too.

3How it solves it the office as a service, with its own plan

In the week after, Sam and Eli write the office continuity plan:

  • In the catalogue: sign-in, email, the office network and laptops become services with owners. Support tooling moves to tier 2, because an hour without it costs customers.
  • Break-glass: two sealed local accounts for the helpdesk and two for the cloud console, with the same alarm as production break-glass.
  • Cached sign-in: laptops keep a cached sign-in for 7 days, so they can unlock without the provider.
  • Reaching people: a text-message group for all office staff, outside the sign-in provider, used for status updates.
  • Network: two 4G hotspots in the office, and a printed sheet of contacts and steps.

The plan becomes the fifth continuity plan. Its first test was the real outage. Its second is a game day on 8 June, with the sign-in provider blocked for the support team on purpose: they're back in the helpdesk in 6 minutes.

  1. Put workplace services in the catalogue

    Sign-in, email, network, laptops.

  2. Tier them by what an hour costs

    Support tooling may be tier 2.

  3. Give critical tools a break-glass path

    Sealed, alarmed, tested.

  4. Make devices work offline for a while

    Cached sign-in.

  5. Have a channel outside your own tools

    To tell staff what's happening.

  6. Test it with a game day

    Block the dependency on purpose.

Vocabulary for the exam

Workplace services
Sign-in, email, network, laptops: the tools staff need to work.
Identity provider
The service that signs people in to everything else.
Single sign-on dependency
When one sign-in service failing blocks every tool.
Cached credentials
A device remembering a recent sign-in, to work offline.
Out-of-band channel
A way to communicate that doesn't depend on the failing systems.
Break-glass (workplace)
An emergency account for a critical tool, outside normal sign-in.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Only planning for production

    The people who respond can't work. Fix → the office is a service too.

  • Break-glass only for production

    Support stays locked out. Fix → break-glass for critical support tools.

  • Communicating through the failed tool

    Status updates by email during an email outage. Fix → an out-of-band channel.

  • Never testing

    Cached sign-in turned off by a policy change. Fix → a game day.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Everyone signs in through one provider.
  • Support or operations work from an office.
  • A questionnaire asks about workplace continuity.
  • After any outage of email or sign-in.

Skip it when…

  • Fully separate tools with separate sign-in (rare today).

8Self-check quiz

Why did the stores keep working on 16 May?

The handhelds sign in with device certificates, not the sign-in provider.

What couldn't support do, and for how long?

Open the helpdesk, for 2 hours 10 minutes.

What's an out-of-band channel?

A way to reach people that doesn't depend on the failing systems (an SMS group).

What changed in the catalogue?

Sign-in, email, network and laptops added; support tooling moved to tier 2.

What did the 8 June game day show?

Support back in the helpdesk in 6 minutes using break-glass.

what would you do?The office's internet line fails at 09:00 on a Monday. What happens under the plan?

Sam declares it after 15 minutes, the SMS group gets a status, the two 4G hotspots go on for support first, and the line provider is called from the printed sheet.

try it

Imagine your sign-in provider is down for two hours. Which of your teams can still work? How would you tell them what's happening? Write the three steps that would get your support team working again.

remember!!

The office is a service too. Plan for losing sign-in and email, and keep one channel outside your own tools.

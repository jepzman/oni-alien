Who Still Has Access?

TL;DR

Joiners-leavers processes remove access when they see someone leave. An access review catches what they miss: agency workers, role changes, exceptions that were never ended. Every quarter, each tool's owner confirms who has access and why, against a staff list that includes everyone (contractors and agency staff too). Remove the rest that week, review privileged roles twice, keep the evidence, and fix the cause of every miss.

1The situation Larder, January 2028

In May 2027 Lena's review for Ana's identity work found a support leaver who kept helpdesk and billing access for three weeks. Eli runs Larder's first full access review in January 2028: 14 tools, 412 active accounts. 23 shouldn't exist. 9 of them belong to agency pickers who had left. Agency workers were never on HR's hiring form, so the leavers process never saw them leave.

  • A process can only remove access it knew about.
  • Role changes add access more often than they remove it.
  • Nobody owns the question “is this list still right?”

2Real-life analogy the sports club's key fobs

Once a season, a sports club's treasurer sends each team captain the list of key fobs for their changing room. Captains tick who's still playing, and the rest are switched off. But the fobs the bar handed to guest players at the summer tournament were never on any team's list, so they still open the door.

3How it solves it each owner confirms quarterly, and every miss fixes the process

From January, every quarter:

  • Each of the 14 tools' owners gets the list of accounts and roles and compares it with a staff list that now includes agency and contract workers.
  • They mark each account keep, change or remove within two weeks. Privileged roles are reviewed by two people.
  • Removals happen that week and are logged. The review's evidence (who reviewed, what changed) is kept for auditors.

Every miss gets its cause fixed. Agency and contract workers now go onto HR's form, entered by store managers, with an end date. Accounts without an end date or owner are flagged monthly. Q1 removes 23 accounts and reduces 31 roles. Q2 (April) removes only 6: the process is catching more on its own. The review checks the process; the process does the daily work.

  1. List every tool and its owner

    From the catalogue and the asset register.

  2. Build a complete staff list

    Including agency and contract workers.

  3. Send each owner their list quarterly

    Keep, change or remove in two weeks.

  4. Review privileged roles twice

    Two people.

  5. Remove that week; keep evidence

    Logged.

  6. Fix the cause of each miss

    So the next review finds less.

Vocabulary for the exam

Access review
A regular check by owners that every account and role is still needed.
Recertification
Another name for confirming access is still valid.
Privileged role
Access that can change systems or see sensitive data.
Access creep
People collecting access over time as roles change.
End date
When a temporary worker's access should stop.
Evidence
The record that a review happened and what it changed.

4The maturity ladder where are we, and what is the next rung?

1By hand

nobody checks

  • Access collected for years.
  • Leavers missed silently.

2Written down Larder now

  • Privileged roles twice.

reviews from the identity provider

  • Lists generated; owners click keep or remove.

  • Temporary access ends by itself.
  • Reviews find almost nothing.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Reviewing without fixing the cause

    The same misses every quarter. Fix → fix the process for each miss.

  • A staff list without temporary workers

    Agency staff never appear. Fix → everyone who gets an account.

  • Rubber-stamping

    Owners tick “keep” for everything. Fix → two reviewers for privileged roles; spot checks.

  • No evidence kept

    Auditors can't see it happened. Fix → keep who reviewed what, and what changed.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • You've found access for someone who left.
  • Temporary or agency workers get accounts.
  • Clients or auditors ask about access control.
  • Before a security questionnaire.

Skip it when…

  • Tools with access that expires on its own and is checked automatically.

8Self-check quiz

What does an access review catch that joiners-leavers doesn't?

Accounts the process never saw: agency workers, role changes, unended exceptions.

Who reviews each tool's list?

Its owner; privileged roles by two people.

Why did 9 agency pickers still have access?

Agency workers were never on HR's form, so the leavers process never saw them leave.

How was the cause fixed?

Store managers add agency and contract workers to HR's form with an end date.

What did Q2 show?

Only 6 removals: the process catches more on its own.

what would you do?A tool owner marks all 61 accounts “keep” in five minutes. What do you do?

Spot-check a few against the staff list; if any are wrong, ask the owner to redo it properly, and consider a second reviewer for that tool. A review that changes nothing for a growing team is a warning sign.

try it

Export the accounts from one important tool and compare them with a complete staff list, including temporary workers. How many shouldn't exist? Why did your process miss them?

remember!!

The leavers process removes what it saw join. A quarterly review by each owner catches the rest, and fixes the process.

Which Risks Keep Us Up at Night?

TL;DR

An operational risk register writes down what could go wrong before it does: each risk with a cause, a likelihood and impact, an owner, actions with dates, and a trigger that would make it urgent. Review it monthly in a few minutes and send the top few to leadership. Include the uncomfortable risks, especially the ones about people, because those are usually the biggest.

1The situation Larder, March 2028

In March Eli writes down, for the first time, the risks he carries in his head: a region outage, another phishing attack, the old handhelds' batteries failing at the peak, the billing vendor, DNS. And the one he finds hardest to write: operations depends on two people, Eli and Bram. Each had been discussed in a corridor at least once. None had an owner, a score or a next step.

  • Risks nobody owns turn into incidents somebody has to handle.
  • The scariest risks (people) are the least likely to be written down.
  • Leadership can't fund what it can't see.
meanwhile, in Ana’s notebook
  • Risk Register (Ana, vol. 6): Ana's architecture risk register of 24 April 2028: twelve risks, number 1 “architecture depends on Ana” (4 × 4 → 2 × 4 by September).

2Real-life analogy the farmer's list on the kitchen wall

A farmer keeps a list on the kitchen wall: the barn roof before winter, the old tractor, a dry summer, the farmhand leaving. Each has how likely and how bad, what she's doing about it, and the sign that would make her act sooner (the first leak). She looks at it on the first of every month. The farmhand line is the one she most wanted to leave off.

3How it solves it cause, score, owner, actions, trigger; monthly; top five up

Eli writes 14 risks, each with a cause, likelihood and impact (1–5 each), an owner, actions with dates, and a trigger. The top five:

  • R1 Operations depends on two people (4 × 4, Eli): hire two SREs; runbooks for the last undocumented systems. That becomes the reliability team (vol. 6).
  • R2 Phishing (4 × 3, Eli): passkeys for support and admin accounts by May.
  • R3 Old handhelds' batteries at the peak (4 × 2, Dee): replace HH-2024 by September.
  • R4 Billing vendor (3 × 3, Cho): exit plan and export test before June's renewal.
  • R5 Region outage (2 × 5, Eli): pilot light (ADR 0026) and invocation rules.

The register is reviewed monthly in 15 minutes, with the top five going to Femke alongside the incident page. Near misses and game-day findings feed it. By June, R1 is 3 × 4 (two SREs hired) and R2 2 × 3 (passkeys live).

  1. Write the risks you carry in your head

    Including the uncomfortable ones.

  2. Give each a cause, a score and an owner

    Likelihood × impact, 1–5.

  3. Add actions with dates

    And who does them.

  4. Add a trigger

    The sign that makes it urgent.

  5. Review monthly, briefly

    Top five to leadership.

  6. Feed it from near misses and game days

    Evidence, not guesses.

Vocabulary for the exam

Risk
Something that might happen and would affect what matters.
Likelihood × impact
A simple score for ranking risks.
Risk owner
The person accountable for managing a risk.
Treatment
What you do: reduce, transfer, avoid or accept.
Trigger
A sign that a risk is about to become an issue.
Key-person risk
Too much depending on one or two people.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Leaving out people risks

    The biggest risk isn't on the list. Fix → write it, however uncomfortable.

  • No owner

    Everyone agrees; nothing happens. Fix → one owner per risk.

  • Scores without actions

    A ranked worry list. Fix → actions with dates.

  • Never reviewed

    Stale by summer. Fix → monthly, 15 minutes.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • You carry risks in your head.
  • Leadership asks what could go wrong.
  • Planning a budget or a team.
  • After near misses or game days.

Skip it when…

  • Very small, stable setups: a short list in the service review may do.

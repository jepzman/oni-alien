What Does It Cost to Run?

TL;DR

A cloud bill is just a number until it's split by service and owner. Tag everything, show each owner their service's bill and its cost per order on the monthly review, and hold a short monthly waste hunt for the usual suspects: environments running when nobody uses them, forgotten snapshots, things sized for a peak that's over. Most waste is ordinary, and it comes back unless someone looks every month.

1The situation Larder, January 2028

In January the cloud bill is €78,000 a month, and cost per order is €3.05 against Femke's target of €2.80. Marco asks which services cost what. Eli can't say: 61 % of the spending carries no tag for a service or an owner. Staging runs all night and every weekend, old snapshots pile up, and the browse replica sized for Christmas is still at Christmas size in January.

  • Nobody sees the bill for the thing they own.
  • Peak sizing never gets undone.
  • Savings depend on someone remembering to look.
meanwhile, in Ana’s notebook
  • Cost-Aware Architecture (Ana, vol. 5): Ana's cost work of 21 February 2028: cost per order €3.05 at the end of 2027; cloud bill €78k → €58k a month; cloud cost per order ≈ 9.6 → 7.1 cents.

2Real-life analogy the electricity bill by meter

One household electricity bill says nothing useful. With a meter per thing (the heat pump, the car charger, the rest of the house), the family notices that the car charges at the peak price, and the heat pump runs when nobody's home. The total never told them that. Someone still has to read the meters every month.

3How it solves it tags, a bill per owner, a monthly waste hunt

From February:

  • Tags: every resource carries its service and owner. Untagged spending falls from 61 % to 2 %, and the pipeline now refuses to create untagged resources.
  • A bill per owner: each service's monthly cost, and its cost per 1,000 orders, goes on its monthly service review, so Ben sees ordering's bill next to its SLOs.
  • A monthly waste hunt: 30 minutes, Eli and Bram, looking at the usual suspects:
    • Staging off at night and at weekends: €4,100 a month.
    • Old snapshots and unattached disks: €2,600.
    • The browse replica back to normal size after Christmas: €1,900.
    • Log retention matched to what's actually needed: €800.

Operations' share is about €9,400 a month. With Ana's architecture changes, the bill goes from €78k to €58k a month, and cost per order moves towards €2.80 for the first time.

  1. Tag every resource with service and owner

    Refuse untagged resources in the pipeline.

  2. Show each owner their bill

    Next to their SLOs, monthly.

  3. Use a unit cost

    Cost per 1,000 orders, not just euros.

  4. Hunt waste monthly

    Idle environments, snapshots, peak sizes, retention.

  5. Undo peak sizing after the peak

    Put it on the calendar.

  6. Count savings honestly

    And say who made them.

Vocabulary for the exam

Service financial management
Knowing and managing what services cost, and their value.
Tagging
Labelling resources so costs can be traced to services and owners.
Unit cost
Cost per unit of business (per order), not just in total.
Showback
Showing teams their costs without charging them.
Waste
Spending that buys nothing: idle, forgotten or oversized resources.
Right-sizing
Matching a resource's size to what it actually needs.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Untagged resources

    Costs nobody owns. Fix → the pipeline refuses them.

  • Bills only to finance

    Owners never see them. Fix → on each service's review.

  • Peak sizing forgotten

    Christmas paid for until Easter. Fix → undo it on a calendar date.

  • Savings once

    Waste creeps back. Fix → hunt monthly.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Nobody can say what a service costs.
  • Cost per order matters to the business.
  • After a peak.
  • Before a budget round.

Skip it when…

  • Tiny bills where the time costs more than the savings.

8Self-check quiz

Why couldn't Eli answer Marco in January?

61 % of cloud spending had no service or owner tag.

What's a unit cost, and Larder's?

Cost per unit of business; here, cost per 1,000 orders per service.

What did the first waste hunt find?

Staging running nights and weekends, old snapshots and disks, a Christmas-sized replica, over-long log retention.

How much did operations save?

About €9,400 a month; with Ana's changes the bill fell from €78k to €58k.

How are untagged resources prevented?

The pipeline refuses to create them.

what would you do?Ben asks why ordering's cost per 1,000 orders went up 15 % in March. What do you look at?

The service's tagged costs by resource for February vs March: a new resource, a bigger size, more logs, or fewer orders. Then decide together whether it's waste or value.

try it

Find out what share of your cloud bill has no owner tag. Pick one service and work out its monthly cost and cost per business unit. Find one thing that runs when nobody uses it.

remember!!

A bill nobody owns never shrinks. Tag it, show it to its owner, and hunt the ordinary waste every month.

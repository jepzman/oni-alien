What Matters Most If Everything Fails?

TL;DR

Before you can say whether a recovery is fast enough, decide how long each service may be down (RTO) and how much data it may lose (RPO). Base both on what an hour of loss costs the business: orders, client credits, idle staff, trust. Set them with the business, group services into a few tiers, and then test whether you can actually meet them.

1The situation Larder, Monday 10 January 2028

On 10 January Larder runs its first ever full restore of the production database, onto a fresh server. It takes 3 hours 40 minutes. Eli asks the obvious question afterwards: is that acceptable? Nobody can say. There's no agreed answer, for any service, to how long it may be down or how much data it may lose if everything fails.

  • Without targets, “we have backups” sounds like enough.
  • Every service gets the same effort, or none.
  • Spending on recovery (a second region?) can't be judged.

2Real-life analogy what to grab in a fire

A family agrees, calmly, at the kitchen table, what to grab in a fire: people first, then the pets, then the passports and the laptop with the photos, then nothing else. They keep the passports in one drawer so the plan works in two minutes. Deciding during the fire would be too late.

3How it solves it cost per hour, tiers, and targets the business owns

From 17 to 21 January Eli meets Femke, Joost, Marco and Mo, one service at a time, with one question: what does an hour without it cost? That's counted in lost orders, Korenwolf credits, staff standing idle, and trust. Then how much data could we lose before someone has to re-enter it or a customer notices.

  • Tier 1 (back within 15 minutes, at most 1 minute of data lost): ordering and checkout, payments, Dispatch. An hour down at a Friday peak is about €45,000 of orders.
  • Tier 2 (1 hour, 15 minutes of data): business portal and API, store sync, the picker app's back end.
  • Tier 3 (1 day, 24 hours of data): billing integration, helpdesk, reporting.
  • Tier 4: internal tools, rebuilt when convenient.

The tiers match Ana's DR tiers, and the tier goes onto every catalogue line. By that measure, the ordering database's 3 h 40 is far outside tier 1, which is the next note. Restoring from scratch is the worst case; a replica failover (under 2 minutes) covers most failures.

  1. List services from the catalogue

    One at a time.

  2. Ask what an hour down costs

    Orders, credits, idle staff, trust.

  3. Ask how much data may be lost

    Minutes? Hours? A day?

  4. Group into a few tiers

    Three or four, not one per service.

  5. Agree with the business

    They own the targets.

  6. Test against them

    Restores, failovers, drills.

Vocabulary for the exam

RTO (recovery time objective)
How long a service may be down before the loss is unacceptable.
RPO (recovery point objective)
How much data, in time, may be lost.
Business impact analysis
Working out what each service's loss costs, to set RTO and RPO.
Tier
A group of services with the same recovery targets.
Continuity
Keeping the business running through a serious disruption.
Worst case
Restoring from backup onto new infrastructure, not just failing over.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Engineers set the targets

    Nobody pays for them. Fix → agree them with the business.

  • Everything tier 1

    No priorities. Fix → cost per hour decides.

  • Only RTO, no RPO

    Fast back, with a day of data gone. Fix → both.

  • Targets never tested

    A number on paper. Fix → restore and fail over against them.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • A restore took long and nobody knew if that was OK.
  • Deciding on recovery spend (a second region).
  • Clients or auditors ask about recovery.
  • Before writing continuity plans.

Skip it when…

  • Truly disposable services: tier 4 says it.

8Self-check quiz

What are RTO and RPO?

How long a service may be down, and how much data (in time) it may lose.

How were Larder's tiers set?

By what an hour down costs, agreed with Femke, Joost, Marco and Mo.

Which services are tier 1?

Ordering and checkout, payments, Dispatch: 15 minutes, 1 minute of data.

Why was 3 h 40 a problem?

It's far outside tier 1's 15 minutes for the ordering database's worst case.

Replica failover, under 2 minutes.

what would you do?Grace's team launches a new “recipes” feature. Which tier is it?

Ask what an hour without it costs. If customers can still order without it, probably tier 3 or 4; it goes on the catalogue with that tier, and its backups match.

try it

Take your five most important services. For each, write what an hour down costs and how much data you could lose. Group them into tiers. When did you last test the worst case for tier 1?

remember!!

Decide what to grab first before the fire: recovery time and data loss per service, set by what an hour costs.

Leaving a Vendor

TL;DR

Every critical vendor needs an exit plan written while you're still on good terms: what data you'd need and in what format, how to get it out, the steps and time to move, the cost, the alternative, and which contract terms help or hurt. Test the riskiest step (usually the data export). A tested exit plan is insurance, and the best negotiating position at renewal.

1The situation Larder, April 2028

The billing vendor's contract runs to June 2028, and its scorecard has been amber since the 2027 silence. Before the renewal, Cho and Marco ask Eli: if we didn't renew, how would we leave? Nobody knows how to get every invoice and payment record out, how long it would take, or what it would cost. The vendor knows that as well as Larder does.

  • A vendor you can't leave sets the price and the terms.
  • The data you'd need is in their format, behind their portal.
  • The time to plan an exit is before you need one.

2Real-life analogy the careful tenant

Before signing a lease, a careful tenant reads the notice period, photographs the meter readings, and keeps copies of every letter in a folder of their own, not just in the landlord's portal. Then leaving is a month's notice and a box of papers, not a battle. Even the landlord is easier to deal with, knowing that.

3How it solves it one page per critical vendor, and a test of the riskiest step

In April and May, each critical vendor's owner writes a one-page exit plan: what data and in what format, how to get it out, the steps and time to move, the cost, the alternative, and the contract terms that help or hurt.

  • Billing (Cho): the export test on Tuesday 9 May was expected to take an hour. It took three days: exports were limited to 1,000 invoices per file, and a full export needed a support ticket. At the June renewal Cho and Marco ask for a full-export API and a 90-day exit-assistance clause, and get both, plus a lower price.
  • PSP: the exit is already live, in the form of PSP B.
  • Cloud: the pilot light in Paris (ADR 0026), plus infrastructure code that could rebuild elsewhere.
  • WMS B: plan written; readiness is low and leaving would take months. The risk is accepted on purpose and goes in the register.

Eight critical vendors, eight pages. Each plan's readiness goes on that vendor's quarterly scorecard.

  1. List critical vendors

    From the dependency maps.

  2. Write one page each

    Data, how out, steps, time, cost, alternative, terms.

  3. Test the riskiest step

    Usually a full data export.

  4. Use it at renewal

    Ask for the terms the test showed you need.

  5. Accept some lock-in on purpose

    Written in the risk register.

  6. Review with the scorecards

    Quarterly.

Vocabulary for the exam

Exit plan
How you'd move away from a vendor, written in advance.
Lock-in
How hard and costly it is to leave a vendor.
Data portability
Being able to get your data out in a usable format.
Exit assistance
A contract promise that the vendor helps you leave.
Exit test
Actually trying the riskiest step of leaving.
Accepted risk
A risk you choose to live with, on purpose and on record.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Planning the exit when you need it

    No leverage, no time. Fix → while you're still friends.

  • Assuming export works

    1,000 per file and a ticket. Fix → test it.

  • Exit plans for every tool

    Effort on things that don't matter. Fix → critical vendors only.

  • Hiding accepted lock-in

    A surprise for leadership later. Fix → accept it on purpose, in the register.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • A critical vendor's contract is coming up.
  • A vendor's scorecard is amber.
  • Clients ask about supplier risk.
  • Choosing a new vendor (ask about exit first).

Skip it when…

  • Tools you could replace in a day with nothing lost.

Data and format, how to get it out, steps and time, cost, alternative, contract terms.

What did the billing export test show?

what would you do?Marketing wants to sign with a new email vendor quickly. What one question do you ask before signing?

How do we get our data (lists, consent records, history) out if we leave, and is that in the contract? It's the cheapest moment to ask.

try it

Pick your most critical vendor. Write one page: what data you'd need, how you'd get it out, how long and how much. Then try the export, today, and time it.

remember!!

Plan the exit while you're still friends, and test the export. A vendor you can't leave sets the price.

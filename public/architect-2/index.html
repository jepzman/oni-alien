Decisions & Trade-offs

Volume 2 of The Solution Architect's Notebook: how architects choose between options, record why, and live with the consequences. 15 notes in four collections: write decisions down, choose well, make the big build-buy-and-platform calls, and keep living with what you chose. Every note follows Ana at Larder from March to September 2026, through the Rotterdam launch and the Korenwolf pilot, and ends with a try it exercise for your own project.

the story so far

March 2026. The modular monolith is taking shape, Dispatch is being pulled out into its own service, Rotterdam opens on 27 April and Korenwolf's pilot starts in May. Every week brings a decision someone will question a year from now. This volume follows Ana from March to September 2026: how she and the team choose, record and live with their decisions.

Recording decisions write down what was decided, why, and who decides

  1. 01Architecture Decision RecordsOne short file per significant decision: context, options, the choice and its consequences.p. 01
  2. 02Decision LogOne line per decision, open or closed, with an owner and a date, so nothing is decided twice or never.p. 02
  3. 03RFCs & Design DocsWrite big designs down and open them for comments by a date, before anyone builds.p. 03
  4. 04Decision Rights (RACI/DACI)Name who drives, who decides and who is heard, with exactly one approver.p. 04

Choosing well compare options honestly, and decide at the right time

  1. 05Options AnalysisCompare real options, including doing nothing, against weighted criteria taken from the drivers.p. 05
  2. 06Trade-off SlidersAgree up front which qualities give way when they collide, because they can't all be at maximum.p. 06
  3. 07One-way vs Two-way DoorsDecide reversible things fast and close to the work; slow down only for the doors that lock behind you.p. 07
  4. 08Last Responsible MomentDecide when waiting starts to cost more than it saves, not earlier and not later.p. 08

Big choices build or buy, which technology, which platform

  1. 09Build vs Buy vs Open SourceBuild what makes you different, buy what doesn't, and know what open source really costs.p. 09
  2. 10Technology EvaluationFrom long list to shortlist to a bake-off on your own data, scored against criteria set in advance.p. 10
  3. 11Choosing a Cloud Service ModelPick how much of the stack you run yourself, from virtual machines to functions to SaaS.p. 11
  4. 12Technology RadarPublish which technologies to adopt, trial, assess or hold, so teams choose fast and alike.p. 12

Living with decisions track the debt, keep an exit, and look again on purpose

  1. 13Technical Debt RegisterList the shortcuts you took on purpose, with their interest and a payback trigger.p. 13
  2. 14Exit Plans & ReversibilityFor every vendor and platform, know how you'd leave, and keep the way out cheap.p. 14
  3. 15Revisiting DecisionsGive every decision a review trigger, and check them on purpose instead of re-arguing at random.p. 15

?How every note is laid out same eight sections, every time

  1. The situation: a scene from the case study where it goes wrong, with a doodle.
  2. Real-life analogy: an everyday version, plus a “translating it to the job” table.
  3. How it solves it: diagrams, steps, vocabulary and a template card.
  4. Gains vs losses: the trade-off spread.
  5. Ratings: nine qualities, before → after.
  6. Common pitfalls: each with its fix.
  7. When to use it: and when to skip it.
  8. Self-check quiz: tap a question to see the answer.

★The nine architect ratings what a good architecture practice is judged on

Every note scores the pattern from 1 (poor) to 5 (great) on the same nine things, comparing an app without the pattern to the same app with it.

Clarity
A newcomer can understand the system and the reasoning behind it.
Stakeholder alignment
Business, product and engineering share the same what and why.
Decision speed
How quickly the team can choose and get back to building.
Risk reduction
The big risks are surfaced and handled before they bite.
Adaptability
How cheaply the direction can change later.
Traceability
Months later, anyone can find why something was done.
Cost awareness
Money and effort are part of the decision, not a surprise.
Low overhead
Time and ceremony it takes. High = light.
Team autonomy
Teams can decide and act without waiting for the architect.

first law

Everything in architecture is a trade-off. If you think you've found one that isn't, you haven't found the cost yet.

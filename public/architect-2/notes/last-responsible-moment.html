Last Responsible Moment

TL;DR

Decide too early and you decide with the least information you'll ever have. Decide too late and the lead time has run out. The last responsible moment is in between: the point where waiting starts to cost more than it saves, usually the target date minus the decision's lead time. Work out that date for each big decision, put it in a decision calendar, and until then do the cheap things that keep options open.

1The situation Larder, 20 April 2026

A week before Rotterdam opens, Ana sees both failure modes at once. Too late: Rotterdam's store internet line was ordered on 30 March, with a six-week lead time; it goes live in mid-May, so the store opens on a 4G router (the offline-first picker app, ADR 0004, earns its keep). Too early: in the same week Lena and Marco want to choose a Belgian invoicing system for Antwerp, a city planned for 2027, before anyone knows what Larder for Business will need in Belgium.

  • Early decisions lock in guesses that later facts would have corrected.
  • Late decisions run into lead times: contracts, hardware, hiring, translations.
  • “Decide later” without a date is just “forget”.

2Real-life analogy renovating a kitchen

You don't choose the kitchen tiles on day one: you don't know the room yet. But the tiles take six weeks to arrive, and the tiler is booked. So the moment to choose is six weeks before the tiler, not earlier and not later. Until then you keep options open: you measure the walls, you don't glue anything.

3How it solves it work back from the date

For each big decision, ask two questions: when do we need the answer in place, and how long does it take once decided? The difference is the last responsible moment. Put it in the decision log as a due date. And list the cheap moves that keep options open until then.

  1. List the decisions a date depends on

    For Antwerp: store, invoicing and VAT, language, payment methods, couriers.

  2. Find each lead time

    Ask the people who know: legal, finance, vendors, operations. Add a buffer.

  3. Work back from the date

    Target date − lead time − buffer = last responsible moment. Put it in the log.

  4. Keep options open meanwhile

    Cheap moves that don't commit: translatable strings, a billing vendor that supports Belgian VAT.

  5. Learn while you wait

    What would make the decision easier? Collect it before the date.

  6. Decide on the date

    Waiting past it is the last irresponsible moment.

Vocabulary for the exam

Last responsible moment
The latest point where deciding still leaves enough time to act.
Lead time
How long it takes from deciding to having it in place.
Decision calendar
Big decisions with their last responsible moments.
Option value
The worth of keeping a choice open while facts arrive.
Premature commitment
Deciding before you need to, on guesses.
Last irresponsible moment
Waiting until the lead time no longer fits.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • Better-informed decisions: made with more facts.
  • No lead-time surprises: dates are worked back, not guessed.
  • Options stay open cheaply, on purpose.
  • Less rework from early commitments that turned out wrong.
  • “Later” has a date, so it doesn't mean “never”.

What we lose

  • Needs lead-time knowledge people may not have.
  • Feels uncomfortable: open questions look like indecision.
  • Easy to slide into the last irresponsible moment.
  • Keeping options open costs a little (translatable strings, flexible vendors).

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • No date

    “We'll decide later” and nobody does. Fix → every deferred decision gets its last responsible moment in the log.

  • Unknown lead times

    The internet line takes six weeks; nobody asked. Fix → ask suppliers and operations for lead times, add a buffer.

  • Deferring two-way doors

    A reversible choice waits months for “more information”. Fix → two-way doors: decide now, change later.

  • Options that cost too much

    Supporting three billing vendors “just in case”. Fix → keep options open cheaply, or decide.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Dated launches with long-lead items: contracts, hardware, hiring, legal.
  • One-way doors where facts are still arriving.
  • When people push to decide everything up front.
  • Planning new markets or cities.

Skip it when…

  • Two-way doors: just decide.
  • When the information won't improve with time.

8Self-check quiz

How do you find a decision's last responsible moment?

Target date minus its lead time (and a buffer).

What's wrong with deciding as early as possible?

You decide with the least information, and lock in guesses.

What do you do until the last responsible moment?

Keep options open with cheap moves and learn what would make the decision easier.

Does this apply to two-way doors?

Rarely: reversible decisions can be made now and changed later.

try it

Pick your next big date. List five decisions it depends on, and ask the right person for each one's lead time. Write a decision calendar with the last responsible moment for each and one cheap way to keep options open until then. Add the dates to your decision log.

remember!!

Not on day one, not after the lead time. Measure the walls; order the tiles in time.

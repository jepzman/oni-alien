Decision Log

TL;DR

ADRs explain single decisions; a decision log is the index of all of them, including the open ones. One line per decision: a number, the question, the status (open, decided, superseded), an owner, a due date and a link to the ADR, or the answer in one line if it's small. Keep it in the repo, review the open lines every week, and “what's still undecided, and who's deciding it?” takes ten seconds to answer.

1The situation Larder, 16 March 2026

Six weeks before Rotterdam opens, Sanne, the delivery manager, asks Ana a simple question: which decisions for Rotterdam are still open, and who's making them? Ana can point to seven ADRs. But she finds nineteen more decisions made in chat threads, ticket comments and meeting notes, and six open questions with no owner at all, including how Rotterdam's opening hours get into the app.

  • ADRs record the decisions someone thought were big enough; the rest are scattered.
  • Open questions have no home, so they're discovered late, usually by the person blocked by them.
  • “Was that decided?” is answered by whoever remembers, differently each time.

2Real-life analogy the court's docket

A court doesn't keep its cases in judges' heads. The docket lists every case, one line each: number, status, judge, the next hearing date, and a pointer to the judgement once there is one. Anyone can see what's pending and who has it. The judgements are the long documents; the docket is how you find them, and how you see what's not decided yet.

3How it solves it one line per decision, open ones first

The log is cheap on purpose: adding a line takes a minute, so small decisions get recorded too. Only the significant ones get an ADR; the log links them. The open lines are the useful part: they're a to-do list of decisions with owners and dates.

  1. Start the file

    docs/decisions/README.md: a table with number, decision, status, owner, due date and link.

  2. Backfill once

    Sweep chats, tickets and meeting notes from the last three months; one line each. Don't chase perfection.

  3. Log open questions too

    A question someone is waiting on gets a line, an owner and a due date, today.

  4. Close with a link or a line

    Significant → write the ADR and link it. Small and reversible → write the answer in the log.

  5. Review weekly

    Ten minutes on Monday: overdue lines, lines without owners, decisions that block a date.

  6. Supersede, don't delete

    A replaced decision stays in the log, marked with the number that replaced it.

Vocabulary for the exam

Decision log
The index of every decision, open or closed, one line each.
Open decision
A question someone is waiting on, with an owner and a due date.
Owner
The one person who will make the call or get it made.
Due date
When the decision must be made so nothing downstream slips.
ADR index
The list of ADRs; here it's the log's “significant” rows.
Superseded
Replaced by a later decision; kept on file.

tools: a Markdown table in the repo is enough. ADR tools generate the index (adr-tools generate toc, Log4brains); some teams keep open decisions on a board column or a spreadsheet, but link it from the repo.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • Open questions get owners and dates before they block anyone.
  • Small decisions are recorded without the weight of an ADR.
  • One place to look: “was that decided?” takes ten seconds.
  • Delivery sees the risk: overdue decisions show up next to the plan.
  • Finds the ADRs: the log is their index.

What we lose

  • Needs a weekly habit, or it goes stale in a month.
  • Duplicates if open decisions also live on a board.
  • One line hides nuance: small decisions lose their reasons.
  • Backfilling the first time takes an afternoon.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Only closed decisions

    The log is a museum of past choices. Fix → log open questions the moment someone is waiting on them.

  • No owner

    “The team” owns D-26, so nobody does. Fix → one named person per open line.

  • A second home

    Open decisions live on a board and in the log, and disagree. Fix → pick one source of truth and link the other to it.

  • Never reviewed

    Due dates pass silently. Fix → ten minutes every week, overdue lines first.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Any project with a fixed date and many teams.
  • When ADRs exist but nobody can find the small decisions.
  • Before a launch, to see what's still open.
  • Onboarding: a newcomer reads the log, then the ADRs that matter.

Skip it when…

  • A two-person team where every decision is made at one desk.
  • If a tracker already holds every decision and everyone uses it: link it, don't copy it.

8Self-check quiz

What does a decision log contain that a folder of ADRs doesn't?

Open decisions, with owners and due dates, and small decisions that don't deserve an ADR.

Which decisions get an ADR, and which only a line?

Significant, hard-to-reverse decisions get an ADR; small, reversible ones get one line.

What must every open line have?

One named owner and a due date.

What happens to a decision that's replaced?

It stays in the log, marked superseded by the new decision's number.

try it

Spend 45 minutes searching your team's chat and tickets from the last month for the words “decided”, “let's go with” and “agreed”. Put each one on a line of a new decision log, then add every open question you know of with an owner and a due date. Show the open lines at your next planning meeting.

remember!!

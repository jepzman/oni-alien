Are They Still With Us?

TL;DR

Monitor engagement on a rhythm: re-score each key stakeholder every month on the engagement matrix, and write the evidence beside each score: what they said, did, signed, objected to. Silence, low attendance and “no complaints” are not support; when evidence is thin, ask. Check communications the same way: were the messages read, understood and acted on? A score that moves the wrong way is an early warning; act on it that week.

  • Family: abroad
  • Lives in: the engagement matrix, re-scored monthly, with evidence
  • Pairs with: the engagement plan, the communications plan, the risk review

1The situation Antwerp, May → June 2027

From March, Sanne re-scores the engagement matrix on the first Friday of each month. On 7 May the neighbours are neutral: only nine people came to the April meeting, fewer than in January, and nobody has emailed. She reads that as calm. The permit application has been in the city's public inquiry since 24 May. It closes on 22 June with fourteen objections from the street: lorries before seven and condenser noise. The neighbours hadn't gone quiet because they were happy. They were waiting for the inquiry, the place where their objections would count.

  • Scores came from attendance and email, not from asking.
  • “No complaints” was taken for support.
  • The inquiry was a new channel the plan didn't watch.

2Real-life analogy the check-up

A patient says “I'm fine.” The doctor still takes the pulse and the blood pressure, and asks how they've been sleeping. A patient who skips the follow-up appointment isn't healthier for it. A good check-up runs on a schedule, measures something, and asks direct questions when the numbers are thin.

3How it solves it a monthly score, with evidence, and action when it drops

Each first Friday, Sanne re-scores the six stakeholders that matter most and writes one line of evidence per score. The permit office goes from unaware to neutral after the pre-application meeting of 11 March (“a complete file, please, and the noise study”); Koen to supportive after the second meeting. After June she adds a rule: no evidence, no score; silence is marked “?” and triggers a question. The neighbours' “resistant” in June turns into action: in the last week of July Sanne and Inès, the new store manager, go door to door on the street with the changed plan (deliveries from 07:00, an acoustic screen on the condenser). In September they're neutral again; nine of them come to the opening. Communications get the same check: the waitlist emails are opened by 58 % in Dutch and 31 % in French; Priya's team rewrites the French subject lines with Grace's writers and the gap halves.

  1. Re-score key stakeholders monthly

    On a fixed day.

  2. Write the evidence beside each score

    Said, did, signed, objected.

  3. Treat silence as a question

    Ask, visit, call.

  4. Watch every channel they can use

    Inquiries, complaints, the press.

  5. Check messages were read and understood

    Not only sent.

  6. Act the same week a score drops

    With an owner.

Vocabulary for the exam

Monitoring engagement
Checking whether stakeholders are where the project needs them, and adjusting.
Evidence
What a stakeholder actually said or did, not your impression.
Monitoring communications
Checking that information reached people and was understood.
Leading indicator
A sign that comes before the problem, like a falling score.
Channel
A way stakeholders can be heard: meetings, emails, inquiries, the press.
Open rate
The share of people who opened an email.

4Light, standard, heavy how much of this do you need?

Light

a few stakeholders you see often

  • Ask them how it's going.
  • Note anything that changes.
  • Monthly.

Standard

Antwerp

  • Matrix re-scored monthly with evidence.
  • Silence triggers a question.
  • Communications checked for reading and understanding.

Heavy

large public projects

  • Surveys and sentiment tracking.
  • Complaint and objection logs.
  • Independent engagement reviews.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Silence as support

    They may be waiting for a better channel. Fix → ask.

  • Scores from mood

    Optimism in a table. Fix → write the evidence.

  • Only your channels

    Objections arrive elsewhere. Fix → watch every channel they can use.

  • Sent = understood

    Nobody read it. Fix → check reading and understanding.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Monthly through the project.
  • Before and during any formal consultation.
  • When a stakeholder goes quiet.
  • After every big message.

Skip it when…

  • When nobody outside the team is affected.

8Self-check quiz

What goes beside each score?

One line of evidence: what they said, did, signed or objected to.

Why was May's “neutral” wrong?

It came from low attendance and no emails; the neighbours were waiting for the inquiry.

What did the French open rate show?

Messages sent weren't read: 31 % vs 58 %; subject lines rewritten.

A key supplier stops replying for two weeks. What does the matrix say to do?

Mark it “?”, call or visit that week, and score from what you learn.

Femke asks why the neighbours are “resistant” in June when nobody complained to Larder. Explain.

They objected in the public inquiry, a channel the plan didn't watch; silence to Larder wasn't support.

try it

For your three most important stakeholders, write their attitude and one piece of evidence from the last month. Where you have no evidence, call them this week.

remember!!

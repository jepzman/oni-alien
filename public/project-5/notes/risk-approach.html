How Will We Handle Risk?

TL;DR

Before listing a single risk, agree how the project will handle risk: the sponsor's appetite (how much slip, overspend or exposure they'll accept), the thresholds that send a decision upstairs, the scales for probability and impact so everyone scores the same way, roles (every risk gets an owner), and the rhythm of reviews. One page, signed by the sponsor. Put a price on both sides of the appetite: what it costs to be safer, and what a week of delay costs.

  • Family: what could happen
  • Lives in: one page, signed by the sponsor
  • Pairs with: the risk register, the budget and its reserves, who decides what

1The situation Larder, 16 November 2026

Before listing any risks, Sanne asks Femke one question: would she pay €9,000 for an extra month's rent, keys on 1 May instead of 1 June, to give the fit-out a month of margin? Femke says no. “A few weeks' slip is survivable. Nine thousand euros on an empty store isn't.” It's a fair answer, and an appetite statement. But it has a price on one side only. In May Marco had worked out that a city opening a week earlier is worth about €15k; nobody puts that number beside the €9k.

  • Appetite is said aloud, not written.
  • The cost of being late isn't on the table.
  • Nobody has agreed how risks will be scored.

2Real-life analogy the hike

A family planning a long hike agrees a few things before choosing the route: no ridges in high wind; if the weather turns, turn back by noon; who carries the first-aid kit; how they'll judge whether a stream is safe to cross. Then they choose a route that fits. Families who agree this on the ridge, in the wind, argue instead of deciding.

3How it solves it one page, five parts, signed

Sanne writes the approach on one page and Femke signs it on 16 November. Appetite: on schedule, a slip of up to two weeks is tolerable; on money, stay within the budget and its contingency. Thresholds: a forecast slip over two weeks, or a draw beyond the contingency, goes to Femke. Scales: probability 1–5 (under 10 % to over 70 %), impact 1–5 (under a week or €2k, up to over eight weeks or €50k); the score is probability × impact; 12 and above is red. Roles: every risk has a Larder owner; Sanne keeps the register. Rhythm: the leads review it every other Thursday, the top five go into Femke's Friday note, the steering group sees it monthly. The categories come from Rotterdam's lessons plus one new one: Belgium. What the page doesn't have is the price of a week of delay. That line is added to the playbook eleven months later.

  1. Ask the sponsor for appetite, with numbers

    Time, money, reputation.

  2. Price both sides

    What safety costs; what a week late costs.

  3. Set thresholds that go upstairs

    Matching the decision rights.

  4. Agree scales before scoring

    Words and numbers for 1 to 5.

  5. Name roles and the rule for owners

    One owner per risk, inside the organisation.

  6. Fix the rhythm

    Who reviews, how often, where it's reported.

Vocabulary for the exam

Risk
An uncertain event that, if it happens, affects the project, for worse (a threat) or better (an opportunity).
Risk appetite
How much uncertainty the sponsor is willing to accept for the reward.
Risk threshold
The level at which a risk needs a decision from someone higher.
Probability and impact scales
Agreed definitions of each score, so everyone rates alike.
Risk categories
Groups that prompt a search for risks: site, people, systems, partners, rules.
Risk owner
The person who watches a risk and makes sure its response happens.

# ANTWERP · HOW WE HANDLE RISK                                  signed: Femke (sponsor)

APPETITE     schedule: a slip of up to 2 weeks is tolerable
             money: within the budget and its contingency; no rent before 1 Jun
THRESHOLDS   to Femke: forecast slip > 2 weeks · a draw beyond the contingency ·
             any risk scoring 20 or more
SCALES       P 1 <10 % · 2 10–30 % · 3 30–50 % · 4 50–70 % · 5 >70 %
             I 1 <1 week or <€2k · 2 1–2 weeks or €2–5k · 3 2–4 weeks or €5–15k ·
               4 4–8 weeks or €15–50k · 5 >8 weeks or >€50k
             score = P × I · red >= 12 · amber 6–11 · green <= 5
CATEGORIES   site + building · people · systems · partners · Belgium (rules, language)
             · market · and opportunities in each
ROLES        every risk has a LARDER owner · Sanne keeps the register
RHYTHM       leads: every other Thursday · top 5 in Femke's Friday note · steering monthly
RESERVES     contingency (Sanne, by change) · management reserve (Femke): sized in Feb

4Light, standard, heavy how much of this do you need?

Light

a short internal piece of work

  • Ask the sponsor what would be too late or too dear.
  • High / medium / low.
  • Review at each check-in.

Standard

Antwerp

  • One page: appetite, thresholds, scales, roles, rhythm.
  • Both sides priced.
  • Signed by the sponsor.

Heavy

large regulated programmes

  • A full risk management plan.
  • Quantified thresholds per objective.
  • Independent risk function and audits.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • No appetite asked

    You guess what the sponsor accepts. Fix → ask, with numbers.

  • Price on one side

    Safety looks expensive, delay looks free. Fix → price a week of delay too.

  • Scales agreed later

    Scores mean different things. Fix → agree them before scoring.

  • A rule nobody checks

    “Every risk has an owner”, on paper. Fix → check it at every review.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • At the start, before the register.
  • When the sponsor changes.
  • When the project enters a riskier phase or place.
  • When scores start to disagree.

Skip it when…

  • Never; on small work it's three sentences.

8Self-check quiz

Name the five parts of the risk approach.

Appetite, thresholds, scales, roles, rhythm.

What is risk appetite?

How much uncertainty the sponsor will accept for the reward.

What did the page leave out?

The price of a week late (≈ €15k contribution + rent).

A lead scores a risk “high” and another “3”. What's missing?

Agreed scales with definitions; use the page's 1–5 scales.

Your sponsor says “no risk is acceptable.” What do you ask?

What a slip or overspend would cost them, and what they'd pay to avoid it; zero risk has a price too.

try it

Ask your sponsor two questions: how late or how far over would be too much, and what would you pay to be safer? Then work out what a week of delay costs, and put both on one page.

remember!!

Agree when you'll turn back before you're on the ridge.

a.k.a. implementing risk responses: owners, actions, triggers

Did We Do What We Said?

TL;DR

A response on the register is a plan. It only becomes real when someone does it: every response action as a task in the plan, with an owner inside the organisation, a date and a clear “done”; every trigger in the regular status, with a named watcher. A supplier or agent can do the work, but someone of yours must own that it happens. Check implementation at every risk review: what was done, what's due, which trigger is closest.

  • Family: doing something about it
  • Lives in: the plan (as tasks) and the Friday status (as triggers)
  • Pairs with: risk responses, the issue log, directing the work

1The situation Antwerp, Friday 2 April 2027

This is Sanne's mistake for this collection. At the risk review she reads R-07's actions aloud. Hilde started on 8 March: done. The meeting with the city on 11 March: done. The cooling supplier's drawings by 15 March: not done. The owner column says “cooling supplier”. The supplier had been waiting since mid-March for Larder's final 640 m² layout, and nobody at Larder knew it was waiting. The trigger, “not lodged by 26 March”, passed a week ago. Hilde thought Sanne was watching it. Sanne thought Hilde was.

  • The action's owner was outside Larder, again.
  • Response actions lived on the register, not in the plan.
  • A trigger with no watcher is a date nobody looks at.

2Real-life analogy the relay race

A relay team plans four runners and three handovers. The race is rarely lost by a slow runner. It's lost at a handover, where two runners each think the other has the baton. The team sheet named every runner. Nobody had practised the third handover.

3How it solves it actions as tasks, owners inside the organisation, triggers with watchers

Sanne fixes the drawings first. They get a Larder owner: Tomás's layout lead sends the 640 m² layout on Tuesday 6 April, the drawings arrive on Friday 9 April, and Hilde lodges the application on Monday 19 April, three and a half weeks after the trigger date. Then three rules for every response, from that day: every action is a task in the plan, with an owner, a date and a definition of done; every owner works at Larder (a supplier or agent is where the work comes from, not who owns it); every trigger is in the Friday status, with a named watcher. The risk review now asks three questions of each red risk: what was done since last time, what's due, which trigger is closest. Sanne tells the steering group on 7 May what the three and a half weeks cost: the public inquiry now runs from 24 May to 22 June instead of early May, and the simulation's P50 has moved from 13 to 20 September.

  1. Turn each response action into a task

    In the plan, not only the register.

  2. Give it an owner inside the organisation

    Suppliers do; your people own.

  3. Write what “done” means

    And the date.

  4. Put triggers in the status

    With a named watcher.

  5. Review implementation, not just scores

    Done, due, nearest trigger.

  6. Say what a late action cost

    In days, in odds, at the next steering group.

Vocabulary for the exam

Implement risk responses
Carrying out the agreed responses as planned work.
Risk action
One task that carries out part of a response.
Action owner
The person inside the organisation who makes sure the action happens.
Trigger
A date or sign that says a risk is starting, or a response is late.
Watcher
The person who checks a trigger and acts when it's hit.
Handover
The point where work passes from one person or organisation to another.

tools: the plan, an owner column that only takes your own people's names, the Friday status, and three questions.

4Light, standard, heavy how much of this do you need?

Light

small work

  • Each risk action on the to-do list.
  • Named, dated.
  • Checked weekly.

Standard

Antwerp

  • Actions as tasks with Larder owners.
  • Triggers in the status with watchers.
  • Reviews ask done, due, nearest trigger.

Heavy

large programmes

  • Risk actions in the schedule with earned value.
  • Action owners report at each review.
  • Audit of response implementation.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Supplier as owner

    Nobody of yours watches. Fix → an owner inside the organisation.

  • Actions only on the register

    Not in anyone's week. Fix → make them tasks in the plan.

  • Triggers without watchers

    Dates nobody looks at. Fix → name a watcher, show it in the status.

  • Reviews that re-score only

    Scores move; nothing gets done. Fix → ask what was done.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • As soon as responses are agreed.
  • At every risk review.
  • When work passes to a supplier or agent.
  • When a trigger date approaches.

Skip it when…

  • Never; it's what makes the register worth having.

8Self-check quiz

What were Sanne's three rules from 2 April?

Actions as tasks in the plan; owners at Larder; triggers in the Friday status with a watcher.

Why were the drawings late?

The supplier was waiting for Larder's layout; the owner was outside Larder; nobody at Larder knew.

What did the delay cost?

Lodged 3½ weeks late; inquiry 24 May–22 Jun; P50 from 13 to 20 September.

An agent says “leave it with me”. What do you still do?

Name a Larder owner, put the agent's steps in the plan with dates, and put the trigger in the status.

At a review, a red risk's score hasn't changed for six weeks. What's the first question?

What was done since last time? A steady score may mean nothing has been done.

try it

For each response in your register, find the task in your plan that carries it out. If there isn't one, create it with an owner from your own organisation and a date.

remember!!

The plan names the runners. The race is the handovers.

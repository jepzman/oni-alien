a.k.a. “risks vs issues”: monitoring risks, closing them, and handing them to the issue log

Is the List Still True?

TL;DR

Monitor risks on a rhythm: at each review ask what's new, what has changed, which responses are done, which triggers are close, and which risks can be closed. A risk might happen; an issue has happened. When a risk happens, close it on the register and open it on the issue log, with an owner and a decision date this week; the reserve and the fallback plan you prepared are now for spending. At the end, turn what the list taught you into lessons the next project will trip over.

1The situation Antwerp, Thursday 15 July 2027

The public inquiry closed on 22 June with fourteen objections: lorries before seven, condenser noise. On Thursday 15 July the city's case officer phones Hilde. Larder must either change the delivery hours or bring an independent noise study, and either way there will be no decision before September. The planning date is 13 September; the cold room needs the permit before it can be installed. R-07 sits on the register at 12, with a contingency and a trigger. It is no longer something that might happen. It has happened.

  • A risk that happens needs a decision, not a score.
  • The reserve and the fallbacks are now for spending.
  • The rest of the register still needs watching.

2Real-life analogy frost and the burst pipe

The forecast says 40 % chance of frost tonight. That's a risk: you lag the pipes and leave the heating on low. In the morning there's water coming through the ceiling. That's an issue: you turn off the water and call the plumber, now. It's the same pipe on a different list, and the second list moves much faster.

3How it solves it close the risk, open the issue, decide, spend, learn

That afternoon Sanne closes R-07 as a risk and opens issue I-12, owner Sanne, decision needed by the end of next week. It's over every threshold, so Femke calls an extra steering group for Friday 23 July with three options. A: wait for an August decision; the odds are poor. B: amend the application now, with deliveries from 07:00 only (Koen agrees a 07:00 drop) and an acoustic screen on the condenser, and plan the opening for Monday 18 October. C: open in September with dry goods only; Lena rejects it on food safety, and customers would get half a store. Femke chooses B: half of the avoid option rejected in February, five months later and five weeks too late. The cost inside the project is €27k (staff starts held €11k, marketing re-booked €9k, the installer re-booked €4k, the screen €3k), from the last €14k of contingency and €13k of Femke's management reserve. Outside it, about €10k of rent and, by Marco's sum, about €75k of orders. In the last week of July Sanne and Inès knock on every door in the street with the new hours. The permit is granted on Monday 6 September, the cold room is in and tested by the 17th, the food-safety visit is on the 29th, and Antwerp opens on Monday 18 October: 2.4 tickets per 100 orders in week one, better than Utrecht's 2.9. Forecast final cost €457k of €465k. In October Sanne writes four lines into playbook v3.

  1. Review on a rhythm

    New, changed, done, near triggers, closable.

  2. Read the low rows too

    Who scored them, and would they still?

  3. When a risk happens, move it

    Close it as a risk, open it as an issue.

  4. Decide fast, with options

    Over a threshold: to the sponsor, this week.

  5. Spend the reserve you prepared

    And report what's left.

  6. Turn the list into lessons

    Into the playbook, not a folder.

Vocabulary for the exam

Risk
Something that might happen.
Issue
Something that has happened and needs action.
Risk review
A regular meeting to update risks, responses and triggers.
Reserve analysis
Comparing the reserve left with the risk left.
Closing a risk
Taking it off the active list because it has passed or happened.
Risk audit
A check that the risk process itself is working.

tools: the register, a fortnightly hour, the issue log, a steering group that can meet at a week's notice, and the playbook.

4Light, standard, heavy how much of this do you need?

Light

small work

  • Look at the risk list each week.
  • Move what happened to the to-do list.
  • One lesson at the end.

Standard

Antwerp

  • Fortnightly review: new, changed, done, triggers, closed.
  • Risks that happen become issues.
  • Reserve left reported; lessons into the playbook.

Heavy

large programmes

  • Risk audits.
  • Reserve analysis against quantified exposure.
  • Risk reports to portfolio boards.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Scoring an issue

    It happened; act. Fix → move it to the issue log.

  • Waiting for the next review

    A week lost. Fix → a happened risk is decided this week.

  • Reserve spent quietly

    Nobody sees what's left. Fix → report reserve left with risk left.

  • Lessons in a folder

    The next city repeats it. Fix → write them into the playbook.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Fortnightly through the project.
  • When a trigger is hit.
  • When a risk happens.
  • At closing: the lessons.

Skip it when…

  • Never; on small work it's a glance at the list each week.

8Self-check quiz

What's the difference between a risk and an issue?

A risk might happen; an issue has happened and needs action.

What happened to R-07 on 15 July?

Closed as a risk, opened as issue I-12, decision by 23 July.

Which option did Femke choose, and why was it ready?

B: deliveries from 07:00 and a screen; it had been studied as the avoid option in February.

A risk's trigger is hit on a Tuesday; the next review is in nine days. What do you do?

Act now: treat it as an issue, tell the owner and, if over a threshold, the sponsor, with options.

What did Antwerp cost, inside and outside the project?

€27k inside (contingency and management reserve); about €10k rent and €75k of orders outside.

try it

At your next review, ask of every risk: has it happened? If yes, move it to the issue log with an owner and a decision date this week. Then write one lesson your next project should start with.

remember!!

Frost is a risk. Water through the ceiling is an issue.

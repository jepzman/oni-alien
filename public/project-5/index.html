People Outside, and Surprises

Volume 5 of The Project Manager's Notebook: the Stakeholders and Risk domains of the PMBOK Guide (eighth edition), all thirteen of their processes. 15 notes in four collections: finding everyone the project touches, working abroad, finding and sizing what could happen, and doing something about it. The worked example is the Antwerp launch, and every template card is a page of its project file. Four notes go further with a going deeper chapter: the engagement matrix, the risk register, simulation, and risk responses.

the story so far

October 2026 → October 2027: Antwerp. Rotterdam opened on time and on budget, and Utrecht and The Hague open in 2027 from its playbook. Antwerp is different: the first city abroad, with Belgian rules, Flemish and French, a city permit office, a food-safety agency and a street of forty-six houses behind the loading yard. Sanne planned it for Monday 13 September 2027. It opened on Monday 18 October, five weeks late, and the reason had been on her risk register since November, rated low, with no owner. This volume is how that happened, and everything else that went right around it.

Who's involved? find everyone the project touches, and decide how much of you each one needs

  1. 01Who Cares About This?Find everyone the project affects or who can affect it, from more than one source, and write down what each one needs.p. 01
  2. 02Who Matters Most, and How Much? + goes deeperPlace stakeholders by power and interest, mark where they are and where you need them, and act on the gaps.p. 02
  3. 03Who Hears What, and When?One table: what each audience needs, how, how often, in which language and from whom.p. 03
  4. 04How Do We Keep Them With Us?Meet people early, promise only what you control, and turn every concern into something the plan can see.p. 04

Abroad new rules, a new language, and knowing whether people are still with you

  1. 05Are We Being Understood?A meeting is only finished when everyone leaves with the same decisions, in writing, in their language.p. 05
  2. 06Different Country, Different RulesA new country brings new regulators, new laws and new habits. Map them with someone local, early.p. 06
  3. 07Are They Still With Us?Re-score every key stakeholder each month, from what they say and do, not from what you hope.p. 07

What could happen? plan for risk, list it, and size it in days and euros

  1. 08How Will We Handle Risk?Agree, before listing risks, how much risk the sponsor will take, the scales, the owners and the rhythm.p. 08
  2. 09What Could Happen? + goes deeperWrite each risk as cause, event and effect, with an owner and a response, and include the good surprises.p. 09
  3. 10How Likely, and How Bad?Score each risk on scales everyone agreed, and re-score it whenever someone who knows more speaks up.p. 10
  4. 11What Is It Worth?Put a likely cost on each risk, add them up, and size the contingency and the management reserve from it.p. 11
  5. 12What If We Ran It a Thousand Times? + goes deeperRun the plan a thousand times with its uncertainties and read the date off as odds, not a single day.p. 12

Doing something about it choose responses, carry them out, and keep the list true

  1. 13What Do We Do About It? + goes deeperAvoid, transfer, mitigate or accept a threat; exploit, enhance, share or accept an opportunity; escalate what isn't yours.p. 13
  2. 14Did We Do What We Said?A response is only real when a named person does it by a date, and someone watches its trigger.p. 14
  3. 15Is the List Still True?Review risks on a rhythm, close the dead ones, and when one happens, move it to the issue log.p. 15

★The nine project ratings what a good project practice is judged on

Every note scores the pattern from 1 (poor) to 5 (great) on the same nine things, comparing an app without the pattern to the same app with it.

Value focus
Keeps the project aimed at the benefit, not at the paperwork.
Predictability

Risk reduction
Trouble is found while it is still cheap.
Transparency
Anyone can see where the project really is.
Stakeholder confidence
Sponsors and partners believe the updates.
Team ownership
The people doing the work own the plan.
Adaptability
Change is absorbed without chaos.
Quality built in

first law

The people outside the project and the things that might happen to it are the same list seen twice. Whoever can stop you is a stakeholder and a risk: give them both an owner.

Identity Federation & SSO

TL;DR

When every tool has its own password, people reuse weak ones, and leavers keep access because nobody removes fourteen accounts. Use one identity provider for staff, with single sign-on to every tool and automatic provisioning and removal where tools support it. For partners and business clients, federate: trust their identity provider, so their people use their own company accounts and lose access when they leave. One identity per person; switched off in one place.

1The situation Larder, 24 May 2027

Lena's access review finds that Larder staff use fourteen tools, each with its own password. A support agent who left in April still had access to the helpdesk and the billing service three weeks later. Meanwhile two new business clients, like Korenwolf before them, want their office managers to log in with their own company accounts, not another Larder password. Identity is scattered across every tool.

  • Leavers keep access because removal is manual, tool by tool.
  • Password reuse and resets cost time and security.
  • Business clients can't control who at their company has access.

2Real-life analogy the building badge

A company badge opens every door in the building. When someone leaves, security deactivates one badge and every door closes to them. Visitors from a partner company come in on their own company's badge, trusted by agreement, without a new key for each door. Nobody keeps a drawer of keys.

3How it solves it one identity, federated outward

ADR 0021 put all staff on one identity provider, with SSO to every tool and automatic provisioning and removal (SCIM) for the eleven that support it; the other three are removed by a checklist on the leaver's last day. Business clients federate their own identity providers, as Korenwolf did in December: when an office manager leaves Korenwolf, they lose Larder access automatically. Pickers got personal badges on the handhelds instead of shared store logins.

  1. Pick one identity provider

    Usually the one staff already log in to every day.

  2. Connect every tool by SSO

    SAML or OpenID Connect; list tools that can't, and plan for them.

  3. Automate joiners and leavers

    SCIM provisioning; a checklist for the rest.

  4. Federate with partners and clients

    Trust their identity provider; no Larder passwords for them.

  5. Remove shared accounts

    Personal identities, even on store devices.

  6. Review access regularly

    Quarterly, with the data protection officer.

Vocabulary for the exam

Identity provider (IdP)
The system that holds identities and logs people in.
Single sign-on (SSO)
One login for many tools.
Federation
Trusting another organisation's identity provider.
SAML / OIDC
Standard protocols for SSO and federation.
SCIM
A standard for creating and removing accounts automatically.
Joiner-mover-leaver
The lifecycle of a person's access.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • Leavers lose access in one step.
  • Fewer passwords, fewer resets.
  • Clients control their own users.
  • Audit-ready: one place to see who has access.
  • Personal accountability: no shared logins.

What we lose

  • SSO costs extra on some SaaS plans.
  • One provider is critical: its outage locks everyone out.
  • Federation setup per client.
  • Some tools can't do SSO or SCIM.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Tools left out

    Three tools still on passwords, forgotten. Fix → list them; a leaver checklist until they support SSO.

  • Shared accounts

    “noord-picker-1” used by six people. Fix → personal identities, even on store devices.

  • Client passwords

    Larder accounts for client staff who leave their job. Fix → federate with the client's IdP.

  • No break-glass

    The IdP is down; nobody can get in. Fix → emergency accounts, stored safely, tested.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Any company with more than a handful of SaaS tools.
  • B2B products with client users.
  • Before an audit or security review.
  • When joiners and leavers are frequent.

Skip it when…

  • Very small teams with two or three tools (still use strong passwords and MFA).
  • Public consumer logins: a different problem (customer identity).

8Self-check quiz

What's the difference between SSO and federation?

SSO: one login for your own tools. Federation: trusting another organisation's identity provider.

What does SCIM do?

Creates and removes accounts in tools automatically.

Why federate with business clients?

Their staff use their own accounts and lose access when they leave their company.

What triggered Larder's change?

A leaver kept helpdesk and billing access for three weeks.

try it

List every tool your team logs in to, and mark which support SSO and automatic removal. Pick someone who left in the last six months and check which tools still have their account. Fix those today, and plan SSO for the rest.

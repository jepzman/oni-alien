Capability Map

TL;DR

A capability map lists what the business must be able to do (sell, fulfil, deliver, serve, expand), in a few levels, independent of systems, teams and projects. It changes slowly, so it's a stable frame for big questions. Colour it for the question at hand (readiness for next year, cost, risk), and the conversation moves from “which projects?” to “which abilities are weak?”. Then map systems and projects onto it.

  • Family: the landscape
  • Origin: business capability modelling (enterprise architecture; BIZBOK, TOGAF)
  • Lives in: one page, coloured per question, reviewed yearly
  • Pairs with: application portfolio, architecture roadmap, business drivers

1The situation Larder, 1 March 2027

At the March board meeting an investor asks Ana: is Larder ready for four more cities, a new country and more business clients this year? Ana has a list of systems and a list of projects. Neither answers the question, because the question is about what Larder must be able to do, not which software it runs or which projects are underway.

  • Systems lists answer “what do we run?”, not “what can we do?”.
  • Project lists mix the important with the merely active.
  • Executives can't see where the weak spots are.

2Real-life analogy the hospital's services

A hospital plans from its services: emergency care, maternity, cardiology, imaging, pharmacy. The list doesn't say which building, team or machine provides each; it says what the hospital must be able to do. When it plans a new wing, it colours the list: which services are ready, which need investment. Buildings come after.

3How it solves it what we must do, coloured by readiness

Ana drew seven areas with three capabilities each and coloured them for 2027 with Femke, Joost and Grace in an hour. Red: languages (French and Dutch), Belgian rules (VAT, e-invoicing), city setup without code. Yellow: stock (mid-migration), vans, business self-service, data. The board saw five boxes to fund, not sixty projects, and asked better questions: why is city setup still red after ADR 0003?

  1. List level-1 capabilities

    Five to eight areas of what the business does, as nouns or verbs.

  2. Break into level 2

    Three to six capabilities each; stop there for most uses.

  3. Keep systems out

    No product names; a capability outlives any system.

  4. Colour for one question

    Readiness, cost, risk, strategic importance: one at a time.

  5. Agree colours with the business

    Not the architect alone; an hour with the people who know.

  6. Map systems and projects onto it

    Which systems serve which capability; where projects land.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • A stable frame for big questions.
  • Executives see weak spots at a glance.
  • Investment by ability, not by loudest project.
  • Systems and projects get context.
  • Shared language between business and IT.

What we lose

  • Abstract for some audiences.
  • Easy to over-model (level 4, 300 boxes).
  • Colours are judgement.
  • Needs refreshing when the business changes.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Systems as capabilities

    “WMS B” on the map. Fix → write what the business does, not what it runs.

  • Too many levels

    300 boxes nobody reads. Fix → level 2 is enough for most questions.

  • Colouring alone

    The architect's opinion in colour. Fix → colour with the business.

  • One map, all questions

    Colours mean five things. Fix → one colouring per question.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Annual planning and board questions about readiness.
  • New markets, mergers, big programmes.
  • Deciding where to invest or cut.
  • Mapping applications and projects to purpose.

Skip it when…

  • Small teams with one product.
  • Detailed design: too coarse.

8Self-check quiz

What does a capability map describe?

What the business must be able to do, independent of systems and projects.

Why keep systems off it?

Capabilities outlive systems; the map stays stable.

What does colouring add?

An answer to one question, e.g. readiness for 2027.

Which Larder capabilities were red?

City setup, languages and Belgian rules.

try it

List your organisation's five to eight main capabilities and three to five under each. Colour them with two business people for one question (readiness for next year). Compare the red boxes with your current project list.

remember!!

Plan from the services, not the buildings. Colour for one question.

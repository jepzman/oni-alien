Application Portfolio (TIME)

TL;DR

Applications pile up: SaaS trials that stuck, tools for one team, systems you're half-way off. Keep an application portfolio: one list of every application, with its owner, cost, the capabilities it serves, and a TIME verdict: Tolerate (keep, don't invest), Invest (keep and improve), Migrate (replace with something better) or Eliminate (switch off). Sort by business value and technical fit, review yearly, and let Eliminate pay for Invest.

1The situation Larder, 19 July 2027

Marco asks a simple question: how many applications do we pay for, and what for? Nobody knows. Ana and Eli count twenty-three, about €61,000 a month, including a survey tool and a second chat tool nobody uses, a legacy reporting database, and StockPilot, still running during the migration. Nobody owned the whole list, so nothing ever left it.

  • Tools accumulate; nobody's job is to remove them.
  • Spend, ownership and purpose are scattered.
  • Every tool looks equally permanent.

2Real-life analogy clearing the garage

Clearing out a garage works with four labelled piles: keep and use more, keep but don't spend on, replace soon, and throw away. Every item goes on exactly one pile; nothing stays unsorted in the corner. Once a year is enough, if you actually carry the bin bags out.

3How it solves it every application in one of four boxes

Ana, Eli and Marco listed all 23 with owner, monthly cost and the capabilities each serves, then placed each on business value and technical fit. Four were eliminated (≈ €2,300 a month), two put on migrate with dates, and the invest box matched the capability map's weak spots. Each application now has a named owner, and new SaaS needs a line in the table before a contract.

  1. List every application

    Including SaaS on company cards; ask finance for the bank lines.

  2. Add owner, cost, users, capabilities

    One line each; a name, not a team.

  3. Score value and fit

    Business value (from the capability map) and technical fit (from engineers).

  4. Give each a TIME verdict

    Tolerate, Invest, Migrate or Eliminate.

  5. Act on Eliminate and Migrate

    Dates and owners; use exit plans.

  6. Review yearly, gate new tools

    A line in the table before any new contract.

Vocabulary for the exam

Application portfolio
The managed list of all applications.
TIME
Tolerate, Invest, Migrate, Eliminate.
Business value
How much an application matters to capabilities.
Technical fit
How healthy, secure and maintainable it is.
Shadow IT
Tools bought outside any central list.
Rationalisation
Reducing the number of applications.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • Spend visible, with owners.
  • Unused tools removed: ≈ €2,300 a month.
  • Migrations dated instead of drifting.
  • Investment matches capabilities.
  • A gate for new tools.

What we lose

  • Collecting the list takes a few days the first time.
  • Scores are judgement.
  • Teams defend their tools.
  • Needs the yearly habit.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Missing the card spend

    Half the SaaS is on credit cards. Fix → ask finance for bank lines, not just contracts.

  • Teams as owners

    “Engineering” owns the BI tool. Fix → one name per application.

  • Verdicts without action

    Eliminate, but still paid. Fix → dates and owners for every Eliminate and Migrate.

  • No gate

    New tools appear the next month. Fix → a line in the table before any contract.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Yearly budget and planning.
  • After growth, mergers or a hiring spree.
  • Before big migrations.
  • When finance asks what you pay for.

Skip it when…

  • Tiny companies with a handful of tools.
  • As a substitute for exit plans on the critical ones.

8Self-check quiz

What does TIME stand for?

Tolerate, Invest, Migrate, Eliminate.

Which two axes decide the verdict?

Business value and technical fit.

Where did the hidden tools come from?

SaaS bought by teams, often on company cards.

How do you stop new sprawl?

A line in the portfolio before any new contract.

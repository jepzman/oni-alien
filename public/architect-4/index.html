Integration & the Enterprise Landscape

Volume 4 of The Solution Architect's Notebook: how architects connect systems, protect their boundaries, see the whole landscape and change it safely. 15 notes in four collections: integration styles, boundaries, the landscape, and changing it. Every note follows Ana at Larder from March to September 2027, through a warehouse migration and three city launches, and ends with a try it exercise for your own project.

the story so far

March 2027. Larder is moving its warehouses from StockPilot to WMS B, store by store, while Utrecht and The Hague open and Antwerp gets ready for September. Korenwolf wants to order from its own systems, a second billing flow is live, and the landscape now holds more than twenty applications. This volume follows Ana from March to September 2027: connecting systems without tangling them, drawing honest boundaries, mapping the landscape, and changing it while the business keeps running.

Integration styles a style per need: events, APIs, files

  1. 01Choosing an Integration StyleShared database, files, APIs or events: choose per need, and mix them behind one adapter.p. 01
  2. 02API-First DesignWrite the contract first, in the consumer's words, review it with them, and build against it.p. 02
  3. 03Event-Driven IntegrationTreat events as owned, registered contracts with known consumers and compatible changes.p. 03
  4. 04Batch & File TransferFiles on a schedule, done properly: checked, loaded once, and loud when late.p. 04

Boundaries keep other models out, and change contracts safely

  1. 05Anti-Corruption LayerTranslate another system's model at the border so it never leaks into yours.p. 05
  2. 06Backend for FrontendOne small backend per kind of client, shaped for what that client needs.p. 06
  3. 07API Versioning StrategyAdd without breaking, version when you must, and retire old versions on a schedule.p. 07
  4. 08Canonical Data Model (and Its Traps)Why one model for everything fails, and the small shared language that works instead.p. 08

★The nine architect ratings what a good architecture practice is judged on

Every note scores the pattern from 1 (poor) to 5 (great) on the same nine things, comparing an app without the pattern to the same app with it.

Clarity
A newcomer can understand the system and the reasoning behind it.
Stakeholder alignment
Business, product and engineering share the same what and why.
Decision speed
How quickly the team can choose and get back to building.
Risk reduction
The big risks are surfaced and handled before they bite.
Adaptability
How cheaply the direction can change later.
Traceability
Months later, anyone can find why something was done.
Cost awareness
Money and effort are part of the decision, not a surprise.
Low overhead
Time and ceremony it takes. High = light.
Team autonomy
Teams can decide and act without waiting for the architect.

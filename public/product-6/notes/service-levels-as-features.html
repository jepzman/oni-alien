Service Levels as Features

TL;DR

A service level in a contract is a promise, and promises customers can't see breed doubt. Make service levels features: show status live, generate the report automatically using the contract's exact definition, and pay credits automatically when you miss. The promise becomes part of the product, the account manager stops building spreadsheets, and trust grows even on bad days.

  • Family: business customers
  • Origin: SaaS status pages and SLA reporting; service design
  • Lives in: the business portal, the invoice and the monthly report
  • Pairs with: availability maths (Ana), B2B vs B2C, dashboards that get used

1The situation Larder, 4 October 2027

Korenwolf's contract promises 98 % complete deliveries by 09:00. Each month Mo builds the report by hand from three exports; it arrives on the tenth; credits for missed mornings are argued by email. Office managers have no idea whether today's delivery is on track until it's late. A promise nobody can see.

  • Hand-made reports are late and disputed.
  • Customers learn about misses after the fact.
  • Arguing over credits damages the relationship.

2Real-life analogy the departure board

A railway that promises trains on time shows each train's status on a live departure board, publishes monthly punctuality, and pays delay compensation automatically to season-ticket holders. Passengers still dislike delays, but they trust the railway to be honest about them.

3How it solves it live status, automatic report, automatic credits

Grace's team built the promise into the business portal: live status for each office's morning delivery (picked, loaded, on the way, delivered); a monthly report generated on the first working day, per office, using the contract's definition; and credits added to the next invoice automatically when a morning misses. Mo's report work went from two days a month to none; complaints to Hanna about missing deliveries: none in Q4.

  1. Write the promise precisely

    The contract's definition, as a calculation.

  2. Show it live

    Today's status, where customers look.

  3. Report automatically

    Same definition, on a fixed day.

  4. Pay credits automatically

    No arguing.

  5. Alert yourselves first

    Before the customer sees a miss.

  6. Review with the client

    Trends, not disputes.

# Service levels in the business portal · live from 4 Oct 2027 · owner: Grace

Promise     98 % of office mornings complete (every ordered item, or an agreed swap) by 09:00
Definition  per office, per delivery morning, as in the contract (§4.2); not per crate
Live        portal → "This morning": picked 06:40 · loaded 07:05 · on the way · delivered 08:31
Report      generated 1st working day, per office + total; same numbers Mo and Ruben see
Credits     missed morning → 10 % of that delivery credited on the next invoice, automatically
Alerts      Mo and the store manager alerted at 08:15 if a delivery is at risk
Q4 2027     Korenwolf 99.0 % · Mo's report work 2 days/month → 0 · complaints to Hanna: 0

tools: the portal, delivery events from Dispatch, the billing service for credits, a scheduled report job.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • Trust: the promise is visible.
  • No hand-made reports.
  • No credit disputes.
  • Early alerts to fix mornings.
  • A selling point for new clients.

What we lose

  • Misses are visible too.
  • Automatic credits cost money.
  • Definitions must be exact.
  • Building it takes a few sprints.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Vague promises

    “High completeness.” Fix → an exact definition.

  • Hiding misses

    Only the monthly average. Fix → live status, honestly.

  • Manual credits

    Argued case by case. Fix → automatic, by rule.

  • Different numbers

    Report vs portal disagree. Fix → one calculation.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Contracts with service levels.
  • Business clients that ask for reports.
  • When account managers build reports by hand.
  • Selling to procurement teams.

Skip it when…

  • Consumer products without contractual promises (use a status page).

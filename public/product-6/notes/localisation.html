Localisation for a New Country

TL;DR

Localisation is more than translation. A new country brings language (with the right tone and dialect), money and tax, rules, product data, and habits. Work through each, get product data right at its source, and above all test with local customers before launch, more than once. The problems customers notice are rarely the words.

1The situation Larder, 2 August 2027

The French version of the app shipped in May, translated. In July, forty Antwerp households tested it, and the problems weren't the words: product names in Netherlands Dutch that sound odd to Flemish customers, prices not showing Belgian VAT correctly, a curt French swap message, and 212 products whose names and VAT differed between systems. Translated, not localised.

  • Translation fixes words, not prices, rules or tone.
  • Product data errors multiply across systems.
  • Only local customers notice what feels foreign.

  • Data Ownership & Master Data (Ana, vol. 4): the same week, Ana's ADR 0024 makes the catalogue the single master for product names and VAT.

2Real-life analogy the cookbook for another country

A good cookbook adapted for another country isn't just translated. It uses local measures, ingredients you can actually buy there, the oven temperatures people use, and every recipe is tested in a local kitchen. Otherwise the words are right and the cake is wrong.

3How it solves it language, money, rules, products, habits, testing

Grace made a country checklist. Language: French and Flemish Dutch by native writers, tone checked with testers. Money and tax: Belgian VAT on prices and invoices, Bancontact first. Rules: age limits by city. Products: names and VAT from one master, the catalogue her team owns, with French and Dutch names (the 212 mismatches fixed there). Habits: bigger weekend slot orders. Testing: forty households in July, again in September. Antwerp opened on 18 October: 2.4 tickets per 100 orders in week one.

  1. Language, with native writers

    Dialect and tone, not just words.

  2. Money and tax

    Prices, VAT, invoices, payment methods.

  3. Rules

    Age limits, consumer law, data rules.

  4. Product data at the source

    One master, every language.

  5. Habits

    How people shop, when, and what they expect.

  6. Test with local customers

    Before launch, twice.

Vocabulary for the exam

Internationalisation (i18n)
Building so a product can be adapted.
Localisation (l10n)
Adapting it to a specific place.
Locale
Language plus region, e.g. nl-BE, fr-BE.
Master data
The single source for shared data like products.
Tone of voice
How the product sounds, beyond the words.
Beta test
Real customers trying it before launch.

# Country checklist: Belgium (Antwerp, opening 18 Oct 2027) · owner: Grace

Language  fr-BE, nl-BE by native writers · tone reviewed with testers (swap message rewritten)
Money     prices incl. Belgian VAT (6 % / 21 %) · invoices in Belgian format · Bancontact first
Rules     age checks per city setting (BE: 16 beer/wine, 18 spirits) · consumer law: help pages (Lena)
Products  names + VAT category from the catalogue (master, ADR 0024) · 212 mismatches fixed by 30 Aug
Habits    more weekend slot orders · substitutions: "same brand or nothing" more common (flag default?)
Testing   40 Antwerp households, 12–23 Jul (round 1) · 9–20 Sep (round 2: 6 issues, all fixed)
Week 1    2.4 tickets per 100 orders (Rotterdam week 1: 7.1)

tools: native writers, a translation management system, locale settings, the catalogue as product master, a local beta group.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • A product that feels local.
  • Correct prices and tax.
  • Clean product data everywhere.
  • Fewer tickets at launch.
  • A checklist for the next country.

What we lose

  • Native writers and testers cost money.
  • Two rounds of testing take time.
  • More languages to maintain.
  • Some habits need product changes.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Translation only

    Machine-translated strings. Fix → native writers, tone checked.

  • Fixing data downstream

    Names corrected in the app. Fix → fix it at the master.

  • One country, one language

    French only in Belgium. Fix → check every language spoken.

  • No local testing

    Launch is the test. Fix → local customers, twice.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Entering a new country or language.
  • Serving a region with different rules or habits.
  • When translated products underperform.
  • Before marketing abroad.

Skip it when…

  • Markets with the same language, money and rules (a city playbook is enough).

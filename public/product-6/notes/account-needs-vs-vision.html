Account Needs vs Product Vision

TL;DR

A big account's request can pull a product away from its vision, one exception at a time. Before saying yes or no, find the need behind the request, then apply a vision test: would this serve all customers like them, or only this one? Usually there's an answer that meets the need for everyone. When there isn't, say no clearly, with the reason, and offer what you can. Exceptions for one become the product's shape.

  • Origin: B2B product strategy; “build for the segment, not the customer”

1The situation Larder, 13 September 2027

Three months before Korenwolf's renewal, Ruben asks for stock reserved only for Korenwolf and a dedicated picker every Monday morning. Mo says the renewal depends on it. Grace's vision is one shared store serving everyone well. Yes builds a Korenwolf store inside Larder; no risks the biggest account.

  • Big accounts' requests feel non-negotiable.
  • Each exception makes the next one easier to ask for.
  • Saying no without an alternative damages the account.

2Real-life analogy the private row

A theatre's biggest subscriber asks for their own private row every night. The theatre can't give away a row, but it can offer every subscriber priority booking a week early. The subscriber's real need, a good seat without stress, is met, and the theatre stays a theatre.

3How it solves it the need, the vision test, a solution for all

Grace and Mo asked about the need: Monday mornings with nothing missing, because missing items there reach Korenwolf's board. The vision test: would this serve every business account? The answer did: stock reserved the evening before for every business account's standing orders, and standing orders picked first from 06:00. Korenwolf's Monday completeness rose from 96.8 % to 99.1 %; the other three offices improved too. The renewal was signed in December.

  1. Ask for the need

    What goes wrong without it, for whom?

  2. Apply the vision test

    Would it serve every customer like them?

  3. Design for the segment

    A setting or feature for all.

  4. Say no clearly when it fails

    With the reason and an alternative.

  5. Involve the account manager

    They carry the answer.

  6. Measure for all accounts

    Not only the one who asked.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • The account kept.
  • The vision kept.
  • Every client benefits.
  • No custom forks.
  • A precedent for future asks.

What we lose

  • Takes longer than a quick yes.
  • Not every ask generalises.
  • Account managers carry hard answers.
  • Reserved stock can't go to households.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Yes as asked

    A Korenwolf picker. Fix → find the need; solve for all.

  • No without help

    “Not in our vision.” Fix → an alternative that meets the need.

  • Vision as an excuse

    Ignoring real needs. Fix → test honestly.

  • Measuring one client

    Others forgotten. Fix → measure for all accounts.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Big accounts' special requests.
  • Before renewals.
  • When custom work starts to pile up.
  • Setting precedents for a segment.

Skip it when…

  • Truly unique, paid custom work agreed as a separate service (rare, and priced).

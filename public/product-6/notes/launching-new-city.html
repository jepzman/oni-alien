Launching a New City

TL;DR

Launching a new city (or store, or region) gets cheaper and safer when it's a playbook, not a project. Separate what's configuration (the same everywhere, set without code) from what's local (store layout, offers, partners, help pages), give the launch a fixed rhythm (staff orders, waitlist, hypercare), and agree the few numbers to watch daily in the first weeks. Update the playbook after every launch.

1The situation Larder, 5 April 2027

Utrecht opens on 12 April, The Hague in June, Antwerp in the autumn. Rotterdam's launch a year ago took Grace's team six weeks of app work, a long checklist and a lot of memory. Nobody wrote down what was configuration, what was local, and what to watch in the first weeks.

  • Launches from memory repeat old mistakes.
  • Product work per city doesn't scale to six cities.
  • The first weeks decide whether customers come back.

2Real-life analogy the new branch

A restaurant chain opening a new branch uses the same recipe book and kitchen layout everywhere. What's local is the specials board, the suppliers and the opening hours. And for the first month, the manager checks a few things every evening: covers, waiting times, complaints, who comes back.

3How it solves it configuration, local edges, rhythm, five numbers

Grace wrote a city playbook from Rotterdam's lessons. Configuration is the same everywhere and set in the admin tool: zones, slots, prices, hours (ADR 0003). Local: store layout in the picker app, first-week offers, help pages, partners. A fixed rhythm: staff orders a week before, the waitlist push on the day, two weeks of hypercare. Five numbers watched daily: orders, on time, swap refunds, tickets per 100 orders, second orders within 14 days. Utrecht's app work: six days instead of six weeks.

  1. List last launch's work

    Every task, from the checklist and memory.

  2. Sort: configuration or local

    Push everything you can into configuration.

  3. Fix the rhythm

    Staff orders, waitlist, opening, hypercare.

  4. Choose the first-week numbers

    Five, watched daily.

  5. Run it, then update it

    A short review after every launch.

  6. Give it an owner

    Someone keeps the playbook current.

Vocabulary for the exam

Playbook
A repeatable, written way to do something.
Configuration
Settings changed without code.
Localisation
Adapting to a place: language, rules, habits.
Hypercare
Extra support right after a launch.
Activation
A new customer's first good experience.
Second-order rate
Share of new customers who order again.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • Launches in days of product work.
  • Lessons kept between launches.
  • Calm openings: a known rhythm.
  • Early warnings from five numbers.
  • Others can run it, not just Grace.

What we lose

  • Upfront work to configure everything.
  • Local surprises still happen.
  • Playbooks go stale without updates.
  • Not every market fits the same mould.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Everything local

    Each city coded by hand. Fix → push it into configuration.

  • No first-week numbers

    Problems found in month two. Fix → five numbers, daily.

  • Never updated

    v1 forever. Fix → a review after every launch.

  • Copy-paste abroad

    A country treated as a city. Fix → add language, rules and money checks.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Opening cities, stores, regions.
  • Rolling out a product to new customer groups.
  • Any launch you'll do more than twice.
  • After a launch that went badly.

Skip it when…

  • A one-off launch you'll never repeat.

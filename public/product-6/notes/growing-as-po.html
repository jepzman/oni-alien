Growing as a Product Owner

TL;DR

Product owners are busy enough to stop growing without noticing. Once a year, look back at decisions you're proud of and ones you'd redo, ask the people around you a few specific questions, name your strengths and growth areas, and choose two things to get better at, with your manager's support and a quarterly check-in. Growth comes from choosing, not from being busy.

  • Family: growing yourself
  • Origin: career development practice; after-action reviews applied to yourself
  • Lives in: a yearly review, your notebooks and quarterly check-ins

1The situation Larder, 8 November 2027

Grace has been at Larder for two years. Her area has grown from one problem (swaps) to three teams, a platform, business customers and Larder Plus. She's busy every day, but she can't say what she's got better at, what she hasn't, or what she wants next. Priya asks: where do you want to be in a year?

  • Busy isn't the same as growing.
  • Blind spots need other people's eyes.
  • Without a choice, growth is accidental.

2Real-life analogy the climber's season review

At the end of a season a climber reviews it: the routes climbed, what went well, where she fell, what her partners noticed, and two skills to train next season. Not more climbing; better climbing.

3How it solves it look back, ask, choose two

Grace looked back through her notebooks: decisions she's proud of, ones she'd redo. She asked six people (Priya, Ben, Noor, Mo, Joost, Ana) three questions each. Strengths they named: discovery, numbers, keeping promises honest. Growth areas: delegating, saying no to Femke sooner, strategy beyond a quarter. She chose two for the next year: hire and grow a product owner, and write the 2028 strategy for ordering. Agreed with Priya, checked every quarter.

  1. Look back with evidence

    Notebooks, decisions, results.

  2. Ask specific questions

    What should I keep, start, stop?

  3. Name strengths and gaps

    In the words people used.

  4. Choose two

    Not ten; two you'll really work on.

  5. Make them concrete

    A project, a habit, a mentor.

  6. Agree with your manager

    And check in quarterly.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • Growth on purpose.
  • Blind spots found by others.
  • Clear asks for your manager.
  • Better product work, too.
  • A story of your progress.

What we lose

  • Uncomfortable feedback.
  • Time for reflection.
  • Two things means not ten.
  • Needs follow-through.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Only your view

    Self-assessment alone. Fix → ask six people.

  • Vague questions

    “Any feedback?” Fix → keep, start, stop.

  • Ten goals

    Nothing changes. Fix → two.

  • No follow-up

    A plan in a drawer. Fix → quarterly check-ins.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Once a year, at least.
  • When your role has grown.
  • Before asking for a promotion or change.
  • When you feel busy but stuck.

Skip it when…

  • Never.

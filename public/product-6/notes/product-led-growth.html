Product-Led Growth

TL;DR

Product-led growth means the product itself does much of the work of winning and keeping customers: a first experience good enough to come back for, offers at the right moment (a trial after a good experience, not a banner), and sharing that's natural. Measure activation and retention, not just sign-ups. Paid growth into a product that doesn't keep people is water into a leaky bucket.

1The situation Larder, 6 September 2027

Larder grows mostly through paid ads and city launch campaigns. A new customer's first order is often their last: only 41 % order again within 30 days. Plus has 5,400 members, but most found it through a banner. Marketing spend per new customer keeps rising. Water into a leaky bucket.

  • Paid growth stops when the budget does.
  • A bad first experience loses the customer for good.
  • Offers at the wrong moment are ignored.

2Real-life analogy the café that needs no ads

A neighbourhood café grows without advertising: the first visit is so good people come back, a loyalty card makes the tenth coffee free, and regulars bring friends. The café itself does the selling.

3How it solves it first order, the right moment, sharing

Grace worked on three moments. First order: a welcome screen explaining swaps and the delivery promise, a guaranteed slot, and a follow-up asking how it went: second orders within 30 days rose from 41 % to 49 %. Plus: offered after the second good order, first month free, instead of a banner: trial-to-paid 62 %. Sharing: give €10, get €10, from the order-delivered screen: 12 % of new customers by December. Paid cost per new customer fell 18 %.

  1. Measure activation

    The first good experience and the second order.

  2. Fix the first experience

    Explain what's different; remove first-time risks.

  3. Offer at the right moment

    After a good experience, not on day one.

  4. Make sharing natural

    At a happy moment, with a reason to share.

  5. Watch retention cohorts

    Not just sign-ups.

  6. Then spend on acquisition

    Into a bucket that holds water.

Vocabulary for the exam

Product-led growth

Retention

Trial
Using a paid offer free for a period.
Acquisition cost
What it costs to win a new customer.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • Customers stay.
  • Cheaper growth.
  • Offers that convert.
  • Customers bring customers.
  • Marketing and product share goals.

What we lose

  • Slower than switching on ads.
  • Referral fraud to watch.
  • More product work on onboarding.
  • Not every product spreads by sharing.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Sign-ups only

    Downloads celebrated. Fix → activation and retention.

  • Offers on day one

    Plus before the first order. Fix → after a good experience.

  • Forced sharing

    Spam your contacts. Fix → a happy moment, a real reason.

  • Ads into leaks

    Spending more as retention falls. Fix → fix the first experience first.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Consumer products with repeat use.
  • When acquisition costs rise.
  • Subscriptions and memberships.
  • When first-time customers don't return.

Skip it when…

  • Products sold through long sales cycles only (business deals).

Handing Over a Product Area

TL;DR

Handing over a product area well means passing on the why, not just the backlog: a state-of-the-product document (purpose, outcomes, open bets, decisions with reasons, promises made, people), introductions in person, a period of shadowing, then the new owner leading with you as backup, and finally a clear, public step out. Hand over too little and they stumble; never let go and they never own it.

  • Origin: transition planning; leadership hand-off practice

1The situation Larder, 13 December 2027

Jonas starts on 3 January to take over Larder for Business. Everything he needs lives in Grace's head and notebooks: why decisions were made, which promises Korenwolf heard, which bets are half-done, who to call. Grace is tempted to do a one-hour walkthrough, and stay on every thread just in case.

  • Backlogs pass on what, not why.
  • Promises made to clients get lost.
  • A previous owner who never leaves keeps the new one from owning it.

2Real-life analogy the relay changeover

In a relay, both runners are in the changeover zone at full speed for a few metres, the baton is passed hand to hand, and then the first runner steps aside. Dropping the baton loses the race; so does the first runner trying to run alongside to the finish.

3How it solves it the why on paper, people in person, then step out

Grace wrote a state-of-the-product document: why Larder for Business exists, outcomes and numbers, open bets, decisions with reasons, promises made to clients, people. She introduced Jonas in person to Hanna and Ruben (with Mo) and to Joost's morning crew. Two weeks of shadowing; four weeks of Jonas leading with Grace as backup; then Grace stepped out on 28 February 2028 and said so publicly. Jonas's first decision alone: the February request review with Mo. Grace kept ordering, the apps, Plus, and the 2028 strategy.

  1. Write the state of the product

    Why, outcomes, bets, decisions, promises, people.

  2. Introduce people in person

    Clients, partners, operations.

  3. Let them shadow

    A couple of weeks, in real meetings.

  4. Let them lead, with you as backup

    Weeks, not months.

  5. Step out, publicly

    A date, announced.

  6. Stay available, not involved

    Questions welcome; decisions theirs.

Vocabulary for the exam

Handover
Passing ownership to someone else.
State of the product
A document of the product's why, numbers, bets and decisions.
Shadowing
Joining someone's work to learn it.
Backup
Available for help, not making the decisions.
Decision log
Decisions with their reasons.
Stepping out
Visibly giving up ownership.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • The why survives the handover.
  • Promises kept to clients.
  • A confident new owner.
  • Grace freed for strategy.
  • Clear ownership for everyone.

What we lose

  • Writing the document takes days.
  • Letting go is hard.
  • Some decisions will differ from yours.
  • Weeks of overlap.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Backlog only

    Tickets, no reasons. Fix → the why and the promises.

  • Email introductions

    Clients meet a name. Fix → in person.

  • Never letting go

    Grace on every thread. Fix → a public step-out date.

  • Disappearing

    Gone on day one. Fix → shadow, then backup.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Handing a product area to a new owner.
  • Leaving a role or a company.
  • Splitting a growing area.
  • Parental leave and long absences (a temporary version).

Skip it when…

  • Never; only the length changes.

Self-Service for Business Customers

TL;DR

When business customers must ask a person for every change, that person becomes a keyboard and a bottleneck. Self-service lets customers do routine things themselves (change, skip, add, see), any time, inside sensible rules (cut-off times, approvals). The account manager keeps the relationship: reviews, renewals, growth. Start with the changes people request most.

1The situation Larder, 7 June 2027

Office managers change their standing orders by emailing Mo before 08:00 the day before, as agreed for January. With four office clients, Mo spends about twelve hours a week re-typing changes, mistakes reach invoices, and nobody can change anything after Mo goes home.

  • Routine changes don't need a person.
  • Re-typing creates errors.
  • The account manager's time goes on admin, not the relationship.
meanwhile, in Ana’s notebook
  • Backend for Frontend (Ana, vol. 4): the same day, Ana's portal backend cuts the order screen from seven calls and 2.8 seconds to one call and 0.6.

2Real-life analogy online banking

Banks once required a phone call to the branch for every transfer. Online banking let customers do it themselves, any time, within limits. Branch staff stopped typing transfers and started giving advice.

3How it solves it routine changes, with rules; people for the relationship

The business portal went live in May with the most-requested changes from Mo's inbox: skip a week, change quantities, add one-off items, see the next delivery, until 16:00 the day before. Orders above an office's budget go to its approver automatically. Mo keeps the relationship. Four weeks in: 80 % of changes in the portal; Mo's re-typing from twelve hours a week to two; invoice corrections from nine a month to one.

  1. Read the inbox

    The requests people make most.

  2. Build those first

    Skip, change, add, see.

  3. Set rules

    Cut-off times, approvals, budgets.

  4. Move people to it gently

    Show office managers; keep email for a month.

  5. Free the account manager

    For reviews, renewals, growth.

  6. Measure the shift

    Share of changes self-served, errors, time saved.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • Changes any time, not office hours.
  • Fewer errors from re-typing.
  • The account manager freed for the relationship.
  • Scales to more clients.
  • Office managers in control.

What we lose

  • A portal to build and support.
  • Some clients prefer a person.
  • Rules must be clear.
  • Training needed at first.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Building everything

    A portal of every possible setting. Fix → the most-requested changes.

  • No rules

    Changes at 05:00. Fix → cut-off times and approvals.

  • Forcing it overnight

    Email stopped on day one. Fix → a gentle move, with training.

  • Account manager replaced

    Nobody owns the relationship. Fix → free them for it.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Business clients making routine changes through people.
  • When account managers are buried in admin.
  • Before growing the number of clients.
  • When errors come from re-typing.

Skip it when…

  • Rare, complex changes that need judgment (keep a person).

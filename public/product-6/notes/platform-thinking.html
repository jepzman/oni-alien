Platform Thinking

TL;DR

When several products need the same capability, copies drift apart and every change happens three times. Platform thinking turns that capability into one owned, documented internal product with its own customers (the other teams) and its own outcomes. Do it when the capability is truly shared and stable enough; don't build a platform for something only one product needs.

1The situation Larder, 19 July 2027

Substitution logic now lives in three places: the consumer app's approvals, the business standing orders, and Larder Plus's never-swap preferences. Each team changed it separately; a rule fixed in one place stayed broken in another, and the picker app received three slightly different kinds of swap instruction.

  • Copies of a capability drift apart.
  • Every rule change has to be made several times.
  • Nobody owns the capability as a whole.

2Real-life analogy one waterworks

Every house in a town once had its own well, each tested differently. A shared waterworks with a published standard, a team that runs it and a tap in every house means every house gets the same safe water, and nobody digs a well again.

3How it solves it one owner, an interface, internal customers, outcomes

Grace made substitutions a platform: one capability, owned by Ben's team, with a documented interface: offer a swap, record the answer, apply rules (never-swap flags, allergens, price limits), tell the picker. Its customers are internal: the consumer app, the business portal, Plus and the picker app. Its own outcomes: answers within the window, wrong swaps per 1,000 orders. A rule now changes once, everywhere; the three copies were retired by October.

  1. Find the shared capability

    Used by several products, copied or about to be.

  2. Check it's stable enough

    Its core rules shouldn't change weekly.

  3. Give it an owner

    One team, with a product owner's eye.

  4. Define the interface

    Documented, versioned, easy to use.

  5. Treat other teams as customers

    Roadmap, support, feedback.

  6. Give it outcomes

    And retire the copies.

Vocabulary for the exam

Platform
A shared capability other products build on.
Internal customer
Another team that uses your platform.
Interface
How others use the platform (an API).
Platform team
The team that owns and runs it.
Self-service
Teams use it without asking the platform team.
Duplication
The same logic in several places.

# Substitution platform · charter · 19 Jul 2027 · owner: Ben's team · PO: Grace

Does     offer a swap · record the customer's answer · apply rules (never-swap flags, allergens,
         price limit, business account settings) · send the instruction to the picker app
Doesn't  choose suggestions (Catalogue: similarity ranking) · refunds (Payments)
Interface  documented API + events (swap.offered, swap.answered, swap.applied), versioned
Customers  consumer app · business portal + standing orders · Larder Plus · picker app
Outcomes   answered within the window ≥ 85 % · wrong swaps packed ≤ 1 per 1,000 orders
Copies retired  consumer app rules (Aug) · business orders (Sep) · Plus preferences (Oct)

tools: a documented API and events, a platform team, an internal roadmap and support channel, usage metrics per internal customer.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • One change, everywhere.
  • Consistent for customers and pickers.
  • Clear ownership.
  • Faster new products on top.
  • Its own outcomes.

What we lose

  • A team dedicated to it.
  • Dependencies for other teams.
  • Interface design takes care.
  • Too early and it fits nobody.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Platform too early

    Built for one user. Fix → wait until it's truly shared.

  • No owner

    Everyone's, so nobody's. Fix → one team.

  • Hard to use

    Teams copy it again. Fix → a simple, documented interface.

  • Copies kept

    Old logic lingers. Fix → retire them on a date.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Capabilities used by several products.
  • When copies drift and bugs repeat.
  • Before launching another product that needs it.
  • Growing product organisations.

Skip it when…

  • Capabilities only one product needs.

8Self-check quiz

When does a capability deserve a platform?

When several products need it and its rules are stable enough.

Who are the platform's customers?

Internal teams: consumer app, portal, Plus, picker app.

What outcomes does it have?

Answers within the window; wrong swaps per 1,000 orders.

What changed?

A rule changes once, everywhere; three copies retired.

try it

Find one piece of logic that exists in more than one of your products. List where it lives and how the copies differ, and sketch who would own it as a platform and what its interface would be.

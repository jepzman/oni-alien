When Do We Go Over Their Heads?

TL;DR

Escalating is legitimate; how you do it decides whether you have a relationship afterwards. Use a ladder agreed in advance, with time limits at each rung. Tell the person first that you're going to escalate, and why. Bring a proposal, not a complaint. Escalate the problem, never the person. And once it's solved, climb back down and work with them as before.

  • Family: other people's moves
  • Lives in: Schedule 7, §4 (escalation ladder)
  • Pairs with: governance (vol. 2), test environments

1The situation The Grid, July 2028

One of the design authority's four conditions was a performance test by July. It needs a production-sized test environment from MetroVolt IT, promised for 15 May.

  • On 29 May, 12 June and 26 June, Hollis's team says “next week”. On 10 July, the same.
  • Without the test, the go / no-go criteria for wave 1 can't be met. The go / no-go is 6 September.
  • Hollis's team is short of people since Horizon. Escalating over Hollis feels like betraying the man who overruled Hana gracefully and then asked her to teach his team.

2Both sides of the table the same moment, seen twice

MetroVolt Hollis

Hollis's infrastructure team lost three people in Horizon. He has four programmes asking for environments and no authority to buy more. Saying “next week” is easier than saying “I can't”. He'd rather not be escalated over, and he'd rather the problem were solved.

“I'm drowning. Please don't make it look like it's my fault.”

Wrenfield Ines

Ines needs the environment by the end of July. She's waited eight weeks, kindly. The contract has an escalation ladder for exactly this. The question is how to use it without losing Hollis, who she'll need every week until 2030.

“Escalate the environment, not Hollis.”

Most escalations are really requests for resources someone below doesn't have the authority to give. Escalating them helps the person you escalate past, if you do it right.

3Real-life analogy the referee and the captain

A team captain who disagrees with a decision doesn't shout at the referee or run to the league. She tells the referee she'll raise it, follows the agreed procedure with a clear request, and the next week shakes the referee's hand before kick-off. The procedure exists so that disagreements don't become feuds.

4How it works a ladder, tell them first, a proposal, then back down

The escalation ladder is in Schedule 7: working group (five days), then Elaine and Callum (five days), then Priya, with Graham as Wrenfield's executive counterpart. The issue had sat on rung one for eight weeks because nobody wanted to climb.

Tell the person first. On 10 July Ines goes to Hollis's desk: “I'm going to take the performance environment to Elaine this week, not because of you, but because it needs a decision your team can't make alone. Here's what I'll propose. Is there anything you'd change?” Hollis, relieved, suggests the proposal himself.

Bring a proposal. To Elaine: use the pilot's production-grade servers on two weekends (they're idle then), with MetroVolt IT's on-call cover, paid from the change pool. Elaine takes it to Priya the same day; Priya agrees in one email. The test runs on 22 and 29 July and passes with 18 % headroom.

Climb back down. On Monday, the working group runs as usual. Ines thanks Hollis's team publicly in the status report. Hollis says the escalation got him two extra contractors for the autumn.

  1. Agree the ladder in advance

    Rungs, time limits, names.

  2. Don't wait too long on a rung

    The time limit is the trigger.

  3. Tell the person first

    Why, and what you'll propose.

  4. Escalate the problem, not the person

    Resources, decisions, conflicts.

  5. Bring a proposal

    Something the senior person can say yes to.

  6. Climb back down

    Same meetings, same respect, credit given.

Vocabulary

5What the contract says the ladder in the contract

Lodestar SOW · Schedule 7, §4 (escalation)v1.1 · Aug 2027
rung 2

4.1 Issues not resolved by a working group within 5 Working Days are referred to the Programme Director and the Supplier's Engagement Manager, and then within a further 5 Working Days to the Sponsor and the Supplier's Executive.

we waited eight weeks on a five-day rung. that was our mistake, not Hollis's.

4.3 Escalation is a normal part of governance and does not imply fault.

we put this sentence in. it helps every time.

In plain words: a contractual escalation ladder, with the sentence that escalation implies no fault, lets you climb without it feeling like an accusation. Use the time limits; waiting too long is unkind to everyone.

9Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Waiting too long

    A five-day rung becomes eight weeks. Fix → use the time limits.

  • Going straight to the top

    The person below is humiliated. Fix → climb rung by rung.

  • Not telling them first

    They hear it from their boss. Fix → two minutes at their desk.

  • Escalating a complaint

    The senior person has nothing to approve. Fix → bring a proposal.

  • Staying escalated

    Every issue goes up from now on. Fix → climb back down.

10When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • An issue has sat past its time limit.
  • It needs a decision or resources the person can't give.
  • Delay threatens a milestone.

Skip it when…

  • The person can still solve it within the time limit: help them instead.

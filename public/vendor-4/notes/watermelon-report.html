Why Is Everything Green Except the Truth?

TL;DR

A status report built from team opinions turns green on the outside and red inside: each team is “on track” for its own sprint while the programme slips. Build the colour from measures with agreed thresholds: progress against plan, tests passed, rehearsal results, open serious defects. Make the overall colour the worst of them, put the numbers in the first line, and keep the teams' opinions underneath, where they belong.

  • Family: telling the truth
  • Lives in: delivery/lodestar/status/ (measures sheet)
  • Pairs with: the first status report (vol. 2), re-planning

1The situation The Grid, April 2028

Callum's weekly dashboard on Friday 14 April: four squads, four greens. Each squad lead has said honestly that their sprint is on track.

  • Oskar's own notes say 61 % of planned test cases are blocked by test environments.
  • Rehearsal 1, on Saturday 8 April, reconciled 98.1 % of balances. The criterion is 100 %.
  • The burn-up chart Hana keeps privately shows the build 2.4 weeks behind the baseline.
  • Every square on the dashboard is true. The picture is false.

2Both sides of the table the same moment, seen twice

MetroVolt Elaine

Elaine has been here before. Orion's reports were green for 22 months. When she sees four greens in April, two months before a pilot, she doesn't feel reassured; she feels the old dread. She'll trust a colour only if she can see what it's made of.

“Green again. What's it made of?”

Wrenfield Callum and Ines

Callum isn't hiding anything: he reports what the squads tell him. But squads see their sprint, not the programme. Ines needs a status built from measures that can't be green unless the programme is, before Vexmoor's assurance team finds the red inside.

“Opinions are honest. Measures are true.”

Watermelons are rarely lies. They're what you get when you add up local truths without measuring the whole.

3Real-life analogy the car dashboard

A car doesn't ask the engine, the brakes and the tyres how they feel. It measures: oil pressure, brake fluid, tyre pressure, each with a threshold. If any one crosses it, a warning lights up, whatever the others say. Nobody would trust a car that showed green because three of four parts felt fine.

4How it works measures, thresholds, the worst one wins

Ines and Callum rebuild the status around four measures, each with a threshold agreed with Elaine:

  • Burn-up against plan (story points delivered vs baseline v2.0): amber if more than one week behind, red if more than three. Now: −2.4 weeks, amber.
  • Tests passed out of those planned to date: amber below 70 %, red below 30 %. Now: 39 %, amber (most are blocked by environments, not failing).
  • Last rehearsal reconciled: red below 99.9 %. Now: 98.1 %, red.
  • Open severity 1–2 defects: amber above 5. Now: 7, amber.

The overall colour is the worst of the four: red for reconciliation. The first line: “RED: rehearsal 1 reconciled 98.1 % of balances (target 100 %); cause found (rounding rule R7 in the back-feed); rehearsal 2 in July.” The squads' own words stay, underneath the numbers. Elaine reads it and says: “Finally a red I believe.”

  1. Choose three to five programme measures

    Progress, quality, readiness.

  2. Agree thresholds with the client

    Written, before the numbers arrive.

  3. Overall colour = worst measure

    No averaging.

  4. Numbers in line one

    And the cause, if known.

  5. Keep the teams' words underneath

    Context, not colour.

  6. Show the trend

    This week and the last four.

Vocabulary

Watermelon report
A status that's green outside and red inside.
Burn-up chart
Work completed over time against the total planned.
Threshold
The value at which a measure changes colour.
Leading indicator
A measure that warns before the outcome (e.g. blocked tests).
Lagging indicator
A measure of results already happened (e.g. a failed rehearsal).
Roll-up
Combining team reports into a programme view; dangerous if it's an average.

9Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Rolling up opinions

    Every sprint green, the programme red. Fix → measures, not moods.

  • Averaging colours

    One red hides behind three greens. Fix → the worst measure wins.

  • Thresholds set after the numbers

    They bend to fit. Fix → agree them in advance.

  • No cause with the red

    Panic instead of action. Fix → cause and fix in line one.

  • Arguing colours down

    The client stops believing them. Fix → argue the plan, not the colour.

10When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Any programme with several teams.
  • When reports have been green for a long time.
  • Before an outside assurer looks.

Skip it when…

  • A single small team where everyone sees the same board.

11Self-check quiz

Why can a report be green outside and red inside without anyone lying?

Each team reports its own sprint honestly; nobody measures the whole programme.

What four measures did Lodestar use?

Burn-up vs plan, tests passed, last rehearsal reconciliation, open severity 1–2 defects.

How is the overall colour set?

It's the worst of the measures.

What goes in line one?

The colour, the numbers and the cause if known.

Why agree thresholds in advance?

So they can't be bent to fit the numbers.

what would you do?Graham asks whether the report could be amber “since the cause is found”. What do you say?

No: the colour describes progress against the milestone, irrespective of cause. The cause and fix are in line one; that's what will reassure Elaine.

try it

Take your project's status. Pick three measures that would show trouble early, with thresholds. Make the overall colour the worst of them. What colour are you?

Why Do the Test Environments Never Work?

TL;DR

On a client's estate, test environments are shared, old and owned by someone else, and they quietly eat your schedule. Count the loss every day, by cause. Book shared environments like meeting rooms, agreed with the other projects. Get realistic, masked test data. Test without the environment wherever you can (contract tests, recorded behaviour, synthetic data). And report the lost days as a measure, so the owner can fix the cause.

  • Family: holding the line
  • Lives in: test/environment-log.md · the booking calendar
  • Pairs with: escalation, the watermelon report, reading legacy code (vol. 3)

1The situation The Grid, 9 May 2028

Oskar has kept a quiet log since March. On 9 May he shows Ines the total: 31 % of planned test days in March and April were lost.

  • Lodestar shares MetroVolt's three test environments with four other projects. Whoever arrives first on Monday wins.
  • The MAVIS test copy dates from 2019: thousands of today's customers don't exist in it.
  • The performance environment hasn't been built yet. Hollis's team owns all of it, and is three people short.

2Both sides of the table the same moment, seen twice

MetroVolt Hollis's team

MetroVolt's environments were sized for one project at a time. Horizon cut the team that runs them. Five projects now compete for three environments, and each one thinks it's the priority. Nobody has counted what that costs.

“Everyone needs the environment this week.”

Wrenfield Oskar

For Oskar, a lost test day is a day the team can't prove anything, on a fixed price. Complaining hasn't worked; the other projects complain too. He needs the loss measured, the time shared fairly, and as much testing as possible that doesn't need the environments at all.

“If we can't control them, we can measure them, share them and need them less.”

Environment problems are rarely anyone's fault. They're a shared resource nobody manages as one.

3Real-life analogy the shared kitchen

Five flatmates share one kitchen and everyone wants it at seven. Shouting doesn't help. A rota on the fridge does, along with a note of how often dinner was late and why, fresh food instead of last year's tins, and doing the chopping at your own desk so you need the cooker for less time.

4How it works count, book, refresh, test without

Count it. Oskar's log becomes a daily record: test days planned, test days usable, and the cause of every loss (environment down, taken by another project, data missing, deployment failed). March–April: 31 % lost, half of it from collisions with other projects.

Book it. Oskar proposes, and Hollis's team adopts, a shared booking calendar for all five projects, in half-days, agreed at a fifteen-minute meeting every Friday. Collisions drop to almost nothing in two weeks.

Refresh the data. A new MAVIS test copy from 2028 data, with personal fields masked (the DPIA allows it). Thousands of “missing customer” failures disappear.

Test without it. The 58 Halvorsen contract tests, the golden master for CUSTBL07 and synthetic data for the migration tooling all run on Wrenfield's own build servers, no shared environment needed. About 40 % of the test suite moves there.

The loss goes into the weekly status as a measure. By July it's 9 %.

  1. Log every lost test day, by cause

    Daily, in a shared sheet.

  2. Share the numbers with the environment owner

    As data, not blame.

  3. Book shared environments

    A calendar, half-days, agreed weekly.

  4. Refresh test data, masked

    Recent enough to be realistic.

  5. Move tests off shared environments

    Contract tests, recorded behaviour, synthetic data.

  6. Report the loss as a measure

    Weekly, with the trend.

Vocabulary

Test environment
A copy of the systems used for testing, not for customers.
Environment booking
Reserving a shared environment for a period.
Data masking
Replacing personal data with realistic but fake values.
Synthetic data
Generated data with the right shape and oddities.
Environment drift
Test and production configurations diverging.
Shift left
Testing earlier, often without full environments.

5What the contract says whose environments they are

Lodestar SOW · Appendix C, D-12signed 30 June 2027
dependency

D-12 MetroVolt provides integration and user-acceptance test environments, with MAVIS test data no older than 12 months, as set out in Schedule 9.

“no older than 12 months”. the 2019 copy was a dependency miss, and nobody had counted it.

3.4 Where a Dependency is late, the Supplier is entitled to relief day for day on affected Milestones.

we didn't claim. we fixed. the log meant we could have.

In plain words: test environments and test data are usually the client's dependencies. Counting the lost days gives you the evidence for relief, and, better, the evidence the owner needs to fix the cause.

9Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Complaining without numbers

    Everyone complains; nothing changes. Fix → a daily log, by cause.

  • Grabbing environments

    A war between projects. Fix → a shared calendar.

  • Old test data

    Failures that aren't real. Fix → refresh, masked.

  • Everything needs the environment

    Testing stops when it's down. Fix → contract tests and synthetic data.

  • Silent loss

    The slip appears as “testing behind”. Fix → report lost days as a measure.

10When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Shared test environments on a client site.
  • Testing is behind and nobody knows why.
  • Old or unrealistic test data.

Skip it when…

  • You own your environments end to end: still count the loss.

11Self-check quiz

What share of test days was lost in March and April?

31 %.

What was the biggest cause?

Collisions with other projects using the same environments.

What fixed collisions?

A shared booking calendar in half-days, agreed weekly.

How did the team test without environments?

Contract tests, the golden master and synthetic data on their own build servers.

What did the contract say about test data?

MetroVolt's MAVIS test data should be no older than twelve months (D-12).

what would you do?Another project books the UAT environment for the whole of rehearsal week. What do you do?

Raise it at the Friday booking meeting with the numbers: rehearsal 3 is on the wave 1 critical path. Offer to swap half-days. If it can't be settled, escalate via the ladder with a proposal.

try it

For two weeks, log every test day you planned and whether you could use it, with the cause. What share was lost? Which tests could run without the shared environment?

remember!!

You can't control shared environments. You can count the loss, book them fairly and need them less.

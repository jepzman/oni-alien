What Did the Night Teach Us?

TL;DR

On the night, run the book: call each step, log real times, decide at the checkpoints. When something happens that the book didn't foresee, follow the book's rule for that: stop the step, gather the facts, fix the smallest thing that works, and let the agreed decision maker decide. Then, before anyone goes home, write down what happened: times, decisions, lessons. Tomorrow you'll remember it differently.

  • Family: the night
  • Lives in: migration/wave1-night-log.md · cutover report (M4)
  • Pairs with: the cutover plan, go / no-go

1The situation The Grid, floor 19, Saturday 23 – Sunday 24 September 2028

At 18:00 on Saturday 23 September, MAVIS is frozen for the Northern district. Forty people on a bridge call and on floor 19: Wrenfield, MetroVolt IT, Customer Ops' exception desk, Sven on call for Halvorsen, Elaine with a flask of tea.

  • 23:00, checkpoint one: migration at 52 %, exceptions 0.29 %. Go.
  • 02:10: the batch job runs, with MAVIS's feed for 1.9 million accounts and Lodestar's for 380,000. Halvorsen accepts both. Go.
  • 04:10: the migration stops. The MetroBus queue is full. Twenty minutes to the point of no return. Nothing in the runbook says “queue full”.

2Both sides of the table the same moment, seen twice

MetroVolt Elaine

Elaine has waited three years, since Orion, for a night like this to go right. At 04:10 she has to decide whether to keep going past the point of no return with a problem nobody planned for, or roll back and lose the weekend. She needs facts in minutes, not opinions.

“Tell me what's wrong, what you've done, and what you recommend.”

Wrenfield Ines and Hana

The runbook covers 312 steps and has one rule for everything else: stop, gather facts, call the checkpoint early. At 04:10 Ines has to make that rule work: keep forty tired people calm, get Hana and MetroVolt IT on the problem, and give Elaine a decision she can make.

“The book's last rule: when the book runs out, stop and think together.”

Every cutover has a moment the plan didn't foresee. The plan's job is to make that moment the only thing anyone has to think about.

3Real-life analogy the pilot's unexpected warning

When an unexpected warning light comes on, pilots have a rule older than any checklist: aviate, navigate, communicate. Keep the plane flying, work out where you are, then talk. They don't all shout ideas at once. And after landing, before anyone leaves, they write down exactly what happened, while it's fresh.

4How it works run the book; when it runs out, stop, facts, fix, decide; then write

Run the book. Ines calls each phase; Lottie logs real times against planned. Checkpoints one and two pass on time. Callum updates the bridge every 30 minutes and texts Priya at each checkpoint.

When the book runs out (04:10). The runbook's last rule: stop the step, call the checkpoint early, decide on facts. Ines stops the migration. Hana and MetroVolt IT's on-call find the cause in nine minutes: the back-feed to MAVIS and the migration are both writing to one MetroBus queue, which hit its limit. Neither rehearsal ran them at the same moment. They drain the queue, raise its limit, and restart the migration: 22 minutes.

Decide. At 04:52, 22 minutes late, checkpoint three: reconciliation complete, counts and balances equal. Ines recommends continuing; Elaine decides to go past the point of no return. At 07:40, the router goes live. 380,000 accounts migrated (less the three excluded); 1,206 in the exception queue (0.32 %), worked by Customer Ops' desk through Sunday.

Write it down before sleeping. At 08:15 Lottie and Ines write the night log: every checkpoint, the 04:10 problem, who decided what, and 14 lessons for wave 2. Monday's 02:10 file to Halvorsen goes through. The first Lodestar bills go out on Monday. Customer Ops' phone lines are quiet.

9Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Everyone fixing at once

    Noise, and new problems. Fix → one team on it, everyone else waits.

  • Deciding without facts

    Rolling back, or forward, on a hunch. Fix → nine minutes for the cause.

  • The vendor deciding

    Authority argued at 04:30. Fix → the agreed person decides.

  • No log

    Wave 2 repeats the problem. Fix → write it before sleeping.

  • Ignoring the people

    Burnout after the success. Fix → notice who carried the night.

10When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Any cutover or major go-live.
  • Any long operation with several teams.
  • Whenever the plan meets something it didn't foresee.

Skip it when…

  • Never skip the log.

11Self-check quiz

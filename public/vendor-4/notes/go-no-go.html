a.k.a. the go / no-go decision

Do We Go?

TL;DR

A go / no-go decision is only as good as the criteria agreed before it. Fix them weeks ahead, with owners and evidence. On the day, each owner reports their criterion; anything not green comes with its cause, impact and a mitigation. The sponsor decides, not the vendor and not the loudest adviser, and any dissent is recorded with its reasons. “Go with conditions” is a legitimate answer; “go because we're tired” isn't.

1The situation The Grid, room 27A, 6 September 2028

The go / no-go for wave 1 is on Wednesday 6 September, seventeen days before the night. Fourteen criteria were agreed in June. Thirteen are green.

  • C9, rehearsal reconciliation: 99.97 %. Three accounts of 380,000 have balances off by a few pence. The cause is known (rounding in a rare back-feed path); the fix is tested on those three, but not in a full run.
  • Oliver Thane, for Horizon assurance, advises no-go: “100 % means 100 %.”
  • Graham, from Leeds, wants Ines to “push for go”. It isn't Ines's decision, and she knows it.

2Both sides of the table the same moment, seen twice

MetroVolt Priya

Priya owns the customers and the decision. A no-go means a new date, another Horizon board and a long winter. A go with a known defect means three customers might get a wrong bill. She needs the facts, an honest recommendation and room to decide.

“Tell me what's true, then let me decide.”

Wrenfield Ines

Wrenfield wants to go, badly: margin, morale, months of work. That's exactly why Ines mustn't push. Her job is to present the evidence straight, propose a mitigation that makes the amber safe, and accept whatever Priya decides.

“Present, propose, then step back.”

The worst go / no-go decisions are made by people who've stopped being able to say no. The criteria are there so somebody can.

3Real-life analogy the launch director's poll

Before a rocket launch, the director polls each station: “Fuel?” “Go.” “Weather?” “Go.” The criteria were written months before. If a station says “no-go”, nobody argues them out of it; the director asks what it would take, and decides. The poll is recorded, every voice. And “hold for ten minutes” is a perfectly good answer.

4How it works agreed criteria, evidence, a mitigation, the sponsor decides

Criteria agreed in advance. The fourteen were signed in June (DL-049): reconciliation, exception rate, performance, severity 1–2 defects at zero, Halvorsen contract tests, the exception desk staffed, rollback rehearsed, communications ready, and so on. Nobody can add or soften one on the day.

Evidence per criterion. Each owner reports in two minutes: Oskar on defects (zero severity 1–2), Rosa on UAT, Sven on the billing tests, Customer Ops on the exception desk. Thirteen green.

The amber, with facts. Hana presents C9: three accounts, the cause, the fix, its test, and a mitigation: exclude those three accounts from wave 1 (they stay on MAVIS) and migrate them by hand the following week, after a full test of the fix. With them excluded, C9 is 100 % for the accounts that move.

The sponsor decides. Ines gives Wrenfield's recommendation (go, with the condition) and stops talking. Oliver gives his (no-go) and his reasons. Priya asks two questions, then decides: GO, with the condition. The minutes (DL-058) record the decision, the condition and Horizon assurance's dissent, with its reasons. Lord Varnish isn't there; Priya tells him afterwards.

Vocabulary

Go / no-go
A formal decision whether to proceed with a launch or cutover.
Go-live criteria
Conditions that must be met to proceed, agreed in advance.
Conditional go
Proceeding with a specific mitigation or limit.
Mitigation
An action that reduces a risk's likelihood or impact.
Dissent
A recorded disagreement with the decision.
Go fever
Pressure to proceed because of effort already spent.

5What the contract says who decides, and on what

Lodestar SOW · Schedule 9, §2 (go / no-go)as amended 14 Jun 2028
GO

2.1 The go / no-go decision for each wave is made by MetroVolt's Sponsor against the criteria in Annex A, agreed no later than 12 weeks before the wave.

Priya decides. criteria from June. not negotiable on the day.

2.3 The Sponsor may decide to proceed subject to conditions. Recommendations of the Supplier and of Horizon Assurance, and any dissent, are recorded.

conditional go: allowed. dissent: minuted.

In plain words: put the decision maker, the criteria deadline and the right to proceed with conditions into the contract. Then the go / no-go is a decision against agreed facts, not a negotiation under pressure.

9Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Criteria invented on the day

    The decision follows the mood. Fix → agree them weeks before.

  • Softening an amber

    “99.97 % is basically 100 %.” Fix → cause, impact, mitigation.

  • The vendor deciding

    Go fever. Fix → the sponsor decides.

  • Ignoring dissent

    It returns as blame. Fix → record it with reasons.

  • No-go seen as failure

    People push to go. Fix → no-go is a legitimate result.

10When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Any major go-live or cutover.
  • When several parties have a stake in the decision.
  • When someone influential will disagree.

Skip it when…

  • Small, easily reversible releases: a checklist and the owner's call.

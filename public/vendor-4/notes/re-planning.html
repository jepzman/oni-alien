How Do We Re-plan Without Lying?

TL;DR

Re-planning goes wrong when the new plan is built from the old hopes. Start from real velocity and the critical path, show the client options (scope, date, people) with their costs, and make the buffer visible as buffer, not hidden inside tasks. A re-plan you'll have to re-plan again in six weeks costs more trust than the first slip.

1The situation The Grid, 3 July 2028

On Monday 3 July the pilot's milestone (M3) is accepted after its fix: exceptions down to 0.3 %. The same day, Callum shows Ines the numbers for wave 1.

  • The plan assumed 45 points a sprint. The last eight sprints averaged 38.
  • At 38, the remaining work finishes on 21 October. Wave 1 night is 23 September.
  • Callum's proposal: re-plan at 45 (“the side-door work has stopped, we'll speed up”) and absorb any gap in the nine days of buffer. Nobody outside Wrenfield would need to know.

2Both sides of the table the same moment, seen twice

MetroVolt Elaine

Elaine has just survived a steering ambush and a rejected milestone. She wants to know whether 23 September is real. If the plan changes again in August, she'll stop believing any date, and so will Priya.

“Is 23 September real? Tell me now, not in August.”

Wrenfield Ines and Callum

Callum's plan might work. If it doesn't, Wrenfield will be re-planning in August with no buffer and no options, after promising it was fine. Ines would rather show the slip now, while there are still choices.

“Plan at the speed we are, not the speed we wish we were.”

A buffer hidden inside tasks gets spent without anyone noticing. A buffer on the plan can be watched, and protected.

3Real-life analogy the long-distance runner

A marathon runner at mile 15, slower than planned, doesn't decide to run the last eleven miles at her original pace “because she'll feel better”. She works out her real pace, decides what matters (a finish, or a time), and tells her coach. If she lies to herself at mile 15, she finds out at mile 22, when nothing can be done.

4How it works real velocity, critical path, options, visible buffer

Real velocity. The re-plan uses 38, the average of the last eight sprints. Not 45, not a hoped-for 42.

Critical path. Hana and Oskar trace what must happen in order before wave 1 night: reconciliation tooling (the longest chain), then rehearsal 3 on 26 August, then the cutover runbook. Work not on that path can move without moving the date.

Options, with costs. Ines takes three to Elaine. A: keep 23 September by moving two low-value features (bulk address edit, a reporting screen) to wave 2. B: keep the scope, move wave 1 to 21 October (a re-baseline, and Rupert's board). C: add two engineers from Leeds (it shortens the build by about three weeks and costs margin).

Elaine chooses A plus one engineer. The plan becomes v2.3. The nine days of buffer sit on it as a separate bar, labelled buffer, with a rule: any day used is reported in that week's status. By wave 1 night, four of the nine have been used, all reported.

  1. Measure real velocity

    Recent average, not the plan.

  2. Find the critical path

    What must happen in order before the date.

  3. Build options

    Scope, date, people, each with its cost.

  4. Let the client choose

    With a recommendation.

  5. Make the buffer visible

    A separate bar, reported when used.

  6. Re-plan once, properly

    Not every two weeks.

Vocabulary

Velocity
How much work a team completes per sprint, on average.
Critical path
The longest chain of dependent tasks; it sets the earliest finish.
Buffer
Time held in reserve for the unexpected.
Scope trade
Moving or removing features to protect a date.
Brooks's law
Adding people to a late project often makes it later; it depends when and who.
Re-plan
A revised plan from current facts, agreed with the client.

5What the contract says what can move without a re-baseline

Lodestar SOW · §4.3 and Schedule 4baseline v2.0
replanned

4.3 The Supplier may re-plan work within a Milestone without approval, provided Milestone dates and the scope in Schedule 2 are unaffected.

moving two features to wave 2 changes Schedule 2. so: Elaine's decision, DL-047.

Sch. 2, 7.1 Bulk address edit and the operational MI screen are delivered in Wave 2.

two features out, in writing. not quietly dropped.

In plain words: you can re-plan inside your own work freely, but moving scope between waves changes the contract's description of the work. Make that a decision, recorded, not a quiet omission.

9Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Re-planning at the hoped-for speed

    Another re-plan in six weeks. Fix → real velocity.

  • Hiding buffer in tasks

    It's spent unseen. Fix → a separate, reported buffer.

  • Only one option

    The client can't choose. Fix → scope, date, people.

  • Adding people late

    They slow the team down. Fix → add early, and only experienced people.

  • Dropping scope quietly

    Discovered at acceptance. Fix → move it in writing.

10When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Velocity is below plan for several sprints.
  • A milestone is at risk.
  • After any major surprise.

Skip it when…

  • A one-sprint dip with a known, fixed cause.

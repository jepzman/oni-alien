When Do We Quote the Contract?

TL;DR

The contract is your safety net, not your argument. Read it aloud rarely: only when the relationship can't settle something on its own. When you do, acknowledge the need first, read the clause calmly, and immediately offer a way to get what they want (a change, a later wave) and, if possible, an interim. A clause read to win an argument loses the relationship; a clause read to solve a problem keeps both.

  • Lives in: Appendix C, X-07 · change register

1The situation The Grid, 8 August 2028

Six weeks before wave 1, Kofi brings a request from Gareth Pound: migrate ten years of meter-reading history for every wave 1 customer. Network Assets' analysts use it for load forecasting.

  • The contract excludes history older than 24 months: exclusion X-07, listed in the bid in March 2027.
  • Gareth has already emailed Rupert: “Lodestar is refusing to migrate essential data.”
  • Ten years of readings for 380,000 customers is about 40 million rows. Six weeks before the night.

2Both sides of the table the same moment, seen twice

MetroVolt Gareth and Kofi

For Network Assets, meter history is how they plan the grid: which streets will overload in a cold January. Losing easy access to it feels like Lodestar breaking their work. Gareth wanted Lodestar in his own programme in 2027; this is also about that.

“You're taking away data we need.”

Wrenfield Ines

Gareth's need is real. The exclusion is also real, and was in the bid. Doing it now would put wave 1 at risk; refusing it bluntly hands Gareth the story he's already telling Rupert. Ines needs to use the contract without using it as a weapon.

“Read the clause, then solve the problem.”

Quoting the contract is like pulling a handbrake: useful when you need it, and noticed by everyone in the car.

3Real-life analogy the landlord and the lease

A tenant asks the landlord to repaint the whole flat. The lease says the landlord repaints every five years; it's been three. A good landlord doesn't wave the lease. He says: “I understand, it's looking tired. The lease says five years, so it's due in 2030. I could do the living room now at cost, and the rest then. Meanwhile, here are the paint codes if you want to touch up.”

4How it works acknowledge, read calmly, offer a way, an interim

Ines asks for the request to come to the data working group the same day, with Kofi and Gareth's deputy, rather than answering Gareth's email.

  • Acknowledge the need. “Load forecasting depends on this history. That's a real need, and Lodestar shouldn't make it harder.”
  • Read the clause, calmly. “When we bid, we listed X-07: meter history older than 24 months isn't migrated. It's in Appendix C. Wave 1 migrates the last 24 months.”
  • Offer a way. “We can assess migrating the full history as a change, within ten days. Our rough figure is about £140k, and it's safer in wave 2 than six weeks before wave 1.”
  • Offer an interim. “Until then, all ten years stay readable in MAVIS for migrated customers: Network Assets' reports keep working.”

Gareth's deputy takes the interim back. Gareth accepts it by email, and replies to his own message to Rupert: “Resolved with Lodestar.” The change request is assessed and scheduled for wave 2. It's the second time in fourteen months Ines has read a clause aloud; the first was “absorb”, in June 2027.

  1. Try everything else first

    Most issues settle without a clause.

  2. Move it to the right room

    A working group, not an email thread.

  3. Acknowledge the need

    Before any clause.

  4. Read the clause calmly, once

    Where it is, what it says.

  5. Offer a way to get it

    A change, a later phase, a price.

  6. Offer an interim if you can

    Something that helps now.

5What the contract says the exclusion, quoted

Lodestar SOW · Appendix C, X-07signed 30 June 2027
quoted (2nd time)

X-07 Meter-reading history older than 24 months is not migrated. MAVIS history remains available read-only.

the second sentence was the interim, written in 2027. we just had to read it out.

3.5 Exclusions are outside the Services and may be added only by Change Request.

and we offered one.

In plain words: a good exclusion often contains its own interim (“remains available read-only”). Read the whole clause, and lead with what it allows, not just what it excludes.

6Account health the same move, green, amber and red

Green

trust is high

  • Rarely needed.
  • A conversation settles it.
  • Offer the change.

Amber Lodestar now

a powerful stakeholder escalating

  • Right room, calm clause, a way and an interim.
  • Reply to the escalation with the outcome.
  • Credit their need.

Red

repeated demands outside scope

  • Quote, offer, record each time.
  • Escalate patterns via the ladder.
  • Never refuse without offering a route.

9Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Quoting first

    The need is ignored; they escalate. Fix → acknowledge the need first.

  • Quoting often

    Every conversation becomes legal. Fix → rarely: twice in fourteen months.

  • Quoting without an offer

    A wall, not a door. Fix → a change, a later phase, a price.

  • Answering the escalation email

    The thread grows. Fix → solve it in the right room, then reply with the outcome.

  • Giving in to pressure

    Wave 1 at risk. Fix → protect the critical path.

10When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • A request is clearly outside the agreed scope.
  • Pressure is applied through senior people.
  • Doing it would put a milestone at risk.

Skip it when…

  • A small request the change pool can handle: use the pool, not the clause.

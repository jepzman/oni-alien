What Do We Do When Our Rival Advises the Client?

TL;DR

When a rival becomes your client's adviser, don't fight them and don't fear them. Ask the sponsor to define the reviewer's scope in writing. Give everything the contract entitles the client to, quickly and completely; protect what the contract protects (your intellectual property, your people's time) and offer a fair alternative. Keep an evidence pack ready, and never say a word about the rival. Let the evidence answer.

1The situation The Grid, May 2028

Since June 2027 Vexmoor has attended Lodestar's steering as “Horizon assurance”, with no vote. On 24 May 2028, Oliver Thane tells Priya that Rupert wants a “Lodestar acceleration review”.

  • Bea emails Ines the same afternoon: Vexmoor needs access to Wrenfield's source code repository by Friday, and one-to-one interviews with Hana, Bilal and Oskar.
  • Vexmoor built Orion's hub, lost Lodestar to Wrenfield, and will bid for phase 2 in 2029.
  • Graham's reaction from Leeds: “Absolutely not. Tell them where to go.” Rupert chairs the board that approves phase 2.

2Both sides of the table the same moment, seen twice

MetroVolt Priya

Priya didn't ask for the review; Rupert did. She trusts Wrenfield more than she did a year ago, and she can't refuse Horizon. She needs Wrenfield to cooperate visibly, and she needs the review to stay a review.

“Help me give Rupert his review without losing my programme.”

Wrenfield Ines

Wrenfield has nothing to hide in its delivery, and a lot to protect in its code and its people's attention three weeks before the pilot. Refusing looks guilty; complying fully hands a future rival its tooling. The answer has to be fair to MetroVolt and to Wrenfield.

“Transparent about the work. Careful about the code.”

A rival adviser is uncomfortable, and normal. Big clients use consultants who also bid. The contract, not your feelings, decides what they see.

3Real-life analogy the second opinion

A patient's family asks another hospital for a second opinion. A good surgeon doesn't take offence and doesn't refuse. She sends the scans, notes and test results promptly, offers to talk the other doctor through them, and doesn't hand over her private research notes. If the second opinion agrees, good. If it disagrees, the evidence is on the table.

4How it works scope from the sponsor, give, protect, evidence

Scope in writing, from the sponsor. Ines doesn't reply to Bea. She asks Priya and Elaine for a one-page terms of reference: purpose, questions, access, dates, and who receives the findings. Priya writes it in two days. Purpose: “confidence in the wave 1 date”.

Give what the contract gives. MetroVolt is entitled to programme documents, test results and records. Wrenfield sends a complete evidence pack in three days: baseline v2.0, the status measures, both rehearsal reconciliation reports, the RAID page, the decision and change logs.

Protect what it protects. Wrenfield's migration tooling is its own pre-existing intellectual property (clause 12). Instead of repository access, Hana offers a supervised two-hour walkthrough of how it works and how it's tested. Interviews happen with Elaine present, capped at an hour each.

Let evidence answer. Ines tells the team one rule: nobody says anything about Vexmoor, in any room or email. The review's report, in June, says the date is “achievable with risk”, which is what Wrenfield's own status already said.

  1. Don't answer the reviewer directly

    Go to the sponsor first.

  2. Get terms of reference in writing

    Purpose, questions, access, dates.

  3. Give what the contract gives, fast

    Complete and well organised.

  4. Protect what it protects, fairly

    Offer an alternative (a walkthrough).

  5. Protect your people's time

    Capped interviews, the client present.

  6. Never comment on the rival

    Let your evidence do it.

Vocabulary

Independent assurance
A review by a third party to give the sponsor confidence.
Terms of reference
A written description of a review's purpose, scope and access.
Intellectual property (IP)
Rights in what you create, such as code and tools.
Background IP
IP a supplier owned before the contract and uses in it.
Evidence pack
Organised documents and records ready to show a reviewer.
Conflict of interest
When an adviser may benefit from the advice they give.

5What the contract says what MetroVolt can see, and what it can't

Lodestar SOW · §12 (intellectual property) and §13 (records)signed 30 June 2027
background IP

12.2 The Supplier's Background IP, including its migration tooling, remains the Supplier's. MetroVolt receives a licence to use it for the Services. Disclosure of Background IP source code to third parties requires the Supplier's consent.

Vexmoor is a third party. a walkthrough is consent, on our terms.

13.1 The Supplier shall keep and, on request, provide MetroVolt and its auditors with records of the Services, including plans, test results and reports.

everything in the pack. promptly. completely.

In plain words: the contract usually separates what the client may inspect (records, results) from what the supplier keeps (its own tools and code). Being generous with the first makes protecting the second look fair rather than defensive.

9Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Refusing

    You look as if you're hiding. Fix → give what the contract gives.

  • Handing over everything

    Your tooling goes to a future rival. Fix → protect background IP; offer a walkthrough.

  • Answering the reviewer directly

    Scope grows by email. Fix → terms of reference from the sponsor.

  • Commenting on the rival

    It becomes the story. Fix → silence; evidence.

  • Unprepared evidence

    Days lost assembling it. Fix → keep an evidence pack current.

10When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • A third party reviews your work for the client.
  • The reviewer is, or may be, a competitor.
  • Requests exceed what the contract gives.

Skip it when…

  • An agreed audit under the contract with an independent auditor: just comply.

11Self-check quiz

Why go to the sponsor before answering the reviewer?

So the review's scope is defined by the client in writing, not grown by email.

What did MetroVolt get under clause 13?

Plans, test results, reports and records: the evidence pack.

Why not give repository access?

The tooling is Wrenfield's background IP; disclosure to third parties needs consent.

What was offered instead?

A supervised two-hour walkthrough with Hana.

What was the house rule?

Nobody at Wrenfield comments on Vexmoor, anywhere.

what would you do?In an interview, Bea asks Bilal whether Wrenfield “is really on track”. Elaine is present. What should Bilal say?

What the status measures say, with the numbers, and that he's happy to show the data. No opinions about the plan beyond his area, and nothing about Vexmoor.

Imagine a rival reviewing your work tomorrow. What would you give them under your contract, and what would you protect? Assemble the evidence pack you'd send.

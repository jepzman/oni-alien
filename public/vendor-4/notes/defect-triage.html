Is It a Defect or a Change?

TL;DR

In acceptance testing on a fixed price, the word “defect” means “fix for free”, so it's used for everything. Agree definitions in advance: a defect fails an acceptance criterion or the agreed design; a change is new or different behaviour; plus duplicate and not reproducible. Triage together, client and vendor, several times a week, with agreed severities. Then fix defects by severity and send changes to the change register.

  • Family: holding the line
  • Lives in: test/triage-board (Jira LODE, triage view)

1The situation The Grid, July 2028

User-acceptance testing starts on 3 July, with MetroVolt's testers led by Rosa. By 27 July they've logged 412 defects.

  • Some are real: a balance off by a penny for a tariff Bert forgot; a screen that crashes on Welsh characters.
  • Many aren't: “the button should be blue like MAVIS”, “add a field for the meter reader's name”, “sort by street, not postcode”.
  • Every one is labelled defect, which under the fixed price means fix for free, before wave 1.

2Both sides of the table the same moment, seen twice

MetroVolt Rosa and the testers

MetroVolt's testers are Customer Ops staff who'll use Lodestar every day. To them, anything that makes the job harder is a defect. They aren't trying to get free work; they're trying to make the system usable, and “defect” is the only label the tool gives them.

“It doesn't work the way we need. That's a defect.”

Wrenfield Oskar and Ines

Fixing 412 items before 23 September is impossible, and many of them aren't Wrenfield's to fix for free. But arguing item by item with the people who'll use the system is the fastest way to lose them. Wrenfield needs shared definitions and a shared board.

“Same words, same board, decided together.”

Most “defect versus change” fights are really about missing definitions. Agree the words, and most items sort themselves.

3Real-life analogy the new-build snagging list

When you move into a new-build house, you walk round with the builder making a snagging list. A cracked tile is a snag: the builder fixes it free. “I'd like a socket here” isn't a snag: it's an extra, priced. Good builders and buyers agree the difference before the walk-round, and do it together, so nobody argues about each item.

4How it works definitions, a joint board, severities, two routes

Definitions, agreed between Oskar and Rosa and approved by Elaine: a defect is behaviour that fails an acceptance criterion (Schedule 4) or contradicts the agreed design (Schedule 2); a change is new or different behaviour; a duplicate is the same as another item; not reproducible can't be repeated with the steps given.

Severities: 1 stops wave 1; 2 serious, no workaround; 3 has a workaround; 4 cosmetic. Wave 1 needs zero severity 1 and 2 defects (a go / no-go criterion).

A joint triage board, Monday, Wednesday and Friday at 11:00, thirty minutes: Rosa and Oskar decide each new item together, with the tester who raised it if needed. The first sessions clear the backlog: of 412, 188 defects, 161 changes, 41 duplicates, 22 not reproducible.

Two routes. Defects are fixed by severity: all 23 severity 1–2 fixed by 25 August. Changes go to the change register; Elaine takes 19 into the change pool (they matter for go-live), the rest to wave 2. Rosa says the definitions helped her testers too: “Now they know what to write.”

  1. Agree definitions before testing starts

    Defect, change, duplicate, not reproducible.

  2. Agree severities

    And which block go-live.

  3. Triage jointly, often

    Client and vendor leads, short sessions.

  4. Decide every item into one box

    No “TBD” category.

  5. Fix defects by severity

    Severity 1–2 first.

  6. Route changes through the register

    Pool now, or a later wave.

7.6 No Change is binding unless recorded in the Change Register and approved under this clause.

In plain words: the contract usually defines “defect” already. Showing that definition to the client's testers, and triaging with them, turns 412 arguments into one agreement.

9Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • No definitions

    Every item is an argument. Fix → agree them before testing.

  • Vendor-only triage

    The client feels overruled. Fix → decide together.

  • A “to be decided” pile

    It grows forever. Fix → every item, one box.

  • Changes fixed for free

    The fixed price leaks. Fix → change register.

  • Changes refused outright

    Users hate the system. Fix → pool what matters now, schedule the rest.

10When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • User-acceptance testing on a fixed price.
  • Testers who aren't professional testers.
  • A large defect backlog.

Skip it when…

  • Time and materials, where changes are simply prioritised work: still agree severities.

what would you do?A tester insists “sort by street” is a defect because MAVIS did it. What do you decide?

Check Schedule 2: if it specifies sorting, it's a defect; if not, it's a change. If it's a change that matters for go-live, propose it for the pool. Either way, decide it at the board with Rosa.

try it

Take your project's bug list. Write definitions for defect and change from your contract or agreement, and sort twenty items. How many “defects” were changes?

remember!!

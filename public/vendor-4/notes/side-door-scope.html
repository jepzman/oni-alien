Where Did This Extra Work Come From?

TL;DR

On a blended team, most scope creep doesn't come through the change process; it comes through side doors: chat messages, corridor favours, “while you're in there”. Each request is small and reasonable; together they're the slip. Count the work that wasn't on the board, log each as a change, triage weekly with the client (change pool, later, or no), and make the front door easier than the side door.

1The situation The Grid, April 2028

The burn-up says the build is 2.4 weeks behind. The squads say they're working hard. Both are true. On Thursday 20 April, Ines asks every squad member one question: “What did you do in the last six weeks that wasn't on the board?”

  • 23 small requests: an extra field for Kofi's metering team, three reports for Gareth's analysts, a filter for Customer Ops, a screen tweak asked for at lunch.
  • Each took a day or two. Together: 41 days, about the size of the slip.
  • None of them are in the change register. All of them came from friendly MetroVolt colleagues in the mixed squads.

2Both sides of the table the same moment, seen twice

MetroVolt MetroVolt colleagues

For MetroVolt people in the squads, asking Lottie for a field is just teamwork: that's what “one team” meant in August. Filling in a change request for a one-day job feels bureaucratic and rude. None of them think of it as scope.

“We're one team. It's a tiny thing.”

Wrenfield Ines and the squads

For Wrenfield, every favour is fixed-price time spent on things outside Schedule 2, and invisible in the plan. Saying no to colleagues feels terrible; saying yes 23 times has cost two and a half weeks.

“Every yes is fine. Every invisible yes isn't.”

Side-door scope is the price of a good blended team. The answer isn't fewer friends; it's a door that's easier to use than the side one.

3Real-life analogy the shop assistant's favours

A shop assistant who gives regulars a free extra slice, a small discount, a delivery on the way home, is lovely. At the end of the month, the till is short and nobody knows why. A sensible shop doesn't ban kindness; it gives the assistant a notebook for favours, and decides each week which ones become a loyalty scheme.

4How it works count, log, triage weekly, and make the front door easy

Count it. Every Friday, each squad member answers one question: what did you do this week that wasn't on the board? Lottie collects the answers in five minutes.

Log it. Each item becomes a change note with its days, the person who asked and why. The 23 from the last six weeks are logged retroactively.

Triage weekly with Elaine. Fifteen minutes after the RAID review. Each note goes one of three ways: into the change pool (it's worth doing now), to wave 2, or no. Of the 23: 9 into the pool (£58k, at the rate card), 11 to wave 2, 3 declined. The slip is explained, and partly paid for.

Make the front door easy. A pinned two-line form in the #lodestar channel: what you need, and why. Lottie answers within a day with “on the board”, “to Elaine for triage” or “wave 2”. MetroVolt colleagues use it because it's faster than catching Hana in the corridor.

  1. Ask what wasn't on the board

    Every Friday, every person.

  2. Log every item with its days

    Who asked, why.

  3. Triage with the client weekly

    Now (pool), later, or no.

  4. Use the change pool for “now”

    It's what it's for.

  5. Make the front door faster

    A two-line form, answered in a day.

  6. Never blame the askers

    They were being good teammates.

5What the contract says the clause that makes side doors visible

Lodestar SOW · §7.6 and §8.5signed 30 June 2027
only recorded changes count

7.6 No Change is binding unless recorded in the Change Register and approved under this clause.

so the 23 favours weren't changes. they were gifts. now they're changes.

8.5 Instructions to the Supplier are valid only if given by MetroVolt's Programme Director or the Steering Committee.

Kofi asked. Elaine decides. the form routes it.

In plain words: the contract already says only recorded changes count and only named people can instruct. Side-door work is outside both, which is why it must be counted and routed, not just done.

9Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Banning favours

    The one-team spirit dies. Fix → route them, don't ban them.

  • Not counting

    The slip is a mystery. Fix → ask what wasn't on the board.

  • Logging but not triaging

    The list grows, nothing decided. Fix → weekly, with the client.

  • A slow front door

    People go back to the corridor. Fix → answer in a day.

  • Blaming the askers

    Trust collapses. Fix → they were good teammates.

10When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Blended or co-located teams.
  • Fixed-price work.
  • When the plan slips and nobody knows why.

Skip it when…

  • Pure time and materials where the client directs daily: still count it.

11Self-check quiz

Where does most scope creep come from on a blended team?

Side doors: chats, corridors and favours, not the change process.

What's the Friday question?

What did you do this week that wasn't on the board?

What are the three triage outcomes?

Into the change pool now, to wave 2, or no.

How many of the 23 went where?

9 into the pool (£58k), 11 to wave 2, 3 declined.

Why make the front door easy?

People use the fastest route; if the form is faster than the corridor, they'll use the form.

what would you do?Gareth's analyst asks Lottie for a report “by Friday, it's for Rupert”. What does Lottie do?

Thank her, put it through the front-door form, and tell Ines so it can go to Elaine's triage that day; if it's urgent and small, it can come from the change pool quickly.

try it

remember!!

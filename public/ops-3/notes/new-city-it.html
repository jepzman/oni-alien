A New City by Checklist

TL;DR

Opening a new site is a repeated service transition: the same kinds of IT work every time, in the same order. Write it once as a versioned runbook in three parts (the site, the platform, operations) and run it for each launch. After every launch, add what was missed, script what repeated and remove what wasn't needed. The runbook should get shorter each time, and so should the launch.

1The situation Larder, February 2027

Utrecht has eight weeks from lease (15 February) to opening on 12 April. The store IT kit from May 2026 covers the store. But the city also has to be set up in the platform: delivery zones and slots, the store in the new WMS router, vendor settings, monitoring, the catalogue, on-call and the status page. For Rotterdam that was about three weeks of Eli's scattered work, written down nowhere.

  • Steps are remembered, or forgotten, by one person.
  • The order of work matters (the line, the router) and isn't recorded.
  • Four more cities are planned for 2027.

2Real-life analogy the pre-flight checklist

A pilot's pre-flight checklist started long and handwritten. Every flight crossed out a step that was never needed and added one that had been missed. After thousands of flights it's a short laminated card, in order, with nothing on it that hasn't earned its place. Nobody flies from memory, however experienced they are.

3How it solves it one versioned runbook in three parts, shorter every launch

Eli writes the city IT runbook in February, in three parts:

  • The store: the store IT kit. Utrecht's line is ordered on 16 February and live on 30 March.
  • The platform: the city configuration file (zones, slots, opening hours: Ana's city as configuration), the store in the WMS router, payment and address vendor settings, the status page.
  • Operations: monitoring and the store heartbeat, the catalogue, the on-call handover, store leads in the service desk, known errors shared with the new store.

Version 1 has 61 steps. Utrecht opens on Monday 12 April with 2.9 tickets per 100 orders in week 1 (Rotterdam: 7.1). After Utrecht, v2 has 48 steps, 11 of them now scripts (zones from a file, monitoring and on-call from the catalogue). v3, for The Hague, has 39 steps. The Hague opens on 14 June, born on WMS B, with its IT ready in 9 working days. The runbook is workstream 8 in Sanne's launch playbook.

  1. Write everything the last launch needed

    From tickets, the issue log, the change feed.

  2. Group it: site, platform, operations

    And order it by lead time.

  3. Run it for the next launch

    Tick each step; note what was missed.

  4. After the launch, change the runbook

    Add, script, remove; bump the version.

  5. Script what repeated

    Config files over clicking.

  6. Track how long IT takes per launch

    It should fall every time.

Vocabulary for the exam

Service transition
Moving a new or changed service into live use.
Runbook (launch)
The ordered steps to bring a new site or service live.
City as configuration
A new city is mostly data in a file, not new code (Ana's principle).
Versioned
Numbered, with dated changes, so everyone uses the latest.
Early life support
Close support just after go-live (vol. 6).
Lead-time ordering
Doing the slowest-to-arrive steps first.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • A runbook that only grows

    Every launch adds steps, none are removed. Fix → remove what wasn't needed; script what repeated.

  • Not ordered by lead time

    The line arrives after opening, again. Fix → the slowest steps first.

  • Operations last, or forgotten

    The store opens before on-call knows it exists. Fix → operations is one of the three parts.

  • Nobody owns the runbook

    Changes after launch never happen. Fix → one owner: workstream 8.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • You'll open similar sites, cities or customers again.
  • A launch depended on one person's memory.
  • Launches take longer than they should.
  • Before a planned series (four cities in 2027).

Skip it when…

  • A one-off site you'll never repeat.

8Self-check quiz

What are the three parts of the city IT runbook?

The store (the IT kit), the platform (config, router, vendors, status page), operations (monitoring, catalogue, on-call, desk).

How did the runbook change from Utrecht to The Hague?

61 steps → 48 (11 scripted) → 39; IT ready in 21 working days → 9.

What step was missed at Utrecht?

Adding the store leads to the service desk.

Why order steps by lead time?

The slowest things (the line) decide the earliest possible date.

What should happen to the runbook after each launch?

Add what was missed, script what repeated, remove what wasn't needed, bump the version.

what would you do?After The Hague, the team wants to add six new checks to the runbook. Nothing was removed. What do you ask?

Which existing steps weren't needed this time, and which of the six can be a script instead of a step? A runbook that only grows stops being read.

try it

Take the last time you set up something new (a site, an office, a customer). Write the steps in three parts and order them by lead time. Mark which three could be scripted before the next one.

remember!!

Nobody should open a city from memory. One versioned runbook, and every launch makes it shorter.

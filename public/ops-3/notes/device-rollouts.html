Updating 120 Handhelds

TL;DR

Pushing an app update to every device at once turns one bad version into a whole-company problem. Roll out in rings: a few test devices covering every model, then one site, then the rest. Leave time and a check between rings, do it outside working hours, keep the previous version ready to push back, and give the people at the first site a say in going on.

1The situation Larder, Tuesday 16 February 2027

Since January Dee's team treats the picker app as a product and ships it every week. Version 3.4 is pushed to every handheld at 23:30 on Monday, all stores at once. At 06:00 on Tuesday the barcode scanner doesn't work on Zuid's older model, bought in 2024: 31 devices. Pickers type product codes by hand for 40 minutes until version 3.3 is pushed back.

  • A weekly product is still being pushed like a one-off.
  • Nobody had tested on the older model, because nobody knew which stores had it.
  • The first test was every store's first shift.

2Real-life analogy the chain's new recipe

When a restaurant chain changes a recipe, the head chef cooks it first in the test kitchen, then one restaurant serves it for a night, then all of them. If guests at the first restaurant complain, the change stops there, and the old recipe is still in every kitchen's book.

3How it solves it rings, checks between them, and the old version kept

From 22 February every picker app release goes out in three rings through the device management tool:

  • Ring 0: five test devices covering every model, at Noord, in the hands of Tomás's most experienced pickers, on release day.
  • Ring 1: one whole store, overnight at 23:30. The store rotates week by week.
  • Ring 2: all other stores, the next night at 23:30.

Between rings there are three checks: crash-free rate ≥ 99.5 %, scan errors no higher than last week, and the store lead's go. If a check fails, the rollout halts, and the previous version, always kept on the server, is pushed back in 10 minutes. The asset list gets a model column, so “every model” is checkable. In April ring 0 catches version 3.9 failing on the same older model. No store notices.

  1. Know your fleet's models

    A model column in the asset list.

  2. Ring 0: one device of every model

    Used by experienced people, on release day.

  3. Ring 1: one site, outside working hours

    Rotate which site goes first.

  4. Check between rings

    Crashes, errors, and the site's own go.

  5. Keep the previous version ready

    Pushed back in minutes.

  6. Halt on any failed check

    And note it as a known error if needed.

Vocabulary for the exam

Ring (deployment ring)
A group of devices or users that gets a release at the same stage.
Staged rollout
Releasing to growing groups, with checks between them.
Fleet
All the devices of one kind you manage.
Crash-free rate
Share of sessions without an app crash.
Halt
Stopping a rollout part-way when a check fails.
Device model coverage
Making sure each model is tested before wide release.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Ring 0 of one model

    The old model is the one that breaks. Fix → one of every model.

  • Rolling out in working hours

    Updates freeze devices mid-shift. Fix → overnight, at 23:30.

  • No way back

    The old version isn't kept. Fix → keep it on the server, test pushing it back.

  • Same first store every week

    One store always carries the risk. Fix → rotate ring 1.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • You push updates to many devices.
  • A release broke devices at one site.
  • Your fleet has more than one model.
  • Releases are frequent.

Skip it when…

  • A handful of identical devices: test one, then update the rest.

8Self-check quiz

What went wrong on 16 February?

v3.4 was pushed to every handheld at once; the scanner failed on Zuid's older model.

What is ring 0 at Larder?

Five test devices covering every model, at Noord, used by experienced pickers on release day.

What checks happen between rings?

Crash-free ≥ 99.5 %, scan errors no higher than last week, and the store lead's go.

What's the way back?

The previous version, kept on the device management server, pushed back in about 10 minutes.

Why rotate the ring 1 store?

So one store doesn't always carry the risk.

what would you do?Ring 1 at Rotterdam looks fine on the numbers, but Yara says pickers find the new screen slower. Do you go on to ring 2?

Not yet: the store lead's go is one of the checks. Talk to Dee's team; maybe it's a habit that settles, maybe a real slowdown. Measure picking time for a day before deciding.

try it

List your device fleet by model. For your next update, choose a ring 0 that covers every model, a ring 1 site and a time outside working hours. Where is the previous version kept, and how fast can you push it back?

remember!!

Never make every store's first shift the test. Rings that cover every model, checks between them, and the old version kept.

When Must We Not Change Anything?

TL;DR

Some hours are worse than others for change: the evening peak, the morning delivery run, the week before Christmas. A change calendar writes them down as critical windows (weekly) and freezes (seasonal), shares them with whoever plans launches, and enforces them in the pipeline, so “not now” doesn't depend on memory. Keep one clear emergency door, with a name on it.

1The situation Larder, January 2027

Two of Larder's changes were made at the worst moment: the network rule tidy-up of 29 October, applied at 18:10 as the evening peak started, and the database primary resized in a hurry on 16 December, two days before the Christmas peak. On 12 January ADR 0017 says “no changes in the critical windows”. But nobody has written down when the windows are, and nothing stops anyone. King's Day is in April, and the WMS B cutovers start the same month.

  • A rule in a decision record isn't a rule in the pipeline.
  • Launch dates and change plans live in different calendars.
  • Without an emergency door, people will just bypass the rule.

2Real-life analogy the city's roadworks calendar

A city keeps a roadworks calendar: no digging on the ring road in rush hour, none on the shopping street in December, none near the stadium on match days. It's public, and the permit system enforces it: you can't get a permit for those times. A burst water main can still be fixed at any time, but the crew phones the city first.

3How it solves it one calendar, enforced by the pipeline, with an emergency door

From 1 February 2027 Larder has one change calendar:

  • Weekly critical windows: weekdays 07:00–09:00 (business deliveries, Korenwolf's mornings) and Friday 17:00–20:00 (the peak).
  • Freezes: campaign days, King's Day 26–28 April, and Christmas 13 December – 3 January. During a freeze only standard changes that don't touch customers run.
  • Store cutovers and city openings go on the same calendar, shared with Sanne's launch calendar, so a WMS B cutover never lands on an opening week.

The pipeline checks the clock. A deploy or normal change in a window is refused, unless it's marked emergency with the incident lead's name, which also posts to the change feed. From February to June there are no changes in windows except 2 emergencies, and average lead time doesn't change: people simply plan around the windows.

  1. Write down your critical windows

    When does an outage hurt most?

  2. Add seasonal freezes

    Peaks and campaign days, kept short.

  3. Share it with launch planning

    One calendar for changes and openings.

  4. Enforce it in the pipeline

    Refused in windows, not just discouraged.

  5. Keep an emergency door with a name

    The incident lead's, posted to the feed.

  6. Review the windows yearly

    Businesses change; so do their peaks.

Vocabulary for the exam

Critical window
A recurring time when changes are not made.
Change freeze
A longer period (a peak, a holiday) with only the safest changes.
Change calendar
The shared schedule of windows, freezes and planned big changes.
Emergency exception
A change allowed inside a window, with a named approver.
Maintenance window
An agreed time when planned disruption is acceptable.
Freeze debt
Changes that pile up during a freeze and go out together after it: plan for it.

4The maturity ladder where are we, and what is the next rung?

1By hand

“don't change things at busy times”

  • In people's heads.
  • Peak-time changes happen.

2Written down Larder now

a written, enforced calendar

  • Windows and freezes in the pipeline.
  • Emergency door with a name.

3Scripted

all change paths checked

  • Flags, consoles and vendors' maintenance too.
  • Freeze debt planned.

4Automatic

windows matter less

  • Safe change (canaries, instant undo) makes most windows unnecessary.
  • Only true peaks stay frozen.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • A rule nobody enforces

    “No changes on Friday evening” until someone forgets. Fix → the pipeline checks the clock.

  • Long freezes

    Changes pile up and go out together afterwards. Fix → keep freezes short; limit the first day back.

  • Forgetting other change paths

    The console, flags and vendors ignore the pipeline. Fix → bring them into the feed and the rule.

  • No emergency door

    People bypass the whole system under pressure. Fix → a named, logged exception.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Changes have caused incidents at peak times.
  • A peak or campaign is coming.
  • Launches and big changes clash.
  • Business clients have fixed delivery windows.

Skip it when…

  • Services nobody uses at particular times: windows add nothing.

what would you do?At 17:30 on a Friday, Ben has a fix for a typo on the checkout page. It's not causing errors. What happens?

It waits until 20:00 (or Monday): it's not an emergency, and the window exists for exactly this kind of harmless-looking change.

try it

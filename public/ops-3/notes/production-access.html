Who May Touch Production?

TL;DR

Every standing permission to change production is a door that can be used by mistake, or by someone else. Keep standing access to the few who need it daily, give everyone else access on request, for a few hours, with a reason and a second person, put every grant in the change feed, and keep one break-glass account for emergencies that raises an alarm when it's used. No shared accounts: every action belongs to a person.

1The situation Larder, 1 March 2027

Eli counts production access on 1 March. 11 people can change the production database. 14 can log in to Larder Core's VMs. There are 2 shared admin accounts, and their password sits in a vault that 9 people can open. Several of them never use production in their normal work. If something were changed by a shared account, nobody could say who did it. Korenwolf's security questionnaire for the renewal will ask exactly this.

  • Access granted once for a reason, and never taken back.
  • Mistakes are possible for people who don't usually work in production.
  • Shared accounts make the change feed and reviews meaningless.

2Real-life analogy the hotel's keys

In a hotel, housekeeping key cards open guest rooms only during the shift, and every use is logged. The master key is in a sealed envelope in the safe, and opening it rings the manager and goes in the book. Nobody carries a master key “just in case”, however senior they are.

3How it solves it few standing, the rest on request, break-glass with an alarm

From April:

  • Standing access: only Eli and Bram, for the platform, through personal accounts with two-factor sign-in.
  • On request: everyone else asks with a chat command, giving a reason. A second person approves (for the database: Eli or Bram), access lasts 4 hours, and the grant is posted to the change feed. Ben, Cho and Dee still have production access, just not always.
  • Break-glass: one emergency account, credentials sealed in the vault, for when the request path itself is down. Opening it pages Eli and messages Femke, and its use is reviewed the next day.
  • The two shared admin accounts are deleted on 9 April, after every script using them is moved to its own service account.
  • Who has what is reviewed quarterly.

In May there are 31 requests, with a median wait of 6 minutes. In May Ana's ADR 0021 moves all staff tools behind one identity provider, which makes the requests one click.

  1. Count who can change production

    Database, servers, cloud console, shared accounts.

  2. Keep standing access for daily work only

    Usually two or three people.

  3. Make access on request fast

    A reason, a second person, a few hours, minutes to get.

  4. Log every grant

    In the change feed, with the reason.

  5. Seal a break-glass account that alarms

    For when the normal path is down.

  6. Delete shared accounts; review quarterly

    Every action belongs to a person.

Vocabulary for the exam

Privileged access
Access that can change or read sensitive parts of production.
Least privilege
Each person has only the access their work needs.
Just-in-time access
Access granted when needed, for a limited time.
Break-glass account
An emergency account, sealed, whose use raises an alarm.
Service account
A non-human account for a script or system, with its own permissions.
Standing access
Access that is always on.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Requests that take a day

    People hoard access instead. Fix → make requests fast: minutes.

  • Break-glass nobody watches

    It becomes the easy way in. Fix → its use alarms and is reviewed.

  • Deleting shared accounts first

    Scripts using them break at night. Fix → move scripts to service accounts first.

  • Never reviewing

    Standing access creeps back. Fix → quarterly review.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Many people can change production.
  • Shared admin accounts exist.
  • A client or auditor asks who can change what.
  • After someone made an accidental production change.

Skip it when…

  • Never: even small teams benefit from personal accounts and a log.

8Self-check quiz

Who has standing production access at Larder after April?

Only Eli and Bram, for the platform.

How does everyone else get access?

On request: a reason, a second person's approval, 4 hours, posted to the change feed.

What is break-glass for, and what happens when it's used?

Emergencies when the request path is down; using it pages Eli and messages Femke and is reviewed next day.

Why delete shared accounts?

Every action must belong to a person, or logs and reviews mean nothing.

What has to happen before deleting shared accounts?

Move every script using them to its own service account.

what would you do?Cho needs to check a payment row in production at 21:00, and Eli and Bram are both offline. What happens?

Cho requests access with a reason; if no approver answers and it's urgent, the on-call lead can approve, or break-glass is used, which alarms Eli and Femke and is reviewed next day. Waiting until morning is fine if it isn't urgent.

try it

List everyone who can change your production database and servers today. For each person, ask: do they need it every day? Draft the request path for the rest, with a reason, an approver and an expiry.

remember!!

Standing access for the few who need it daily; everyone else on request, for hours, logged. And no master keys carried “just in case”.

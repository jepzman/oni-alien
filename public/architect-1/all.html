From Requirements to Architecture

Stakeholders, drivers, context, the requirements that shape a system, first architecture shapes, and testing risky ideas early.

Understanding the problem who cares, what they want, and where the system ends

  1. 01Stakeholder MapList everyone who can affect or is affected by the system, and plan how to work with each.p. 01
  2. 02Business Drivers & GoalsTurn the business plan into a few measurable goals that every design choice can be checked against.p. 02
  3. 03System Context DiagramOne picture of the system as a box, with the people and systems around it, so everyone agrees where it ends.p. 03
  4. 04Domain Discovery (Event Storming)Map the business as a timeline of events on sticky notes with the people who do the work.p. 04

Requirements that shape architecture find the few needs that decide the design

  1. 05Architecturally Significant RequirementsFind the few requirements that would change the architecture, and design for those first.p. 05
  2. 06Quality Attribute ScenariosTurn “fast” and “reliable” into testable sentences: a stimulus, a context and a measurable response.p. 06
  3. 07Constraints vs AssumptionsSeparate the fixed rules you must obey from the beliefs you still have to check.p. 07
  4. 08NFR ChecklistA short list of quality questions to ask on every project, so nothing important is forgotten.p. 08

First shapes pick a starting structure you can grow

  1. 09Architecture Styles ComparedCompare monolith, modular monolith, microservices, event-driven and serverless against your drivers.p. 09
  2. 10Modular Monolith FirstStart as one deployable app with strict internal modules; split out a service only when you must.p. 10
  3. 11Bounded ContextsDraw boundaries where a word changes meaning, and give each area its own model and owner.p. 11
  4. 12Walking SkeletonBuild the thinnest end-to-end slice through every part first, then add flesh.p. 12

Validating early test the risky ideas while they're still cheap

  1. 13Spike vs Prototype vs PoCPick the right small experiment: a spike answers a question, a prototype tests an idea, a PoC proves it works.p. 13
  2. 14Fitness FunctionsTurn architecture rules into automated checks that run in CI, so the design can't quietly decay.p. 14
  3. 15Risk-StormingThe team marks risks on the architecture diagram together, then decides what to do about the biggest.p. 15

Opportunity Cost

TL;DR

A team's weeks are fixed; every yes spends some of them. The real cost of a yes isn't its effort, it's the next best thing those weeks would have done: its opportunity cost. Before agreeing to a request, name that next best thing and price the delay it would suffer. Often the honest answer isn't yes or no but a cheaper yes: a manual version, a smaller slice, a later date.

  • Origin: economics: Friedrich von Wieser (1914); in product, Marty Cagan, Teresa Torres and others
  • Lives in: a one-paragraph comparison in the reply to a request
  • Pairs with: cost of delay, saying no, options analysis

1The situation Larder, 25 March 2026

Mo joined in March to look after Larder for Business. The Korenwolf pilot starts on 4 May, and Mo asks Grace for office ordering in the app: “just four weeks for Ben's team”. Four weeks feels small, and the team is right there. But Ben's team is building approvals for Amsterdam (13 April) and the Rotterdam musts (done by 20 April). The four weeks aren't free; they'd come out of something.

  • Requests are judged by their own effort, not by what they push out.
  • The work pushed out has no advocate in the room.
  • Saying no outright to a colleague's first big request damages a new relationship.

2Real-life analogy the Saturday

A friend asks you to help them move on Saturday. “It's only a day.” But Saturday is also the kids' football final. The day of lifting boxes isn't the cost; missing the final is. Seeing that, you might find a cheaper yes: help on Sunday morning, or lend them your van.

3How it solves it name the next best thing, price it

Grace didn't say no to Mo. She showed him the comparison: four weeks of office ordering would push approvals to mid-May, about €30,000 of refunds, and leave Rotterdam with no slack. Then she offered a cheaper yes: run the twelve-week pilot by hand. Mo and Grace took both options to Ana's options analysis on 13 April, where “by hand” won.

  1. Ask what the weeks would replace

    Look at the plan: which item moves if this one comes in?

  2. Price the delay

    Use its cost of delay: €/week × weeks of delay.

  3. Add the non-money costs

    Slack lost on a fixed date, a promise broken, a team switched mid-flow.

  4. Compare openly

    Put the request and the next best use side by side for the asker.

  5. Look for a cheaper yes

    By hand, smaller, later, or someone else's team.

  6. Decide with the asker

    Or with whoever owns both, if it's not your call.

Vocabulary for the exam

Opportunity cost
The value of the best alternative you give up.
Next best use
What the same time would otherwise have done.
Capacity
The team's available weeks; fixed in the short term.
Cheaper yes
A smaller or manual way to meet the request.
Sunk cost
Time already spent; irrelevant to the next decision.
Trade-off
Giving up some of one thing to get more of another.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • Hidden costs become visible, in euros.
  • The pushed-out work gets an advocate.
  • Cheaper yeses instead of flat refusals.
  • Better relationships: the asker sees the trade-off.
  • Plans stay intact on fixed dates.

What we lose

  • Estimates are rough; the comparison can be disputed.
  • Takes time to write, for every big ask.
  • The asker may still escalate.
  • Cheaper yeses cost someone (support's hours).

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Effort only

    “It's only four weeks.” Fix → ask what those four weeks would otherwise do.

  • No price

    “Approvals would slip.” So what? Fix → put a euro figure on the slip.

  • Flat no

    Mo hears no in his first month. Fix → offer a cheaper yes.

  • Deciding alone

    Grace overrules Mo on a business question. Fix → bring both options to whoever owns the trade-off.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Any request that needs real team time.
  • Requests from new or senior colleagues.
  • When a plan has a fixed date nearby.
  • When “we're here anyway” is the argument.

Skip it when…

  • Tiny requests: triage handles them.
  • When the team has genuine spare capacity (rare).

8Self-check quiz

What is opportunity cost?

The value of the next best thing the same time or money would have done.

How did Grace price Mo's request?

Four weeks of delayed approvals at about €30k, plus Rotterdam losing its slack.

What's a cheaper yes?

A smaller, manual or later way to meet the request, like running the pilot by hand.

Why compare openly with the asker?

They see the trade-off themselves and can help decide.

try it

Take the next request that needs more than a week of your team's time. Write down what it would push out, put a euro figure on that delay, and propose one cheaper yes. Send it to the asker as three short paragraphs.

remember!!

The price of a yes is the football final you'll miss. Look for the cheaper yes.

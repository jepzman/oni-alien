Feature Request Triage

TL;DR

Requests reach a product owner through a dozen doors: chat, corridors, email, meetings. Give them one front door (a short form or a single channel), look at everything new in a short weekly triage, and give each request one of four answers: it joins an existing opportunity, it's a quick fix done this week, it's parked with a review date, or it's declined with a reason. Everyone hears back within a week, and the list is public.

  • Family: saying no
  • Origin: service-desk and support triage practice; product intake processes
  • Lives in: a form, a channel and a public list

1The situation Larder, 2 March 2026

Since January, requests have reached Grace through Slack messages, corridor chats, emails, stand-ups and lunch: 47 of them by the end of February, from Sam, Joost, Tomás, Priya, Femke and the team. Grace answered each one in her head. Nobody else could see them. Sam asked about his auto-refund button three times; Tomás waited six weeks for a one-line font-size change on the picker screen.

  • Requests through many doors get lost or answered inconsistently.
  • Askers don't know where they stand, so they ask again, louder.
  • Tiny fixes wait as long as big features.

2Real-life analogy the triage nurse

In an emergency room, everyone who walks in is seen within minutes by a triage nurse and sorted: now, soon, later, or see your GP instead. Not everyone is treated at once, and some are sent elsewhere. But nobody sits in the waiting room wondering if anyone knows they're there.

3How it solves it one door, one weekly look, four answers

The first triage cleared the 47-request backlog in 90 minutes: 9 joined existing opportunities, 6 were quick fixes (Tomás's font size shipped that week), 21 were parked with review dates and 11 declined with a reason. After that, Mondays took 30 minutes. Sam's button? Parked, with a link to the approvals work that makes most of his refunds unnecessary, and he could see that on the list.

  1. Open one front door

    A five-field form (who, what, why, how often, how urgent) posting to one channel.

  2. Redirect the other doors

    Kindly: “can you drop that in the form? I'll answer by Monday.”

  3. Triage weekly, briefly

    30 minutes with a tech lead and a designer; every new request gets one of four answers.

  4. Four answers only

    Joins an opportunity · quick fix (< 1 day) · parked with a review date · declined with a reason.

  5. Answer within a week

    Reply to the asker with the answer and a link.

  6. Keep the list public

    Anyone can see every request, its answer and its date.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • Nothing lost: every request is written down.
  • Everyone hears back within a week.
  • Quick fixes ship quickly instead of waiting.
  • Fewer repeat asks: the answer is public.
  • Patterns show up: many requests point to one opportunity.

What we lose

  • A weekly habit to keep up.
  • Redirecting people to a form feels bureaucratic at first.
  • Public declines can sting.
  • Quick fixes can crowd out planned work if not capped.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Many doors stay open

    Requests still arrive in DMs and get answered there. Fix → kindly redirect every one to the form.

  • Triage becomes planning

    Monday's 30 minutes turns into two hours of debate. Fix → four answers only; debate belongs elsewhere.

  • Silent parking

    “Parked” with no reason or date. Fix → every park gets a reason and a review date.

  • Quick fixes unbounded

    Twenty one-day fixes a week. Fix → cap quick fixes, e.g. two days of team time a week.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • When requests reach you through many channels.
  • When people ask the same thing repeatedly.
  • Teams serving many internal stakeholders.
  • After a busy period, to clear a backlog of asks.

Skip it when…

  • Very small teams with one or two stakeholders.
  • Incidents and bugs: they have their own process.

8Self-check quiz

What are the four triage answers?

Joins an existing opportunity, quick fix, parked with a review date, declined with a reason.

How fast should askers hear back?

Within a week.

Why keep the list public?

Askers see where they stand without asking again, and patterns become visible.

What should the form ask for?

The problem, who has it and how often, not just the solution.

try it

Collect every request you've received in the last month from chat, email and memory onto one list. Give each one of the four answers in a 45-minute session with a tech lead, and send every asker a one-line reply this week. Then set up the form.

remember!!

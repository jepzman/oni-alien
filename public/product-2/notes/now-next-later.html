Now / Next / Later Roadmap

TL;DR

A Gantt chart of features with dates to December promises precision nobody has, and is wrong by February. A now / next / later roadmap has three columns: now (being built, committed), next (likely, being shaped) and later (possible, still fuzzy). No dates beyond “now”, every card names the bet or outcome it serves, and the board is updated monthly. Honest uncertainty earns more trust than precise bars that slip.

1The situation Larder, 6 February 2026

Grace inherited a roadmap: a Gantt chart from 2025 with 14 features and exact dates through December. By February, nine are already late, two have nothing to do with the strategy she just agreed, and Femke asks a fair question: is the roadmap still true? Nobody can say, so nobody trusts it, so everyone keeps their own list.

  • Dates far ahead are guesses presented as facts.
  • Each slip erodes trust in the whole roadmap.
  • Features without a why can't be questioned or swapped.
meanwhile, in Ana’s notebook
  • Walking Skeleton (Ana, vol. 1): the “Rotterdam in the app” card in Grace's Now column runs on this skeleton.

2Real-life analogy the renovation plan

Renovating a house: the leaking roof is now (quotes in, starts Monday, detailed). The kitchen is next (a rough sketch, likely this year). The garden is later (an idea). Nobody books the gardener for next September on day one; the plan is honest about what's known, and gets sharper as each job comes closer.

3How it solves it three columns, a why on every card

Grace replaced the Gantt chart with three columns on 6 February. Every card names the bet it serves; two old features with no bet disappeared. The board is updated on the first Monday of each month, with a one-line change log. On 31 March, after the Rotterdam trade-off sliders, “approvals in Rotterdam” moved within Next to June, and because the roadmap had never promised a date, nobody felt lied to.

  1. Start from the strategy

    Every card should serve one of the bets or outcomes on the strategy page.

  2. Fill Now honestly

    Only what's being built or about to be: committed, detailed.

  3. Shape Next

    Likely in the coming months; problems more than solutions.

  4. Keep Later fuzzy

    Possible directions, written as problems or themes.

  5. No dates beyond Now

    Dates belong in the release plan, with confidence levels.

  6. Update monthly, log changes

    One line per change: what moved and why.

Gantt chart
Bars on a timeline: good for plans, bad for roadmaps.
Change log
A dated line for each move on the roadmap.

tools: a board with three columns (Miro, FigJam, Trello, ProdPad, Productboard), or a Markdown page. ProdPad popularised the format and has a template.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • Honest about uncertainty, so it stays true.
  • Every card has a why, linked to the strategy.
  • Easy to update as you learn.
  • Trust grows: fewer broken promises.
  • Readable in a minute by anyone.

What we lose

  • Some stakeholders want dates: give them the release plan.
  • Can feel vague to people used to Gantt charts.
  • Later can become a dumping ground.
  • Needs the monthly update to stay credible.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Dates sneak back

    “Next (by 15 May)”. Fix → dates go in the release plan, not the roadmap.

  • Later as a dump

    Fifty cards in Later. Fix → keep Later to a handful of themes.

  • Features without a why

    Cards that serve no bet. Fix → every card names its bet or outcome.

  • Never updated

    February's board in June. Fix → update monthly, with a change log.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Replacing a date-heavy roadmap that keeps slipping.
  • Sharing direction with leadership and other teams.
  • Products with a lot of uncertainty.
  • After writing a strategy on a page.

Skip it when…

  • Contractual delivery dates: those need a release plan.
  • Very short projects: a release plan is enough.

8Self-check quiz

What goes in each column?

Now: committed work being built. Next: likely, being shaped. Later: possible, fuzzy.

Where do dates go?

In the release plan, with confidence levels, not on the roadmap.

What must every card say?

Which bet or outcome it serves.

How often should it be updated?

Monthly, with a change log.

try it

Redraw your current roadmap as three columns. For each card, write the bet or outcome it serves; anything without one goes on a separate list to question. Remove every date beyond Now and share the board with one stakeholder who used to ask for dates.

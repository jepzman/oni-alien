Where Will the Money End Up?

TL;DR

Two ways to forecast where the money will end up. By formula: assume the cost performance so far continues, so the estimate at completion (EAC) ≈ budget ÷ CPI. Quick, objective, blind to what's coming. Bottom-up: each line's owner forecasts from what they know (the trip next week, the quiet weekend). Do both and explain the difference. Then say what's left to spend (ETC), how far over or under you'll land (VAC) and how efficiently the rest must go to fit (TCPI).

1The situation Larder, 20–27 March 2026

Two forecasts reach the steering group of 27 March. Marco, from earned value: €374k ÷ 0.98 ≈ €380k, the whole reserve gone, because hiring keeps costing more. Sanne, line by line with each owner: €379k after the weekend work. The totals are close. The stories behind them are completely different, and the steering group will act on the story, not the number.

  • A formula only knows the past.
  • Owners know what's coming, and can be optimistic.
  • A forecast without its reasons can't be acted on.

2Real-life analogy the phone bill

Halfway through the month you've used your phone allowance faster than usual. One forecast: multiply the pace; you'll be over. Another: think about what's coming: a trip abroad next week (worse), a quiet weekend (better). The first is quick and knows only the past. The second knows things the formula can't. Sensible people do both and look at why they differ.

3How it solves it two forecasts, one explanation

Sanne puts both forecasts on one page. Formula (20 March): EAC ≈ €374k ÷ 0.98 ≈ €380k; variance at completion ≈ −€6k, the whole reserve as it then stood. Line by line (27 March): builder €153k (including the €5k weekend work), cold room €78k, equipment €48.5k, people €62.5k, marketing €37k: €379k. Then she explains the gap: the formula assumes hiring keeps overrunning; the owners know the agency has agreed a fixed fee for the last hires and marketing is holding €3k back. The steering group gets one sentence: “we'll land at about €379k–€380k; the reserve is nearly gone; any new cost needs a decision.” On 22 May the actual was €379.6k. The formula had the total right and the reasons wrong: the reserve went on the weekend work and the router, not on hiring.

Vocabulary for the exam

Estimate at completion (EAC)
The expected final cost.
EAC by CPI
BAC ÷ CPI: assumes cost performance stays as it is.
Bottom-up EAC
Actual cost so far + a fresh estimate of what's left.
Estimate to complete (ETC)
EAC − AC: what's still to spend.
Variance at completion (VAC)
BAC − EAC: the expected over- or underspend.
To-complete performance index (TCPI)
(BAC − EV) ÷ (BAC − AC): the efficiency needed on the rest to finish on budget.

4Light, standard, heavy how much of this do you need?

Light

small budgets

  • Ask the owner where it will end.
  • One number with a reason.
  • Monthly.

Standard

Rotterdam

  • Formula and line-by-line, side by side.
  • ETC, VAC, TCPI.
  • One sentence for the steering group.

Heavy

large contracts

  • Several EAC formulas (CPI, CPI × SPI).
  • Independent EAC review.
  • Formal re-baselining when VAC passes a threshold.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Formula only

    Blind to what's coming. Fix → ask the owners too.

  • Owners only

    Optimism. Fix → check against the formula.

  • A number without reasons

    Nobody knows what to do. Fix → explain the difference.

  • Forecast = budget

    Every month. Fix → a real number, even if uncomfortable.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Monthly, and after any big change.
  • When the reserve is getting small.
  • Before a steering group decision about money.
  • Once earned value is available.

Skip it when…

  • Tiny budgets with nothing left to buy.

8Self-check quiz

BAC 374, EV 175, AC 178. EAC by formula?

CPI = 175 ÷ 178 ≈ 0.983, so EAC = 374 ÷ 0.983 ≈ €380k.

What does a TCPI above 1 mean?

The rest of the work must be done more efficiently than planned to finish on budget.

Why did the two forecasts differ?

The formula assumed hiring would keep overrunning; the owners knew the agency fee was now fixed and marketing was holding €3k back.

What was the actual final cost?

€379.6k.

In April the formula says €385k and the owners say €378k. What does Sanne do?

Ask which owner forecast is hopeful and which risk the formula is seeing; report a range with the reason, and treat €385k as possible until explained.

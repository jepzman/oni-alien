Whose Money Is It?

TL;DR

Before spending a euro, agree the rules for the money: what the budget covers (and what it doesn't), the units (with or without VAT, which currency), who may spend how much, how spending is counted (committed, spent, forecast) and reported, and how the reserve may be used. It fits on a page and takes an hour with the finance lead. Without it, one number, “the budget”, means something different to everyone who hears it.

  • Family: planning the money
  • Lives in: one page with the finance lead's signature
  • Pairs with: the charter, who decides what, the budget

1The situation Larder, 27 January 2026

Sanne has been told, more than once, that Rotterdam's budget is €380k. She asks Marco three questions. Does it include our own engineers' time? No. May I sign a purchase order? There's no rule yet. Is it with or without VAT? Without, which Joost had assumed the other way. The number was real. Nothing around it had been written down.

  • “The budget” means different things to different people.
  • Nobody knows who may commit money, or how much.
  • Some real costs aren't in it at all, and nobody has said so.

2Real-life analogy the holiday fund

A family saving for a holiday agrees a few things before anyone spends from the fund: what it covers (travel and the hotel, not shopping), who may pay from it (either parent, up to a limit without asking the other), and how they keep track (every receipt in one envelope). Families that skip this don't argue about the holiday. They argue about the shoes.

3How it solves it one page of money rules, signed by the finance lead

Sanne and Marco spend an hour and write one page. Covers: external spend and new store staff until opening, in euros excluding VAT. Doesn't cover: Larder's own people's time (about 170 person-days of engineers, Tomás's team and Sanne, which Marco tracks separately), the e-bike lease and rent from opening day (operations). Who may spend: Sanne up to €5,000 within a budget line, Joost above that, Marco with a veto on anything outside the budget. These are the same limits as the change triggers. Counted three ways, committed, spent and forecast, by line, every Friday. The reserve moves only through a logged change. Marco signs it. Joost, reading it, finally understands why his VAT-inclusive sums never matched.

  1. Ask the finance lead what the number covers

    And what it doesn't.

  2. Fix the units

    Currency; with or without VAT.

  3. Write who may commit how much

    Match the change triggers.

  4. Decide how money is counted

    Committed, spent, forecast.

  5. Decide how and when it's reported

    By line, on a fixed day.

  6. Write how the reserve may be used

    And get it signed.

Vocabulary for the exam

Budget
The approved amount for the project, with its breakdown.
Commitment
Money promised by an order or contract, even if not yet paid.
Actual cost
Money for work done or goods received.
Internal cost
The cost of the organisation's own people's time.
Delegated authority
How much a person may commit without asking.
Excluding VAT
Amounts without value-added tax, which a business usually reclaims.

              Marco      veto on anything outside the budget · approves moves between lines
ORDERS        through Marco's team: a purchase order number before any supplier starts
COUNTED       committed (orders, contracts) · spent (work done, goods received) ·

RESERVE       €20k, drawn only through a logged change, never by moving lines quietly
REPORTS       Friday status (one line) · monthly to Marco (the table)

tools: an hour with the finance lead, one page, and the change triggers you already have.

4Light, standard, heavy how much of this do you need?

Light

a small internal job

  • Ask: what does it cover, with or without VAT?
  • Who may spend: you, up to a limit.
  • A note in chat.

Standard

Rotterdam

  • One page of money rules.
  • Spending limits matching the triggers.
  • Three numbers, by line, weekly.

Heavy

large or public money

  • A cost management plan.
  • Formal delegated financial authority.
  • Audit trail and cost accounting codes.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • A number with no rules

    Everyone reads it differently. Fix → write what it covers.

  • Hidden costs

    Your own people's time isn't free. Fix → say what's excluded and who tracks it.

  • VAT confusion

    Sums that never match. Fix → fix the units.

  • No spending limits

    Every purchase becomes a meeting. Fix → delegate up to a threshold.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Before the first commitment.
  • When you inherit a budget number.
  • When finance and the sponsor read the budget differently.
  • At the start of each phase with new money.

Skip it when…

  • Never; for tiny jobs it's two sentences.

8Self-check quiz

Does Rotterdam's budget include Larder's own engineers' time?

No. Marco tracks it separately (about 170 person-days).

Up to how much may Sanne commit alone?

€5,000 per commitment, within a budget line.

Name the three numbers counted every Friday.

Committed, spent and forecast.

Joost wants to order €7,000 of signage. Who decides?

Joost himself as sponsor (above €5,000), within the budget; Marco only if it's outside a line.

Priya asks to move €3k from marketing to people. What does Sanne do?

Moves between lines need Marco's approval: she takes it to him with the reason.

try it

Ask your finance contact four questions about your project's budget: what does it cover, what doesn't it, with or without VAT, and who may commit how much? Write the answers on one page and ask them to sign it.

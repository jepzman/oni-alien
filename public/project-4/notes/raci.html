a.k.a. planning resource management: the responsibility matrix (RACI)

Who Does What?

TL;DR

A RACI table answers “who does what?” for each deliverable or decision. Responsible: does the work. Accountable: answers for it and signs it off; exactly one per row. Consulted: asked before. Informed: told after. Most confusion on a project is two people who each think they're the A, or none. Draw the table early, with the people in it, and fix every row with no A or two.

  • Family: getting the people and things
  • Lives in: one table in the plan, rows = deliverables, columns = people
  • Pairs with: the role card, who decides what, decision rights

1The situation Larder, 2 February 2026

Kees needs the shelving layout signed off before he can place the electrical points. Tomás thinks Joost signs it: it's a store. Joost thinks Tomás does: he'll run it. Each is waiting for the other, and the builder is waiting for both. In the same week Priya and Grace each believe they have the last word on the launch messages to customers.

  • Some decisions have no owner; some have two.
  • People wait politely for each other.
  • The role card covered Sanne and Joost, not everyone else.

2Real-life analogy the wedding

At a wedding, the couple decides. A planner books the venue and the band. The parents are asked about the guest list. The guests are told the date. Four roles, all clear. Trouble starts when the planner thinks they decide the menu, or two parents each think the guest list is theirs.

3How it solves it one table, one A per row, agreed in the room

Sanne draws the table with the leads on 2 February: rows are the deliverables, columns the people. The rule: exactly one A per row, at least one R. Two rows change in the room. The shelving layout moves into “stocked and laid out”, where Tomás is A (he'll run the store; Joost is consulted), and Kees gets his signature that afternoon. The launch messages: Priya is A, Grace is consulted on anything about the app. Sanne is A for nothing except the plan's integrity under Joost; on most rows she's I or C, which is the point. The table goes into the plan with three more lines: how people will be found (hire, borrow), trained (at Noord), and released (borrowed staff home by 8 May).

  1. List the deliverables or decisions as rows

    Not tasks.

  2. List the people as columns

    Including suppliers.

  3. Fill in R, A, C, I with the people in the room

    Not alone.

  4. Exactly one A per row

    Fix every row with none or two.

  5. Check no one is A for everything

    Including you.

  6. Add how people are found, trained, released

    The rest of the resource plan.

Vocabulary for the exam

RACI
Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed.
Responsible
Does the work.
Accountable
Answers for the result and signs it off. One person.
Consulted
Asked for input before; two-way.
Informed
Told after; one-way.
Resource management plan
How people and things will be found, used, looked after and released.

4Light, standard, heavy how much of this do you need?

Light

one small team

  • Say who decides each big thing.
  • One sentence each.
  • No table.

Standard

Rotterdam

  • A RACI by deliverable.
  • One A per row.
  • Three lines on finding, training and releasing people.

Heavy

many teams and suppliers

  • RACI per work package.
  • A full resource management plan.
  • Team charters per team.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Two A's

    Two people decide; neither does. Fix → one A per row.

  • No A

    Everyone waits. Fix → fill every row.

  • Project manager as A for everything

    A bottleneck, and wrong. Fix → A where you answer; C or I elsewhere.

  • Drawn alone

    Nobody agrees with it. Fix → draw it with the people in it.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Early, after the deliverables are known.
  • When decisions wait for each other.
  • When suppliers or new people join.
  • When two people claim the same decision.

Skip it when…

  • A two-person job where who does what is obvious.

8Self-check quiz

What's the rule for A?

Exactly one per row.

Who signs off the shelving layout?

Tomás: A for “stocked and laid out”.

R or A: Kees fitting the store?

R. Joost is A.

Why is Sanne A on only one row?

She answers for the plan, not for the deliverables; owners answer for those.

On 10 March the cold-room supplier asks who approves the door position. Whose call?

Joost (A for the store fitted), with Tomás consulted.

try it

Draw a RACI for your project's five most important deliverables. Count the A's in each row. Fix every row with none or more than one, with the people involved.

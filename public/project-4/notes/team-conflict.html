Why Is the Team Arguing?

TL;DR

New teams go through stages: polite at first (forming), then friction (storming), then shared ways of working (norming), then real flow (performing). Conflict is part of the second stage, not a sign of failure. Work it through by separating positions (“the app is broken”) from interests (“no wrong items on opening day”), agreeing the facts, and finding a rule both sides can live with. Collaborate when both needs matter; compromise when time is short; don't avoid.

  • Family: leading and balancing
  • Lives in: a short, regular meeting with both sides and a shared log
  • Pairs with: leading without authority, disagree and commit, issue log

1The situation Larder, rehearsal week, 20–24 April 2026

Rehearsal week. Rotterdam's new pickers run real orders on real handhelds. By Tuesday, Tomás's pickers say the app is broken; Ben's team says the pickers are using it wrong. By Wednesday the two groups talk about each other, not to each other. Go / no-go is on Friday. Both sides are partly right, and neither has said what it actually needs.

  • Each side defends a position.
  • Nobody has agreed what the facts are.
  • The clock makes everyone less patient.
meanwhile, in Grace’s notebook
  • Disagree and Commit (Grace, vol. 5): Grace's disagree and commit: once a decision is made, people who argued against it commit to making it work.

2Real-life analogy the orange

Two children argue over one orange. A parent cuts it in half; both are unhappy. If the parent had asked why, they'd have found that one wanted the juice and the other the peel for a cake. Each could have had all of what they needed. The argument was about positions; the solution was in the interests.

3How it solves it interests, facts, a rule, a short daily meeting

Sanne sees the stage for what it is: the team formed politely in the March training; this is storming. On Wednesday she gets Tomás and Ben in a room and asks one question each: what do you need from this week? Tomás: no wrong items on opening day. Ben: no risky code changes three days before release. Neither need contradicts the other. They agree the facts first: every problem goes in a log with the time and a photo of the screen. Then a rule: a fifteen-minute triage at 16:00 with both; if a problem can cause a wrong item, Ben's team fixes it in code; otherwise it becomes a training note for Yara's team. By Friday: 14 problems, 4 fixed in code (2 of which really did cause wrong items), 7 training notes, 3 parked. Norming, just in time. By the second week of hypercare the store is performing.

  1. Recognise the stage

    Storming is normal.

  2. Get both sides in one room

    Not about each other.

  3. Ask each what they need

    Interests, not positions.

  4. Agree the facts

    A shared log, with evidence.

  5. Agree a rule both can live with

    Who decides what, by when.

  6. Meet briefly and often until it settles

    Then stop.

Vocabulary for the exam

Forming
A new team, polite and unsure.
Storming
Friction as people find out how to work together.
Norming
Shared ways of working emerge.
Performing
The team works smoothly towards the goal.
Position
What someone says they want.
Interest
Why they want it: the need underneath.
Collaborating
Finding a solution that meets both sides' needs.

4Light, standard, heavy how much of this do you need?

Light

a small team

  • Ask each side what they need.
  • Agree one rule.
  • Done in an hour.

Standard

Rotterdam

  • Interests, facts, a rule.
  • A short daily triage until it settles.
  • Results logged.

Heavy

large or long conflicts

  • A facilitator or mediator.
  • Team agreements in writing.
  • Escalation paths for unresolved conflict.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Treating storming as failure

    Panic, blame. Fix → it's a stage; work through it.

  • Splitting the orange

    A compromise nobody wanted. Fix → find the interests first.

  • Arguing without facts

    Opinions harden. Fix → a shared log with evidence.

  • Avoiding it

    It surfaces on opening day. Fix → one room, this week.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • New teams, especially before a deadline.
  • When groups talk about each other, not to each other.
  • When a disagreement repeats.
  • During rehearsals and hand-offs.

Skip it when…

  • Healthy debate that's moving towards a decision: let it run.

Which stage was rehearsal week?

Storming.

Position or interest: “no wrong items on opening day”?

Interest.

What was the rule?

Log every problem; if it can cause a wrong item, fix it in code; otherwise, a training note.

How many of the 14 problems were fixed in code?

Four (two of which caused wrong items).

After opening, Yara and the couriers' lead clash over the loading bay. What does Sanne do first?

Ask each what they need from the bay, agree the facts (when, who, how long), and help them find a rule; Yara, as store manager, owns it from there.

How Many People, and What Else?

TL;DR

Work out the people and things a project needs from the demand they must serve: the volume of work, a productivity rate you trust, the hours, the shifts, a margin for absence and growth. Equipment follows from the same sum. “As many as last time” copies last time's demand and last time's mistakes. Write the rates down, so the estimate can be checked when real demand arrives.

1The situation Larder, 10 February 2026

Joost's first answer to “how many people does Rotterdam need?” is sixty, like Zuid. At €1,500 each to recruit and train, that's €90k against a people line of €60k. But Zuid serves a different number of orders from a different-sized store. Sixty is a habit, not an estimate. Sanne asks the question underneath: how many orders will Rotterdam handle in its first month, and how many people does that take?

  • Headcount copied from another store copies its demand too.
  • The budget line and the headcount were never compared.
  • Equipment was being ordered from a separate guess.

2Real-life analogy the caterer's sum

A good caterer doesn't send “as many waiters as last time.” They ask: how many guests, how many courses, sit-down or standing? One waiter per twelve guests for a sit-down dinner, two at the bar. The plates and glasses come from the same sum, with a few spares. Last time's party had different guests.

3How it solves it from demand to people to equipment

Joost's plan for the first month is about 350 orders a day, open 08:00–22:00, rising to 500 by the end of May. Pickers: Noord picks about 9 orders an hour per picker (6 minutes 40 an order), so 350 orders is about 39 picker-hours a day; add 10 for receiving and stocking: about 7 shifts a day, 49 a week, so 15 pickers at three or four shifts each. Couriers: about 2.5 deliveries an hour, so 140 courier-hours a day, 20 shifts, 140 a week, so 25 couriers at five or six shifts. Forty people, and the €60k line holds. Equipment from the same sums: 40 e-bikes (24 on the road at the Friday peak, plus charging spares and growth) and 30 handhelds. The growth to 500 a day becomes a second hiring wave in May, paid by operations.

  1. Start from demand

    Volume, hours, peaks.

  2. Use a productivity rate you trust

    From a running operation.

  3. Turn hours into shifts, shifts into people

    With realistic shifts per person.

  4. Add margins for absence and growth

    And say how big.

  5. Derive equipment from the same sums

    Peak use plus spares.

  6. Compare with the budget line

    Before hiring anyone.

Vocabulary for the exam

Resource requirements
The kinds and numbers of people and things the work needs.
Productivity rate
Output per person per hour: orders, deliveries.
Shift
A block of working hours for one person.
Full-time equivalent (FTE)
Total hours expressed as full-time people.
Resource breakdown structure
People and things grouped by kind.
Peak load
The busiest period the resources must cover.

4Light, standard, heavy how much of this do you need?

Light

a small team

  • Ask: how much work, how fast does one person do it?
  • One sum.
  • A little margin.

Standard

Rotterdam

  • Demand → hours → shifts → people.
  • Equipment from the same sums.
  • Checked against the budget line.

Heavy

large operations

  • Workforce models with absence rates and peaks.
  • Resource breakdown structures.
  • Capacity planning tools.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • “As many as last time”

    Last time's demand. Fix → start from this demand.

  • No margin

    One sick day and the shift fails. Fix → add absence and peaks.

  • Equipment from another guess

    Bikes don't match couriers. Fix → same sums for both.

  • Never checked

    Real demand differs. Fix → re-check in week one (note 15).

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Before hiring or ordering equipment.
  • When a headcount is quoted without a reason.
  • When demand changes.
  • For every new site.

Skip it when…

  • A project done entirely by an existing team.

8Self-check quiz

Pickers needed for 350 orders a day at 9 an hour?

About 39 hours of picking, plus receiving: about 15 pickers on part-time shifts.

Why not sixty like Zuid?

Zuid's demand is different; and sixty would cost €30k over the people line.

Where did 40 e-bikes come from?

24 on the road at the Friday peak, plus charging spares, plus growth.

Who pays for the second wave in May?

Operations: it's running the store, not launching it.

In week one orders are 540 a day, not 350. What does Sanne do with the estimate?

Re-run the sum with the real demand and pull the second wave forward (it went from mid-May to 4 May).

try it

Write your project's demand in one line, find one productivity rate you trust, and work out the people it needs. Compare with the number you were planning to hire.

remember!!

Count the guests before you book the waiters.

What Will It Cost?

TL;DR

A cost estimate puts a money figure on each piece of work. Build it bottom-up from the work breakdown (quotes, rates, catalogue prices) and check it against any top-down number you were given. Write, for every line, where the number came from and how sure it is. A quote you hold is firmer than a quote you expect; a rate times a guess is softer still. The gap between bottom-up and top-down is your first real piece of information about the budget.

1The situation Larder, 28 January 2026

The €380k didn't come from Rotterdam. Femke and Marco set it per city when the Series B closed in September, from what Zuid had cost and a bit for being a new city. Nobody has added up what Rotterdam actually needs. If the pieces come to €420k, the launch is underfunded from the start; if they come to €300k, there's money nobody has decided about.

  • A top-down number says what someone hopes, not what the work costs.
  • Without a breakdown, nobody can say which line is at risk.
  • Estimates without sources can't be defended or improved.
meanwhile, in Grace’s notebook
  • Business Case One-Pager (Grace, vol. 2): Grace's business case for approvals (12 Feb) counts engineers' time in money: 8 person-weeks × €6k ≈ €48k.

2Real-life analogy the kitchen renovation

Planning a new kitchen, you get one number from a friend: “kitchens cost about €20k.” You get another by adding up the quotes: units, worktop, appliances, the fitter. The friend's number tells you whether to start. The quotes tell you whether you can afford it, and which line to argue about. If they differ a lot, the difference is the first thing to understand.

3How it solves it five lines, each with its source and how sure

Sanne builds the estimate bottom-up from the workstreams, writing the source next to every number. Builder: €148k from Kees's indicative quote, ± 10 %. Cold room and fridges: €64k, a firm quote. Equipment and store IT: €48k at catalogue prices. People: 40 hires at €1,500 each (€500 to recruit, €1,000 for paid induction and training), ± 20 %. Marketing: €40k, Priya's plan. Total: €360k, €20k under the top-down €380k. Sanne doesn't spend the gap. She proposes it as the reserve, and on 30 January the budget is €380k with €20k set aside. Marco confirms it on 6 February, now that he can see what's under it.

  1. Start from the work breakdown

    A line per workstream or work package.

  2. Price each line from the best source you have

    Quote, catalogue, rate × quantity.

  3. Write the source and how sure

    Firm, ± 10 %, ± 20 %.

  4. Add it up

    Bottom-up total.

  5. Compare with any top-down number

    Understand the gap.

  6. Decide what the gap is for

    Usually: the reserve.

Vocabulary for the exam

Cost estimate
A money figure for a piece of work, with its basis.
Top-down estimate
A total set from above or from a similar past project.
Bottom-up estimate
The sum of estimates for the pieces.
Basis of estimate
Where a number came from and what it assumes.
Indicative quote
A supplier's price that isn't yet binding.
Accuracy range
How far the real cost might be from the estimate.

4Light, standard, heavy how much of this do you need?

Light

a small purchase

  • One quote, one number.
  • Say how sure.
  • No table.

Standard

Rotterdam

  • Bottom-up by line, with sources.
  • Compared with the top-down figure.
  • Accuracy per line.

Heavy

large or public projects

  • Estimates per work package with a basis-of-estimate document.
  • Independent cost review.
  • Estimate classes (from rough to definitive).

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Trusting the top-down number

    Underfunded, or slack nobody owns. Fix → add up the pieces.

  • No sources

    Can't be defended. Fix → a source next to every number.

  • Indicative treated as firm

    The quote moves. Fix → mark how sure each is.

  • Spending the gap

    The reserve disappears into lines. Fix → name it and set it aside.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • When you're handed a budget number.
  • Before asking for money.
  • Before signing big contracts.
  • At each phase, with better information.

Skip it when…

  • Never; for tiny work, one quote and a range.

8Self-check quiz

Top-down or bottom-up: €380k per city?

Top-down.

Which line was softest, and why?

People, ± 20 %: hiring costs and training time depend on who is hired.

What did Sanne do with the €20k gap?

Proposed it as the reserve, set aside, not spread into lines.

On 24 Feb the builder offers a fixed price of €148k. What changes in the estimate?

The builder line goes from indicative (± 10 %) to firm at the same €148k.

The bottom-up had come to €410k. What would Sanne do?

Take the €30k gap to Joost and Marco before starting: more money, less scope, or a different plan.

try it

Build a bottom-up estimate of your project in five to ten lines, with a source and an accuracy for each. Compare it with the budget you were given. What is the gap, and who should decide what it's for?

remember!!

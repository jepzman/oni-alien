a.k.a. monitoring and controlling resourcing: availability, suppliers and release

Are the People and Things Still There?

TL;DR

A plan assumes people and things are there when needed. Check that it's still true, as often as the work demands: daily during a launch, weekly otherwise. Compare what's available (people on the rota, equipment working, deliveries due) with what the work needs today. When something's short, act before the peak: borrow (with every owner's agreement), hold suppliers to their terms, pull plans forward. And release people and things on the date you promised.

  • Family: leading and balancing
  • Lives in: a short daily check during busy phases, weekly otherwise
  • Pairs with: estimating resources, acquiring resources, hypercare

1The situation Larder, 29 April 2026

Day three. Orders are at 470 a day, well above the 350 planned for the first month. Three couriers are sick. Four e-bikes have flat tyres, and the lease supplier, who promised repairs within 24 hours, hasn't come. Two handhelds are broken. Everything in the plan was available on paper. At 08:30 today, a lot of it isn't, and the evening peak is nine hours away.

  • Plans assume availability; reality removes some every day.
  • Demand above plan uses up the margins faster.
  • Suppliers' promises need checking too.

2Real-life analogy the pilot's walk-round

Before every flight, a pilot walks round the aircraft: tyres, fuel, lights, and checks the crew are present and fit. The aircraft was certified months ago. The walk-round isn't about whether it was ever airworthy; it's whether it is today. Nobody thinks that's a waste of the pilot's time.

3How it solves it a daily check, action before the peak, release on time

During hypercare Yara and Sanne do a five-line check at 08:30: couriers on the rota against today's forecast, pickers, e-bikes ready, handhelds working, supplier deliveries due. On 29 April three lines are short. By 10:00: Zuid lends three couriers for four days, agreed with Joost and Zuid's manager this time; the lease supplier is held to its 24-hour repair clause (bikes back by evening, a credit for the delay); two spare handhelds go into use, leaving four. And because orders are running at 470, not 350, Joost pulls the second hiring wave forward from mid-May to 4 May. The other end of resourcing matters too: on 8 May the borrowed pickers go home to Noord and Zuid, on the day promised, and Joost checks it happened.

  1. List what the work needs today

    People, equipment, deliveries.

  2. Check what's actually available

    At a fixed time, early.

  3. Compare with today's demand

    Not last month's plan.

  4. Act on shortfalls before the peak

    Borrow, chase suppliers, re-plan.

  5. Hold suppliers to their terms

    And record when they miss.

  6. Release on the promised date

    And confirm it happened.

Vocabulary for the exam

Availability
Whether a person or thing can actually be used when needed.
Shortfall
Less available than the work needs.
Service level (SLA)
A supplier's promised response, like repair in 24 hours.
Absence rate
The share of planned staff not available on a given day.
Release
Returning people and things when the project no longer needs them.
Contingency staffing
A plan for covering absence: on-call lists, loans.

4Light, standard, heavy how much of this do you need?

Light

a small team

  • “Who's here, what's working?” at the start of the day.
  • Fix the gaps.
  • No board.

Standard

Rotterdam

  • A five-line availability check at 08:30 in busy phases.
  • Act before the peak.
  • Release on the promised date.

Heavy

large operations

  • Workforce management systems.
  • Supplier performance reports and penalties.
  • Formal resource release procedures.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Trusting the plan

    Availability changes daily. Fix → check at a fixed time.

  • Checking after the peak

    Too late to act. Fix → first thing in the morning.

  • Supplier promises unchecked

    Repairs drift. Fix → hold them to the terms.

  • Forgetting the release

    Borrowed people never go home. Fix → release on the date, and confirm.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Busy phases: launches, hypercare, peaks.
  • When demand runs above plan.
  • With borrowed people or leased equipment.
  • At the end: releasing resources.

Skip it when…

  • Quiet steady phases: weekly is enough.

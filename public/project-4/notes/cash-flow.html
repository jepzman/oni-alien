When Does the Money Leave?

TL;DR

A budget answers how much; cash flow answers when. Money is committed when an order is signed, but it leaves the bank when invoices fall due, and suppliers' payment terms decide that: deposits, stage payments, retention, 30 days. Plan the cash by month with the finance lead, make sure the funding is there when the steep part comes, and never sign payment terms your organisation can't actually meet.

  • Family: planning the money
  • Lives in: a monthly cash table, agreed with finance
  • Pairs with: the budget's S-curve, sourcing, the contract

1The situation Larder, 26 February → 2 March 2026

This is Sanne's mistake for this collection. The builder's contract, signed on 26 February, says 20 % on signing: €29,600. Sanne agreed it; it's normal. What she didn't check: Larder pays suppliers once a month, 30 days after the invoice. On Friday Kees phones: he needs the money to buy materials. No money, no materials, no start on Monday 2 March, the first day on the critical path.

  • The total was in the budget; the timing wasn't.
  • Contract terms were agreed without the finance lead.
  • A cash problem became a schedule problem in a day.

2Real-life analogy the holiday deposit

A family can afford a holiday over the year. But the deposit is due on 3 March, and the salary arrives on the 25th. “Can we afford it?” and “do we have the cash on the day?” are different questions. Households learn to ask the second one before they book.

3How it solves it plan cash by month, and check terms before signing

Sanne phones Marco that afternoon and says it was her mistake. Marco makes an exception payment on Monday morning, and Kees starts on time. Then they fix the cause. Every contract's payment terms go into one table: the builder (20 % signing, 40 % at first fix, 35 % at handover, 5 % retention after the snags), the cold room (30 % with the order, 70 % on delivery), equipment (on delivery, net 30), recruiting per hire, marketing monthly. From that, a cash plan by month: €4k, €58k, €138k, €132k, €48k. About €270k leaves in March and April, which Marco needs to know for Larder's runway. And a rule: no payment terms signed without Marco. In the end the cash came within €3k of the plan every month.

  1. Collect every contract's payment terms

    Deposits, stages, retention, days.

  2. Turn them into cash by month

    With the finance lead.

  3. Find the steep months

    And check the funding covers them.

  4. Check your organisation's payment habits

    Monthly runs, approval steps.

  5. Never sign terms finance can't meet

    Ask before, not after.

  6. Compare plan and paid each month

    And explain the gaps.

Vocabulary for the exam

Cash flow
Money actually coming in and going out, by date.
Payment terms
When a supplier must be paid: on signing, on delivery, in 30 days.
Stage payment
A payment due when a stage of work is complete.
Retention
Part of the price held back until defects are fixed.
Funding
The money made available, and when.
Runway
How long the organisation's cash lasts at its current spending.

4Light, standard, heavy how much of this do you need?

Light

a few small invoices

  • Ask finance when they'll be paid.
  • Avoid deposits if you can.
  • No table.

Standard

Rotterdam

  • A table of payment terms.
  • Cash by month, planned and paid.
  • No terms signed without finance.

Heavy

large contracts

  • Cash-flow forecasts by week.
  • Funding released in tranches.
  • Payment certificates and retention schedules.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Terms signed without finance

    No cash on the day. Fix → finance sees the terms first.

  • Total only

    The steep months surprise everyone. Fix → cash by month.

  • Commitment = payment

    They happen weeks apart. Fix → track both.

  • Forgetting retention

    A payment months later. Fix → put it in the table.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Before signing any contract with deposits or stages.
  • When finance asks for a forecast.
  • When the organisation's cash is tight.
  • Monthly, until the last invoice.

Skip it when…

  • Internal work with no external payments.

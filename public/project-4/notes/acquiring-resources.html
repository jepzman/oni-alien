Where Do They Come From?

TL;DR

People and things reach a project in five ways: hire them, borrow them from another part of the organisation, lease or buy them, or negotiate a share of a team's time. Every one of them belongs to someone first. Agree each one with the owner and everyone who depends on it, put the dates on paper, and plan when it goes back. Most resource trouble starts with a yes from the wrong person.

1The situation Larder, 30 March → 2 April 2026

This is Sanne's mistake for this collection. Rotterdam's forty are new, and opening day needs a few people who've picked a thousand orders. On 30 March she agrees with Tomás to borrow four of Noord's most experienced pickers for the opening fortnight, from 27 April. On 2 April Joost reads the plan and stops her: 27 April is King's Day, Noord's busiest day of the year, five times a normal peak. She asked the store manager, not the person who plans Noord's King's Day.

  • A yes from one owner isn't a yes from everyone who depends on the resource.
  • The calendar knew it was King's Day; the loan didn't look.
  • Borrowing the best people weakens the place they come from.

2Real-life analogy borrowing the neighbour's car

You ask to borrow the neighbour's car for the weekend, and the neighbour says yes. On Saturday morning their teenager comes out for a driving test. The car belonged to the household, not to the person who said yes. Borrowing well means asking everyone who depends on the thing, and writing down when it comes back.

3How it solves it five ways in, each agreed with everyone who depends on it

Sanne fixes the loan the same day: two pickers from Noord and two from Zuid, from 28 April to 8 May, agreed with Joost and both store managers in writing. On opening day Rotterdam runs on its own fifteen, with Yara and Grace on the floor and Tomás where he's needed most: at Noord, for King's Day. Then she writes every source down. Hired: forty hourly staff through an agency (22), job boards (10), referrals (4) and internal moves (4); the first fifteen start on 30 March and train at Noord; the last joins on 17 April. Central hires: Marco's cap of four for 2026 is for salaried central roles, and Rotterdam used one: Sanne. Leased: forty e-bikes, by operations. Bought: the equipment and cold room (Vol. 2). Negotiated: 8–10 story points a sprint from Ben's and Dee's teams, agreed with Grace.

  1. For each resource, choose: hire, borrow, lease, buy, negotiate

    From the estimate.

  2. Find its owner

    And everyone who depends on it.

  3. Check the calendar on both sides

    Their peaks, not just yours.

  4. Agree dates in writing

    Start, end, return.

  5. Check limits

    Hiring caps, budgets, contracts.

  6. Plan the return

    When, and who checks it happened.

Vocabulary for the exam

Acquisition
Getting the people and things the project needs.
Pre-assignment
People named before the project starts.
Internal loan
People borrowed from another part of the organisation.
Resource owner
Whoever the person or thing belongs to day to day.
Headcount cap
A limit on permanent hires.
Release
Returning people and things when they're no longer needed.

4Light, standard, heavy how much of this do you need?

Light

one borrowed person

  • Ask their manager and them.
  • Dates in a message.
  • Return on time.

Standard

Rotterdam

  • A sources table.
  • Each loan agreed with every owner, in writing.
  • Returns planned.

Heavy

large programmes

  • Resource requests through a PMO.
  • Formal secondment agreements.
  • Hiring plans approved by HR and finance.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • A yes from one owner

    The others find out later. Fix → everyone who depends on it.

  • Only your calendar

    Their peak clashes with yours. Fix → check both.

  • No return date

    Loans become permanent, or vanish. Fix → write when it comes back.

  • Borrowing the best everywhere

    The home team suffers. Fix → spread loans; plan the gap.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Before hiring, borrowing or leasing.
  • When a resource is shared.
  • Around anyone's peak days.
  • When a cap or budget applies.

Skip it when…

  • The team is fixed and nothing is borrowed.

8Self-check quiz

Name the five ways resources reach a project.

Hire, borrow, lease, buy, negotiate.

Why was the first loan wrong?

It took four of Noord's best pickers on King's Day; it was agreed with Tomás, not with Joost, who plans Noord's peak.

Did Rotterdam's forty pickers and couriers count against Marco's cap of four?

No: the cap is for salaried central roles. Rotterdam used one: Sanne.

When did the borrowed pickers go home?

8 May.

On 20 April Ben offers to lend two of his engineers to Eli for a week. What does Sanne check?

With Grace, who negotiated the team's time, and with Ben's sprint plan; then dates and the return in writing.

try it

List every person and thing your project borrows. For each, write the owner, everyone else who depends on it, the return date, and whether their busiest day falls in your loan.

remember!!

The neighbour said yes. Ask about the driving test before you take the car.

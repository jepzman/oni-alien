Risk Register

TL;DR

A risk register is a short list of the things that could go wrong, each with likelihood and impact, an owner, actions that reduce it, and a date to look again. Keep it small (ten to fifteen), review it briefly and often, and close risks when they're gone. It turns worries raised in meetings into work someone owns, and shows leaders the few things that could really hurt.

  • Family: risk & transition
  • Origin: project and enterprise risk management (ISO 31000)
  • Lives in: one page reviewed every fortnight
  • Pairs with: risk-storming, failure mode analysis, migration planning

1The situation Larder, 24 April 2028

The 2028 plan has Ghent opening in June, Haarlem in September, Ordering's database split, Larder Plus growing fast, and Ana as the only architect. Risks come up in every meeting, get nodded at, and vanish. The last list is a wall photo from the December 2025 risk-storm.

  • Risks without owners don't get reduced.
  • Old lists go stale; new risks go unlisted.
  • Leaders hear about risks only when they happen.

2Real-life analogy the captain's list of dangers

On the chart table a captain keeps a list of dangers: shallows ahead, a storm forecast, a tired crew. Each has how likely, how bad, what we're doing, and who's watching it. It's read every watch, and crossed off when the ship is past.

3How it solves it likelihood, impact, owner, action, date

Ana started a register with twelve risks, scored 1–5 for likelihood and impact, each with an owner, actions and a review date. Top: Ana as a single point of failure (4 × 4; grow two more architects by September); Ghent's fit-out late (3 × 4, Joost; weekly builder check); Plus orders beating Friday capacity (3 × 3, Eli); the Ordering split corrupting orders (2 × 5, Ben; rehearse, keep rollback two weeks). Fifteen minutes every fortnight; the top five go into Femke's monthly report.

  1. List the risks

    From the plan, risk-storms, incidents, people.

  2. Score likelihood and impact

    1–5 each; impact in the business's terms.

  3. Give each an owner

    Someone who can act on it.

  4. Write the actions

    Reduce likelihood, reduce impact, or accept.

  5. Review briefly and often

    Fortnightly, fifteen minutes.

  6. Close and report

    Close what's past; top five to leaders.

Vocabulary for the exam

Risk
Something that might happen and would hurt a goal.
Likelihood
How probable it is.
Impact
How bad it would be.
Mitigation
An action that reduces likelihood or impact.
Risk owner
The person responsible for watching and acting.
Single point of failure
One part (or person) whose loss stops everything.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • Owned risks, actually reduced.
  • New risks listed early.
  • Leaders see the few that matter.
  • People risks on the list too.
  • Closed risks show progress.

What we lose

  • Scores are judgments.
  • Can become a bureaucratic list.
  • Fortnightly upkeep.
  • Uncomfortable to list people risks.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Hundreds of risks

    Every worry listed. Fix → ten to fifteen that matter.

  • No owners

    “The team” owns it. Fix → one named person.

  • Write once

    A list from January. Fix → fortnightly review.

  • Only technical risks

    People and suppliers missing. Fix → people, suppliers, dates too.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Programmes with several launches or migrations.
  • When risks keep coming up and nothing changes.
  • Reporting to leadership.
  • Before go/no-go decisions.

Skip it when…

  • Small, short pieces of work (a risk-storm is enough).

The Architecture Backlog

TL;DR

Architecture work (debt, ADR follow-ups, failing fitness functions, migration steps, incident actions) gets lost when it's scattered and loses to features one item at a time. Keep one ordered architecture backlog in the same tool as the feature backlogs; give each item the quality it protects and the cost of waiting; order it with the product owners in the same language; and agree a fixed share of capacity.

  • Family: architecture in delivery
  • Origin: agile architecture practice; technical debt management
  • Lives in: the team planning tool, next to the feature backlogs
  • Pairs with: technical debt register, cost of delay, tech-debt talks with the product owner

1The situation Larder, 21 August 2028

Architecture work is everywhere: 23 open items in the debt register, follow-ups from six ADRs, two failing fitness functions, the remaining Ordering split steps, incident actions. At every planning meeting it competes with features item by item, and loses each time.

  • Scattered work can't be compared or ordered.
  • Architecture items rarely say why they matter in product terms.
  • Without agreed capacity, urgent features always win.

2Real-life analogy the home maintenance list

A household keeps a maintenance list (roof, boiler, windows), ordered by risk, with a fixed share of the budget each year. Without the list, every year's money goes on holidays, until the roof leaks in the middle of winter.

3How it solves it one list, product language, fixed capacity

Ana merged every source into one ordered list, in the same tool as the feature backlogs. Each item names the quality it protects and the cost of waiting (in money, risk or hours), the same cost-of-delay language Grace uses. It's ordered quarterly with the product owners, inside 20 % of each team's capacity agreed with Priya. Top three for Q4: Ordering's switch in the last two cities, FF-03 failing at Friday peak, and retiring the old order export.

  1. Gather every source

    Debt register, ADRs, failing checks, migrations, incidents.

  2. Write each item in product terms

    Quality protected, cost of waiting.

  3. Keep it in the same tool

    Next to the feature backlogs.

  4. Agree a capacity share

    With product leadership.

  5. Order it with the product owners

    Quarterly, in cost-of-delay terms.

  6. Report what it bought

    Incidents avoided, costs cut, checks green.

Vocabulary for the exam

Architecture backlog
An ordered list of architecture work.
Technical debt
Shortcuts that make future change harder.
Cost of waiting
What delay costs: money, risk, time.
Capacity allocation
A fixed share of team time for a kind of work.
Enabler
Work that enables future features (SAFe term).
Follow-up
Work an ADR committed to after the decision.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • Architecture work done, not just listed.
  • Comparable with features in product terms.
  • Capacity protected.
  • Product owners help order it.
  • Results reported.

What we lose

  • 20 % less for features.
  • Writing items in product terms takes effort.
  • Quarterly ordering needs discipline.
  • Some items still wait.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Separate tool

    A wiki page nobody plans from. Fix → the same tool as features.

  • Engineer language

    “Refactor OrderService.” Fix → the quality protected and cost of waiting.

  • No capacity

    Fighting every sprint. Fix → an agreed share.

  • Never reporting

    Work invisible. Fix → say what it bought.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Any team with debt, migrations or architecture follow-ups.
  • When architecture work keeps losing to features.
  • Quarterly planning.
  • After an architecture review or incident.

Skip it when…

  • Teams with no architecture work outstanding (rare).

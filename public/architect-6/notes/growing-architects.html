Growing Other Architects

TL;DR

An architect who makes every important decision becomes the organisation's single point of failure. Growing other architects spreads the thinking: pair with engineers on decisions, let them lead reviews with you as second, give them domains where they own the one-way doors, run a guild and katas open to all, and make architecture a path on the career ladder. Your job shifts from deciding to teaching, and the organisation keeps thinking about the whole when you're not there.

1The situation Larder, 18 September 2028

Since April the top risk on the register has been: architecture depends on one person, Ana. With eight cities and five teams, every one-way door still finds its way to her. If she's ill, on holiday or leaves, decisions stop, or get made without anyone thinking about the whole.

  • One architect doesn't scale with the organisation.
  • Engineers who never decide never learn to.
  • Knowledge in one head leaves with that person.

2Real-life analogy growing sous-chefs

A head chef grows her sous-chefs: first they cook beside her, then they run a station, then they design a dish while she tastes, then they run the kitchen on her day off. She stops cooking every plate and starts teaching, and the kitchen gets better, not worse.

3How it solves it pair, lead, own; a guild and a path

From May, Ana grew Cho (payments and data) and Dee (mobile and customer apps): pairing on ADRs; leading reviews with Ana second; running the monthly kata; then owning one-way doors in their domains alone. A fortnightly architecture guild is open to any engineer, and the career ladder now has an architecture path. In September Cho led ADR 0029 (a third payment method) without Ana. Risk #1 dropped from 4 × 4 to 2 × 4.

  1. Pick people with the interest

    Engineers who already think about the whole.

  2. Pair on real decisions

    ADRs, reviews, workshops.

  3. Let them lead, with you second

    And give feedback afterwards.

  4. Give them domains

    Where they own the one-way doors.

  5. Open the practice to all

    A guild, katas, shared reviews.

  6. Make it a career path

    Recognised in the ladder and pay.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • No single point of failure.
  • Decisions closer to teams.
  • Engineers grow and stay.
  • Ana thinks about the whole again.
  • Better decisions, from more viewpoints.

What we lose

  • Slower decisions while people learn.
  • Some decisions differ from Ana's.
  • Teaching takes time.
  • Pay and titles must follow.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Delegating without teaching

    “You decide now.” Fix → pair first, then lead with you second.

  • Only titles

    Architects in name, Ana decides. Fix → real one-way doors to own.

  • A closed club

    Architecture for the chosen few. Fix → a guild open to all.

  • No career path

    They grow, then leave. Fix → a recognised path and pay.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • When the architect is a bottleneck or a single point of failure.
  • Growing organisations.
  • Engineers asking how to grow beyond their team.
  • Before the architect moves on.

Skip it when…

  • Never; even a team of one benefits from a second opinion.

8Self-check quiz

What's the sequence for growing an architect?

Pair, lead with you as second, then own a domain's one-way doors.

Who did Ana grow?

Cho (payments and data) and Dee (mobile and customer apps).

What showed it worked?

Cho led ADR 0029 alone; risk #1 dropped to 2 × 4.

Why a career path?

So people who grow are recognised and stay.

try it

Pick one engineer who thinks about the whole system. Pair with them on your next decision, let them lead the one after with you as second, and agree a domain they'll own within six months.

remember!!

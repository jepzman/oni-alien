Influencing Without Authority

TL;DR

Architects rarely have authority over the teams whose behaviour they need to change. Influence works through their interests (what do they need?), an easier path (make the right way less work than the old one), allies they already trust, and credit (their name on the result). Escalation is a last resort: it may win once and cost the relationship for years.

  • Family: influence
  • Origin: Allan Cohen and David Bradford, Influence Without Authority (1990); Robert Cialdini
  • Lives in: conversations, and the paths you build
  • Pairs with: golden paths, negotiating with stakeholders, architecture principles

1The situation Larder, 7 August 2028

Two teams still read Larder Core's order tables every night through the old export: the data team for reporting and finance for the monthly close. Neither reports to Ana. FF-11 now flags it, and Ana could escalate to Femke, but forcing them would make enemies of two teams she needs for years.

  • Architects advise; teams decide.
  • Rules imposed from above get followed minimally.
  • Escalation wins once and costs trust.

2Real-life analogy the planner and the square

A town planner wants people to stop parking on the square. Instead of fines, she asks the shopkeepers what they need, builds a car park two minutes away with a free first hour, and lets the shopkeepers announce it. The square empties by itself, and the shopkeepers thank her.

3How it solves it needs, an easier path, allies, credit

Ana first asked what they needed: finance needs every order by 06:00 on the first working day; the data team needs history. She made the new way easier: order events loaded into the warehouse with a ready-made order model, faster than the export and with history. She found an ally, Cho, whose payment events finance already trusted. She let the data team lead present it at the engineering meeting and gave them the credit. The old export was retired on 2 October, without an escalation.

  1. Ask what they need

    Their goals and fears, not your rule.

  2. Make the new way easier

    Less work, faster, better than the old.

  3. Find allies they trust

    Peers, not managers.

  4. Let them own it

    Their team builds or presents it.

  5. Give credit away

    Their name on the win.

  6. Escalate last

    Only after the easy path exists and is refused.

Vocabulary for the exam

Influence
Changing what others do without authority over them.
Currencies
What each side values and can trade (Cohen and Bradford).
Ally
Someone the other side trusts who supports the change.
Easy path
Making the desired behaviour the simplest one.
Escalation
Asking someone with authority to decide.
Credit
Public recognition for the result.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • Change that sticks.
  • Relationships kept.
  • Better solutions from their needs.
  • Allies for next time.
  • No escalation needed.

What we lose

  • Slower than an order.
  • Building the easy path takes work.
  • Credit given away.
  • Doesn't always work.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Leading with the rule

    “FF-11 says you must.” Fix → start with their needs.

  • No easier path

    Asking them to do more work. Fix → make the new way easier.

  • Keeping the credit

    “Architecture fixed it.” Fix → their name on it.

  • Escalating early

    Femke decides on day one. Fix → escalate last.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • Changes across teams you don't manage.
  • Retiring old integrations others depend on.
  • Adopting principles, templates or platforms.
  • Any time you're tempted to escalate.

Skip it when…

  • Safety, legal or security emergencies: act, then explain.

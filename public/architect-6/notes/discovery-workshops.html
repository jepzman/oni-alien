Discovery Workshops

TL;DR

A discovery workshop brings the people who know the business, the customers, operations, the law and the systems into one room to explore a new idea before anyone designs it. Map the journey and the events, surface the riskiest assumptions, sketch a few options (from manual to fully built), and leave with a cheap test rather than a design. The architect's job is to facilitate and to make the technical options and costs visible.

  • Family: influence
  • Origin: event storming (Alberto Brandolini), design sprints, lean discovery
  • Lives in: a one- or two-day workshop and a short write-up
  • Pairs with: domain discovery (event storming), assumption mapping, spike vs prototype vs PoC

1The situation Larder, 3 July 2028

Femke has met a rail company and wants Larder lockers at train stations: order on the train, collect on the way home. She asks Ana for a technical design by Friday. Nobody has asked customers, Joost, Lena or Marco, and nobody knows which assumptions matter.

  • Designs made too early solve the wrong problem.
  • Each function knows a different risk.
  • Big ideas need a cheap test before a build.

2Real-life analogy everyone at the map

Before an expedition chooses a route, the guide, the doctor, the cook and the sponsor stand around the map. Each marks what they know and what worries them: a river crossing, altitude sickness, how much food fits, how much it costs. Only then do they choose, and they choose a short trial walk first.

3How it solves it the right people, assumptions, options, a test

Instead of a design, Ana ran a two-day workshop with Femke, Joost, Grace, Lena, Marco, Eli and a rail company manager. Day one: the customer journey and an event storm of a locker order; the riskiest assumptions: do commuters want it, can a locker keep chilled food safe for two hours, who pays for the lockers. Day two: three options from manual to fully integrated, and a test. Outcome: a six-week pilot with two chilled lockers at Utrecht Centraal, orders routed by hand, no new systems.

  1. Invite every kind of knowledge

    Business, customers, operations, legal, engineering, partners.

  2. Start from the customer journey

    And the events in the domain.

  3. Surface the riskiest assumptions

    What must be true; how sure are we?

  4. Sketch options

    From manual to fully built, with rough costs.

  5. Choose a cheap test

    For the riskiest assumptions.

  6. Write it up in a page

    Assumptions, options, the test, the decision date.

Vocabulary for the exam

Discovery

# Discovery: station lockers · 3–4 Jul 2028 · facilitated by Ana
With: Femke, Joost, Grace, Lena, Marco, Eli, a rail company manager

Riskiest assumptions                               confidence   test
  A1 commuters will order for station pick-up       low          pilot demand
  A2 a locker keeps chilled food safe for 2 h       medium       temperature logging
  A3 the rail company pays for lockers, we pay rent  low          contract talk (Femke)
  A4 lockers fit our dispatch (vans before 15:00)   high         —
Options   1 manual: lockers, orders flagged, couriers by hand (≈ €8k)
          2 semi: locker codes by SMS from Ordering (≈ 4 weeks)
          3 integrated: locker partner API, slots, live status (≈ 3 months)
Decision  option 1 as a 6-week pilot, 2 chilled lockers, Utrecht Centraal, from 4 Sep
          review 16 Oct: A1 and A2 results → option 2 or stop

tools: a room, sticky notes or a whiteboard tool, a journey map, event storming, an assumption map; a one-page write-up.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • The right questions before designs.
  • Every function's risks on one wall.
  • A cheap test instead of a big build.
  • Shared ownership of the idea.
  • Faster than design-review cycles.

What we lose

  • Two days of busy people.
  • Needs good facilitation.
  • Leaders may want an answer, not a test.
  • Partners in the room need care.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Designing first

    A solution by Friday. Fix → explore before designing.

  • Only engineers

    Operations and legal missing. Fix → every kind of knowledge.

  • No assumptions

    Everything taken as true. Fix → map the riskiest.

  • A design as the output

    Months of work agreed. Fix → a cheap test.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • New business ideas and partnerships.
  • When leaders bring a solution, not a problem.
  • Before big investments.
  • Cross-functional changes.

Skip it when…

  • Small, well-understood changes.

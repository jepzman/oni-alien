Go-Live Readiness

TL;DR

Go-live readiness means agreeing, well before the date, what ready means (criteria for the product, the platform, operations, support and rollback), checking each with evidence rather than opinion, and making a clear go / no-go decision with named decision makers. If a criterion is red, you fix it, accept it in writing, or wait. It turns a round of “we're fine” into a decision.

1The situation Larder, 12 June 2028

Ghent opens on 19 June, Larder's seventh city and second in Belgium. A week before, the go-live meeting is a round of “we're fine”: each team reports green on its own part. Nobody has agreed what ready means, and the last two city launches each had a surprise on day one.

  • Each team checks its own part; nobody checks the whole.
  • “Green” by opinion hides untested things.
  • Without criteria, go/no-go becomes a mood.

2Real-life analogy the pre-take-off checklist

Before take-off a pilot reads a checklist: each item read aloud and confirmed by looking, not by memory. It was agreed long before the flight, and if any item fails, the plane doesn't go, however late it's running.

3How it solves it criteria early, evidence for each, a clear decision

Ana and Sanne agreed criteria a week before the go/no-go call, each needing evidence: a test order end to end in the configured store; 3× expected load on the new Ordering database; Bancontact live with a real €1 order; picker offline mode tested in the store; age rules set to 16 and 18; Dutch support macros; on-call and rollback rehearsed. On 16 June one was red: Dutch help pages. Fixed by the 18th; go. Ghent opened on 19 June with no day-one surprise.

  1. Agree criteria early

    Product, platform, operations, support, rollback.

  2. Require evidence

    A test, a real transaction, a rehearsal.

  3. Name the decision makers

    Who says go or no-go.

  4. Check a few days before

    Time to fix reds.

  5. Decide clearly

    Go, go with accepted risks in writing, or wait.

  6. Review after launch

    Update the criteria for next time.

Vocabulary for the exam

Readiness criteria
What must be true to go live.
Go / no-go
The decision to launch or wait.
Evidence
Proof that a criterion is met.
Accepted risk
A known gap, accepted in writing by a named person.
Operational readiness
Operations and support prepared to run it.
Dress rehearsal
A full practice run.

4Gains vs losses nothing is free

What we gain

  • Ready by evidence, not opinion.
  • The whole checked, not just parts.
  • Reds found with time to fix.
  • A clear decision with named owners.
  • Better each launch.

What we lose

  • Preparation work before launch.
  • Real tests in production need care.
  • A no-go can be unpopular.
  • Checklists can grow too long.

6Common pitfalls the usual ways this goes wrong

  • Green by opinion

    “We're fine.” Fix → evidence per criterion.

  • Criteria the day before

    No time to fix reds. Fix → agree a week or more ahead.

  • Parts, not the whole

    Each team checks itself. Fix → an end-to-end test order.

  • Nobody decides

    A mood, not a decision. Fix → named deciders.

7When to use it (and when not)

Use it when…

  • City or market launches.
  • Big migrations' switch days.
  • New products and client go-lives.
  • Any launch that's had day-one surprises.

Skip it when…

  • Small, flag-protected releases (the flag is the rollback).

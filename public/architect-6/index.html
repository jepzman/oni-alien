the solution architect's notebook · book 1 · vol. 6

Delivery, Governance & Influence

Volume 6 of The Solution Architect's Notebook, the last: how architects work at scale, through others. 15 notes in four collections: architecture in delivery, governance, risk and transition, and influence. Every note follows Ana at Larder from April to September 2028, as the platform grows to eight cities, and ends with a try it exercise for your own project.

the story so far

April 2028. Larder has five product teams, thirty engineers, six cities and a board plan for eight by the end of the year, with 60,000 orders a day. Ana can no longer review every change, attend every design or carry every decision. This volume follows her from April to September 2028: designing just enough, governing with principles and guardrails instead of gates, taking risks and migrations to go-live and hand-over, and leading through influence, including growing the architects who come after her.

Architecture in delivery design enough, evolve the rest, keep a backlog

  1. 01Just-Enough Up-Front DesignDecide the hard-to-change things before building; leave the rest to learn while building.p. 01
  2. 02Evolutionary ArchitectureDesign for change: small steps, fitness functions that guard what matters, and room to turn.p. 02
  3. 03The Architecture BacklogOne ordered list of architecture work, planned with product, with capacity agreed.p. 03

Governance principles and guardrails instead of gates

  1. 04Architecture PrinciplesA handful of rules with reasons and implications, so teams decide alike without asking.p. 04
  2. 05Guardrails Not GatesAutomate the checks that matter and review only one-way doors, so teams move without waiting.p. 05
  3. 06Lightweight Architecture ReviewA short, written-first review for decisions that are hard to undo, with a clear outcome.p. 06
  4. 07Golden PathsMake the right way the easy way: templates and platforms that start a service well.p. 07

Risk & transition from risk list to migration, go-live and hand-over

  1. 08Risk RegisterA short, owned list of risks with likelihood, impact, actions and a date to look again.p. 08
  2. 09Migration PlanningMove a live system in reversible steps, each with a test, a rollback and a stop point.p. 09
  3. 10Go-Live ReadinessAgree in advance what “ready” means, check it with evidence, and decide go or no-go.p. 10
  4. 11Handover to OperationsHand a system to the people who'll run it with runbooks, alerts, practice and a warranty period.p. 11

Influence lead without authority, and grow the next architects

  1. 12Influencing Without AuthorityChange what teams do by making it their idea, their win and the easy path.p. 12
  2. 13Negotiating with StakeholdersTrade on interests and options when architecture needs time, money or a change of plan.p. 13
  3. 14Discovery WorkshopsBring business, product and engineering into one room to explore a new idea before deciding.p. 14
  4. 15Growing Other ArchitectsSpread architecture thinking: pairing, katas, decision ownership and a path for engineers.p. 15

★The nine architect ratings what a good architecture practice is judged on

Every note scores the pattern from 1 (poor) to 5 (great) on the same nine things, comparing an app without the pattern to the same app with it.

Clarity
A newcomer can understand the system and the reasoning behind it.
Stakeholder alignment
Business, product and engineering share the same what and why.
Decision speed
How quickly the team can choose and get back to building.
Risk reduction
The big risks are surfaced and handled before they bite.
Adaptability
How cheaply the direction can change later.
Traceability
Months later, anyone can find why something was done.
Cost awareness
Money and effort are part of the decision, not a surprise.
Low overhead
Time and ceremony it takes. High = light.
Team autonomy
Teams can decide and act without waiting for the architect.
